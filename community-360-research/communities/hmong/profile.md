# Hmong in Minnesota

## 1. People and place

**How many.** Minnesota Compass, using the American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024, counts 102,491 Hmong Minnesotans, give or take 5,546. It places 93.8 percent in the Twin Cities and 6.2 percent in Greater Minnesota ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/hmong)). Pew Research Center, counting only people who name Hmong alone in the 2021 to 2023 survey, reports about 95,000 in Minnesota, second to about 110,000 in California and ahead of about 55,000 in Wisconsin ([Pew, May 1, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-hmong-in-the-u-s/)). The two counts differ because they ask different questions, and both are estimates. Compass also calls Hmong people the state's largest Asian cultural community ([Minnesota Compass, May 30, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-asian-communities)).

**Young, and mostly born here.** In the same Compass profile, 38.0 percent are 17 or younger and 3.7 percent are 65 or older. Foreign-born residents are 31.5 percent, give or take 1.9 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/hmong)). Pew puts the national median age at 26.8 ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-hmong-in-the-u-s/)).

**Where.** More than half of Hmong Minnesotans live in Ramsey County, MPR News reported in 2024 ([MPR News, March 6, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2024/03/06/designs-take-shape-for-first-hmong-community-center)). Lee Pao Xiong estimated about 36,000 Hmong people in St. Paul itself in early 2026; that is one scholar's estimate ([MPR News, Jan. 2, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/01/02/first-woman-hmong-american-kaohly-her-to-lead-st-paul-as-new-mayor)). Sahan Journal describes Maplewood as about 20 percent Hmong ([Sahan Journal, Oct. 19, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/democracy-politics/maplewood-minnesota-mayoral-race-2026/)). Named places in daily life include Frogtown, the East Side and University Avenue's Little Mekong in St. Paul, plus Maplewood, Brooklyn Center, Brooklyn Park and Coon Rapids, where schools and churches are found (sections 5 and 10). In southwest Minnesota, a local organizer put the region's Hmong population at "a little bit over 1,000" in October 2026, centered on Marshall, Tracy and Walnut Grove ([Marshall Independent, Oct. 20, 2026](https://www.marshallindependent.com/news/local-news/2026/10/a-colorful-new-year-affair/); [United Hmong Southwest Minnesota](https://www.unitedhmongswmn.org/)). Students at the University of Minnesota Duluth organized a Twin Ports New Year in 2025, and attendees described a small community where people know one another ([WDIO, 2025](https://www.wdio.com/?p=701972)).

**How people came.** The Minnesota Historical Society's MNopedia dates the first family, Dang Her and Shoua Moua, to November 5, 1975 ([MNopedia, last modified Aug. 12, 2026](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/hmong-and-hmong-americans-minnesota)). The Ramsey County Historical Society dates the same family to December 1975 and notes that Dang Her came as a parolee because of his U.S. aid work, while Leng Wong's family became the first Hmong refugees resettled here in 1976 ([Ramsey County History, Spring 2025](https://rchs.com/publishing/catalog/hmong-lives-in-ramsey-county/)). CBS gives Wong's arrival as February 7, 1976 ([WCCO, May 5, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/leng-wong-first-minnesota-hmong-refugee-vietnam-war/)). Behind these arrivals is the CIA-backed Secret War in Laos. About 2,500 people were airlifted from Long Tieng on May 12 to 14, 1975, and many others left on foot or by vehicle for Thailand ([Sahan Journal, June 16, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)).

Federal policy spread families across the country, and many then moved to join clan members ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/hmong-and-hmong-americans-minnesota)). A 1984 study cited jobs, public housing and welfare benefits as reasons people chose Minnesota, and Lutheran Social Services, Catholic Charities and World Relief offered early help ([Star Tribune via Sahan Journal, Sept. 8, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/news-partners/minnesota-how-did-hmong-people-become-largest-asian-group-in-minnesota-curious-minnesota/)).

**Generations and languages.** Sahan Journal describes elders who remember the war and camps alongside U.S.-born children and grandchildren with less exposure to the language and the history ([Sahan Journal, June 16, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)). Among Hmong Minnesotans 5 and older, 75.5 percent speak a language other than English at home and 31.1 percent speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/hmong)). Hmong ranks among the state's top three non-English languages, with Spanish and Somali ([Sahan Journal, Sept. 22, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-non-english-languages-census-data/)). The principal of St. Paul's Hmong language school said in 2024 that almost none of her students now speak Hmong at home, which is why many families seek the school out ([Sahan Journal, Jan. 23, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/education/st-paul-schools-halt-enrollment-slide-hmong-spanish-east-african-programs/)).

## 2. All walks of life

No single number describes this community. All figures below are from the Compass 2020 to 2024 profile unless noted, in 2024 dollars ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/hmong)).

- **Comfortable and well-off households.** Median household income is $92,644, give or take $6,476. Nearly half of households, 46.9 percent, take in $100,000 or more. Large households matter here: the average is 4.27 people. The state demographer's analysis for 2016 to 2020 showed a median of about $74,000 per household but about $38,900 per individual earner ([Sahan Journal, Sept. 25, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-indian-asian-minnesota-us-census-data-income-poverty/)).
- **Professionals and owners.** Among adults 25 and older, 28.8 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more, including 8.0 percent with a graduate or professional degree. Visible careers include St. Paul's mayor, Kaohly Her, who began in finance ([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/01/02/first-woman-hmong-american-kaohly-her-to-lead-st-paul-as-new-mayor)); Judge Sophia Vuelo, described as the first state-appointed judge of Hmong descent ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)); five Hmong family doctors who came to practice together at one Allina clinic ([Allina Health, April 25, 2024](https://www.allinahealth.org/allina-news/2024/04/the-importance-of-belonging-for-providers-and-patients)); and chefs Yia Vang and Diane Moua ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine, March 15, 2026](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/the-best-restaurants-in-the-twin-cities-2026/)).
- **Middle-income and working families.** Owner-occupied homes are 62.1 percent of households. Employment among adults 18 to 64 is 78.0 percent, 80.7 percent for men and 75.1 percent for women. Another 22.3 percent of people live between the poverty line and twice that line, above poverty but not by much.
- **People in poverty.** Ten percent of people live below the poverty line, give or take 2.7 points. That includes 13.3 percent of children and 18.7 percent of people 55 to 64, though that second figure has a wide margin of 7.1 points. Among renters, 40.3 percent spend too much of their income on housing. And 17.4 percent of adults 25 and older did not finish high school. The City of St. Paul said in January 2025 that about half of some 40 people at an encampment near the Bruce Vento Nature Sanctuary identified as Hmong or Southeast Asian, and that a separate East Side camp also had a large Hmong population ([Sahan Journal, Jan. 10, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/housing/st-paul-homeless-encampment-hmong-eviction/)).

**Over time.** MNopedia's account, drawing on 2010 figures, describes about a quarter of Hmong Minnesotans below the poverty line ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/hmong-and-hmong-americans-minnesota)). Compass reported median household income of $77,500 and poverty of 15 percent in its 2024 article ([Minnesota Compass, May 30, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-asian-communities)). Survey periods overlap and margins are wide, so these show a direction rather than exact change. A National CAPACD metro profile, posted in 2025, reports 17.3 percent poverty among Hmong residents of the Minneapolis-St. Paul metro, without a data year in the part read ([National CAPACD](https://www.nationalcapacd.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/18.-MN-WI-Metro.pdf)).

No Minnesota figure for Hmong household wealth could be found. Income and home ownership are the nearest available measures.

## 3. Work and the economy

**Farming.** MNopedia records 1980s farm cooperatives in Homer and in Oakdale, later Farmington. They ended by 1985, but many participants went on to rent or buy land and sell at farmers markets ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/hmong-and-hmong-americans-minnesota)). The Hmong American Farmers Association, formed in 2011, closed on a farm of more than 150 acres in Vermillion Township in September 2022, with $2 million from the Legislature. In 2023 about 100 farmers worked there, and the reporting said about half of Twin Cities farmers market vendors are of Hmong descent ([MPR News via Sahan Journal, Sept. 18, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/news-partners/minnesota-dakota-county-hmong-immigrants-farming-hafa-farm/)). The association was still selling Thanksgiving shares this fall ([HAFA](https://www.hmongfarmers.com/)).

**Early jobs and today.** Sahan Journal describes early work as low-wage and physically demanding ([Sahan Journal, June 16, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)). Hmong American Partnership now runs career and technical training through HAP Academy OIC ([HAP](https://www.hmong.org/)).

**Entrepreneurs.** MNopedia dates the Minnesota Hmong Chamber of Commerce to 1996 and reports over 150 members, $100 million in annual business revenue and $1 billion in spending power, all as community estimates of unstated date ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/hmong-and-hmong-americans-minnesota)). Current figures could not be confirmed, and the chamber's own website was only a placeholder when checked ([mnhcc.org](https://www.mnhcc.org/)).

**Unions.** No Minnesota source was found on Hmong union membership or labor organizing, and no occupation table for Hmong Minnesotans turned up either.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

A fuller list, with what shows each place is operating, accompanies this profile. Here is the shape of it.

**The two big markets, one in trouble.** HmongTown Marketplace, 217 Como Avenue, opened in 2004 with more than 200 vendors. On October 7, 2026, MPR News and Sahan Journal reported that Stearns Bank had sued owner Toua Xiong in mid-September over about $6.6 million in loans, interest and late fees, tied partly to his purchase of the former Maplewood Mall Sears as a planned "Hmongtown 2." The bank has proposed a receiver, and a hearing is set for November 24. Xiong has filed counterclaims alleging fraud, and his attorney says the sides are talking. Reporting says the market could close but has not announced closing. Vendors described it as their main income and a gathering place for elders ([MPR News/Sahan Journal, Oct. 7, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/07/hmongtown-marketplace-faces-foreclosure-leaving-st-paul-vendors-uncertain)). The market's site lists over 125 stores and 11 restaurants, and has shown temporary hours since January 17, 2026, because of "community safety concerns" ([HmongTown Marketplace](https://hmongtownmarketplace.com/)).

Hmong Village, 1001 Johnson Parkway, opened in 2009 by MNopedia's account or 2010 by Racket's ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/hmong-and-hmong-americans-minnesota); [Racket, Dec. 13, 2023](https://racketmn.com/how-hmong-village-changed-what-it-meant-to-be-hmong-in-st-paul)). Woven Magazine in June 2026 counted more than 250 vendors and a food court ([Woven, June 30, 2026](https://wovenmag.com/where-to-eat-hmong-food-in-the-twin-cities/)). St. Paul's mayor said in early 2026 that business there was down 60 to 70 percent during the immigration surge; this comes from a Stop AAPI Hate report quoting her ([Stop AAPI Hate, February 2026](https://stopaapihate.org/2026/02/11/keeping-count-who-are-hmong-americans-whats-happening-in-minnesota-explained/)).

**Food.** Vinai in northeast Minneapolis is Yia Vang's restaurant, and it lists current hours ([Vinai](https://www.vinaimn.com/)). His earlier Union Hmong Kitchen says it has closed its Uptown and North Loop locations and now appears at the State Fair, where its Hmong corndog was sold in 2026 ([Union Hmong Kitchen](https://www.unionkitchenmn.com/); [Sahan Journal, Aug. 27, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/minnesota-state-fair-new-foods-reviews-2026/)). Diane's Place is Diane Moua's northeast Minneapolis restaurant ([Diane's Place](https://dianesplacemn.com/)). In St. Paul, Marc Heu Patisserie Paris, run by a French-born Hmong pastry chef and his partner, and SoYen Desserts in Lowertown mix European and Hmong influences ([Sahan Journal, June 2, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/marc-heu-patisserie-paris-st-paul-dale-street-hmong-chef/); [Sahan Journal, March 29, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/soyen-desserts-st-paul-yen-fang-asian-bakery/)).

**Shops, law, funeral and media.** Billy's Hmong Shop in Maplewood Mall sells handsewn funeral garments ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 26, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=86945)). Moua Law Office on St. Paul's East Side advertises attorneys bilingual in English and Hmong ([Moua Law Office](https://www.moualawoffice.com/)). Hmong Times covers Hmong Minnesota ([Hmong Times](https://hmongtimes.com/)). Rainbow Garden Farm in Harris opened to visitors in 2023 ([Sahan Journal, Oct. 27, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/rainbow-garden-farm-hmong-owned-harris-minnesota/)).

**Little Mekong.** The district on University Avenue is presented by the Asian Economic Development Association and is pan-Asian rather than only Hmong. The latest night market dates on its own page were June 28 and 29, 2025 ([Little Mekong](https://www.littlemekong.com/night-market)).

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

**Traditional practice.** Sahan Journal describes a shaman or household head performing soul-calling at New Year to bring family members home, with food offerings to ancestors ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 29, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minnesota-hmong-new-year-st-paul/)). A Maplewood shaman held a ceremony in 2008 to clear spirits from St. Paul's former Hmong funeral home ahead of planned demolition, with community groups helping to organize it ([MPR News, July 31, 2008](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2008/07/31/shaman)).

**Funerals in practice.** The Hmong Funeral Home at 625 Dale Street opened in 1994 and, as the only one of its kind in the Twin Cities at the time, sometimes had families waiting weeks. The owner closed it in late 2007 ([MPR News, July 31, 2008](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2008/07/31/shaman)). A Hmong Funeral Home is licensed today at 199 Plato Boulevard East, per a directory listing, without dated proof of current operation ([Funeralocity](https://www.funeralocity.com/funeral-homes/mn/saint-paul/hmong-funeral-home-45554)). Traditional funerals lean on community members who guide the rites rather than a funeral director, and songs can take years to master. In 2013 the Hmong Cultural Center ran a Sunday class in funeral procedures and qeej ([MPR News, May 6, 2013](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2013/05/06/preserving-a-tradition-that-prepares-hmong-souls-for-eternity)). The center still lists wedding and funeral procedure classes online ([Hmong Cultural Center](https://www.hmongcc.org/)). Hospitals are starting to respond: North Memorial Health offers handsewn funeral garments at no cost at its Robbinsdale and Maple Grove hospitals, through a $25,000 grant and a partnership with Billy's Hmong Shop. The shop owner explains that families traditionally receive these garments from their parents at marriage, and that the garments are understood to let the soul return to the ancestral homeland ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 26, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=86945)).

**Christian life.** The Christian and Missionary Alliance's Hmong District lists 15 Minnesota congregations, in Maplewood, Coon Rapids, Brooklyn Center, St. Paul, Spring Lake Park and Walnut Grove ([Hmong District](https://www.hmongdistrict.org/area-04-1)). Hmong Catholics found a home at St. Vincent de Paul in Frogtown in January 1995, where services were held in Hmong. The community went through a painful split in 2003. The church closed on June 30, 2024, and the congregation moved to a new home the account does not name ([Hmong Museum story map](https://www.storymaps.hmongmuseummn.org/stories/st-vincent-de-paul-hmong-catholic-church)).

**Differences within.** No current, reliable share of Hmong Minnesotans who are Christian, traditional, both or neither could be found, and a guess would not help anyone. Sahan Journal's 2024 New Year story quotes the organizer's president calling the celebration "a way to honor our ancestors" ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minnesota-hmong-new-year-st-paul/)). Nothing was found on Hmong Minnesotans with no faith.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

**Minnesota Hmong New Year, November 28 and 29, 2026.** Saint Paul RiverCentre, 8 a.m. to 8 p.m., hosted by The United Hmong Family, Inc. ([RiverCentre](https://www.rivercentre.org/events/detail/minnesota-hmong-new-year-3)). Traditionally called Noj Peb Caug, or "eat 30," it marks the end of the harvest. The public opening includes a symbolic sweeping away of the old year ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 29, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minnesota-hmong-new-year-st-paul/)). Expect dance and singing competitions, the Hmong Minnesota Pageant and a ball toss ([Visit Saint Paul](https://www.visitsaintpaul.com/blog/guide-to-hmong-new-year/)). The 2025 event was the 45th ([RiverCentre, 2025](https://www.rivercentre.org/news/detail/minnesota-hmong-new-year-returns-to-saint-paul-rivercentre-november-29-30-2025)). More than 26,000 attended by RiverCentre's count ([RiverCentre, Dec. 19, 2025](https://www.rivercentre.org/news/detail/celebrating-culture-and-community)). The organizer's testimony in March 2025 cited over 31,000 in two days and 106 vendors ([Senate testimony, March 25, 2025](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2025/envlegacy/20250325/envlegacy_20250325_SF2590-Maycee-Vang-testimony.pdf)). That same testimony says the group became an official nonprofit in 2017 and that the New Year has been held in Minnesota for 44 years.

**Is there more than one?** A search for a rival Twin Cities event, including anything called Hmong American New Year or held at another large venue, found no 2026 announcement of one. What did turn up are smaller New Years with their own dates: United Hmong Southwest Minnesota at Red Baron Arena in Marshall on October 17, 2026 ([United Hmong Southwest Minnesota](https://www.unitedhmongswmn.org/)); Brooklyn Park on December 13, 2025, with no 2026 date found ([CCX Media](https://ccxmedia.org/event/brooklylns-hmong-new-year-celebration)); and the University of Minnesota Duluth Hmong Student Association's event in the Twin Ports in 2025 ([WDIO](https://www.wdio.com/?p=701972)).

**Hmong Special Guerrilla Units Remembrance Day, May 14.** This is a Minnesota statute, first enacted in 2019. It marks May 14, 1975, directs the governor to issue a proclamation and to fly flags at half-staff on Capitol grounds, encourages schools to teach Hmong history, and says businesses may close and "an employee may request the day off" ([Minn. Stat. 10.5805](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/10.5805)). St. Paul Public Schools marks May 14 as Hmong American Day and says the state formally recognized that name in 2013 ([SPPS](https://www.spps.org/families/family-engagement-community-partnerships/parent-advisory-councils/hmong-parent-advisory-council/hmong-american-day)). A 2026 bill for a Hmong Heritage Week, May 14 to 20, was at the introduction stage when posted; whether it passed could not be established ([HF 4734](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/bills/94/2026/0/HF/4734/versions/latest/)).

**Hmong American Day Festival.** A community calendar lists May 16 and 17, 2026, at the Pan Asian Center in Maplewood ([Northern Star Scouting, April 2026](https://www.northernstar.org/Portals/0/2026%20Outreach_Web_Cultural%20Events_260415a.pdf)). Mpls.St.Paul Magazine placed the 2025 festival at the State Capitol ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine, May 15, 2025](https://mspmag.com/arts-and-culture/hmong-americans-celebrate-50-years-in-minnesota/)).

**Hmong International Freedom Festival, or "J4."** The 2026 event was June 27 and 28 at Como Park's McMurray Field, billed as the 44th and marking 50 years of Hmong freedom in Minnesota ([Visit Saint Paul](https://www.visitsaintpaul.com/blog/guide-to-the-hmong-international-freedom-festival/)). KARE 11 reported tens of thousands on the first day ([KARE 11 via Yahoo, June 28, 2026](https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/articles/hmong-international-freedom-festival-held-025231411.html)). Sports include kato, or sepak takraw, soccer, volleyball, flag football and tuj lub. Attendance claims vary: 35,507 in 2023 from a University of Minnesota Extension survey, as reported by Hoodline ([Hoodline, June 22, 2026](https://hoodline.com/2026/06/hmong-freedom-fest-to-swamp-st-paul-with-55-000-fans/)); about 57,000 in 2024 per the organizer ([Sahan Journal, June 28, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/hmong-international-freedom-fest-2025-como-park-st-paul/)). Origins vary too. Visit Saint Paul says the 1970s; the Park Bugle wrote in 2013 that it began in 1980 on Harriet Island and was then run by Lao Family Community of Minnesota ([Park Bugle, June 25, 2013](https://parkbugle.org/3981/news/hmong-freedom-celebration-will-fill-como-regional-park-july-6-7/)). It was cancelled in 2020 and 2021 ([WCCO, June 30, 2021](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/hmong-freedom-festival-canceled-for-second-straight-year/)). By 2023 it was run by The United Hmong Family ([Sahan Journal, June 30, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/culture-community/hmong-interntional-freedom-festival-j4-soccer-tournament-st-paul-como-park/)). A link to J.K. Sports as a past organizer could not be confirmed. How the event passed from one organizer to the other also could not be documented.

**Hmong Minnesota Day at the State Fair.** Held on Labor Day, the fair's last day. Begun in 2015 by Tou Ger Bennett Xiong and Liz Xiong, it helped set a Labor Day attendance record that year, according to the fair's general manager ([MPR News, Sept. 2, 2016](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2016/09/02/big-day-at-fair-minnesota-hmong)). In 2026 it ran September 7 at Dan Patch Park, 10 a.m. to 5 p.m., the 11th year by the fair's count ([Minnesota State Fair](https://www.mnstatefair.org/schedule/hmong-mn-day-at-dan-patch-park/); [Hmong Times](https://hmongtimes.com/minnesota-state-fair/)). The Hmong Museum demonstrated backstrap loom and paj ntaub at the Creative Arts Building the same day ([Hmong Museum](https://www.hmongmuseummn.org/programs)).

**Qeej and Hmong Arts Festival.** Free, at Union Depot, May 10, 2026, the fifth year, run through the Hmong Cultural Center, with a record 2,438 people ([Sahan Journal, May 8, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/twin-cities-things-to-do-nahuatl-book-hmong-qeej-fest-somali-museum/); [Hmong Times](https://hmongtimes.com/a-successful-2026-qeej-and-hmong-arts-festival/)).

**Hmong Arts Celebration at Mall of America.** October 24, 2026, its second year ([Minnesota Parent](https://www.minnesotaparent.com/event/hmong-arts-celebration-at-the-mall-of-america-2026/)).

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Households.** Average household size is 4.27. Married couples with children under 18 make up 30.0 percent of households, single mothers with children 6.6 percent, and people living alone 16.2 percent. Compass's table also reports 23.6 percent as unmarried couple households, a surprisingly high share that deserves caution ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/hmong)).

**Clan.** The United Hmong Family names the clan structure as central to its mission ([United Hmong Family](https://www.theunitedhmongfamily.org/)), and the Hmong 18 Council works with clan leaders ([Hmong 18 Council](https://www.hmong18council.org/)). A University of Minnesota stroke study relied on 18 clan surnames, in 51 spelling variants, to identify Hmong patients, because the hospital recorded only "Asian" ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 8, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/health/hmong-health-stroke-disparity-university-of-minnesota-study-dr-haitham-hussein/)).

**Changing roles.** Sahan Journal's 50-year story describes older gender norms in which boys more often went to school than girls, and current debate about gender roles and the place of LGBTQ Hmong people ([Sahan Journal, June 16, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)). The organizer of the 2026 southwest Minnesota New Year said it is harder now to get young people to take part ([Marshall Independent](https://www.marshallindependent.com/news/local-news/2026/10/a-colorful-new-year-affair/)).

**Food and dress.** Papaya salad, Hmong sausage with purple sticky rice, stuffed chicken wings and khao poon are the dishes a June 2026 guide singled out at market stalls ([Woven](https://wovenmag.com/where-to-eat-hmong-food-in-the-twin-cities/)). RiverCentre noted attendees in traditional attire at the 2025 New Year ([RiverCentre](https://www.rivercentre.org/news/detail/celebrating-culture-and-community)).

**Aging.** Only 3.7 percent of Hmong Minnesotans are 65 or older ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/hmong)), yet vendors describe HmongTown as a gathering place for elders ([MPR News/Sahan Journal](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/07/hmongtown-marketplace-faces-foreclosure-leaving-st-paul-vendors-uncertain)), and elders led the 2023 campaign after a police shooting ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/public-safety/yia-xiong-st-paul-police-shooting-minnesota-hmong-community/)). No Minnesota study of Hmong elder care was found.

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Writers and stage.** Kao Kalia Yang has written about St. Paul's East Side and the Hmong refugee experience, and her *The Song Poet* was adapted as the first Hmong story in opera ([Sahan Journal headline listing](https://sahanjournal.com/tags/hmong/)). Katie Ka Vang's *HMong Futures*, commissioned for the 50th anniversary of Hmong life in Minnesota, premiered with Theater Mu at the Gremlin Theatre, April 9 to May 3, 2026 ([American Theatre, Aug. 26, 2026](https://www.americantheatre.org/2026/08/26/whats-in-hmong-futures/)).

**Music and resistance.** In March 2026, Tou SaiKo Lee and other Hmong artists held "Interwoven" at the Pan Asian Center, with a goal of about $30,000 for families affected by the enforcement surge ([MPR News, March 18, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/03/18/hmong-artists-weave-music-poetry-and-visuals-to-help-those-impacted-by-ice-surge)).

**Institutions.** The Hmong Museum at Hmong Village is open Saturdays and Sundays, 10 to 4 ([Hmong Museum](https://www.hmongmuseummn.org/programs)). The Hmong Cultural Center runs a storefront museum and a library it calls the most comprehensive Hmong collection in North America ([Hmong Cultural Center](https://www.hmongcc.org/)). The Arboretum's "Verdant Remedies" drew on Hmong art and plant knowledge in 2025 ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine](https://mspmag.com/arts-and-culture/hmong-americans-celebrate-50-years-in-minnesota/)).

**Sports.** Kato, soccer and other tournaments at J4 draw nearly 200 teams ([Sahan Journal, June 28, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/hmong-international-freedom-fest-2025-como-park-st-paul/)). Suni Lee became the first Hmong American Olympic gold medalist, at the Tokyo Games ([Star Tribune via Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/news-partners/minnesota-how-did-hmong-people-become-largest-asian-group-in-minnesota-curious-minnesota/)).

**Media.** Hmong Times is a Minnesota Hmong newspaper with current coverage of the deportation series and the State Fair ([Hmong Times](https://hmongtimes.com/)). 3HmongTV has a Hmong-language site and YouTube channel, though the page carried no date ([3HmongTV](https://3hmongtv.com/)). HMOOB.TV shares Hmong news and video ([HMOOB.TV](https://www.hmoob.tv/articles/st-pauls-hmongtown-marketplace-faces-foreclosure-leaving-longtime-vendors-uncertain/)).

## 9. Civic and political life

**The mayor.** Kaohly Her was sworn in January 2, 2026, as St. Paul's first woman and first person of Hmong ancestry to serve as mayor, after defeating Melvin Carter on the second ballot of ranked-choice voting. She was first elected to the House in 2018 ([MPR News, Jan. 2, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/01/02/first-woman-hmong-american-kaohly-her-to-lead-st-paul-as-new-mayor)).

**Legislature.** APIAVote's Sieng Lee counted one Hmong legislator in 2015, nine by 2024 plus nine in local offices, and nearly 60,000 Hmong adults of voting age ([AAPI Data, Aug. 7, 2025](https://aapidata.com/blog/from-refugees-to-representation-cultivating-hmong-american-political-power-in-minnesota/)). MNopedia dates Choua Lee's St. Paul school board win to 1992 and Mee Moua's special election to 2002 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/hmong-and-hmong-americans-minnesota)). Cy Thao was the second Hmong American state legislator anywhere ([MPR News, May 28, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/05/28/former-minnesota-lawmaker-cy-thao-paved-the-way-for-hmong-politicians)).

**On the ballot November 3, 2026.** Chonburi Lee, a Maplewood council member, led the August mayoral primary with 44 percent to Mayor Marylee Abrams's 31 percent and could become the city's first Hmong mayor ([Sahan Journal, Oct. 19, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/democracy-politics/maplewood-minnesota-mayoral-race-2026/)).

**Veterans.** A 2026 law, Chapter 96, adds an "SGU Veteran" designation to driver's licenses starting January 1, 2027, sets an eligibility process and a civil service preference, and takes effect mostly on August 1, 2026 ([Minnesota House, New Laws 2026](https://www.house.mn.gov/NewLaws/story/2026/5772)). Gov. Walz signed it May 8, by one report; the same report counts about 900 surviving veterans and 3,500 widows, and says recognized veterans still may not be buried in a Minnesota veterans cemetery ([Monticello Times, July 4, 2026](https://www.hometownsource.com/monticello_times/secret-no-more-minnesota-expanding-recognition-benefits-for-secret-war-veterans/article_8d638f9c-8831-4c2c-baf8-888cf0ccc559.html)). Other reports described burial benefits differently ([Report for Minnesota, April 24, 2026](https://reportformn.umn.edu/minnesota-senate-approves-new-benefits-secret-war-veterans); [FOX 9, April 22, 2026](https://fox9.com/news/hmong-special-guerrilla-units-honored-mn-senate-partial-veteran-benefits-approved-april-22)), so check with the Department of Veterans Affairs before relying on any summary.

**Organizations.** Hmong American Partnership says it serves more than 25,000 immigrants and refugees ([HAP](https://www.hmong.org/)). The Hmong 18 Council received a $3 million state appropriation for a community center that MPR expected to open around the end of 2025; whether it has opened could not be confirmed ([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2024/03/06/designs-take-shape-for-first-hmong-community-center)). Other groups named in the reporting include Minnesota 8, an anti-deportation group ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 2, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/deportations-minnesota-mn8-hmong-laos-cambodia-vietnam/)).

**Debates.** Advocates object that the Census classifies Hmong people as East Asian rather than Southeast Asian, and the Bureau declined to revise 2020 data ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 16, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-hmong-community-us-census-bureau-robert-santos/)). Hmong elders led a campaign after St. Paul police fatally shot a 65-year-old Hmong man in February 2023 ([Sahan Journal, Feb. 23, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/public-safety/yia-xiong-st-paul-police-shooting-minnesota-hmong-community/)). And when DHS posted a badly translated Medical Assistance reminder in 2024, a Concordia scholar called the lack of checking "outright insulting" ([Sahan Journal, March 11, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/health/hmong-translation-minnesota-department-of-human-services/)).

## 10. Education

**Attainment.** Among adults 25 and older, 82.6 percent finished high school and 28.8 percent hold a bachelor's or higher ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/hmong)).

**St. Paul Public Schools.** Txuj Ci HMong Language and Culture, formerly Phalen Lake, had nearly 700 K-5 students in 2024, the district's largest elementary, with a Hmong studies magnet since 2008, dual-language immersion since 2011 and a middle school campus since 2022 ([Sahan Journal, Jan. 23, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/education/st-paul-schools-halt-enrollment-slide-hmong-spanish-east-african-programs/)). The pathway now runs PreK through 12, finishing at Harding or Washington Technology ([SPPS](https://www.spps.org/academics/dual-language-immersion)). In December 2025 parents planned a stay-home day over crowding and the school's future; the district was projecting a preK-8 building between 2031 and 2033 ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 8, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/education/hmong-parents-frustration-st-paul-public-schools-txuj-ci/)). How that action and the board vote turned out could not be confirmed.

**Charter schools.** Community School of Excellence in St. Paul calls itself a PreK-12 Hmong language and culture school and is enrolling for 2026-27 ([CSE](https://www.csemn.org/)). Hmong College Prep Academy, 1515 Brewster Street, K-12, has a current calendar and Hmong dance team, though its home page did not describe language instruction ([HCPA](https://www.hcpak12.org/)). Noble Academy in Brooklyn Park was founded in 2007 by Hmong leaders; a 2018 article describes Hmong literacy as a core focus ([The 74, March 27, 2018](https://www.the74million.org/article/williams-how-one-hmong-charter-school-in-minnesota-is-preserving-student-culture-raising-test-scores-and-attracting-non-hmong-students/)). Current pages for Noble Academy, Prairie Seeds Academy, HOPE Community Academy and the state charter directory could not be opened.

**Higher education.** Concordia University's Center for Hmong Studies holds thousands of research items, has hosted six international conferences and is "in a period of transition"; the library director is now its listed contact ([Concordia library, updated Aug. 31, 2026](https://library.csp.edu/chs)). Founding director Lee Pao Xiong now works at Keystone Community Services and was named to the Metropolitan Airports Commission in June 2026 ([MAC](https://metroairports.org/people/lee-pao-xiong)). The University of Minnesota's Hmong language director, Bee Vang-Moua, was quoted on translation quality in 2024 ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/health/hmong-translation-minnesota-department-of-human-services/)).

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Coverage.** 5.5 percent of Hmong Minnesotans lack health insurance ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/hmong)).

**Stroke and chronic disease.** A study at Regions Hospital of 128 Hmong stroke patients from 2010 to 2019 found them on average 11 years younger than white patients and about twice as likely to have the more dangerous bleeding type. Only 40 percent with blocked-artery strokes arrived by ambulance ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 8, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/health/hmong-health-stroke-disparity-university-of-minnesota-study-dr-haitham-hussein/)). University of Minnesota theses have looked at diabetes and healing at a St. Paul clinic, and at gout in Minnesota Hmong ([Hmong Studies Virtual Library](https://www.hmonglibrary.org/hmong-studies-virtual-library---dissertations-and-theses.html)).

**Providers.** Allina's Bandana Square Clinic has had five Hmong family doctors together, and one runs a monthly Hmong diabetes group ([Allina Health](https://www.allinahealth.org/allina-news/2024/04/the-importance-of-belonging-for-providers-and-patients)). West Side Community Health Services' SoLaHmo partnership helped carry out the state's 2014 autism study ([Minnesota Department of Health, 2014](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/2014/mandated/140404.pdf)). A current list of Hmong-owned clinics could not be confirmed.

**Healing alongside medicine.** In the state's 2014 report, some Hmong parents described autism partly in terms of a displaced soul or spirit, and one recalled grandparents asking for a hu plig, or soul-calling ceremony, and a name change for a child with delays ([Minnesota Department of Health, 2014](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/2014/mandated/140404.pdf)). The same parents were also dealing with doctors, insurers and wait lists.

**Addiction.** Sahan Journal and MPR held a 2023 community conversation on substance use among Hmong and Karen youth ([Sahan Journal headline listing](https://sahanjournal.com/tags/hmong/)).

## 12. Disability

**The basic number.** Compass reports that 8.4 percent of Hmong Minnesotans have a disability, give or take 1.2 points, and 6.4 percent of those under 65 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/hmong)). With 38 percent of the group being children and only 3.7 percent 65 or older, what does the figure say about age rather than anything else? What might it miss among elders and people answering in a second language? No study that settles either question was found.

**Autism: the picture has moved.** The University of Minnesota's monitoring project, part of the Institute on Community Integration, reported in September 2025 that 1 in 28 (3.62 percent) of Hmong 8-year-olds were identified with autism, using 2022 data from parts of Anoka, Hennepin and Ramsey counties. Overall, 1 in 28 of all 8-year-olds in that study were identified. Among Hmong children with IQ tests in their records, 54 percent had intellectual disability, compared with 37 percent of all Minnesota children with autism who had tests ([MN-ADDM, Sept. 26, 2025](https://addm.umn.edu/detailed-findings/press-briefing-2025-09-26)). The project says it does not examine causes.

Older data look different. Combining 2014 to 2016, researchers found lower prevalence among Hmong children than among white children (ratio 0.6) and non-Somali Black children (0.7), and wrote that the differences "may suggest barriers to service utilization" ([Esler et al., Journal of Autism and Developmental Disorders, Feb. 9, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41661527/)). So: was the earlier low number a true low number, or a gap in who was reaching a diagnosis? Why would a child's chance of having an IQ test on file vary by community? And what does a high share with intellectual disability say, if families may be reaching diagnosis mostly when needs are greatest? None of these sources answers them, so they stay open.

**What parents said.** The state's 2014 report interviewed 15 Hmong parents, all individually at their preference, about half partly or fully in Hmong. Foreign-born parents averaged about 24 years in the U.S., and 64 percent had more than a high school education. Many had never heard of autism. The report ties stigma to a belief that families may be "genetically flawed or cursed" and describes parents waiting because of work, time and other children. The top barriers named were wait lists, insurance and cost, a complicated system and transportation. Parents asked for family support and for other parents to talk to ([Minnesota Department of Health, 2014](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/2014/mandated/140404.pdf)). These are 15 people's accounts from more than a decade ago. A 2022 University of Minnesota doctoral study by Pang ChaXiong continued this line of work; only its listing could be opened ([Hmong Studies Virtual Library](https://www.hmonglibrary.org/hmong-studies-virtual-library---dissertations-and-theses.html)).

**School.** A 2018 Hamline thesis observed one mostly Hmong charter school and interviewed three tenth-grade boys and five staff. It raises the question of whether the broad "Asian American" category hides disproportion in special education, and found less of it at that school than the writing on the subject predicted ([Hamline University, 2018](https://digitalcommons.hamline.edu/hse_all/4415)). A federal grant page says St. Paul data show Hmong preschoolers at particular risk, in a sentence that cuts off, in a project about language barriers in assessment ([Institute of Education Sciences](https://ies.ed.gov/about/organization/saint-paul-public-schools-isd-625)). No current Hmong-specific special education figures from the state were found. Who is misread as disabled when a test is in the wrong language, and who is missed?

**Who defines disability here, and what does a label open or close?** Formal systems decide eligibility, but in some Hmong families, by these accounts, the question starts much earlier. Sue Yang, board president of United Hmong with Disabilities, has said publicly that her parents first attributed her disability to a curse. Houa Moua, the group's co-founder, describes Hmong words for disability as loss-based, and says she felt "othered" in special education classes. Mai Thor, now chief program officer at The Arc Minnesota, said in 2024 that "disability is so taboo in the Hmong community" ([Sahan Journal, May 7, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=83519); [MPR News via Sahan Journal, July 10, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=58426); [Minnesota Council on Disability, Feb. 6, 2026](https://www.disability.state.mn.us/2026/02/06/mai-thor-disability-activist-receives-champion-of-change-award/)). These are individual voices, not a verdict on a people. Others in the same exhibit, including a nurse raising a daughter with Down syndrome, describe insisting on full inclusion in family and social life.

> **Content note:** This passage describes the 2023 fatal police shooting of a Hmong elder in St. Paul. Readers may pause or skip to the next paragraph.

**What produced an impairment?** The war raises the question directly. The family of the elder shot by St. Paul police in 2023 told news outlets he did not speak English and was hard of hearing, and they attributed the hearing loss to fighting in the Secret War; officers gave commands in English on body camera video. The Ramsey County Attorney and Attorney General announced on March 20, 2024, that charges were not warranted ([Sahan Journal, Feb. 23, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/public-safety/yia-xiong-st-paul-police-shooting-minnesota-hmong-community/); [MPR News, March 20, 2024](https://mprnews.org/story/2024/03/20/no-criminal-charges-against-st-paul-officers-in-the-death-of-yia-xiong)). How many veterans and elders live with injury or trauma from the war, and how many have it on record? No Minnesota figure was found. Minnesota's own 2026 law adds some recognition for about 900 surviving veterans, but no health care in the 2025 version, and the Department of Veterans Affairs says it does not open all veterans' benefits ([Sahan Journal, April 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-senate-legislature-hmong-war-veterans-bill/); [Report for Minnesota](https://reportformn.umn.edu/minnesota-senate-approves-new-benefits-secret-war-veterans)).

**People and organizations.** United Hmong with Disabilities made "Many Ways of Being: Hmong Disability Stories in Photos," profiling eight people, shown at Hmong Village in April 2026 and the Walker Art Center in May ([Sahan Journal, May 7, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=83519)). Access matters too: Moua has described wheelchair barriers in Frogtown snow and at hard-to-reach festival food stalls. The Hmong Cultural Center notes elevator access from its back lot, on request ([Hmong Cultural Center](https://www.hmongcc.org/)). No Hmong Deaf community source was found, and no Minnesota source on how Hmong families use waivers, personal care assistance or adult day programs. Older scholarship from northern California, not Minnesota, reports shifting views and continued reliance on family and shamans as helpers ([Lee and Yuen, 2003, catalog record](https://hmongstudies.library.wisc.edu/catalog/HmongStudies1739)).

## 13. What is changing now

**Deportation to Laos, in order.**
- *Before 2025.* Hmong deportations were rare, and Laos typically accepted few deportees. A Lao consular officer told Sahan Journal it typically took about 10 a year ([Sahan Journal, June 27, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-deportation-ice-minnesota-laos/)).
- *Early 2025.* Under U.S. pressure, Laos began accepting more, issuing travel documents for 145 U.S. deportees by mid-June. There is no formal repatriation agreement, attorney Mai Neng Moua said ([Sahan Journal, June 27, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-deportation-ice-minnesota-laos/); [Sahan Journal, Oct. 17, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-deportations-minnesota-ice-laos/)).
- *June 2025.* Agents arrested at least 15 Hmong Minnesotans, most born in camps and brought here as children, with final orders tied to convictions, some decades old. The Hmong 18 Council put the number at 24 by July ([Sahan Journal, June 27, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-deportation-ice-minnesota-laos/); [NextShark via Yahoo, July 9, 2025](https://www.yahoo.com/news/two-dozen-hmong-men-minnesota-142234546.html)).
- *August 2025.* A flight left Louisiana on August 18 with at least 57 Southeast Asian deportees by Minnesota 8's count. About 100 people rallied at East Side Freedom Library on August 17. Rep. Kaohly Vang Her asked Gov. Walz and Attorney General Ellison for more pardons ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 13, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-minnesotans-deported-laos/); [Sahan Journal, Aug. 18, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-deportations-rally-st-paul-minnesota-laos/)). Minnesota 8 reported more than 150 Southeast Asians deported from Minnesota since May ([NextShark via Yahoo, Aug. 29, 2025](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/minnesota-officials-urged-act-hmong-173536870.html)).
- *October 2025.* Five of the 15 had been deported ([Sahan Journal, Oct. 17, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-deportations-minnesota-ice-laos/)).
- *January 2026.* Operation PARRIS began to reexamine refugee cases, reported as aimed at 5,600 Minnesota refugees without green cards. Federal Judge John Tunheim issued a restraining order January 28 and extended it as a preliminary injunction February 27, barring arrest and detention of these refugees while the lawsuit goes on ([Stop AAPI Hate, February 2026](https://stopaapihate.org/2026/02/11/keeping-count-who-are-hmong-americans-whats-happening-in-minnesota-explained/); [Sahan Journal, March 3, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-refugees-operation-parris-judge-extends-temporary-protection/)). On January 18, agents forced the door of a U.S. citizen's East Side home; DHS confirmed he was a citizen and not the target. Ramsey County announced an investigation in April; DHS rejected the characterization ([Sahan Journal, Jan. 19, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/st-paul-east-side-hmong-man-detained-ice-chongly-scott-thao/); [PBS NewsHour, April 2026](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/minnesota-investigating-ices-arrest-of-hmong-american-man-as-possible-kidnapping)).
- *May to October 2026.* Sahan Journal's "Taken" series follows a South St. Paul family after a father's arrest in February and deportation in May ([Sahan Journal, Oct. 26, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/deportation-hmong-minnesota-yang-family-south-stpaul-metro-surge/)). The paper wrote in June that it is "unclear how many" Hmong and Southeast Asian residents have been sent to Laos ([Sahan Journal, June 22, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/south-st-paul-family-deportation-zong-linda-yang-hmong-laos/)).
- *September 2026.* Using federal records through August 6, 2026, Stop AAPI Hate reported that Minnesota has 9.0 percent of Laos-born noncitizens but 18.3 percent of ICE arrests of people from Laos. It estimated 75 percent of Laos-born noncitizens in Minnesota are Hmong, since ICE does not record ethnicity ([Stop AAPI Hate, September 2026](https://stopaapihate.org/2026/09/15/how-asians-and-pacific-islanders-are-being-impacted-by-ice/)).

About two-thirds of Hmong Minnesotans were born in the U.S., by Compass's 31.5 percent foreign-born figure ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/hmong)). Stop AAPI Hate reported that 85 percent of Hmong immigrants who arrived as adults are naturalized ([Stop AAPI Hate, February 2026](https://stopaapihate.org/2026/02/11/keeping-count-who-are-hmong-americans-whats-happening-in-minnesota-explained/)).

**Other changes.** HmongTown's foreclosure case (section 4). A first Hmong mayor in the capital, and a possible first in Maplewood (section 9). New state recognition of veterans (section 9). The crowding dispute at Txuj Ci (section 10). Fears about young people losing language, voiced by UHF in testimony and by school staff ([Senate testimony, 2025](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2025/envlegacy/20250325/envlegacy_20250325_SF2590-Maycee-Vang-testimony.pdf)). Generational debate about gender roles and LGBTQ inclusion ([Sahan Journal, June 16, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)). The Pan Asian Center at Maplewood Mall, identified by Sahan Journal in 2025 as owned by Toua Xiong, hosted major 2026 events even as his finances went to court ([Sahan Journal, June 16, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/); [MPR News, March 18, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/03/18/hmong-artists-weave-music-poetry-and-visuals-to-help-those-impacted-by-ice-surge)).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

Fifty years after the first arrivals, Hmong Minnesotans have built schools, markets, farms, political power and cultural institutions. Hmong business owners helped revive University Avenue ([Longfellow Nokomis Messenger, Aug. 8, 2025](https://www.longfellownokomismessenger.com/stories/hmong-community-reshaping-st-paul,119428)). Hmong growers make up about half of Twin Cities farmers market vendors, by one report ([MPR News via Sahan Journal, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/news-partners/minnesota-dakota-county-hmong-immigrants-farming-hafa-farm/)). The New Year and the Freedom Festival bring tens of thousands to St. Paul, and the New Year's organizer says people travel internationally to attend ([Senate testimony, 2025](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2025/envlegacy/20250325/envlegacy_20250325_SF2590-Maycee-Vang-testimony.pdf)). Minnesota sent the country's first Hmong state legislators to office, and St. Paul now has a Hmong mayor (section 9). Veterans who waited decades have state recognition in law (section 9). Disabled Hmong leaders are telling their own stories at the Walker and at Hmong Village (section 12). And St. Paul's schools teach Hmong to children whose families want the language to last (section 10).
