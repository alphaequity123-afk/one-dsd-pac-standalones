# Rural and Greater Minnesota
Checked: October 30, 2026

Rural Minnesota is a place and the systems that serve it. It is not an ethnicity, and it has no single culture. A dairy family near Brooten, a laid-off miner in Hibbing, a Karen worker in Austin, a retired teacher on a lake in Aitkin County and a doctor in Blue Earth may share only long drives and thin services. This page describes those places and systems. It does not speak for Tribal Nations, whose own governments and offices speak for them.

## 1. People and place

**What counts as rural depends on who is counting.** The Minnesota Department of Health (MDH) uses federal commuting codes and treats census tracts coded 4 to 10 as rural. That covers micropolitan areas, small towns and isolated places, and comes to about 29.7 percent of Minnesotans, or 1.69 million people. The same document says ten of Minnesota's 11 Tribal Nations are in rural areas ([MDH Rural Health Transformation narrative, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.health.mn.gov/facilities/ruralhealth/ruraltrans/docs/rhtpnarrative.pdf)). The Center for Rural Policy and Development (CRPD), using groups first built by the state demographer's office, sorts all 87 counties into four kinds: 14 entirely rural, 35 town/rural mix, 25 urban/town/rural mix and 13 entirely urban ([State of Rural Minnesota 2026, March 2026](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/State-of-Rural-2026.pdf)). "Greater Minnesota" usually means the 80 counties outside the seven-county Twin Cities area. CRPD counted about 2.6 million people there in 2025 ([CRPD, April 11, 2025](https://www.ruralmn.org/?p=13886)).

These definitions disagree on purpose. In a 2011 MPR post, then state demographer Tom Gillaspy said the definition should fit the need at hand, and the writer pointed out that Cook, with 574 residents, can count as metro because it sits in St. Louis County ([MPR News, June 30, 2011](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2011/06/30/defining-what-rural-means-without-using-the-word-zucchini)). A reader should always ask which rural a number means.

**Who lives in the 80 counties.** The figures below add up county tables from the American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024. They are survey estimates, and the sums and margins are simple arithmetic on those tables. Greater Minnesota had about 2,570,000 people. About 85.2 percent were non-Hispanic white, compared with 68.3 percent in the seven metro counties. About 5.1 percent were Hispanic or Latino, 2.8 percent Black and 1.7 percent Asian, and 4.3 percent were born abroad, compared with 12.2 percent in the metro. About 19.4 percent were 65 or older, compared with 15.6 percent in the metro. Margins on these shares are 0.2 points or less ([race and ethnicity](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B03002&geo_ids=050%7C04000US27,04000US27); [birthplace](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05002&geo_ids=050%7C04000US27,04000US27); [age](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B01001&geo_ids=050%7C04000US27,04000US27), all checked Oct. 30, 2026).

Those averages hide sharp differences. Aitkin County was 34.4 percent 65 and older and Cook 31.2 percent, while Sherburne was 12.3 percent. Nobles County was 21.3 percent foreign-born and about 47.8 percent people of color, while Aitkin was 0.9 percent foreign-born ([same tables](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05002&geo_ids=050%7C04000US27,04000US27)). CRPD lists Mahnomen (55 percent), Nobles (48), Watonwan (32) among the counties with the highest shares of Black, Indigenous, Hispanic and other people of color, alongside Ramsey and Hennepin ([CRPD, March 2026](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/State-of-Rural-2026.pdf)).

**Where immigrant communities live outside the metro.** Minnesota Compass places 39.2 percent of Mexican Minnesotans in Greater Minnesota, give or take 1.3 points, out of 231,993 ([Minnesota Compass, 2020 to 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/mexican)), and 22.7 percent of Somali Minnesotans, give or take 3.6, out of a pooled estimate of 85,533 ([Minnesota Compass, 2020 to 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/somali)). In 2019 reporting, community leaders estimated about 1,500 Karen people around Albert Lea and Austin, with others in Marshall and Willmar; the article notes there was no census count for those cities ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 13, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/)). In Austin in 2021, a Hormel employee said the Beninese community had grown from about five people in 2010 to more than 500 ([Sahan Journal, Sept. 8, 2021](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/us-2020-census-minnesota-diversity-immigrants-worthington-austin/)). Long Prairie has had a Haitian community that grew around its meat plant ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 4, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/haitian-tps-humanitarian-parole-job-loss-immigration-status/)).

**How people came, and who came earlier.** The Iron Range drew 43 nationality groups. Its population went from under 5,000 in 1885 to more than 100,000 by 1920, and immigrants made up 85 percent of the mine workforce ([MNopedia, Immigration to the Iron Range](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/immigration-iron-range-1880-1930)). Worthington's mayor said in 2018 that churches there began sponsoring Southeast Asian refugees about 40 years earlier ([Minneapolis Fed, July 18, 2018](https://minneapolisfed.org/article/2018/spotlight-on-worthington-a-global-community-in-rural-minnesota)). The Minnesota Historical Society's podcast traces how meatpacking moved from cities to rural towns and came to rely on immigrant labor ([MNHS, March 6, 2025](https://www.mnhs.org/unraveled/episode-109)).

**The population story has shifted.** In 2019, 46 counties, all rural, had fewer people than in 2010. From 2020 to 2024 only 20 counties lost people, and one of those was Ramsey. CRPD credits migration, especially from abroad, and warns that deaths now clearly outnumber births in rural areas. Most rural counties still lose people in their 20s and gain people from 30 to 49 ([CRPD, March 2026](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/State-of-Rural-2026.pdf)).

## 2. All walks of life

Median household income for 2020 to 2024 was $89,062 statewide. Metro counties ran from $81,568 in Ramsey to $125,946 in Carver. In the 80 other counties, the middle county stood near $73,090, with a range from $59,441 in Mahnomen to $107,209 in Wright ([Census table B19013](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B01003,B19013,B25003,B25077&geo_ids=050%7C04000US27,04000US27)). So some "Greater Minnesota" counties out-earn Ramsey and even Hennepin. The highest earners, Wright and Sherburne, border the metro.

- **Wealth and ownership.** Named fortunes in rural Minnesota often began with a local firm. George A. Hormel started his company in Austin in 1891 ([Hormel Foods, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.hormelfoods.com/about/our-history/)). Ron Stordahl started DigiKey as a small mail-order business in 1972; it now reports more than 4,700 employees and calls itself a major employer in northwest Minnesota ([DigiKey](https://www.digikey.com/en/resources/about-digikey)). Land is a kind of wealth that does not show in wages. Minnesota farmland averaged $4,778 an acre in 2020, per CRPD ([State of Rural, 2021](https://www.ruralmn.org/the-state-of-rural-2/)). No county-level figure for household wealth could be found.
- **Professionals and business owners.** About 28.9 percent of adults 25 and older in the 80 counties held a bachelor's degree or more, against 47.8 percent in the metro ([Census table B15003](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B15003&geo_ids=050%7C04000US27,04000US27)). Non-employer businesses and the self-employed can be 15 to 20 percent of jobs in northern counties and 30 percent in Lincoln, Norman, Cook and Hubbard ([CRPD, March 2026](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/State-of-Rural-2026.pdf)). A 2018 Federal Reserve visit to Worthington met immigrant shop owners and a local banker on the same day ([Minneapolis Fed](https://minneapolisfed.org/article/2018/spotlight-on-worthington-a-global-community-in-rural-minnesota)).
- **Middle-income and working families.** About 76.2 percent of households in the 80 counties owned their homes, against 69.0 percent in the metro ([Census table B25003](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B01003,B19013,B25003,B25077&geo_ids=050%7C04000US27,04000US27)). Median home values ran from $114,100 in Traverse to $361,400 in Sherburne, compared with $329,300 statewide. CRPD finds that wages cover the local cost of living in most of Greater Minnesota, though less so in the central lakes and north of the metro, and that per-person income in entirely rural counties grew 166 percent from 2000 to 2024, while a gap of about $20,000 remains with entirely urban counties ([CRPD, March 2026](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/State-of-Rural-2026.pdf)).
- **People in poverty.** About 10.2 percent of people in the 80 counties lived below the poverty line, against 8.6 percent in the metro. County rates ran from 5.1 percent in Sherburne to 18.3 percent in Mahnomen, with Beltrami at 16.1 and Cottonwood at 15.0. College counties such as Blue Earth (15.7) and Winona (14.8) also rank high, which may reflect students ([Census table B17001](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B17001&geo_ids=050%7C04000US27,04000US27)). MDH reports that most rural counties have child poverty at or above the state average, and that uninsured rates are 4.1 percent in rural areas and 3.5 percent in urban ones ([MDH narrative](https://www.health.mn.gov/facilities/ruralhealth/ruraltrans/docs/rhtpnarrative.pdf)). Public assistance per person ranged from $1 in Lake of the Woods to $127 in Mahnomen ([CRPD, March 2026](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/State-of-Rural-2026.pdf)).

## 3. Work and the economy

**The largest employer is care and teaching.** In all four CRPD county groups, education, health care and social assistance is the top sector, near 38 percent of jobs in entirely rural counties. Manufacturing is next at 17 to 22 percent. In entirely rural counties, agriculture and mining rank third at 16 percent. Government was 27 percent of jobs in entirely rural counties in 2024, against 12 percent in entirely urban ones, though CRPD cautions that this overstates government's role because farmers and the self-employed go uncounted ([CRPD, March 2026](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/State-of-Rural-2026.pdf)). Counting the 80 counties together, farming, forestry, fishing and mining came to 3.9 percent of employed residents, and 18.7 percent in Traverse County ([Census table C24030](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=C24030&geo_ids=050%7C04000US27,04000US27)).

**Farms.** USDA's 2025 summary says Minnesota had 1,300 fewer farms in 2025 than 2024, and the average farm grew from 389 to 395 acres ([KTTC, March 2, 2026](https://www.kttc.com/2026/03/02/minnesota-farms-continue-disappear-new-federal-report-says/?outputType=amp)). The 1980s crisis still sits in memory. Minnesota lost farms from 98,671 in 1978 to 85,079 in 1987; average land value fell nearly 40 percent; about 10,000 people rallied at the Capitol on Jan. 21, 1985, after a Paynesville bank protest gave rise to the Groundswell movement; and the state began its Farm Advocate program in 1984 ([MNopedia, Farm Crisis](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/farm-crisis-1979-1987)).

Now the talk of crisis has returned, with disagreement over how deep it runs. Minnesota had 13 Chapter 12 farm bankruptcies in 2025 and 8 in the first quarter of 2026, the most of any state, and 1,429 farm mediation notices from October 2025 to June 2026. Those are small numbers, and American Farm Bureau economist Cameron Castillo called bankruptcy filings "definitely not the whole story." Secretary Brooke Rollins said national filings were a tiny share of farms, while Ortonville attorney David McLaughlin predicted things would get far worse. Brooten dairy farmer Lucas Sjostrom said beef sales from his herd had jumped as prices rose ([Civic Media, July 13, 2026](https://civicmedia.us/news/2026/07/13/minnesota-bankruptcies-rise-as-a-perfect-storm-of-challenges-roils-farm-country)). Thief River Falls farmer Kyle Jore said farmers would "rather have a market than assistance," and southeastern Minnesota farmer Justin Meyer blamed inflation more than current policy ([KAXE, May 26, 2026](https://www.kaxe.org/minnesota-news/2026-05-26/farmers-anxious-as-mn-led-nation-in-q1-farm-bankruptcies)). More than 75 percent of Upper Midwest farm lenders reported lower incomes in early 2026 than a year before ([Twin Cities Business, Aug. 25, 2026](https://tcbmag.com/whats-driving-the-financial-crisis-on-minnesota-farms/)).

**Mines.** The Mesabi's first ore shipped in 1892. Taconite, a lower-grade ore, became its later mainstay, and the 1964 Taconite Amendment on its taxation passed with 86 percent of voters ([MNopedia, Mesabi Iron Range](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/place/mesabi-iron-range)). Labor history is central. Between 10,000 and 16,000 miners, most of them Finnish, struck in 1907. The strike failed, and Finnish workers were blacklisted, falling from 18 to 8 percent of the mining workforce ([MNopedia, 1907 strike](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/mesabi-iron-range-strike-1907)).

The present is hard. Cleveland-Cliffs idled Hibbing Taconite in part and Minorca Mine near Virginia in spring 2025. The Mesabi Tribune put total layoffs at about 650, with Hibbing Taconite running one of three pellet lines and short of crude ore ([Mesabi Tribune, May 19, 2026](https://www.mesabitribune.com/news/local/hibbing-taconite-miners-get-ui-extension/article_e4a976b6-0d50-4112-a8c1-716bb4b8f798.html)). Another 45 jobs at Hibbing Taconite were cut as of Feb. 1, 2026 ([Northern News Now, Jan. 9, 2026](https://www.northernnewsnow.com/2026/01/10/clevelandcliffs-new-cuts-add-growing-mining-uncertainty-iron-range)). John Arbogast of the United Steelworkers tied the slump to weak auto demand and said the industry will rebound; Hibbing's mayor warned that without longer benefits, skilled workers could be lost for good ([Minnesota House, Feb. 18, 2026](https://www.house.mn.gov/SessionDaily/Story/18870)).

**Co-ops.** Farmers built institutions they owned. By 1922 Minnesota had over 4,500 local cooperatives, including 644 creameries and 1,739 rural telephone co-ops, and in 1985 it led the nation in farmer co-ops, with 566 ([Minnesota legislative report, Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2009/other/090394.pdf)). Only about 6.8 percent of Minnesota farms had central electric service by 1934. Meeker Cooperative Light and Power formed in 1935, and 99 percent of farms had service by 1963 ([MNopedia, Rural Electrification](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/rural-electrification-administration-minnesota)). The Minnesota Rural Electric Association counts 50 electric co-ops serving 1.7 million people across 85 percent of the state's land; MNopedia counts 45 distribution co-ops ([MREA](https://www.mrea.org/about/)). American Crystal, a grower-owned sugar company based in Moorhead, finished a 10.8 million ton beet harvest on Oct. 24, 2026 ([Agweek, Oct. 29, 2026](https://www.agweek.com/news/sugarbeet/american-crystal-wraps-up-2026-harvest)).

**Meat plants.** JBS in Worthington had more than 2,200 workers in 2018, mostly immigrants, with a starting wage near $15 an hour ([Minneapolis Fed](https://minneapolisfed.org/article/2018/spotlight-on-worthington-a-global-community-in-rural-minnesota)). A Star Tribune report carried by Sahan Journal calls the 2006 raid the largest one-day raid in U.S. history and says more than 200 workers were deported that day ([Sahan Journal, Feb. 24, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/news-partners/rural-minnesota-immigrants-prepare/)). Workers and their families tell their own stories of the plant in the MNHS podcast ([MNHS](https://www.mnhs.org/unraveled/episode-109)).

**Vacancies and joblessness.** Northeast Minnesota had the highest job vacancy rate, 6.2 percent, against a level CRPD calls healthy of 3 to 4.5 ([CRPD, March 2026](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/State-of-Rural-2026.pdf)). It also had the highest 2025 unemployment among planning regions, 5.0 percent, with Kanabec at 7.6 and Pine at 7.2 ([CRPD, Oct. 16, 2026](https://www.ruralmn.org/minnesotas-unemployment-rate-is-creeping-up-a-regional-look/)). Fires also cost money. A University of Minnesota Duluth analysis estimated $9 million in lost visitor activity in the Arrowhead after the July 2026 Boundary Waters closure ([Timberjay, Oct. 29, 2026](https://www.timberjay.com/stories/wildfires-cost-region-9-million-in-lost-visitor-spending,48307)).

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

No current county-level count of faiths or closed congregations could be found. What can be shown is partial.

On the early Iron Range, churches and synagogues anchored immigrant life, and groups kept separate Catholic parishes in larger towns. Many immigrants were Catholic, Orthodox or Jewish, and local Ku Klux Klan chapters opposed Catholic and Jewish immigrants in the 1920s ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/immigration-iron-range-1880-1930)). Faith groups have also done public work. Worthington's mayor credited churches with sponsoring refugees decades ago ([Minneapolis Fed](https://minneapolisfed.org/article/2018/spotlight-on-worthington-a-global-community-in-rural-minnesota)), and a 2026 faith column there invites everyone to a Community Thanksgiving Dinner ([The Globe](https://www.dglobe.com/)). Faith-based groups joined COPAL's immigration preparation network in 2025 ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/news-partners/rural-minnesota-immigrants-prepare/)).

The Catholic Diocese of New Ulm's site names parishes from Renville to Graceville and uses the term "Area Faith Community," which suggests parishes grouped together, though the page does not explain it or count mergers ([Diocese of New Ulm](https://www.dnu.org/)). People of no faith do not appear in any rural source found.

## 7. Family, home and daily life

Distance shapes the day. For 2020 to 2024, 5.7 percent of households in the 80 counties had no vehicle, lower than the metro's 7.5 percent, and in a place with few buses that small share can mean real isolation ([Census table B25044](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B25044&geo_ids=050%7C04000US27,04000US27)). Rural patients travel about 84 minutes for inpatient mental health and chemical dependency care, against 25 for urban patients ([MDH narrative](https://www.health.mn.gov/facilities/ruralhealth/ruraltrans/docs/rhtpnarrative.pdf)).

Aging is a family matter. Entirely rural counties will reach peak demand for nursing beds about 2037, a decade before the state's peak, and northwest and southwest counties sooner ([CRPD, November 2024](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/The-declining-capacity-of-nursing-facility-beds-in-rural-Minnesota.pdf)). Housing is tight even where populations shrink because households are smaller. From 1970 to 2016, entirely rural counties lost 21 percent of their people but gained 9 percent in households. CRPD's Marnie Werner argues that senior housing could free family homes ([CRPD, reposted Oct. 28, 2026](https://www.ruralmn.org/restoring-the-churn-to-rural-housing-markets-2/)).

Some immigrant families carry another worry. The 2025 Star Tribune report describes families in Worthington preparing guardianship forms in case a parent is detained ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/news-partners/rural-minnesota-immigrants-prepare/)). Food carries history. MNopedia names Cornish pasties, Italian porketta and Slovenian potica as Iron Range foods ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/place/mesabi-iron-range)), and lefse has its own cookoff at Potato Days ([Explore Minnesota](https://www.exploreminnesota.com/event/potato-days-festival/27155)).

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Arts and museums.** The Commonweal Theatre has worked in Lanesboro, a town of about 750, since 1989 ([Commonweal](https://www.commonwealtheatre.org/about)). Lanesboro Arts has gallery shows and concerts this fall ([Lanesboro Arts](https://www.lanesboroarts.org/)). The Hjemkomst Center in Moorhead holds a Viking ship and stave church replica ([HCSCC](https://www.hcscconline.org/)). The Minnesota Discovery Center in Chisholm tells the Iron Range's story ([Discovery Center](https://www.mndiscoverycenter.org/)). The Judy Garland Museum in Grand Rapids still lacks its stolen ruby slippers, which it says have not returned since 2005 ([Museum](https://www.judygarlandmuseum.com/)). The U.S. Hockey Hall of Fame posts current hours ([Hall of Fame](https://www.ushockeyhall.com/)).

**Media.** Local news is alive but thinner. KAXE calls itself the oldest rural community radio station in the U.S., and its site is full of October 2026 stories ([KAXE](https://www.kaxe.org/)). Pioneer PBS in Granite Falls makes Prairie Yard & Garden and Prairie Sportsman ([Pioneer PBS](https://www.pioneer.org/)). The Timberjay in Tower, The Globe in Worthington, the West Central Tribune in Willmar, the Marshall Independent and Agweek all publish current news ([Timberjay](https://www.timberjay.com/); [The Globe](https://www.dglobe.com/); [West Central Tribune](https://www.wctrib.com/); [Marshall Independent](https://www.marshallindependent.com/); [Agweek](https://www.agweek.com/)).

**Sports.** Town life still turns on high school teams. Grand Rapids and Hermantown boys reached the state soccer tournament in 2026 after long droughts ([KAXE, Oct. 26, 2026](https://www.kaxe.org/)).

## 9. Civic and political life

**Representation.** Coya Knutson, who with her husband bought a hotel in Oklee, became Minnesota's first woman in Congress in 1954, and lost in 1958 after the "Coya, Come Home" letters ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/knutson-coya-1912-1996)). Today Pete Stauber, a Duluth native, represents the Eighth District ([Stauber](https://stauber.house.gov/)), and Brad Finstad, a self-described fourth-generation farmer, the First ([Finstad](https://finstad.house.gov/)). St. Cloud appointed its first Somali American council member, Hudda Ibrahim, in December 2024 ([MPR News, Dec. 3, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/12/03/hudda-ibrahim-st-cloud-city-council)).

**Organizing.** Minnesota Farmers Union describes itself as grassroots and member-driven and holds its 2026 convention Nov. 20 to 22 ([MFU](https://www.mfu.org/)). Land Stewardship Project has offices in Montevideo and Lewiston and runs farm transition classes ([LSP](https://landstewardshipproject.org/)). Centro Campesino, formed by a former migrant worker in Owatonna, won a child care center and better pay at a company camp, per a 2011 report; its status today could not be confirmed ([MPR News, July 15, 2011](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2011/07/15/victor-contreras-centro-campesino-steps-down)). COPAL organizes in Worthington ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/news-partners/rural-minnesota-immigrants-prepare/)).

**Debates inside rural places.**

- *Who belongs at city hall.* In 2018, some Worthington residents criticized how few immigrants served in city government and on the police force ([Minneapolis Fed](https://minneapolisfed.org/article/2018/spotlight-on-worthington-a-global-community-in-rural-minnesota)).
- *Data centers.* Hermantown's fight over a Google data center moved its meeting after protests. The Minnesota Center for Environmental Advocacy criticized the city for favoring speed over public input ([KAXE, Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.kaxe.org/local-news/2026-10-30/how-a-google-data-center-settlement-in-pine-island-could-impact-hermantown)).
- *Who keeps county work.* The state proposes moving some eligibility work from counties and Tribal Nations to the state and phasing out contracted case management ([DHS, April 2026](https://www.developtoolmn.org/dhs/assets/2026-04_transforming-human-services-system_tcm1053-738533.pdf)). County social service directors say waiver cost fixes must not shift costs to property taxpayers ([MACSSA 2026 priorities](https://goodhuecountymn.gov/files/agenda_items/2026_macssa_legislative_priorities_76661.pdf)).
- *Hospitals.* A 2026 opinion writer contrasted about $30 million offered to rural nonprofit hospitals with a $705 million package for Hennepin County Medical Center ([Aaron Wittnebel, MinnPost, May 20, 2026](http://www.minnpost.com/community-voices/2026/05/minnesotas-legislative-session-left-rural-healthcare-systems-exposed/)).

## 10. Education

**Consolidation.** Carlton and Wrenshall voted unanimously to combine, with Carlton High School closing and first-year savings estimated at 13 to 16 percent ([WDIO](https://www.wdio.com/?p=719570)). Virginia and Eveleth-Gilbert voted to consolidate in 2020 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/place/mesabi-iron-range)).

**The four-day week.** Ely's board voted 6-0 for it on April 13, 2026, expecting $200,000 in savings, while Rock Ridge voted 6-1 to keep five days ([KAXE, April 14, 2026](https://www.kaxe.org/local-news/2026-04-14/rock-ridge-ely-4-day-school-week-starting-fall)). In 2024 testimony, the Belgrade-Brooten-Elrosa superintendent said test scores held about the same under four days, while the state education department raised concerns about meals, child care, special education and hourly workers ([Minnesota House, Feb. 21, 2024](https://www.house.mn.gov/SessionDaily/Story/18089)).

**Farms pay into schools.** Worthington's district said 41 percent of its tax base is farmland, and the state's Ag2School credit will cover 28 percent of a $9.77 million bond ([The Globe, Oct. 22, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/worthington-isd-518-school-board-approves-trojan-legacy-fund-bond-sale)).

**After high school.** CRPD's 2026 study of graduates from 2008 to 2023 says it complicates the "brain drain" story, and names affordable housing as the biggest obstacle for young people who want to stay ([CRPD, Sept. 2, 2026](https://www.ruralmn.org/pathways-after-high-school/)). The University of Minnesota Morris began fall with 1,042 students, up 6 percent by its own count ([UMN Morris](https://www.morris.umn.edu/)). The Minnesota Rural Education Association holds its summit Nov. 15 to 17, 2026 ([MREA](https://www.mreavoice.org/)).

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**The state of the system.** MDH counts 95 hospitals as rural, reports 76 critical access hospitals statewide, and counts 34 rural hospitals as financially distressed, meaning four or more years of negative margins in the last eight. It says five rural hospitals have closed since 2005; a Rural Health Association leader said six in 2025 ([MDH narrative](https://www.health.mn.gov/facilities/ruralhealth/ruraltrans/docs/rhtpnarrative.pdf); [KAXE, Feb. 28, 2025](https://www.kaxe.org/local-news/2025-02-28/medicaid-cuts-worsen-rural-minnesota-health-care-outlook)).

**How much danger, really?** Sources differ. The Center for Healthcare Quality and Payment Reform found at least nine at immediate risk in 2025 ([KAXE, July 11, 2025](https://www.kaxe.org/local-news/2025-07-11/seismic-medicaid-changes-will-be-rough-on-rural-hospitals)). Opinion writers in MinnPost gave about 20 and 23, without a stated study ([Stinnett, April 16, 2026](http://www.minnpost.com/community-voices/2026/04/rural-health-care-in-minnesota-is-at-risk-of-becoming-a-medical-wasteland/)). A farm magazine reports Minnesota's rural hospitals are managing better than many states, quoting Mark Jones that "nothing is easy these days" ([Progressive Farmer, March 31, 2026](https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/magazine/article/2026/04/01/code-blue)). The state received more than $193 million for the first year of the federal Rural Health Transformation Program ([MDH, Dec. 30, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/news/pressrel/2025/rural123025.html)).

**Birth.** An estimated 45 percent of rural hospitals no longer offered maternity care in early 2024 ([CRPD reposting Axios, Jan. 31, 2024](https://www.ruralmn.org/axios-nearly-half-of-minnesotas-rural-hospitals-dont-offer-labor-and-delivery/)). By one 2026 count, 50 rural hospitals had none, with 8 closures since 2020 and a median 31-minute drive ([Pennsylvania Office of Rural Health, Jan. 28, 2026](https://www.porh.psu.edu/124-rural-hospital-maternity-services-closed-closing-in-6-years-analysis/)). The Attorney General's office launched a rural maternal care project on Oct. 28, 2026 ([West Central Tribune](https://www.wctrib.com/news/minnesota/lake-region-healthcare-joins-statewide-effort-to-strengthen-rural-maternity-care)). There are also hopeful cases. United Hospital District in Blue Earth went from about 50 births a year to 178 in 2025, helped by cross-trained staff and a network of rural hospitals; one patient came after a Fairmont unit closed in 2024 ([NPR via KCLU, Sept. 8, 2026](https://www.kclu.org/2026-09-08/one-rural-hospital-offers-hope-amid-nationwide-rural-maternity-ward-closures)). Northfield and CCM Health have also reported growth ([MPR News, Oct. 23, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2025/10/23/the-state-of-labor-and-delivery-services-in-southern-minnesota)).

**Nursing homes.** Beds fell 41 percent in entirely rural counties from 2005 to 2024. Red Lake County lost all of its beds, and Cass went from 489 to 33. Fifty-eight percent of the loss in entirely rural counties came from closures. CRPD credits a long shift to home care and a recent staffing shortage ([CRPD, November 2024](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/The-declining-capacity-of-nursing-facility-beds-in-rural-Minnesota.pdf)).

**Other gaps.** About 51 percent of rural adults have at least one chronic condition. The suicide rate in entirely rural counties in 2024 was about twice that of metro counties. More than 86,000 people outside metro areas lived over 15 miles from a pharmacy in 2024. Over half of 230 rural ambulances cover more than 200 square miles ([MDH narrative](https://www.health.mn.gov/facilities/ruralhealth/ruraltrans/docs/rhtpnarrative.pdf)). The state's Farm and Rural Helpline is 833-600-2670, or text FARMSTRESS to 898211 ([Minnesota Department of Agriculture](https://www.mda.state.mn.us/about/mnfarmerstress)).

**Broadband and transit, which carry care.** In December 2025, 92.11 percent of households statewide had 100/20 service, against 80.97 percent in non-metro areas, up from 49.33 percent in 2016 ([Office of Broadband Development report](https://mn.gov/deed/assets/2025-broadband-development-annual-report_tcm1045-721220.pdf)). The state signed its first federal BEAD contract July 30, 2026, with East Central Energy, with 93 more needed ([Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/minnesotas-first-bead-contract-lands/)). Experts predicted the state would miss its 2026 goal ([Blandin on Broadband, Aug. 6, 2025](https://blandinonbroadband.org/2025/08/06/mn-star-tribune-says-experts-predict-mn-will-miss-2026-goal-for-total-broadband-access/)). Greater Minnesota had 35 public and 6 tribal transit systems in 2022, in 79 counties, with over 7 million rides ([MnDOT, Dec. 12, 2023](https://www.lcc.mn.gov/aging/12122023/MnDOT%20Presentation%20to%20Legislative%20Task%20Force%20on%20Aging%2012-12-2023.pdf)).

## 12. Disability

**What the numbers show.** Among people not living in institutions, the American Community Survey finds about 12.9 percent with a disability in the 80 counties (328,010 of 2,533,079, give or take about 0.1 point) and 10.6 percent in the seven metro counties, for 2020 to 2024. County figures run from 9.1 percent in Wright to 19.8 in Aitkin, 19.5 in Koochiching and 19.0 in Swift ([Census table C18108](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=C18108&geo_ids=050%7C04000US27,04000US27)). The survey asks six questions about function. It is not a count of anyone found eligible for a service, and small counties have wider margins.

**Questions the numbers raise, not answers.** Aitkin is also the oldest county in this set, at 34.4 percent 65 or older. How much of a high county rate is age, and how much is work, income, or people moving to or from services? No Minnesota study found separates these.

**What may have produced an impairment.** These are questions of work and exposure, not of people.

- *Mining.* The University of Minnesota's Taconite Workers Health Study found miners had mesothelioma at about three times the state's rate, and each year in the industry raised risk about 3 percent. Researchers could not separate commercial asbestos from taconite fibers. Principal investigator Jeffrey Mandel said they could point only to "a relationship." One longtime miner called the findings vague ([MPR News, Dec. 1, 2014](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2014/12/01/iron-range-miners-mesothelioma)).
- *Meatpacking.* In Austin, workers at Quality Pork Processors' "head table" developed a nerve disease that doctors tied to a mist of pig brain matter. In 2008 MPR reported two diagnosed workers still without workers' compensation, though the company said 95 percent of diagnosed workers were covered, and the article notes courts have ruled that compensation does not turn on immigration status ([MPR News, Dec. 17, 2008](https://www.mprnews.org/2008/12/17/qpp_workers/)). Whether current plant workers face comparable risks is something these sources do not settle.
- *Farming.* A University of Minnesota article reports Minnesota once had an AgrAbility program that has ended, and that a graduate hopes to revive it. She surveyed farmers, but the article gives no sample size. It describes farmers with more pain using breaks and energy conservation to keep working ([UMN College of Pharmacy, Dec. 18, 2025](https://www.pharmacy.umn.edu/news/ots-improve-mobility-aging-agricultural-workers)).

**Who decides, and what it opens.** Minnesota is one of a minority of states with a state-supervised, county-administered system. MnCHOICES assessments, which decide whether a person with a disability or an older adult can use home and community services, rest with counties, managed care plans and Tribal Nations, and the state says backlogs and delays prevent timely completion ([DHS, April 2026](https://www.developtoolmn.org/dhs/assets/2026-04_transforming-human-services-system_tcm1053-738533.pdf)). So where a person lives can decide who assesses them and which staff are on hand. Whether waits are longer in rural counties could not be established. County directors warn that changes must not add mandates or require new staff ([MACSSA](https://goodhuecountymn.gov/files/agenda_items/2026_macssa_legislative_priorities_76661.pdf)).

**Staffing.** Around early 2022, Cardinal of Minnesota closed group homes because of staffing, with closures in Olmsted County and in rural parts of Olmsted and Winona counties. Sources give 10 and 11 of its 55 homes. Rochester council member Kelly Kirkpatrick described her sister moving to their mother's home on about 60 days' notice, and doorways too narrow for care ([KAAL](https://www.kaaltv.com/?p=8771); [KSTP](https://kstp.com/5-investigates/as-group-homes-in-minnesota-struggle-to-find-workers-families-forced-to-provide-complex-care-for-loved-ones-with-disabilities/)). Provider CEO Michelle Priggen said capped pay held wages near $15. At the time, the Human Services commissioner said closings spiked in January and then more homes opened than closed. No statewide count of rural direct support vacancies for 2026 could be found. Registered nurses, nursing aides and personal care aides made up 10 to 15 percent of all job openings in rural Minnesota in the CRPD nursing home study ([CRPD](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/The-declining-capacity-of-nursing-facility-beds-in-rural-Minnesota.pdf)).

**Getting there.** The University of Minnesota's January 2026 case series on United Community Action Partnership in Marshall offers the clearest local picture. It runs over 40 lift-equipped buses and about 200,000 transit rides a year, plus about 16,000 volunteer driver rides. Fewer than 20 years ago it had over 100 volunteers for two counties; now it has about 35 for nine, with hired driver jobs open for five years. Mobility Administrator Shelly Pflaum describes a 40-mile trip from Windom to Slayton that would add 342 miles by van. "Each of these trips is a person," she says ([Rural Health Research Center](https://rhrc.umn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Case-study_transportation_disability_Jan-2026-1.pdf)). A CRPD summary notes that volunteer mileage above a rate unchanged since 1986, $0.14 a mile, can count as income and cut retirees' Social Security ([CRPD](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Summary_volunteer-drivers_oachs.pdf)).

**People and places working on this.** SMILES Center for Independent Living has offices in Mankato, Fairmont, New Ulm and Waseca, and says it has worked with people with disabilities since 1990 ([SMILES](https://www.smilescil.org/)). Access North has offices in Hibbing, Duluth and Brainerd, offering ramps, assistive technology and new home and community services ([Access North](https://www.accessnorth.net/)). Disability Hub MN is a free statewide line at 1-866-333-2466 ([Disability Hub MN](https://disabilityhubmn.org/)). In schools, MREA gave 2025 awards to Worthington's Discovery Room Program and Menahga's Adaptive PE and Music program ([MREA](https://www.mreavoice.org/)).

**National studies, labeled as such.** A University of Minnesota team studied barriers to care among rural adults by disability status using a national survey. The summary gives no findings and does not limit the work to Minnesota ([Rural Health Research Gateway, October 2025](https://www.ruralhealthresearch.org/publications/1760)).

## 13. What is changing now

- **Immigration enforcement (dated and attributed).** Sahan Journal reported Aug. 4, 2025, that about 80 Haitian workers at American Food Groups in Long Prairie were laid off after losing work permits, according to union representative Miguel Gutierrez. The company did not respond ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/haitian-tps-humanitarian-parole-job-loss-immigration-status/)). A Minnesota Reformer report on Worthington's economy during the winter 2026 enforcement surge could not be read, so it is not described here.
- **Federal health and food aid changes.** Medicaid work rules take effect in December 2026, and leaders such as Fairview Range's CEO Jean MacDonell expect more pressure ([KAXE, July 11, 2025](https://www.kaxe.org/local-news/2025-07-11/seismic-medicaid-changes-will-be-rough-on-rural-hospitals)). Counties estimate SNAP changes will cut federal funding to the state by $125 million to $168 million a year ([MACSSA](https://goodhuecountymn.gov/files/agenda_items/2026_macssa_legislative_priorities_76661.pdf)).
- **Mining and farm stress** continue, as in section 3.
- **Fires.** Boundary Waters closed in mid-July 2026, and federal disaster loans opened Oct. 26 for small businesses in seven counties. Farmers are not currently eligible ([KAXE, Oct. 29, 2026](https://www.kaxe.org/local-news/2026-10-29/federal-business-loans-available-offset-boundary-waters-wildfire-losses)).
- **Younger arrivals.** Iron Range writer Aaron Brown sees younger professionals in local offices and credits high-speed internet and remote work, while noting decrepit buildings too. That is one observer's view ([Minnesota Brown, March 2026](https://minnesotabrown.com/2026/03/were-not-out-of-the-woods-but-hope-grows.html)).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **They built what no one would build for them.** Farmers made co-ops when private utilities would not string wire ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/rural-electrification-administration-minnesota)).
- **People stand up.** From the 1907 strikers to the 10,000 at the Capitol in 1985 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/mesabi-iron-range-strike-1907); [MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/farm-crisis-1979-1987)).
- **Newcomers renew towns.** Compass researcher Ellen Wolter said in 2021 that growth among residents of color offsets population loss and sustains local economies ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/us-2020-census-minnesota-diversity-immigrants-worthington-austin/)). As of 2018, Worthington had grown 17 percent since 2000, while nonmetro counties overall grew under 2 percent ([Minneapolis Fed](https://minneapolisfed.org/article/2018/spotlight-on-worthington-a-global-community-in-rural-minnesota)).
- **Small places solve hard problems.** Blue Earth's birth center and Marshall's drivers are models that University of Minnesota scholars point to ([NPR via KCLU](https://www.kclu.org/2026-09-08/one-rural-hospital-offers-hope-amid-nationwide-rural-maternity-ward-closures); [Rural Health Research Center](https://rhrc.umn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Case-study_transportation_disability_Jan-2026-1.pdf)).
- **Local giving.** Southwest Initiative Foundation has 31 affiliates and raised over $5.2 million in grant funds in fiscal 2025 ([The Globe](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/worthington-isd-518-school-board-approves-trojan-legacy-fund-bond-sale)).
