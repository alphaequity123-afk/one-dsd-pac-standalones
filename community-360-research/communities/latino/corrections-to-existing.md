# Corrections to the existing Latino Minnesota page

Page: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/latino

## Errors, outdated claims and things that should change

1. **Claim:** Mexican Minnesotans number 229,576 in "ACS 2019–2023," with other origin counts from the same period.
   **Source says:** A newer period is out. The 2020 to 2024 survey gives 223,736 Mexican (give or take 3,903), 18,956 Puerto Rican, 15,993 Ecuadorian, 14,670 Salvadoran, 13,725 Guatemalan, 8,332 Colombian and 6,536 Cuban, using one Hispanic origin table. Compass figures on the page count differently and may run higher, so the page should name which table it uses. All counts need updating and the date should change.
   **Link:** https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=04000US27 .
2. **Claim:** The page warns against adding origin figures into a single "Latino" total and gives no total.
   **Source says:** A published total exists and should be stated: 365,831, or 6.4 percent, in the 2020 to 2024 survey, and 388,435, or 6.7 percent, in the 2024 single-year survey.
   **Links:** https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B03003&geo_ids=04000US27 ; https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=04000US27 .
3. **Claim:** Origin data are given for seven groups only; Honduras, Nicaragua, the Dominican Republic and Venezuela are named without numbers.
   **Source says:** Numbers exist: Honduran 8,493 (Compass profile 9,409, give or take 2,059), Venezuelan 5,144, Peruvian 4,320, Dominican 4,252, Nicaraguan 3,743, Chilean 2,013. Peru is absent from the page, and Chile appears only as an independence-day date.
   **Links:** https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=04000US27 ; https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/honduran .
4. **Claim:** The Dolores Huerta and César Chávez material does not mention the 2026 allegations. Huerta's profile says she met and co-founded with Chávez, and the page does not mention the repeal of Cesar Chavez Day.
   **Source says:** A March 2026 New York Times investigation, as reported locally, alleged that Chávez sexually assaulted girls and women. Huerta confirmed the allegations and was described as one of those abused, per the West St. Paul Reader. Minnesota repealed Cesar Chavez Day through Laws 2026, Chapter 42, signed March 27, 2026. Gov. Walz proclaimed March 31, 2026, Farmworkers Day. The page's Huerta profile and calendar should reflect this.
   **Links:** https://weststpaulreader.com/2026/07/06/renaming-cesar-chavez-street/ ; https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/07/16/st-paul-neighborhood-begins-process-to-rename-csar-chavez-street ; https://www.revisor.mn.gov/laws/2026/0/Session+Law/Chapter/42 ; https://fox9.com/news/minnesota-recognizes-farmworkers-day-following-cesar-chavez-fallout-march-2026 .
5. **Claim:** Calendar and place sections do not mention the West Side street renaming.
   **Source says:** The West Side Community Organization set a vote for Nov. 3, 2026, among Del Sol, West Side, El Rio Vista, La Raza and El Barrio. The page should be updated after that date, and any use of "Cesar Chavez Street" as an address may need to change.
   **Link:** https://www.wsco.org/streetrenaming_about .
6. **Claim:** Minnesota Latino Museum is listed under resources, with a link to an exhibition.
   **Source says:** The museum's home page describes it in the future tense ("will be located"), led by (Neo)Muralismos de México, and shows no dated exhibit. Readers should not be sent there as if it were an open building. The exhibition page itself was not reopened.
   **Link:** https://www.mnlatinomuseum.org/ .
7. **Claim:** Teatro del Pueblo's "most recent listed production" is dated December 2024.
   **Source says:** The home page shows no dated production but promotes a current podcast season and a Heritage Month music offering, with a 2026 footer. The page should not imply it has been inactive since 2024.
   **Link:** https://www.teatrodelpueblo.org/ .
8. **Claim:** d.a.c. (discapacitados abriéndose caminos) has a "latest calendar item dated 2016."
   **Source says:** The home page read shows no dated items and a 2015 footer. Either way, current operation could not be confirmed. It should not be offered as a current referral without a call.
   **Link:** https://dacfamilycenter.org .
9. **Claim:** HACER "co-formed by CLUES in the late 1980s."
   **Source says:** MNopedia gives 1987 for CLUES helping form HACER; HACER's own site says it was founded in 1988. Both should be noted, or the year stated as 1987 to 1988.
   **Links:** https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/minnesotanos-latino-journeys-minnesota ; https://hacer-mn.org/ .
10. **Claim:** Mercado Central opened "with 47 businesses."
    **Source says:** Sahan Journal says 47 at opening; a MNopedia caption says 45 Latino-owned businesses, and a 2009 state report says 45 with over $12 million in sales. These likely describe different dates, but the page should not use one figure as permanent.
    **Links:** https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/mercado-central-lake-street-minneapolis-loan-forgiven-immigration/ ; https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/minnesotanos-latino-journeys-minnesota .
11. **Claim:** Patricia Torres Ray was "the first Latina in the Minnesota Senate," and Edwina Garcia the first known Hispanic woman in the Legislature.
    **Source says:** These can both be true, but sources conflict. Sahan Journal calls Torres Ray the first Latina elected to the Legislature. MNopedia gives Garcia as first Latina in the Legislature, with 1991 in the body and 1998 in the chronology. The page's wording is the safer one, and the conflict is worth noting.
    **Links:** https://sahanjournal.com/democracy-politics/minnesota-legislature-senate-house-latina-lawmakers/ ; https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/minnesotanos-latino-journeys-minnesota .
12. **Claim:** CLUES is described with "offices in Willmar and Austin," with no leader named, while rural parish and worker centers are left to be named locally.
    **Source says:** The Willmar and Austin offices are confirmed, opened in 2017. Liliana Letran-Garcia became President and CEO on Feb. 2, 2026. COPAL also runs offices in Mankato and Rochester and a Minneapolis workers' center, so the page can name more than it does.
    **Links:** https://www.clues.org/about ; https://clues.org/ ; https://copalmn.org/ .
13. **Claim:** The page discusses health access and enforcement fear but does not mention MinnesotaCare or the 2026 coverage change.
    **Source says:** MinnesotaCare ended for undocumented adults on Jan. 1, 2026, affecting about 15,000 adults; children stay eligible. Any practice guidance on coverage should say so.
    **Links:** https://sahanjournal.com/health/undocumented-immigrants-healthcare-minnesotacare/ ; https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261122.pdf .
14. **Claim:** The page lists the Latino Chamber of Commerce with no count of Latino-owned businesses.
    **Source says:** The Chamber says over 12,000 Latino-owned businesses; Sahan Journal in February 2025 said 14,000. If either is added, both should appear.
    **Links:** https://latinochambermn.com/ ; https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/minnesota-latino-businesses-statewide-support/ .
