# Nigerians in Minnesota

"Nigerian" names a country, not a single people. In Minnesota, the people who use the name organize as Igbo, Yoruba, Edo, Egba, Lagosian, Anioma, Ijebu, Ekiti and more, and also as Nigerians together. This profile follows that pattern: it reports the state-level numbers, then the groups, places and institutions that Minnesota sources document.

## 1. People and place

**How many.** Minnesota Compass, using the American Community Survey (ACS), gives two figures. Its best single estimate of the size of the Nigerian cultural community in 2024 is 25,310, give or take 9,024. Its detailed profile uses the larger 2020 to 2024 sample, which counts 21,206 people, give or take 3,847 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/nigerian)). Compass explains that in 2024 the Census Bureau began collecting ancestry in a way that better counts Black and white respondents, which is why it treats the single-year 2024 figure as the most accurate estimate of size ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/nigerian)). The margins of error are wide, so these are estimates, not a head count.

Older figures were smaller and less precise. A 2014 governor's proclamation said "over 13,000" Nigerians lived in Minnesota ([Gov. Mark Dayton, Nigerian Independence Day proclamation, 2014](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/2018/Other/181224/old_original/governor/images/Nigerian_Independence_Day.pdf)), and a 2022 Nigerian news report repeated "more than 13,000" ([ICIR, Aug. 26, 2022](https://www.icirnigeria.org/how-the-igbo-culture-is-gaining-acceptance-in-the-us-state-of-minnesota/)). In 2023, MPR News reported that the Minnesota Department of Employment and Economic Development (DEED) counted 32,500 western African immigrants in the state, with the largest numbers from Liberia and Nigeria ([MPR News, Dec. 26, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/12/26/african-immigrant-entrepreneurs-aim-to-transform-brooklyn-center-shingle-creek-strip-mall)). The Minnesota Institute for Nigerian Development (MIND) describes itself as "a community of over 22,000 members"; that is the organization's own figure, without a stated method ([MIND](https://www.mindmn.org/)).

**Where.** Compass places 81.7 percent of Nigerian Minnesotans in the Twin Cities region and 18.3 percent in Greater Minnesota, each give or take 9.4 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/nigerian)). Inside the metro, the northwest suburbs stand out. Brooklyn Park's city government says Brooklyn Park and Brooklyn Center "both have large and thriving communities from multiple West African countries" ([City of Brooklyn Park](https://www.brooklynpark.org/sister-cities/)), and MPR News reported an estimated 4,500 African immigrants from Somalia, Liberia and Nigeria in the northwest suburbs ([MPR News, Dec. 26, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/12/26/african-immigrant-entrepreneurs-aim-to-transform-brooklyn-center-shingle-creek-strip-mall)). The Igbo festival is held in Brooklyn Park, the Igbo Women League describes itself as near Brooklyn Center, and MIND's mailing address is in Minneapolis 55429 ([North Hennepin Community College](https://www.nhcc.edu/news/igbofest-2026); [Igbo Women League of Minnesota](https://igbowomenleaguemn.org/); [MIND, Naija Groups](https://www.mindmn.org/naija-groups/)). But the community is not confined there. MIND holds its monthly meeting in St. Paul ([MIND FAQ](https://www.mindmn.org/faq/)); Nigerian-owned businesses described below are in Crystal, Burnsville, Lakeville, Minneapolis and St. Paul; and a Yoruba association's list of honored high school graduates names schools in Champlin, Woodbury, Brooklyn Park, White Bear Lake, Stillwater, Oakdale, Shakopee, Wayzata, Blaine, Cottage Grove, Burnsville and St. Paul ([Egbe Omo Oduduwa Minnesota, Our Graduates](https://oduduwamn.org/our-graduates/)). An Igbo photographer told MPR News he first followed a cousin to "a small community of Nigerians" in Brooklyn Park and later lived in Coon Rapids ([MPR News, May 22, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/05/22/this-is-good-food-reclaiming-a-classic-nigerian-dish-in-minnesota)). In Greater Minnesota, St. Cloud State University has had a Nigerian Student Organization ([St. Cloud State, April 23, 2019](https://today.stcloudstate.edu/2019/04/23/nigerian-night-is-april-27/)).

**How people came.** Many early arrivals were students, and many stayed. A Yoruba association's history says one of its founders came to Minnesota in 1976, attending St. Cloud State and then the University of Minnesota, and another arrived in 1972 to study at Southwest State University in Marshall ([Egbe Omo Oduduwa Minnesota, Past Presidents](https://oduduwamn.org/past-presidents/)). A 2016 proclamation said some Igbo residents had lived in Minnesota "for more than forty years" ([Gov. Mark Dayton, Igbo Day proclamation, 2016](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2018/other/181224/governor/assets/2016_08_10_igbo_day_tcm1055-252051.pdf)), and a 2014 proclamation said Lagosians had been in Minnesota "for more than 60 years," with second and third generations here ([Gov. Mark Dayton, Eko Day proclamation, 2014](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2018/other/181224/governor/images/eko_day2014.pdf)). Those are the proclamations' words, drawn from the groups that requested them.

Individual stories show the range of paths. Dr. Fatima Lawson, born in Kano, earned a Ph.D. at the University of Minnesota in 1995 and stayed with her family because of the political situation in Nigeria at the time ([Margaret McNamara Education Grants, April 12, 2023](https://www.mmeg.org/women-building-a-bettter-world-1/2023/4/12/fatima-lawson)). Architect Wale Falade said his whole family came in 2000 because his mother had friends in Minnesota ([Sahan Journal, March 8, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/climate-environment/wale-falade-fihan-north-commons-park-nigerian-architect-minneapolis/)). Father Bruno Nwachukwu arrived on Feb. 1, 2011, to study for the priesthood in St. Paul ([The Catholic Spirit, April 16, 2021](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/practicing-catholic/nigeria-native-journeyed-far-to-priesthood-u-s-citizenship/)). An entrepreneur profiled in 2026 came for a master's degree at a Minnesota university after working in oil and gas ([Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder, April 18, 2026](https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/04/18/amara-anieke-entrepreneur-minneapolis-five-industries/)).

**Newer and older arrivals at once.** Compass reports that 59.0 percent of Nigerian Minnesotans were born abroad, give or take 4.7 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/nigerian)). Among Minnesota's Nigerian-born immigrants, 32 percent had been in the United States five years or fewer, the highest share of any immigrant group Compass listed, while 30 percent had been here more than 20 years ([Minnesota Compass, Aug. 3, 2026](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/10-things-know-about-immigration-minnesota)). So the community holds long-settled families with U.S.-born grandchildren and a large group of recent arrivals.

**Age.** One in three (33.4 percent) is 17 or younger, and 6.8 percent are 65 or older ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/nigerian)).

**Languages.** Among Nigerian Minnesotans 5 and older, 59.3 percent speak a language other than English at home, and 11.3 percent, give or take 3.6 points, speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/nigerian)). Compass does not say which languages. Minnesota sources name Igbo (the Umunne and Igbo Women League communities, and musician Obi Original), Yoruba (the Oduduwa and Egba associations; architect Falade, who grew up speaking it in Lagos), and Edo, Anioma, Ibadan, Ijebu and Ekiti groups ([MIND, Naija Groups](https://www.mindmn.org/naija-groups/); [Sahan Journal, Aug. 26, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/obi-original-afrobeats-nigerian-musician/)). English is widely used, and one Igbo immigrant told MPR News that "the English spoken in Nigeria is different from the English spoken here" ([MPR News, May 22, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/05/22/this-is-good-food-reclaiming-a-classic-nigerian-dish-in-minnesota)). No Minnesota source was found that counts speakers of Hausa, Nigerian Pidgin or any single Nigerian language.

## 2. All walks of life

**Professionals and the comfortable.** Compass reports that 55.3 percent of Nigerian Minnesotans 25 and older hold a bachelor's degree or higher, including 23.7 percent with a graduate or professional degree ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/nigerian)). Among households, 43.9 percent, give or take 12.2 points, have incomes of $100,000 or more. Public examples include a state legislator and Harvard-trained lawyer ([Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/legdb/fulldetail.aspx?ID=15551)); an award-winning St. Paul school principal with a Minnesota doctorate, Dr. Fatima Lawson ([Books For Africa, 2024](https://www.booksforafrica.org/news/star-tribune-fatima-lawson-2024.html)); the founder of a St. Paul architecture firm chosen by the Minneapolis park board to redesign North Commons Park, Wale Falade ([Sahan Journal, March 8, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/climate-environment/wale-falade-fihan-north-commons-park-nigerian-architect-minneapolis/)); a psychiatric nurse practitioner who built her own clinic, Adejoke Adedeji ([MPR News, Sept. 15, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/09/15/practitioner-works-to-erase-stigma-some-african-immigrants-feel-about-mental-health-care)); and a professional nurses' association that has marked its 20th anniversary ([Association of Nigerian Nurses in Minnesota](https://annimn.nursingnetwork.com/)).

**Working and middle-income families.** The median household income is $76,000 in 2024 dollars, give or take $22,344, a margin wide enough that the true figure could be well above or below it ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/nigerian)). About 89.1 percent of adults 18 to 64 are employed (87.8 percent of men and 90.4 percent of women). Many families are building rather than settled: 55.0 percent of households rent and 45.0 percent own, each give or take 11.1 points. Musician Obi Original described his parents, a teacher and a nurse, as having "worked very hard to make sure that I had a comfortable life" ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 26, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/obi-original-afrobeats-nigerian-musician/)).

**Families under strain.** The same Compass profile shows that 17.9 percent of households earn under $35,000, give or take 8.9 points. One in five people (20.5 percent) live between 150 and 199 percent of the poverty line, close enough to poverty that a lost job or a medical bill matters; the share below the poverty line itself is suppressed because the sample is too small. A third of households (33.6 percent) are cost-burdened by housing, and among renters it is 47.4 percent, give or take 15.5 points. About 11.7 percent have no health insurance, and 9.4 percent of households have no vehicle ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/nigerian)). Entrepreneurs told MPR News how they had financed African-owned businesses in the northwest suburbs: by refinancing homes, borrowing from family, or a son cashing out a retirement account ([MPR News, Dec. 26, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/12/26/african-immigrant-entrepreneurs-aim-to-transform-brooklyn-center-shingle-creek-strip-mall)). That comment was about African immigrant businesses broadly, not Nigerians alone.

**Recent arrivals.** With a third of Nigerian-born Minnesotans here five years or fewer ([Minnesota Compass, Aug. 3, 2026](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/10-things-know-about-immigration-minnesota)), many households are still finding footing. Compass reports, for all Minnesota immigrants, that 68 percent of adults who have been here five years or fewer are working, against 81 percent of those here more than 20 years ([Minnesota Compass, Aug. 3, 2026](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/10-things-know-about-immigration-minnesota)). Compass does not break that out for Nigerians.

Taken together, the numbers describe a community with a large professional class and real economic security for many, alongside renters stretched by housing costs and newcomers working their way in. Neither "wealthy" nor "struggling" describes it alone.

## 3. Work and the economy

Compass reports high employment (89.1 percent of working-age adults) and that 81.2 percent of workers drive to work ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/nigerian)). No Minnesota source was found that breaks down Nigerian Minnesotans by industry or occupation.

What the sources do show is a strong presence in health care. The Association of Nigerian Nurses in Minnesota describes itself as a nonprofit "of all the Nigerian Nurses Associations in Minnesota," which means there is more than one, and offers continuing education to members ([ANNIMN](https://annimn.nursingnetwork.com/)). MIND links to it from its own site ([MIND, Helpful Links](https://www.mindmn.org/helpful-links/)). Nigerian Minnesotans also own health and care businesses: a psychiatric practice in the south metro, a prenatal and postpartum company, and an assisted living home in Brooklyn Park (section 4). A Nigerian-born priest who served as a hospital chaplain at North Memorial in Robbinsdale in 2021 now leads a parish in Bayport ([The Catholic Spirit, April 16, 2021](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/practicing-catholic/nigeria-native-journeyed-far-to-priesthood-u-s-citizenship/); [The Catholic Spirit, July 17, 2026](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/special-sections/world-mission-sunday/an-african-irish-priest-shares-his-mission-in-the-archdiocese/)), and a Nigerian Catholic sister works at a Minneapolis substance use and mental health treatment center ([The Catholic Spirit, July 17, 2026](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/special-sections/world-mission-sunday/sharing-christs-love-with-the-vulnerable-in-minneapolis/)).

Other fields in the record include education (a public school principal; an architect who teaches at Dunwoody College of Technology), law and public service, engineering and quality management, architecture, food service, nightlife and live-music promotion, and film ([Books For Africa](https://www.booksforafrica.org/news/star-tribune-fatima-lawson-2024.html); [Sahan Journal, March 8, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/climate-environment/wale-falade-fihan-north-commons-park-nigerian-architect-minneapolis/); [Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder, April 18, 2026](https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/04/18/amara-anieke-entrepreneur-minneapolis-five-industries/); [Sahan Journal, Aug. 4, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/news/minnesota-african-cultural-igbo-fest-lets-gidi-yama-fest/)). Freelance work appears too: an Igbo photographer told MPR News he did not want to be "punching numbers for some guy" and built a living around his camera ([MPR News, May 22, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/05/22/this-is-good-food-reclaiming-a-classic-nigerian-dish-in-minnesota)).

**Entrepreneurship and its barriers.** The Purposed Parent's founders said neither came from a family with a business background and that a support program helped them move from an LLC to an S-corporation and win hospital partnerships ([Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder, June 3, 2026](https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/06/03/purposed-parent-black-maternal-health/)). Architect Falade said he "wasn't sure if, as a Black immigrant in Minnesota, I could succeed" in starting his own firm ([Sahan Journal, March 8, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/climate-environment/wale-falade-fihan-north-commons-park-nigerian-architect-minneapolis/)).

**Ties across the ocean.** Business and family life cross borders, which is why federal visa changes in 2025 and 2026 matter (section 13). Brooklyn Park approved sister-city ties with Udu, in Delta State, in 2023, and when debating a 2025 visit, city leaders pointed to Nigeria's 300 million people and large economy ([CCX Media, March 4, 2023](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-approves-sister-city-relationship-with-udu-nigeria/); [CCX Media, July 15, 2025](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-mayors-planned-african-vacation-turns-into-sister-city-trip-debate/)). After a 2025 trip, the mayor of Brooklyn Park said he saw "so many opportunities for trade, for investment" ([CCX Media, Sept. 11, 2025](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-mayor-points-to-trade-opportunities-following-west-africa-trip/)).

No Minnesota source was found on union membership or labor history specific to Nigerian workers.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

There is no Nigerian commercial district in Minnesota. Nigerian-owned businesses are spread across the metro, often in strip malls and small storefronts, and some operate mainly online or by catering. An Igbo Minnesotan told MPR News in 2023 that Nigerian restaurants had been "very far and few in between," and that most of the earlier ones "have gone under" ([MPR News, May 18, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/05/18/when-fufu-is-a-taste-of-love-and-a-taste-of-home)). The businesses below were found in Minnesota sources; the full list with status is in the accompanying business file.

**Food.**
- **Glamaur African Kitchen**, 8527 Zane Ave. N., Brooklyn Park. Opened in 2020 by Gladys Eta, a Nigerian immigrant who had run a catering business; the name joins her name and her husband Maurice's ([CCX Media, Aug. 17, 2020](https://ccxmedia.org/news/glamaur-african-kitchen-celebrates-grand-opening-in-brooklyn-park/); [Glamaur African Kitchen](https://glamaurafricankitchen.com/)). CCX Media reported in July 2026 that she would serve at IgboFest ([CCX Media, July 31, 2026](https://ccxmedia.org/news/igbofest-returns-to-brooklyn-park-aug-8-celebrating-nigerian-culture/)).
- **3D's African Kitchen**, 117 Willow Bend, Crystal Shopping Center. Opened in April 2024 by Kafayat Abodunrin, who came from Nigeria in 2017, serving Nigerian and Liberian dishes from her grandmother's recipes ([CCX Media, April 25, 2024](https://ccxmedia.org/news/3ds-african-kitchen-brings-nigerian-liberian-cuisine-to-crystal/)). Current operation could not be confirmed.

**Nightlife and entertainment.**
- **Gidi Bar & Lounge**, Minneapolis Warehouse District. Founded in 2024 by Dayo Olateju, who moved from Nigeria in 2010, and his team; it hosts Afrobeats, hip-hop and Amapiano nights and a fall 2026 event calendar ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 4, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/news/minnesota-african-cultural-igbo-fest-lets-gidi-yama-fest/); [Gidi Bar & Lounge](https://www.gidimpls.com/)).
- **Bel Curv Management Group**, Olateju's event production company, which Sahan Journal says has booked major Afrobeats artists such as Burna Boy, WizKid and Yemi Alade and co-produces Let's Gidi Fest ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 4, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/news/minnesota-african-cultural-igbo-fest-lets-gidi-yama-fest/)).

**Health and care.**
- **Nexus of Hope Psychiatry & Mental Health Wellness**, founded in 2019 by psychiatric nurse practitioner Adejoke Adedeji, who was born in the U.S. and grew up in Nigeria. MPR News described it in Lakeville in 2023; a current listing gives a Burnsville office ([MPR News, Sept. 15, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/09/15/practitioner-works-to-erase-stigma-some-african-immigrants-feel-about-mental-health-care); [Psychology Today listing](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/psychiatrists/nexus-of-hope-psychiatry-mental-health-wellness-burnsville-mn/705433)).
- **The Purposed Parent**, Twin Cities prenatal, birth and postpartum support, founded in 2022 by sisters Praise and Peace Olowokere, with 12 specialists in 2026 ([Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder, June 3, 2026](https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/06/03/purposed-parent-black-maternal-health/); [The Purposed Parent](https://www.thepurposedparent.com/)).
- **Unified Health**, an assisted living home near 69th and Zane avenues in Brooklyn Park built by a Nigerian family, which CCX Media described at its 2021 groundbreaking as care with "an African touch" ([CCX Media, Oct. 29, 2021](https://ccxmedia.org/news/construction-begins-on-brooklyn-park-assisted-living-facility-with-an-african-touch/)). Current operation could not be confirmed.

**Design, arts and media.**
- **FIHAN Design + Architecture**, St. Paul. Founded about 2020 by Wale Falade; "fihan" means "to reveal" in Yoruba ([Sahan Journal, March 8, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/climate-environment/wale-falade-fihan-north-commons-park-nigerian-architect-minneapolis/); [FIHAN](https://www.fihan.design/)).
- **Zoma House**, Uptown Minneapolis. A studio, event venue and storefront for Black and underrepresented artists, opened in 2026 by Uzoma Ngwu, whose Igbo first name gives it its name; the city's Vibrant Storefronts Program covers two years of rent ([Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder, Aug. 26, 2026](https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/08/26/zoma-house-creators-path-minneapolis-uzoma-ngwu/)).

**Markets and media.** Brooklyn Park and Brooklyn Center have several pan-African groceries and an African wholesaler, but the sources describing them do not identify Nigerian owners, so they are not listed here as Nigerian businesses ([Minneapolis Northwest Tourism](https://www.minneapolisnorthwest.com/blog/post/shop-the-many-markets-of-minneapolis-northwest/)). No Nigerian-owned newspaper, radio program or chamber of commerce based in Minnesota was found. Minnesotans follow Nigerian news through national and Nigerian outlets, and Minnesota's African-focused press, such as Mshale, covers the community.

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

Nigerian Minnesotans include Catholics, Protestants of many kinds, Pentecostals, Muslims and people who follow other paths or none. Minnesota sources document some of this, not all of it.

**Catholics.** The clearest record is Catholic. In 2017, after a committee of Nigerian Catholics had worked for years to arrange it, the Archdiocese of St. Paul and Minneapolis began a monthly Nigerian Mass at St. Peter Claver in St. Paul, a historically African American parish. Archbishop Bernard Hebda presided at the first one on Aug. 27, 2017, for a standing-room crowd. Parts of the Mass were said in a Nigerian language, with hymns and sometimes dance at the offertory, and it was set for the fourth Sunday of each month ([The Catholic Spirit, Sept. 1, 2017](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/nigerian-mass-begins-st-paul/)). The organizing priest, Father Bruno Nwachukwu, said the Mass would help immigrants who struggle with English and give U.S.-born children a sense of Mass in Nigeria. He also said some Nigerian Catholics had left for Pentecostal churches because they did not feel welcome in Catholic parishes ([The Catholic Spirit, Sept. 1, 2017](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/nigerian-mass-begins-st-paul/)). The Mass continues: in July 2026, The Catholic Spirit reported that Father Nwachukwu, now pastor of St. Charles in Bayport, celebrates a Nigerian Mass every fourth Sunday at St. Peter Claver and serves as chaplain of the Nigerian Catholic Community of Minnesota. He calls himself "African-Irish," crediting Irish missionaries for bringing the faith to Nigeria, and said he took accent-reduction classes in seminary because "if you're not able to communicate, it's very difficult" ([The Catholic Spirit, July 17, 2026](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/special-sections/world-mission-sunday/an-african-irish-priest-shares-his-mission-in-the-archdiocese/)). In September 2026, a newly reconstituted archdiocesan Commission of Black Catholic Ministry named as treasurer a founding member of "the Nigerian Catholic Community in the Twin Cities," and its chair said people from Nigeria and several other African nations attend St. Peter Claver ([The Catholic Spirit, Sept. 17, 2026](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/black-catholic-ministry-commission-seeks-unity-in-diversity/)). Nigerian clergy and religious serve the wider church here as well, including five sisters of the Nigerian-based Congregation of the Sisters of the Immaculate Heart of Mary, Mother of Christ ([The Catholic Spirit, July 17, 2026](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/special-sections/world-mission-sunday/sharing-christs-love-with-the-vulnerable-in-minneapolis/)).

**Protestants and Pentecostals.** The Nigerian American state representative's father is an Episcopal priest ([Voice of America, Jan. 21, 2021](https://www.voanews.com/a/usa_us-politics_daughter-nigerian-immigrants-hopes-lead-example-minnesota-legislature/6201049.html)). MIND holds its monthly general meeting at Camphor Memorial Methodist Church in St. Paul ([MIND FAQ](https://www.mindmn.org/faq/)); the source does not say the congregation is Nigerian. The Yoruba association Egbe Omo Oduduwa lists a pastor among its past presidents ([Egbe Omo Oduduwa Minnesota, Past Presidents](https://oduduwamn.org/past-presidents/)). The Redeemed Christian Church of God, founded in Lagos in 1952 ([RCCG](https://rccg.org/our-history/)), listed a Minneapolis parish, Strong Tower, among parishes "no longer in RCCG" in a 2020 document ([RCCG North America, 2020](https://www.rccgna.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Copy-of-Churches-that-have-left-RCCG-Updated.pdf)). No current Minnesota parish of the Redeemed Christian Church of God, or of other Nigerian-founded Pentecostal churches, could be confirmed from an opened source.

**Faith in daily life.** Faith runs through how people describe their work. Restaurant owners Gladys Eta and Kafayat Abodunrin each told CCX Media they were "grateful to God" at their openings, and the founders of The Purposed Parent credit their faith for their growth ([CCX Media, Aug. 17, 2020](https://ccxmedia.org/news/glamaur-african-kitchen-celebrates-grand-opening-in-brooklyn-park/); [CCX Media, April 25, 2024](https://ccxmedia.org/news/3ds-african-kitchen-brings-nigerian-liberian-cuisine-to-crystal/); [Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder, June 3, 2026](https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/06/03/purposed-parent-black-maternal-health/)). A Nigerian Catholic sister working in mental health here contrasted the U.S. with Nigeria, where, she said, people who struggle with mental health often "go to prayer houses" ([The Catholic Spirit, July 17, 2026](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/special-sections/world-mission-sunday/sharing-christs-love-with-the-vulnerable-in-minneapolis/)). She was speaking about Nigeria, not about Minnesota families.

**Muslims and others.** Nigeria has a large Muslim population, but no Minnesota source was found describing Nigerian Muslim congregations, associations or Eid gatherings. No Minnesota source was found on traditional Yoruba, Igbo or other Nigerian religious practice here, or on Nigerian Minnesotans who are not religious.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

**IgboFest (August).** Minnesota's largest Nigerian public celebration is IgboFest, presented by the Umunne Cultural Association, which Igbo Minnesotans formed in 1985. The first IgboFest was held in 1994 ([Gov. Mark Dayton, Igbo Day proclamation, 2016](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2018/other/181224/governor/assets/2016_08_10_igbo_day_tcm1055-252051.pdf)). It is free and held on the grounds of North Hennepin Community College in Brooklyn Park. The 31st IgboFest took place on Saturday, Aug. 8, 2026, from 11 a.m. to 7 p.m., under the theme "preserving our cultural heritage," co-sponsored by the City of Brooklyn Park, Minneapolis Northwest Tourism and the Minnesota Humanities Center ([North Hennepin Community College](https://www.nhcc.edu/news/igbofest-2026); [CCX Media, July 31, 2026](https://ccxmedia.org/news/igbofest-returns-to-brooklyn-park-aug-8-celebrating-nigerian-culture/)). A gala on Friday, Aug. 7, 2026, at the Brooklyn Park Activity Center invited people to come in traditional Igbo attire ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 4, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/news/minnesota-african-cultural-igbo-fest-lets-gidi-yama-fest/)). The festival features masquerade displays, dances by children and women, clothing and bead vendors, and Igbo food ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/news/minnesota-african-cultural-igbo-fest-lets-gidi-yama-fest/)). In 2019 the association's president described egusi soup, jollof rice, grilled meats, and corn, "oka" in Igbo ([CCX Media, Aug. 3, 2019](https://ccxmedia.org/news/celebrate-west-african-culture-at-igbofest-in-brooklyn-park/)); in 2022 organizers expected performances by 40 dance groups ([CCX Media, Aug. 11, 2022](https://ccxmedia.org/news/annual-igbofest-celebration-will-showcase-diversity-in-brooklyn-park/)), and the gala included the Ada Igbo pageant ([ICIR, Aug. 26, 2022](https://www.icirnigeria.org/how-the-igbo-culture-is-gaining-acceptance-in-the-us-state-of-minnesota/)). Attendance estimates differ: Minneapolis Northwest Tourism says more than 5,000 ([Minneapolis Northwest Tourism](https://www.minneapolisnorthwest.com/events/annual-events-and-festivals/igbofest/)), and the association's president told Sahan Journal about 10,000 a year ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/news/minnesota-african-cultural-igbo-fest-lets-gidi-yama-fest/)). He said the festival also gives African Americans who have never been to Nigeria a chance to learn about their ancestry.

**Igbo Day.** Minnesota governors have proclaimed the IgboFest Saturday as Igbo Day, including in 2016, 2019 and 2022 ([Gov. Dayton, 2016](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2018/other/181224/governor/assets/2016_08_10_igbo_day_tcm1055-252051.pdf); [Office of the Governor, 2019](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/08.10.19%20Igbo%20Day_tcm1055-398324.pdf); [ICIR, 2022](https://www.icirnigeria.org/how-the-igbo-culture-is-gaining-acceptance-in-the-us-state-of-minnesota/)).

**Nigerian Independence Day (Oct. 1).** Oct. 1 marks Nigeria's independence. Gov. Mark Dayton proclaimed Oct. 1, 2014, the 54th anniversary, Nigerian Independence Day in Minnesota ([proclamation, 2014](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/2018/Other/181224/old_original/governor/images/Nigerian_Independence_Day.pdf)). MIND lists an "Independence Celebration in October" among its yearly events, but no date for the 2026 celebration was found ([MIND Events](https://www.mindmn.org/events/)).

**Let's Gidi Fest (August).** A newer outdoor festival of Afrobeats and African food from Gidi Bar & Lounge and Bel Curv, in its second year in 2026. It ran Saturday, Aug. 8, 2026, from noon to 10 p.m. in Minneapolis, with Nigerian artists Flavour and Lojay ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 4, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/news/minnesota-african-cultural-igbo-fest-lets-gidi-yama-fest/)).

**Yoruba Fest.** The Yoruba association Egbe Omo Oduduwa Minnesota invited vendors for a 2026 Yoruba Fest ([Egbe Omo Oduduwa Minnesota](https://oduduwamn.org/egbe-omo-oduduwa-mn-2026-yoruba-fest-arts-and-craftvendor-application-form/)). The opened pages did not give the date or place.

**Other gatherings.** MIND holds an annual family picnic in summer ([MIND Events](https://www.mindmn.org/events/)). The Egba association has held a back-to-school picnic since 2012, giving children backpacks and school supplies, and hosted the Egba National Convention in 2024 with two traditional rulers from Nigeria ([Egba Descendants Association of Minnesota](https://egbamn.org/about-us)). Eko Club Minnesota, an association of Lagosians, marked its 10th anniversary in 2014, which Gov. Dayton proclaimed Eko Day ([proclamation](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2018/other/181224/governor/images/eko_day2014.pdf)). The Igbo Women League staged Egwu Onwa, a traditional moonlight gathering, for children in its summer youth program ([Igbo Women League of Minnesota](https://igbowomenleaguemn.org/)). St. Cloud State's Nigerian Student Organization has held a Nigerian Night of food, dance and drama ([St. Cloud State, 2019](https://today.stcloudstate.edu/2019/04/23/nigerian-night-is-april-27/)).

**Religious holidays.** Christmas and Easter for Christians and the Eids for Muslims are observed by Nigerians worldwide, but no Minnesota source was found describing how Nigerian Minnesotans celebrate them as a community. No Minnesota source was found for the Igbo New Yam festival, the Yoruba Osun or Egungun festivals, or Nigeria's June 12 Democracy Day.

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Households.** Compass counts 7,318 Nigerian Minnesotan households with an average size of 2.92 people. Married couples with children under 18 make up 31.2 percent of households and married couples without children at home 24.5 percent; 23.5 percent are people living alone ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/nigerian)). A third of the community is under 18, so many households are raising children.

**Childhood and generations.** Parents and associations put weight on school and heritage together. Egbe Omo Oduduwa has honored high school graduates and lists where they go next, from the University of Minnesota Duluth and St. Olaf to Minnesota State Mankato and community colleges ([Egbe Omo Oduduwa Minnesota](https://oduduwamn.org/our-graduates/)). The Igbo Women League runs a summer youth mentoring program with Igbo language, cooking, drumming and dance ([Igbo Women League of Minnesota](https://igbowomenleaguemn.org/)). Umunne's president told Sahan Journal that IgboFest gives children who have never been to Nigeria "an opportunity to replicate what life is all about in the Motherland" ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 4, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/news/minnesota-african-cultural-igbo-fest-lets-gidi-yama-fest/)). Not every child grows up the same way: Obi Original, born in Minnesota in 1995, spent his early years in Lagos and returned to Nigeria every summer until 2020 ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 26, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/obi-original-afrobeats-nigerian-musician/)).

**Birth and new parents.** The founders of The Purposed Parent said that as children they followed their mother to the home of whoever had just given birth, and that this kind of care "has always been essential" in their culture as Nigerian Americans ([Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder, June 3, 2026](https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/06/03/purposed-parent-black-maternal-health/)).

**Food.** Fufu, egusi, okra soup and tomato stew appear in MPR News's 2023 portrait of an Igbo cook, who uses powdered yam because whole African yams are hard to import, and serves the meal communally with finger bowls ([MPR News, May 18, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/05/18/when-fufu-is-a-taste-of-love-and-a-taste-of-home)). He spoke openly about stigma: African food "has been demonized," and people were "insulted for smelling like crayfish," so some immigrants distanced themselves from their own food before reclaiming it ([MPR News, May 22, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/05/22/this-is-good-food-reclaiming-a-classic-nigerian-dish-in-minnesota)). That is one person's account.

**Dress.** IgboFest's gala calls for traditional Igbo attire ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 4, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/news/minnesota-african-cultural-igbo-fest-lets-gidi-yama-fest/)). No Minnesota source was found describing everyday dress.

**Aging.** Only 6.8 percent are 65 or older ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/nigerian)), but planning for elders has begun. MIND's proposed Nigerian community and cultural center, which it calls the "Nigerian House," is meant partly as "a gathering place for our elders" ([MIND](https://www.mindmn.org/)). A Nigerian family's Brooklyn Park assisted living home was described by one director as care for people like her 102-year-old grandmother ([CCX Media, Oct. 29, 2021](https://ccxmedia.org/news/construction-begins-on-brooklyn-park-assisted-living-facility-with-an-african-touch/)).

No Minnesota source was found on marriage customs, naming ceremonies or funerals among Nigerian Minnesotans.

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Music.** Obi Original (Obiora Obikwelu), born in Minnesota in 1995, plays Igbo highlife with his band The Black Atlantics, performed at the Minnesota State Fair in August 2025, has hosted dance parties at First Avenue, and planned his first studio album for November 2025 ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 26, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/obi-original-afrobeats-nigerian-musician/)). He describes his music as a classroom: "I don't have a chalkboard. I have my guitar." He also explained that Igbo listeners gravitate to highlife, while Yoruba traditional music is often called jùjú or fújì. On the promotion side, Bel Curv has booked major Nigerian Afrobeats stars, and Let's Gidi Fest brought Flavour and Lojay in 2026 ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 4, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/news/minnesota-african-cultural-igbo-fest-lets-gidi-yama-fest/)).

**Dance and performance.** IgboFest is the main stage, with masquerades and dozens of dance groups, and Umunne describes using "cultural arts and performances to enhance self-esteem in youths" ([CCX Media, 2022](https://ccxmedia.org/news/annual-igbofest-celebration-will-showcase-diversity-in-brooklyn-park/); [Umunne, Programs](https://www.umunne.org/charity-organization-programs)). Coverage of the 2022 festival pictured Umunne's Bende war dance group ([ICIR](https://www.icirnigeria.org/how-the-igbo-culture-is-gaining-acceptance-in-the-us-state-of-minnesota/)).

**Visual art, film and design.** Zoma House in Uptown, founded by Uzoma Ngwu, who also runs an animation and film studio, hosts screenings, concerts, artist talks and classes ([Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder, Aug. 26, 2026](https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/08/26/zoma-house-creators-path-minneapolis-uzoma-ngwu/)). Entrepreneur Amara Anieke is developing a limited television series and a documentary series ([Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder, April 18, 2026](https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/04/18/amara-anieke-entrepreneur-minneapolis-five-industries/)). Photographer Uche Iroegbu counts his photographs from the unrest after George Floyd's murder as his favorite work ([MPR News, May 22, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/05/22/this-is-good-food-reclaiming-a-classic-nigerian-dish-in-minnesota)). Architect Wale Falade helped design a curling-themed piece for the Art Shanties in White Bear Lake ([Sahan Journal, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/climate-environment/wale-falade-fihan-north-commons-park-nigerian-architect-minneapolis/)). Visitors to Minneapolis can also see Nigerian-born, Brooklyn-based artist Olalekan Jeyifous's first solo museum show at the Walker Art Center through Jan. 3, which draws on the Mississippi River and Ben Okri's novel "The Famished Road"; he is not a Minnesotan ([MPR News, Aug. 7, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/08/07/mn-shortlist-aug-7-13-mn-fringe-artcrank-murder-mystery-red-valley-ren-faire-and-more)).

**Media.** No Minnesota-based Nigerian newspaper or broadcast was found. Nigerian outlets have covered Minnesota's Igbo community, and Minnesota's Sahan Journal, MPR News, CCX Media, the Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder and The Catholic Spirit cover Nigerian Minnesotans regularly.

**Sports.** No Minnesota source opened for this profile covered Nigerian Minnesotan athletes or sports clubs. That is a gap, not a finding.

## 9. Civic and political life

**Elected office.** A state representative (DFL, Minneapolis, District 59B) was born in St. Paul in 1985 to parents who "came to Minnesota from Nigeria," was first elected in 2020 and is serving the 2025 to 2026 term, her third ([Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/legdb/fulldetail.aspx?ID=15551)). Voice of America reported in 2021 that she was the first Nigerian American to serve in the Minnesota House, and she credited West African volunteers who "jumped on board" her campaign ([Voice of America, Jan. 21, 2021](https://www.voanews.com/a/usa_us-politics_daughter-nigerian-immigrants-hopes-lead-example-minnesota-legislature/6201049.html)). Sahan Journal counted at least 50 African immigrants who ran for office in Minnesota between 2016 and 2020 ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 30, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/politics/black-immigrants-minnesota-elections/)).

**Organizations.** MIND calls itself "the proud umbrella for the Nigerian community in Minnesota since 1994." It holds open monthly meetings, offers information on Nigerian passport renewals and connects members with the consulate in New York, volunteers with Second Harvest Heartland and Feed My Starving Children, and is raising money for a community center ([MIND](https://www.mindmn.org/); [MIND in Action](https://www.mindmn.org/mind-in-action-2/); [MIND FAQ](https://www.mindmn.org/faq/)). MIND lists these member groups: the Igbo Women League of Minnesota, the Egba Descendants Association of Minnesota, Egbe Omo Oduduwa Minnesota, the Edo Association of Minnesota, the Anioma Cultural Association in Minnesota, the Ibadan Descendants' Union, the Ijebu Descendants Association Minnesota, Ekitileto, and the Umunne Cultural Association ([MIND, Naija Groups](https://www.mindmn.org/naija-groups/)). Their founding dates show how the community built itself: Umunne in 1985, Egbe Omo Oduduwa in 1995, the Igbo Women League about 30 years ago, the Egba association in 2002 and Eko Club in 2004 ([proclamation, 2016](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2018/other/181224/governor/assets/2016_08_10_igbo_day_tcm1055-252051.pdf); [Egbe Omo Oduduwa](https://oduduwamn.org/past-presidents/); [Igbo Women League](https://igbowomenleaguemn.org/); [Egba association](https://egbamn.org/about-us); [Eko Day proclamation](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2018/other/181224/governor/images/eko_day2014.pdf)). Brooklyn Center honored the Igbo Women League in its 2023 Random Acts of Kindness awards ([CCX Media, Dec. 9, 2023](https://ccxmedia.org/news/for-27th-year-brooklyn-center-celebrates-random-acts-of-kindness/)).

**Giving at home and abroad.** Much civic work crosses the ocean. The Igbo Women League supports about 11 motherless babies' homes in Nigeria and sponsors girls' schooling ([Igbo Women League](https://igbowomenleaguemn.org/)). The Egba association gives scholarships in Nigeria and has provided meals to people experiencing homelessness at the Gospel Mission ([Egba association, Events](https://egbamn.org/events)). A Brooklyn Center nonprofit and a credit union collected more than 6,000 books for students in Nigeria in 2019 ([CCX Media, Aug. 20, 2019](https://ccxmedia.org/news/local-fundraising-effort-collects-6000-books-for-kids-in-nigeria/)).

**National diaspora politics in Minnesota.** In November 2024, rival factions of the Nigerians in Diaspora Organisation Americas met in Bloomington and signed a unification agreement, with more than 20 U.S. chapter presidents taking part, according to a statement reported by ThisDay ([ThisDay, Nov. 22, 2024](https://www.thisdaylive.com/2024/11/22/nigerian-groups-in-the-americas-resolve-dispute-unite/)).

**Sister cities and local debate.** Brooklyn Park approved a sister-city relationship with Udu, Nigeria, in 2023 ([CCX Media, March 4, 2023](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-approves-sister-city-relationship-with-udu-nigeria/)). In July 2025, a proposal to add sister-city visits to the mayor's family trip to Lagos drew support and questions from residents at public comment, and online conflict that one council member called "nasty"; the council voted 4-2 to fund a visit to Udu only ([CCX Media, July 15, 2025](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-mayors-planned-african-vacation-turns-into-sister-city-trip-debate/); [CCX Media, July 22, 2025](https://ccxmedia.org/news/council-approves-brooklyn-park-mayors-sister-city-visit-but-only-in-nigeria/)). The mayor said people in Nigeria asked him "what tribe we're from" because "we look like everybody else over there" ([CCX Media, Sept. 11, 2025](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-mayor-points-to-trade-opportunities-following-west-africa-trip/)).

**Advisory roles.** Dr. Fatima Lawson served two terms on the Governor's Council for Minnesotans of African Heritage and has been MIND's vice president ([Margaret McNamara Education Grants, 2023](https://www.mmeg.org/women-building-a-bettter-world-1/2023/4/12/fatima-lawson)).

No Minnesota source was found describing internal debates among Nigerian Minnesotans over Nigerian politics, such as the Biafra question, though the existence of separate Igbo, Yoruba, Edo and other associations under one umbrella shows how the community organizes across those lines.

## 10. Education

Nigerian Minnesotans have high educational attainment: 95.8 percent of adults 25 and older finished high school and 55.3 percent hold a bachelor's degree or higher, each with margins of error ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/nigerian)). Only 4.2 percent did not finish high school.

**Students.** The University of Minnesota has a Nigerian Student Association that aims "to foster a community of Nigerian students in the diaspora" ([GopherLink](https://gopherlink.umn.edu/4571/)), and St. Cloud State's Nigerian Student Organization says Nigeria "is not a monolith" ([St. Cloud State, 2019](https://today.stcloudstate.edu/2019/04/23/nigerian-night-is-april-27/)). Graduates honored by Egbe Omo Oduduwa have gone on to the University of Minnesota Duluth, St. Olaf, the University of St. Thomas, Minnesota State Mankato, St. Cloud State, Bemidji State and community colleges ([Egbe Omo Oduduwa](https://oduduwamn.org/our-graduates/)).

**Educators.** Dr. Fatima Lawson helped start a St. Paul language immersion school that she led for 12 years, and in 2024 won an award for her work as principal of Highwood Hills Elementary in St. Paul, which adopted a polytechnic approach at the urging of Somali, Karen and Hispanic families ([Margaret McNamara Education Grants, 2023](https://www.mmeg.org/women-building-a-bettter-world-1/2023/4/12/fatima-lawson); [Books For Africa, 2024](https://www.booksforafrica.org/news/star-tribune-fatima-lawson-2024.html)). Architect Wale Falade teaches at Dunwoody College of Technology ([Sahan Journal, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/climate-environment/wale-falade-fihan-north-commons-park-nigerian-architect-minneapolis/)).

**Heritage learning.** Language and culture are taught mostly by community groups: the Igbo Women League's summer program and IgboFest workshops (section 7). No Minnesota school program teaching Yoruba, Igbo or another Nigerian language was found.

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Coverage.** About 11.7 percent of Nigerian Minnesotans lack health insurance, give or take 6.0 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/nigerian)).

**Providers from the community.** The Association of Nigerian Nurses in Minnesota works "to eliminate health disparities through the delivery of culturally competent, quality healthcare" ([ANNIMN](https://annimn.nursingnetwork.com/)). The Igbo Women League partnered with the Minnesota Department of Health on vaccination drives using state grants, and in December 2023 gave a $100 gift card to each person vaccinated ([Igbo Women League](https://igbowomenleaguemn.org/)). Umunne says it provides "free mobile culturally specific mental health services" ([Umunne, Programs](https://www.umunne.org/charity-organization-programs)).

**Mental health and stigma.** Adejoke Adedeji, the founder of Nexus of Hope, whose own family losses in 2010 turned her toward mental health care, told MPR News that too many people in African immigrant communities "feel too stigmatized" to seek help, and that privacy laws do not always ease the fear that "things are too close to home." A Nigerian-born patient said finding a Nigerian therapist had been freeing ([MPR News, Sept. 15, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/09/15/practitioner-works-to-erase-stigma-some-african-immigrants-feel-about-mental-health-care)). The musician Obi Original's group made an album titled "Free Therapy" to push mental health up the list in Black and BIPOC circles ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/obi-original-afrobeats-nigerian-musician/)).

**Birth.** The Purposed Parent focuses on Black maternal and infant health and offers some services free to families who cannot pay ([Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder, June 3, 2026](https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/06/03/purposed-parent-black-maternal-health/)).

**What was not found.** No Minnesota Department of Health report with Nigerian-specific health data was found.

## 12. Disability

**Minnesota numbers.** Compass reports that 7.3 percent of Nigerian Minnesotans have a disability, give or take 2.3 points, and 5.6 percent of those under 65, give or take 2.5 points (ACS 2020 to 2024) ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/nigerian)). Several questions follow. Is the low share a real difference, a result of the community's youth (a third are children, and few are over 65), or a sign that people do not describe themselves in survey terms? The numbers alone cannot answer that.

**What a label opens.** A Nigerian family's Brooklyn Park assisted living home announced in 2021 that it would care for people with mental health needs, stroke, quadriplegia and paraplegia, traumatic brain injury and developmental disabilities, among others, with "an African touch" ([CCX Media, Oct. 29, 2021](https://ccxmedia.org/news/construction-begins-on-brooklyn-park-assisted-living-facility-with-an-african-touch/)). Its current operation could not be confirmed. That a community-owned provider set out to serve these needs suggests families were looking for care that matched their culture; how many Nigerian Minnesotans use disability services, and which ones, is not documented.

**Organizations.** No Nigerian-specific disability organization in Minnesota was found. The closest is the Minnesota Community of African People with Disabilities, a pan-African nonprofit founded in 2014 by two physically disabled East African Americans, which offers support services, employment help and life-skills training ([MNCAPD](https://mncapd.org/)).

**Who defines disability, and stigma.** The clearest Minnesota evidence is about mental health, not other disabilities: a Nigerian American provider describes stigma that keeps people from seeking care, and a Nigerian Catholic sister describes prayer houses as the first resort in Nigeria (section 11). These are accounts from individuals, not measurements, and they describe some people, not a community.

**Giving across borders.** The Egba association built a restroom for "the intellectually challenged classroom" at a school for children with special needs in Abeokuta, Nigeria ([Egba association, Projects](https://egbamn.org/projects)). That shows disability is part of the community's charitable concern, at least abroad.

**Questions to carry.** What produced an impairment, where it was first named (Nigeria or Minnesota), whether a family uses a medical, religious or community frame, and whether a label is seen as opening services or closing doors are open questions. No Minnesota study of disability among Nigerian Minnesotans was found, nor any Minnesota data on autism, developmental disability or special education by Nigerian background.

## 13. What is changing now

**A fast-growing, newly arriving community.** Compass's newest numbers show a community larger than earlier estimates and with the highest share of recent immigrants among the groups listed in its 2026 immigration overview ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/nigerian); [Minnesota Compass, Aug. 3, 2026](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/10-things-know-about-immigration-minnesota)).

**Visa limits and travel restrictions.** In July 2025, the State Department limited most Nigerian visitors to three-month, single-entry visas, citing reciprocity; Nigeria's government disputed the claim, and Nigerian Americans told NBC News the change hurt families and businesses ([NBC News, July 16, 2025](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/nbcblk/new-limits-nigerian-travelers-squeeze-families-businesses-us-rcna218756)). A presidential proclamation signed Dec. 16, 2025, and effective Jan. 1, 2026, placed Nigeria under a partial suspension of entry covering immigrant visas and B, F, M and J visas, for people outside the U.S. without a valid visa on that date ([Stony Brook University summary](https://www.stonybrook.edu/visa/resources/news-and-events/immigration-policy-updates/presidential-proclamation-expanding-us-entry-restrictions-12-16-2025.html); [allAfrica, Dec. 17, 2025](https://allafrica.com/stories/202512170598.html)). An unnamed U.S. official told reporters that U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services extended its pause on immigration applications to nationals of the newly added countries, including Nigeria ([allAfrica, December 2025](https://allafrica.com/stories/202512220181.html)). In Minnesota, the change reached a small town: the New York Mills Regional Cultural Center said a Nigerian finalist for its June 2026 Great American Think-Off was "denied entry into the United States" under the proclamation ([Otter Tail Lakes Country Association, June 2026](https://ottertaillakescountry.com/blog/great-american-think-off-announces-finalist-change-for-2026-debate/)). Student visas matter for a community in which many early arrivals were students (section 1) and whose student associations still draw from Nigeria.

**Enforcement.** In December 2025, MPR News reported a Plymouth incident in which Homeland Security Investigations agents tried to question a Nigerian man about an alleged student visa overstay, leading to dueling 911 calls and federal charges against him and his partner, who pleaded not guilty; a magistrate judge ordered them released, and he was then transferred to immigration custody ([MPR News, Dec. 19, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/12/19/911-transcripts-reveal-dueling-kidnapping-claims-in-incident-with-hsi-agents)). A festival organizer told Sahan Journal in 2026 that her pan-African festival's business lounge was created "after Operation Metro Surge," the federal enforcement operation that affected immigrant communities in Minnesota ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 4, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/news/minnesota-african-cultural-igbo-fest-lets-gidi-yama-fest/)). A Nigerian newspaper reported in January 2026 that Nigerians in several U.S. cities were withdrawing from public life out of fear, and that the Minneapolis shootings of Renée Good and Alex Pretti "reverberated far beyond the city"; that report was national, not about Minnesota's community ([BusinessDay, Jan. 31, 2026](https://businessday.ng/news/article/fear-replaces-work-as-nigerian-migrants-retreat-under-trumps-intensified-ice-crackdown/)). No Minnesota source was found that measures how enforcement has affected Nigerian Minnesotans specifically.

**New institutions.** Gidi Bar & Lounge (2024), Let's Gidi Fest (2025), Zoma House (2026) and The Purposed Parent's growth show a younger generation building its own venues ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 4, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/news/minnesota-african-cultural-igbo-fest-lets-gidi-yama-fest/); [Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder, Aug. 26, 2026](https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/08/26/zoma-house-creators-path-minneapolis-uzoma-ngwu/)). MIND's leadership says its "primary focus" is acquiring a Nigerian community and cultural center ([MIND](https://www.mindmn.org/)). The Archdiocese reconstituted its Commission of Black Catholic Ministry in December 2025 with a Nigerian Catholic leader among its officers ([The Catholic Spirit, Sept. 17, 2026](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/black-catholic-ministry-commission-seeks-unity-in-diversity/)).

**Generations and identity.** Second-generation artists describe themselves as bridges. Obi Original says he wants to "bridge that gap between African Americans and Africans because our parents have gone through that" ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/obi-original-afrobeats-nigerian-musician/)). Falade spoke of "trepidation" about raising a Black boy in Minnesota alongside hope for his son's opportunities ([Sahan Journal, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/climate-environment/wale-falade-fihan-north-commons-park-nigerian-architect-minneapolis/)).

**Local disagreement.** The 2025 Brooklyn Park sister-city debate drew both support from residents who spoke at the council and sharp disagreement in the wider city (section 9).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **Education and professional skill.** More than half of adults hold a bachelor's degree, and Nigerian Minnesotans work as nurses, lawyers, engineers, architects, teachers and clergy ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/nigerian); section 3).
- **Care work.** Nurses' associations, a psychiatric practice, a birth-support company, chaplains and Catholic sisters serve all Minnesotans, not only Nigerians (sections 3, 4, 11).
- **Self-organization.** An umbrella group since 1994 and a dense web of Igbo, Yoruba, Edo and other associations run festivals, scholarships, school-supply picnics, vaccination drives and food-shelf volunteering ([MIND](https://www.mindmn.org/naija-groups/); [Egba association](https://egbamn.org/about-us); [Igbo Women League](https://igbowomenleaguemn.org/)).
- **Public culture.** IgboFest has drawn thousands to Brooklyn Park for three decades, free of charge, and governors have marked it as Igbo Day ([North Hennepin Community College](https://www.nhcc.edu/news/igbofest-2026); [Office of the Governor, 2019](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/08.10.19%20Igbo%20Day_tcm1055-398324.pdf)).
- **Civic leadership.** A Nigerian American state representative in the Legislature, Dr. Fatima Lawson in public schools and on state councils, and community voices in Brooklyn Park's sister-city ties ([Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/legdb/fulldetail.aspx?ID=15551); [MMEG](https://www.mmeg.org/women-building-a-bettter-world-1/2023/4/12/fatima-lawson)).
- **Building public places.** A Nigerian-born architect's firm was chosen to redesign a major north Minneapolis park ([Sahan Journal, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/climate-environment/wale-falade-fihan-north-commons-park-nigerian-architect-minneapolis/)).
- **Generosity across borders.** Books, scholarships, school projects and support for children's homes in Nigeria, alongside meals for Minnesotans experiencing homelessness ([CCX Media, 2019](https://ccxmedia.org/news/local-fundraising-effort-collects-6000-books-for-kids-in-nigeria/); [Egba association, Events](https://egbamn.org/events)).

- **Pentecostal and independent churches.** No current Minnesota congregation of a Nigerian-founded Pentecostal church could be confirmed from its own page or a dated report.

- **Businesses.** No Nigerian-owned grocery, law firm, accounting or tax office, travel agency, insurance agency, salon or clinic other than those listed in section 4 was confirmed. Several pan-African groceries exist, but their owners' origins are not stated in the sources found. No Nigerian chamber of commerce or Minnesota-based Nigerian media outlet was found.
- **Dates for 2026 events.** The date and place of the 2026 Yoruba Fest and of MIND's 2026 Independence celebration were not found.
- **Disability and health data.** No Minnesota study of disability, autism or special education among Nigerian Minnesotans was found, and no MDH data specific to Nigerian Minnesotans.
- **Traditional religion, marriage, naming and funeral customs** in Minnesota were not documented in an opened source.
- **Sports.** No opened Minnesota source covered Nigerian Minnesotan athletes or clubs.
- **Pages that could not be read.** Mshale (Minnesota's African newspaper) and Axios Twin Cities blocked access; Star Tribune pages, a Sahan Journal report on the travel ban's effect on Minnesota immigrant businesses, and several Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder pages could not be opened.
