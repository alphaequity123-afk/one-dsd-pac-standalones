# Corrections to the existing "American culture" page

Page checked: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/american
Date checked: October 31, 2026

Most of the existing page is interpretation and advice, not factual claims, and it cites almost nothing. The notes below cover the claims that can be checked. They also flag places where the page states a generalization about "unmarked" Americans without any source.

## Errors or outdated claims

1. **Claim:** The civic year includes "Presidents' Day" and "Indigenous Peoples' Day / Columbus Day (contested)."
   **What the source says:** Minnesota law names the February holiday "Washington's and Lincoln's Birthday." It names the second Monday in October "Indigenous Peoples Day." Public-employer contracts that cite Columbus Day must be amended to cite Indigenous Peoples Day. The state executive branch does not close on Indigenous Peoples Day, and other branches and local governments choose. Columbus Day is no longer a Minnesota legal holiday name.
   **Source:** Minnesota Statutes 645.44, subd. 5, https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/645.44.

2. **Claim:** The civic year list omits the Friday after Thanksgiving.
   **What the source says:** For the state executive branch, the Friday after Thanksgiving is a legal holiday. Other public bodies may choose.
   **Source:** Minnesota Statutes 645.44, subd. 5, same link.

3. **Claim:** "The Minnesota State Demographic Center cited 11.7% of Minnesotans age 5+ in 2018" speaking a language other than English at home.
   **What the source says:** The 2018 figure could not be checked because the State Demographic Center site refused access. The newer Minnesota Compass figure for 2020-2024 is 12.3 percent (plus or minus 0.2) speaking a language other than English at home, with 4.6 percent speaking English less than "very well." The page should use the newer figure with its date.
   **Source:** Minnesota Compass, https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota.

4. **Claim:** "Compass Minnesota Native American ~162,216 (ACS 2019–2023)."
   **What the source says:** Compass's cultural-community table, labeled as 2019-2023 data, shows 161,194. This page does not go further on that community, which is outside its scope.
   **Source:** Minnesota Compass, https://www.mncompass.org/cultural-communities.

5. **Claim:** The Great Migration moved "about six million, 1916–1970, National Archives."
   **What the source says:** The National Archives page is titled "The Great Migration (1910-1970)." It says about six million Black people moved "roughly from the 1910s until the 1970s." The count matches; the start year the page gives (1916) does not match the National Archives' framing.
   **Source:** National Archives, https://www.archives.gov/research/african-americans/migrations/great-migration.

## Claims that need sources or softening

6. **Claim:** Unmarked American culture includes "Lutheran-civic and conflict-avoidance in much of the Upper Midwest."
   **What the source says:** Pew's 2023-24 survey supports the Lutheran part for Minnesota: about 23 percent of adults belong to Lutheran bodies, including 12 percent ELCA. Pew also finds 29 percent religiously unaffiliated. No opened source measured "conflict-avoidance," so that part should be attributed to a named source or presented as a view, not a fact.
   **Source:** Pew Research Center, https://www.pewresearch.org/religious-landscape-study/state/minnesota/.

7. **Claim:** Minnesota has "a progressive self-image next to racial gaps that are among the widest in the country."
   **What the source says:** The gap part is supported for homeownership. The Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis found Black homeownership in the Twin Cities at 29 percent in 2024, the widest Black-white gap among comparable metros (46.9 points). The page should cite this kind of specific, dated measure. "Progressive self-image" is an opinion and should be attributed.
   **Source:** CBS Minnesota, May 10, 2026, https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/black-homeownership-twin-cities-gap-minneapolis-federal-reserve/.

8. **Claim:** "Unions are American too — the Iron Range, plants, the public sector."
   **What the source says:** Supported. Minnesota's union membership rate was 14.2 percent in 2024, against 9.9 percent nationally, and MNopedia documents strong public-employee organizing and the 1916 Mesabi Iron Range strike. Adding these figures would help.
   **Sources:** Minneapolis Regional Labor Federation, https://minneapolisunions.org/news/mlr2025-02-28-union-members-grew-in-MN; MNopedia, https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/labor-and-labor-organizing-minnesota; both.

9. **Claim:** "Insurance tied to employment is structure."
   **What the source says:** Supported for Minnesota: 51.4 percent of Minnesotans had employer coverage in 2025, 39.6 percent public coverage, and 5.8 percent were uninsured, up from 3.8 percent in 2023. The page could add these dated figures.
   **Source:** Minnesota Department of Health and SHADAC, https://www.health.state.mn.us/data/economics/hasurvey/docs/mnha2025.pdf.

10. **Claim:** "Language other than English at home is on the order of one in five nationally."
    **What the source says:** Supported. Pew's analysis of the 2023 ACS found 78 percent of U.S. residents 5 and older speak only English at home, so about 22 percent speak another language.
    **Source:** Pew Research Center, March 17, 2025, https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/03/17/how-americans-feel-about-making-english-the-official-language-of-the-us/.

11. **Broad generalizations without sources.** The page states as facts many claims about what "the unmarked American" believes or does: that time is "a commodity," that people read lateness as "character," that "rest can look like failure," that "talk of salary is often rude," and so on. No Minnesota survey or study supporting these was found. Each should either cite a named source, sample, place and date, or be framed as a question to ask the person, not a description of a group.

## Claims checked with no error found, or not checked

- **ADA (1990), Olmstead as living law:** consistent with the Metropolitan Center for Independent Living's description of Minnesota's Olmstead Plan, named for the 1999 Supreme Court decision (https://mcil-mn.org/mcil-ada-minnesota-submits-comments-on-education-and-employment-to-minnesota-olmstead-plan/).
- **Juneteenth "federal since 2021":** not checked against a federal source. Minnesota made Juneteenth a state holiday when the governor signed the bill on Feb. 3, 2023 (MPR News, https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/02/03/juneteenth-is-now-a-holiday-in-minnesota). The page could add the Minnesota date.
- **2020 Census "about 331 million," Hispanic "~19%," Black "~13–14%," foreign-born "on the order of 14%":** not checked, because census.gov refused access.
- **Crèvecoeur (1782), Bellah's *Habits of the Heart* (1985), Takaki, Tocqueville:** dates of published books, not checked for this page.
