# American culture in Minnesota
Checked: October 31, 2026

This page looks at mainstream American culture as Minnesotans live it: the civic holidays, workplace rules, public schools, churches, food, sports, shopping and family routines that most people in the state share in some form, whatever their background. Separate pages cover Minnesota's own regional identity, European American heritage, rural Minnesota as a place, and African American Minnesotans, so this page does not repeat them. Every general statement below names its source, its place and its date. A pattern in a survey describes a group at one moment. It does not describe the person in front of you.

## 1. People and place

**Who is "American" here.** On this page "American" means the shared national civic culture, not an ancestry: the holidays, schools, laws and habits that people of every background in Minnesota live inside. Minnesota Compass, using the American Community Survey (ACS) for 2020 to 2024, counts 5,739,445 Minnesotans. It places 55.2 percent in the Twin Cities region and 44.8 percent in Greater Minnesota ([Minnesota Compass, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)). Foreign-born residents make up 8.6 percent, give or take 0.2 points, so about 91 percent are counted as native-born (same source).

**Many people stay put.** Stacker's county ranking, built from 2024 five-year Census estimates, shows how rooted some places are. In Morrison County 86.8 percent of residents were born in Minnesota, and every county in its top 50 was above 70 percent ([Stacker, Aug. 7, 2026](https://stacker.com/stories/minnesota/counties-most-born-and-bred-residents-minnesota)). In the Compass profile, 87.8 percent of Minnesotans lived in the same home a year earlier, and 11.8 percent had moved from somewhere else in the U.S. ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)). No statewide born-in-Minnesota figure could be opened for this page.

**People who answer "American" to the ancestry question.** In the 2012 ACS, about 4 percent of Minnesotans wrote "American" as their ancestry, compared with 29 percent who named Nordic roots and 35 percent German. The "American" group grew by 76,500 people, or 55.8 percent, over five years. The Star Tribune report, carried by the Grand Forks Herald, linked the rise to younger people from generations of intermarriage who no longer name one homeland ([Grand Forks Herald, Sept. 21, 2013](https://www.grandforksherald.com/newsmd/fewer-minnesotans-line-up-with-nordic-german-roots)). That is the latest Minnesota figure that could be opened. It is more than a decade old, and newer census tables were not reachable.

**Who shares the mainstream.** Compass's cultural-community table counts 4,354,310 White non-Hispanic Minnesotans, about three in four residents, along with 272,870 African American, 231,993 Mexican, 161,194 Native American, 102,491 Hmong and 85,533 Somali Minnesotans, among others ([Minnesota Compass, cultural communities, 2019-2023 data](https://www.mncompass.org/cultural-communities)). All of them live inside the same civic calendar, school system and workplace law described on this page. Each also has its own page.

**Language.** Among Minnesotans 5 and older, 87.7 percent speak only English at home, 12.3 percent speak another language, and 4.6 percent speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)). As national background, Pew Research Center's analysis of the 2023 ACS found 78 percent of U.S. residents 5 and older speak only English at home ([Pew, March 17, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/03/17/how-americans-feel-about-making-english-the-official-language-of-the-us/)). Minnesota is more English-only than the country as a whole.

**Age.** Children 17 and younger are 22.9 percent of the population and people 65 and older are 17.3 percent ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)).

**Veterans.** The Minnesota Department of Veterans Affairs counted 295,703 veterans in its most recent full-year report, nearly 100,000 of them from the Vietnam era and about 53 percent age 65 or older, KTTC reported on Veterans Day 2025 ([KTTC, Nov. 11, 2025](https://www.kttc.com/2025/11/11/minnesota-home-nearly-300000-veterans-latest-state-data-shows/)).

**Regional variation.** The same national culture looks different across the state. The sections below note where the metro and Greater Minnesota differ: in voting (section 9), health coverage and overdose trends (section 11), church life (section 5) and pastimes such as the firearms deer opener (section 6). The rural page covers Greater Minnesota as a place in depth.

## 2. All walks of life

All figures in this section come from the Compass 2020 to 2024 profile, in 2024 dollars, unless another source is named ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)).

- **Comfortable and wealthy households.** The median household income is $86,893, give or take $560, and 43.6 percent of the state's 2,307,855 households take in $100,000 or more. At the very top, Forbes' 2025 list counted five Minnesota billionaires. Glen Taylor led them at $2.9 billion. He bought a wedding service business for $2 million in 1975 and built it into the printing company Taylor Corp; Jeffrey Michael and Stanley Hubbard of Hubbard Broadcasting were also on the list ([KROC News, April 6, 2025](https://krocnews.com/five-minnesotans-now-on-world-billionaires-list-in-2025/); [KROC News, July 19, 2025](https://krocnews.com/this-billionaire-is-minnesotas-richest-person-in-2025/)).
- **Professionals and owners.** Among adults 25 and older, 39.3 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more, including 13.7 percent with a graduate or professional degree. The state is home to 17 companies on the 2025 Fortune 500, led by UnitedHealth Group at No. 3 ([Twin Cities Business, 2025](https://tcbmag.com/fortune-500-list-contains-some-warnings-for-minnesota/)). Small firms dominate by count: a KROC report on large employers noted that more than 94 percent of Minnesota businesses employ fewer than 50 people ([KROC News, 2025](https://krocnews.com/here-are-the-five-largest-employers-in-minnesota-in-2025/)).
- **Middle-income and working families.** Owners occupy 72.4 percent of homes. Among adults 18 to 64, 80.2 percent are employed: 82.4 percent of men and 78.0 percent of women. Another 12.9 percent of people live between the poverty line and twice that line. Costs press on these families. The Economic Policy Institute put average infant care at more than $22,000 a year in 2025, behind only Washington, D.C., and Massachusetts ([Center for Rural Policy and Development, quoting Minnesota Reformer, March 15, 2025](https://www.ruralmn.org/minnesota-reformer-child-care-costs-more-in-minnesota-than-almost-any-other-state-study-finds/)).
- **People in poverty or close to it.** Statewide, 8.1 percent of people live below the poverty line, including 8.9 percent of children and 16.1 percent of 18- to 24-year-olds, many of them students. Among renters, 47.4 percent spend too much of their income on housing, compared with 19.0 percent of owners. Median rent is $1,280. Minnesotans made 9,026,843 visits to food shelves in 2025, the fourth record year in a row and about 2.5 times the 2019 level, The Food Group reported. It found that 83 percent of monthly users now come more than once a month ([MPR News, March 18, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/03/18/minnesotans-made-9-million-visits-to-food-shelves-in-2025)). Second Harvest Heartland distributed a record 172 million pounds of food in its 2026 fiscal year and named the federal immigration surge, inflation and diesel prices among the causes ([MPR News, Oct. 17, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/17/record-172-million-pounds-of-food-distributed-to-food-shelves-amid-high-inflation)).

**Gaps inside the mainstream.** The same averages hide large differences by race. The Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis found Black homeownership in the Twin Cities fell to 29 percent in 2024, the widest Black-white gap of any comparable metro, at 46.9 points ([CBS Minnesota, May 10, 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/black-homeownership-twin-cities-gap-minneapolis-federal-reserve/)). A Minnesota Housing official traced the gap in part to racial covenants and decades of unequal wealth building ([KTTC, June 17, 2026](https://www.kttc.com/2026/06/17/minnesota-black-homeownership-gap-rooted-racial-covenants/)). Health coverage shows a similar pattern (section 11).

## 3. Work and the economy

**The job market.** Minnesota's unemployment rate was 4.5 percent in September 2026, above the national 4.2 percent. Its labor force participation rate, 66.7 percent, was well above the national 61.8 percent ([DEED, September 2026 Employment Analysis](https://mn.gov/deed/assets/Sep%202026%20Employment%20Analysis_final_tcm1045-283140.pdf)). In the 12 months to July 2026 the state added 44,092 jobs, growth of 1.5 percent against 0.2 percent nationally ([KTTC, Aug. 20, 2026](https://www.kttc.com/2026/08/20/minnesota-annual-job-growth-outpaces-rest-us/)).

**Where people work.** Health care and social assistance is the largest employing industry, with 556,992 jobs in 2025, about 18.9 percent of all jobs. It accounted for about 55.8 percent of the state's job growth from 2015 to 2025, the state's CareerForce site reports ([CareerForce, DEED](https://careerforce.mn.gov/industry/health-care)). Mayo Clinic is the largest single employer, with 56,999 Minnesota employees in KROC's 2025 count ([KROC News, 2025](https://krocnews.com/here-are-the-five-largest-employers-in-minnesota-in-2025/)). The 2025 Fortune 500 list included UnitedHealth Group, Target, U.S. Bancorp, Best Buy, CHS, 3M, General Mills, Ameriprise, C.H. Robinson, Land O'Lakes, Ecolab, Xcel Energy, Hormel, Thrivent, Solventum, Securian and Fastenal. About half saw revenue fall in fiscal 2024 ([Twin Cities Business](https://tcbmag.com/fortune-500-list-contains-some-warnings-for-minnesota/)).

**Labor history and unions.** Unions are part of the American story here. St. Paul tailors struck in 1854, and the city's printers formed the state's first lasting union in 1856. From 1900 to 1910, about 30,000 people marched in St. Paul's Labor Day parade each year and picnicked on Harriet Island. The Teamsters' 1934 coal drivers' and truckers' strikes challenged an employers' alliance that had blocked organizing in Minneapolis, and public employees here became national leaders in winning union recognition, MNopedia records ([MNopedia, Labor and Labor Organizing in Minnesota](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/labor-and-labor-organizing-minnesota)). The Bureau of Labor Statistics put Minnesota's union membership rate at 14.2 percent in 2024, 11th highest in the country, against 9.9 percent nationally ([Minneapolis Regional Labor Federation, Feb. 28, 2025](https://minneapolisunions.org/news/mlr2025-02-28-union-members-grew-in-MN)).

**The rules of work, as Minnesota writes them.** Workplace norms that feel "normal" are often set in law:
- The state minimum wage is $11.41 an hour in 2026 and rises to $11.87 on Jan. 1, 2027 ([Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry](https://www.dli.mn.gov/minwage)).
- Workers earn at least one hour of sick and safe time for every 30 hours worked, up to 48 hours a year ([Minn. Stat. 181.9446](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/181.9446)).
- Noncompete clauses in employment agreements are void ([Minn. Stat. 181.988](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/181.988)).
- Employees may leave work long enough to vote, on Election Day or during in-person early voting, without penalty or loss of pay ([Minn. Stat. 204C.04](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/204C.04)).
- Minnesota Paid Leave began Jan. 1, 2026. In its first six months it drew about 100,000 applications and approved roughly 75,000, paying about $600 million. The average weekly benefit was about $1,083, and medical and bonding leave were most common, a DEED deputy commissioner said ([KTTC, July 7, 2026](https://www.kttc.com/2026/07/07/first-6-months-mn-paid-leave-program-draws-100000-applications/)). Not everyone agrees with it. Republican governor candidate Lisa Demuth has called the mandate "a tax on every business" and wants to roll it back ([MultiState, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.multistate.us/elections/governor-race/minnesota-2026)).

**The office.** In 2025 large employers pulled office staff back. 3M required non-production workers on site at least four days a week from September 2025 ([KSTP, 2025](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/local-news/3m-to-bring-employees-back-to-office-4-days-a-week-starting-in-september/)). Target asked several departments to come in three days a week, General Mills moved to a Tuesday-through-Thursday schedule, and the Minneapolis Downtown Council estimated peak weekday downtown crowds at about 70 percent of the pre-pandemic level ([Twin Cities Business, July 10, 2025](https://tcbmag.com/target-issues-return-to-office-orders/)). In the Compass profile, 76.5 percent of workers get to work by car, truck or van, 2.0 percent by public transit, and 21.5 percent walk, bike, work at home or use other means ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)).

**Unpaid work.** In 2023, 40.3 percent of Minnesotans, about 1.8 million people, volunteered through an organization, third in the country behind Utah and Vermont, according to AmeriCorps data reported by InForum ([InForum, 2024](https://www.inforum.com/news/moorhead/minnesota-ranks-3rd-for-volunteering-as-national-rates-rebound-post-pandemic-study-shows)).

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

Mainstream American commercial life, from the enclosed mall to the big-box store, has deep Minnesota roots. The list of businesses checked for this page is in the accompanying businesses file. Here are the main threads.

**America's first indoor mall.** Southdale Center in Edina, which a Minnesota Historical Society video on MNopedia calls "America's First Indoor Mall," opened on Oct. 8, 1956, with 72 stores and two anchors. About 75,000 people came to the opening. Dayton's department store hired designer Victor Gruen after a study found only 126 "ideal shopping weather" days a year ([MNopedia, Southdale Center](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/structure/southdale-center)). Mall of America in Bloomington opened in 1992 on the old Metropolitan Stadium site with 330 stores. It reports 32 million visitors a year and more than $3 billion in yearly economic impact ([Mall of America, About](https://www.mallofamerica.com/about)). It hosted a free Minnesota America 250 exhibit and ceremony over the July 4, 2026 weekend ([Dakota County](https://dakotacountymn.gov/government/news/america-250-event-to-be-held-at-mall-of-america)).

**National brands with local headquarters.** Target, Best Buy, General Mills and 3M are among the household names based here (section 3). Grocery shopping runs through chains such as Cub and Lunds & Byerlys. Holiday Stationstores now operates under Circle K, whose parent company is named on Holiday's site ([Holiday Stationstores](https://www.holidaystationstores.com/)). Hormel's SPAM Museum in Austin is open daily ([SPAM Museum](https://www.spam.com/museum)).

**American food, Minnesota style.** Minneapolis's Matt's Bar has served the cheese-stuffed "Jucy Lucy" burger since the 1950s and spells it without the "i" on purpose ([Matt's Bar](https://mattsbar.com/)). The 5-8 Club serves its own "Juicy Lucy" at four locations ([5-8 Club](https://www.5-8club.com/)). Steakhouses such as Murray's in downtown Minneapolis, Manny's, Jax Cafe in Northeast Minneapolis (dating to 1933) and Mancini's Char House in St. Paul keep an older dining style alive ([Murray's](https://www.murraysrestaurant.com/); [Jax Cafe](https://www.jaxcafe.com/); [Mancini's](https://mancinis.com/)). Minnesota raises more turkeys than any other state, 32.0 million in 2025, which puts it at the center of the Thanksgiving table ([USDA NASS, Sept. 26, 2025](https://www.nass.usda.gov/Publications/Todays_Reports/reports/tuky0925.pdf)).

**The State Fair as a marketplace.** The Minnesota State Fair is part agricultural show, part food court, part shopping mall. It drew 1,923,325 people over 12 days from Aug. 27 to Sept. 7, 2026. Its competitions drew more than 38,000 entries and nearly $2.5 million in prize money ([Minnesota State Fair](https://www.mnstatefair.org/updates/2026-fair)).

**Business groups and media.** The Minnesota Chamber of Commerce, the St. Paul Area Chamber and the Minneapolis Regional Chamber all keep current 2026 websites ([Minnesota Chamber](https://www.mnchamber.com/); [St. Paul Area Chamber](https://www.saintpaulchamber.com/); [Minneapolis Regional Chamber](https://www.minneapolischamber.org/)). Mainstream news reaches Minnesotans through MPR News, the Star Tribune, the Pioneer Press, KARE 11, KSTP, FOX 9 and CBS Minnesota (WCCO), along with Twin Cities Business, Minnesota Monthly and Mpls.St.Paul Magazine.

**How deep this list goes.** The businesses file is a sample of mainstream institutions and landmarks, not a directory. Countless ordinary firms make up this part of the economy, and listing them would not describe a culture.

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

**What the numbers show.** Pew Research Center's 2023-24 Religious Landscape Study describes Minnesota adults this way: 22 percent mainline Protestant, 20 percent evangelical Protestant, 18 percent Catholic, 1 percent in historically Black Protestant churches, 3 percent Muslim, and smaller shares of other faiths. Lutherans of all kinds make up about 23 percent: 16 percent in mainline Lutheran bodies, including 12 percent in the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America (ELCA), and 7 percent in evangelical Lutheran bodies, including 5 percent in the Lutheran Church-Missouri Synod. The religiously unaffiliated make up 29 percent: 5 percent atheist, 9 percent agnostic and 15 percent "nothing in particular." Pew gives a margin of error of 5.7 points for Minnesota ([Pew Research Center, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/religious-landscape-study/state/minnesota/)). In short, Minnesota's mainstream is still mostly Christian, unusually Lutheran, and home to a large group of people with no religion.

**Older churches are shrinking.** A 2018 report covered by Patch found the ELCA had nearly 200,000 fewer Minnesota members than in 2000 and had lost about 150 churches, with more than 1,000 of its remaining churches under 50 members. Catholic numbers held steady, but about 80 Catholic churches had closed since 2000. The decline reached rural, suburban and urban churches alike ([Patch, July 9, 2018](https://patch.com/minnesota/southwestminneapolis/minnesota-churches-are-steep-decline-report)). Newer figures for the state could not be opened.

**Where growth shows.** Large nondenominational churches are part of the mainstream too. Eagle Brook Church lists 14 campuses across Minnesota plus online services and posted new messages through October 2026 ([Eagle Brook Church](https://www.eaglebrookchurch.com/)). The Archdiocese of Saint Paul and Minneapolis, Temple Israel in Minneapolis and many other congregations post current schedules ([Archdiocese](https://www.archspm.org/); [Temple Israel](https://www.templeisrael.com/)).

**Sunday, sports and family time.** One tension inside mainstream family life is between worship and weekend activities. In August 2025 the Archdiocese of Saint Paul and Minneapolis launched "Reclaiming Sundays," a year-long guide and video series that suggests themes such as screen-free Sundays to help families keep the day for prayer and rest, Archbishop Bernard Hebda said ([National Catholic Register/CNA, Aug. 27, 2025](https://www.ncregister.com/cna/catholic-families-reclaim-the-sabbath)).

**Civil religion.** Some public rituals of national loyalty are written into law. Minnesota law requires every public school to display the U.S. flag, and public and charter school students recite the Pledge of Allegiance at least once a week. Any student or teacher may decline, and handbooks must say so. A school board can vote to waive the weekly recitation ([Minn. Stat. 121A.11](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/121A.11)). On July 8, 2026, the Governor's Committee for Minnesota America 250 and the Minnesota Historical Society held a public reading of the Declaration of Independence in the State Capitol rotunda ([Minnesota Historical Society, July 1, 2026](https://www.mnhs.org/media/news/commemorate-americas-250th-anniversary-fourth-july)).

> **Content note:** This passage describes the 2025 shooting at Annunciation Catholic Church, in which two children were killed. Readers may skip to the next paragraph.

**Faith and tragedy.** On Aug. 27, 2025, a gunman fired into Annunciation Catholic Church in Minneapolis during a school Mass, killing two children and injuring 28 other people, MPR News reported. Afterward, parents organized, the church's new pastor helped children with questions about life, death and God, and gun bills were introduced at the Capitol ([MPR News, Annunciation coverage](https://www.mprnews.org/annunciation-catholic-church-and-school-shooting/3)).

**People of no faith.** The 29 percent who are unaffiliated still take part in the civic calendar, holidays and rites of passage described here. Pew's Minnesota page does not break down how they mark weddings or funerals, and no Minnesota source on that question was found.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

Minnesota law names the legal holidays on which public business stops: New Year's Day (Jan. 1); Martin Luther King's Birthday (third Monday in January); Washington's and Lincoln's Birthday (third Monday in February); Memorial Day (last Monday in May); Juneteenth (June 19); Independence Day (July 4); Labor Day (first Monday in September); Indigenous Peoples Day (second Monday in October); Veterans Day (Nov. 11); Thanksgiving (fourth Thursday in November); and Christmas (Dec. 25). When a fixed-date holiday falls on a Sunday, Monday is the holiday; on a Saturday, Friday is. The state's executive branch also closes the Friday after Thanksgiving but does not close on Indigenous Peoples Day. Other branches, counties and cities choose for themselves ([Minn. Stat. 645.44, subd. 5](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/645.44)). The calendar file lists dates for 2026 to 2028.

- **New Year's Day.** A legal holiday by statute. No Minnesota-specific public tradition was documented for this page.
- **Martin Luther King Jr. Day.** General Mills and UNCF held the 36th annual Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Holiday Breakfast at the Minneapolis Convention Center on Jan. 19, 2026, with Soledad O'Brien and Freeman Hrabowski as keynote speakers ([General Mills, Dec. 16, 2025](https://www.generalmills.com/news-and-stories/press-releases/general-mills-and-uncf-celebrate-36th-annual-dr-martin-luther-king-jr-holiday-breakfast)). Colleges such as St. Olaf hold a month of events ([St. Olaf College](https://wp.stolaf.edu/mlkday/)). The African American page covers the day's deeper meaning.
- **Washington's and Lincoln's Birthday.** Often called Presidents' Day. Minnesota law keeps the older name ([Minn. Stat. 645.44](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/645.44)).
- **St. Patrick's Day.** St. Paul held its 60th annual parade on March 17, 2026, from Mears Park along Fifth Street to Rice Park, led by the year's Miss Shamrock. MPR described the crowd as "green-clad Irish and Irish wannabes" ([MPR News, March 17, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/03/17/thousands-march-through-st-paul-in-60th-st-patricks-day-parade)).
- **Easter.** A major Christian feast. It is not a state legal holiday. No Minnesota-wide public observance was documented for this page beyond congregations' own services.
- **Memorial Day.** Fort Snelling National Cemetery hosts a wreath-laying with music, speeches, a rifle volley and Taps, and the governor usually speaks. In 2025 it began at 9:45 a.m. ([KTTC, May 26, 2025](https://www.kttc.com/2025/05/26/fort-snelling-hold-wreath-laying-ceremony-memorial-day/)).
- **Juneteenth.** Gov. Tim Walz signed the bill making Juneteenth a state holiday on Feb. 3, 2023 ([MPR News, Feb. 3, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/02/03/juneteenth-is-now-a-holiday-in-minnesota)). The African American page covers community celebrations.
- **Independence Day.** In 2026 the holiday marked the nation's 250th anniversary. Because July 4 fell on a Saturday, many people had Friday off. Events ranged from the Minneapolis Park Board's Red, White and Boom! on the riverfront to small-town parades in Delano, Ely and Stillwater ([MPR News, June 26, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/06/26/fourth-of-july-events-guide-fireworks-parades-in-minnesota); [FOX 9, June 30, 2026](https://www.fox9.com/news/things-do-minnesota-5-things-do-fourth-july-weekend-2026)). Historic Fort Snelling and the Oliver Kelley Farm held historical celebrations ([Minnesota Historical Society](https://www.mnhs.org/media/news/commemorate-americas-250th-anniversary-fourth-july)).
- **Labor Day.** For many Minnesotans the holiday is the last day of the State Fair, which runs for the 12 days ending on Labor Day ([Minnesota State Fair](https://www.mnstatefair.org/updates/2026-fair)). The first fair was held in 1859, and the fair moved to its permanent grounds between St. Paul and Minneapolis in 1885 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/minnesota-state-fair-origins-and-traditions)). Public schools generally may not start until after Labor Day ([Minn. Stat. 120A.40](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/120A.40)).
- **Indigenous Peoples Day.** Minnesota law has named the second Monday in October Indigenous Peoples Day, replacing Columbus Day in public-employer contracts ([Minn. Stat. 645.44](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/645.44)). This page does not go further. That history belongs with the Tribal Nations and the offices that serve them.
- **Halloween.** Anoka calls itself the "Halloween Capital of the World." Its organizers say civic leaders started a citywide celebration in 1920 to keep young people from pranks ([Anoka Halloween, history](https://anokahalloween.com/history/)). The 2026 Grande Day Parade ran on Main Street on Saturday, Oct. 24, from 1 to 3:30 p.m. ([Anoka Halloween](https://anokahalloween.com/event/grande-day-parade/)).
- **Election Day.** Minnesota's general election is Nov. 3, 2026. By Oct. 16, election officials had accepted 202,252 absentee ballots, 17 percent more than at the same point in 2022 ([KAXE, Oct. 19, 2026](https://www.kaxe.org/elections/2026-10-19/early-voting-opens-in-mn-midterms-as-absentee-ballots-roll-in)). Workers have a legal right to time off to vote (section 3).
- **Firearms deer opener.** Not a holiday, but in much of the state a family tradition. More than 400,000 hunters were expected for the opener on Saturday, Nov. 7, 2026. The Department of Natural Resources describes it as a time for "rich memories" with friends and family ([Minnesota DNR, Oct. 26, 2026](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/news/2026/10/26/minnesota-dnr-provides-deer-hunting-outlook-and-regulation-reminders)).
- **Veterans Day.** The state's official program has been held in Inver Grove Heights, with a breakfast for veterans and families and music by the 34th Infantry Division Red Bull Band ([CBS Minnesota](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/hundreds-gather-at-state-veterans-day-program/)).
- **Thanksgiving.** Each year the governor welcomes an official Thanksgiving turkey to the Capitol with the Minnesota Turkey Growers Association, honoring the state's more than 600 turkey farms ([WDIO](https://www.wdio.com/top-stories/minnesota-turkey-showcased-at-the-state-capitol/)).
- **Christmas.** A Christian feast and a legal holiday. In Duluth, Bentleyville "Tour of Lights" calls itself America's largest free walk-through lighting display and runs Nov. 21 to Dec. 26, 2026, its 23rd season ([Bentleyville](https://www.bentleyvilleusa.org/)).

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Households.** Compass counts 2,307,855 households averaging 2.41 people. Married couples without children under 18 make up 30.0 percent, married couples with children 19.1 percent, single mothers with children 3.7 percent, single fathers 1.2 percent, and unmarried couples 8.0 percent. People living alone are 29.9 percent of households ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)). The "typical" two-parent family with children is about one household in five.

**Fewer children.** "We haven't had replacement level fertility in Minnesota since 2006," state demographer Megan Dayton told Twin Cities Business. The state's birth rate fell from 14.16 per 1,000 people in 2007 to 11.71 in 2019, and demographers projected deaths would outnumber births around 2040 ([Twin Cities Business, 2021](https://tcbmag.com/what-a-declining-u-s-birth-rate-could-mean-for-minnesota/)). Newer state birth figures could not be opened. Child care costs (section 2) are one pressure that families name.

**Cars and commutes.** Most households have a car: 39.4 percent have two and 23.1 percent have three or more, while 6.6 percent have none. Almost half of workers who leave home get to work in under 20 minutes, and 28.7 percent travel 30 minutes or more ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)).

**Food.** Minnesota supplies 16 percent of all U.S. turkeys, with turkey making up 61 percent of the state's poultry production, according to the governor's office ([WDIO](https://www.wdio.com/top-stories/minnesota-turkey-showcased-at-the-state-capitol/)). Schools in the national lunch program must take part in the state's free school meals program, and schools may not shame or single out a student who owes money for meals ([Minn. Stat. 124D.111](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/124D.111)).

**Childhood.** Organized activities reach a large share of teenagers. In 2024-25, 232,347 students took part in Minnesota State High School League athletics, 10th most in the country. Adding fine arts activities and robotics brought participation to an all-time high of 277,773 ([MSHSL, Aug. 19, 2025](https://www.mshsl.org/about/news/league-news/mshsl-maintains-spot-top-10-nfhs-annual-participation-survey)). At the State Fair, 115 4-H members sold livestock in the 2026 Purple Ribbon Auction, which raised more than $1.35 million ([Minnesota State Fair](https://www.mnstatefair.org/updates/2026-fair)).

**Aging.** People 65 and older are 17.3 percent of Minnesotans. Poverty in that age group is 7.9 percent, slightly below the statewide rate ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)).

**Weddings, dress, divorce.** No Minnesota-specific source on wedding customs, dress norms or divorce was opened for this page. National patterns should not be assumed to describe any one family.

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Minnesota's place in American literature and music.** Sauk Centre's Sinclair Lewis wrote *Main Street* (1920), set in a fictional version of his hometown. It sold 180,000 copies in just over six months, and Lewis became the first American to win the Nobel Prize in Literature ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/lewis-harry-sinclair-1885-1951)). Prince, born in Minneapolis in 1958 and raised on the Northside, is credited by MNopedia as the main creator of the Minneapolis Sound ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/prince-1958-2016)).

**Big-league sports.** The Twins brought major-league baseball to Minnesota in 1961, and the Vikings played their first season the same year at Metropolitan Stadium. The Timberwolves began in 1989, the Lynx in 1999 (winning four WNBA titles in seven years in the 2010s), and the Wild in 2000 ([MNopedia, Minnesota sports](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/gophers-vikings-and-saints-highlights-history-minnesota-sports)). The Minnesota Frost won the first two championships of the Professional Women's Hockey League, in 2024 and 2025 ([MPR News, May 26, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/05/26/minnesota-frost-beat-ottawa-charge-2-1-game-4)).

**School sports as community life.** MNopedia calls Minnesota "a natural leader" in girls' and women's hockey: the state high school league sanctioned girls' hockey as a varsity sport after the 1993-94 season, and the first official season began in November 1994 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/gophers-vikings-and-saints-highlights-history-minnesota-sports)). The boys' state hockey tournament, called "The Tourney," was held in March 2026 at the renamed Grand Casino Arena, formerly the Xcel Energy Center ([Post Bulletin, March 4, 2026](https://www.postbulletin.com/sports/prep/all-you-need-to-know-about-the-tourney-the-2026-minnesota-boys-hockey-state-tournament)). Its 2025 Class AA final drew 20,491 fans, more than the average NHL game that season ([ESPN Sioux Falls, March 11, 2025](https://espnsiouxfalls.com/ixp/65/p/prep-hockey-championship-draws-more-fans-than-a-nhl-game/)).

**Fairs, history and arts.** The State Fair's Fine Arts Exhibition, the largest juried art show in Minnesota, drew 3,185 submissions in 2026 and accepted 343 ([Minnesota State Fair](https://www.mnstatefair.org/updates/2026-fair)). For the nation's 250th anniversary, the Minnesota Historical Society offered "MN250" road trips through what it calls "all-American stories" at its historic sites ([Minnesota Historical Society, MN250](https://www.mnhs.org/mn250)).

**Media.** The state's main news outlets are listed in section 4. Several, such as the Pioneer Press and Minnesota Public Radio, are long-standing Minnesota institutions.

## 9. Civic and political life

**High turnout.** In November 2024, 3,272,414 Minnesotans voted, 76.35 percent of eligible voters ([KEYC, Nov. 22, 2024](https://www.keyc.com/2024/11/22/minnesota-leads-us-voter-turnout-2024-election/)). Final national comparisons put Minnesota second to Wisconsin, at 76.64 percent. A Tufts study found 62 percent of Minnesotans age 18 to 29 voted, the highest youth turnout in the country ([Brainerd Dispatch, April 18, 2025](https://www.brainerddispatch.com/news/minnesota/minnesota-ranks-number-one-for-youth-voter-turnout-in-2024-general-election)).

**A divided electorate.** Kamala Harris carried the state in 2024 with 50.91 percent, extending Minnesota's streak of Democratic presidential wins to 13 ([University of Minnesota, Minnesota Historical Election Archive](https://mn.electionarchives.lib.umn.edu/office/president-vice-president/)). Yet Donald Trump did better than in 2020 in 84 of the state's 87 counties ([Duluth News Tribune, Nov. 2024](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/harris-won-minnesota-trump-surpassed-2020-in-84-of-87-counties)). The metro-versus-Greater-Minnesota divide is one of the clearest forms of regional variation in mainstream civic life.

> **Content note:** This passage describes the 2025 shooting deaths of Melissa and Mark Hortman and the killing of two residents by federal immigration agents. Readers may skip to "An open governor's race."

**Violence against the civic order.** On June 14, 2025, former House Speaker Melissa Hortman and her husband Mark were shot and killed in their Brooklyn Park home. State Sen. John Hoffman and his wife were also shot. Vance Boelter pleaded guilty, and on July 23, 2026, U.S. District Judge John Tunheim sentenced him to two consecutive life terms plus 40 years ([KAXE/Minnesota Reformer, July 23, 2026](https://www.kaxe.org/minnesota-news/2026-07-23/vance-boelter-sentenced-to-two-life-terms-plus-40-years-in-killing-of-hortmans)). The report described an attack that "injected a new level of fear into politics."

**Federal immigration enforcement.** Operation Metro Surge, a federal immigration operation focused on the Twin Cities, began Dec. 1, 2025. Federal officials reported more than 4,000 arrests. Two residents, Renee Good and Alex Pretti, were killed by immigration agents. White House border adviser Tom Homan announced the drawdown on Feb. 12, 2026 ([ABC News 4/AP, Feb. 12, 2026](https://abcnews4.com/news/nation-world/immigration-operation-in-minnesota-coming-to-an-end-tom-homan-immigration-and-customs-enforcement-dhs-operation-metro-surge)). The Associated Press reported that people with no criminal record, including children and U.S. citizens, were among those detained, while the administration described those arrested as dangerous criminals ([News9/AP, Feb. 12, 2026](https://www.news9.com/national-politics-oklahoma/federal-authorities-announce-end-immigration-crackdown-minnesota)). Minneapolis Mayor Jacob Frey said standing with neighbors "is deeply American." Federal officials accused some protesters of obstruction, and Homan said about 200 people were arrested on those allegations ([ABC News 4/AP](https://abcnews4.com/news/nation-world/immigration-operation-in-minnesota-coming-to-an-end-tom-homan-immigration-and-customs-enforcement-dhs-operation-metro-surge)).

**An open governor's race.** Gov. Tim Walz dropped his bid for a third term on Jan. 5, 2026, saying he would focus on fighting fraud in state programs. He faced criticism, including from President Trump, over his handling of it ([FOX 9](https://www.fox9.com/news/minnesota-governors-race-potential-democratic-contenders-after-walz-exit)). The Nov. 3, 2026 race pits U.S. Sen. Amy Klobuchar (DFL) against House Speaker Lisa Demuth (R). Either would be the state's first woman governor ([MultiState](https://www.multistate.us/elections/governor-race/minnesota-2026)).

**Civic participation outside elections.** Minnesota's 40.3 percent formal volunteering rate (section 3) and its long tradition of union, church and civic clubs, such as the Kiwanis and Commercial Club that started Anoka's Halloween in 1920, are part of the same habit of joining in ([Anoka Halloween, history](https://anokahalloween.com/history/)).

**National debates that reach Minnesota.** In an August 2024 Pew survey, 51 percent of U.S. adults said it was extremely or very important to make English the official language. That included 73 percent of Republicans and 32 percent of Democrats ([Pew Research Center, March 17, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/03/17/how-americans-feel-about-making-english-the-official-language-of-the-us/)). That is national background. No comparable Minnesota poll was found.

## 10. Education

**The public school as common ground.** Nearly 90 percent of Minnesota's K-12 students attend public schools, 7 percent attend private schools, and fewer than 4 percent are registered homeschoolers ([Brainerd Dispatch/Forum News Service, March 18, 2025](https://www.brainerddispatch.com/news/minnesota/minnesota-homeschooling-leaped-during-covid-and-counts-continue-to-climb)). State law shapes rituals many people think of as simply "American school": the year starts after Labor Day except in listed cases, and students recite the Pledge of Allegiance weekly unless the board waives it, with an opt-out for anyone ([Minn. Stat. 120A.40](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/120A.40); [Minn. Stat. 121A.11](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/121A.11)). The free school meals program (section 7) adds a newer shared routine.

**Graduation.** Eighty-five percent of the class of 2025 graduated within four years, the highest rate the state has recorded. The rate had dipped to 83 percent in 2021 ([MPR News, April 3, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/04/03/minnesota-graduation-rates-hit-record-high-in-2025)). Education Commissioner Willie Jett warned that the federal immigration surge had cut attendance at some schools with many immigrant children and would affect students "for years" (same source).

**Choosing another path.** The number of homeschooled students rose 18 percent from 2023 to 2025 while public and private enrollment grew less than 1 percent. The Minnesota Association of Christian Home Educators has existed since 1983, and the movement has deep roots in conservative Christianity. But one longtime homeschool supply store owner said the families who homeschool have become more varied, and a New Prague parent described choosing it for non-religious reasons (same Forum report).

**Adults.** Among Minnesotans 25 and older, 94.0 percent finished high school or a GED, 31.3 percent have some college or an associate degree, and 39.3 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)).

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Insurance through work, government, or not at all.** Coverage in Minnesota runs mainly through employers and public programs. The Minnesota Health Access Survey found 51.4 percent of Minnesotans covered through an employer in 2025, up from 49.3 percent in 2023. Public coverage (Medical Assistance, MinnesotaCare, Medicare, Tricare) fell from 44.1 to 39.6 percent, and individually bought coverage held at 3.2 percent. The uninsured rate rose from a record low of 3.8 percent in 2023 to 5.8 percent in 2025, about 116,000 more people. It reached 12.5 percent among adults 26 to 34 ([MDH and SHADAC, March 2026](https://www.health.state.mn.us/data/economics/hasurvey/docs/mnha2025.pdf)).

**Who goes without.** Uninsured rates in 2025 were 3.8 percent for White Minnesotans, 10.1 percent for Black Minnesotans, 20.7 percent for Hispanic Minnesotans and 13 percent for people with incomes below twice the poverty line. Rural areas rose from 4.3 to 6.7 percent and metro areas from 3.4 to 5.7 percent ([Minnesota Department of Health, March 10, 2026](https://www.health.mn.gov/news/pressrel/2026/uninsured031026.html)).

**Health care as an industry.** Health care is the state's largest employer group (section 3), and Mayo Clinic in Rochester is the largest single employer.

**Overdoses.** Overdose deaths fell 26 percent from 2023 to 2024, with the steepest drop, 31 percent, in Greater Minnesota counties, and opioid-involved deaths fell 32 percent, the Minnesota Department of Health reported ([Minnesota Medical Association, Oct. 30, 2025](https://www.mnmed.org/news-and-publications/news/overdose-deaths-minnesota-continue-decrease)).

**Food and stress.** Food shelf demand (section 2) is a health matter too. No Minnesota source opened for this page measured how mainstream habits such as diet or exercise differ across the state.

## 12. Disability

**How many.** Compass counts 11.7 percent of Minnesotans with a disability, and 8.3 percent of those under 65 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)).

**Work.** The Minnesota Council on Disability reports a 2023 employment rate of 22.5 percent for people with disabilities against 65.8 percent for people without, and an unemployment rate of 7.2 percent against 3.5 percent ([Minnesota Council on Disability](https://www.disability.state.mn.us/technical-assistance/employment/)).

**Subminimum wages: a live disagreement.** Section 14(c) of the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, passed in 1938, allows people with disabilities to be paid less than the minimum wage, The Arc Minnesota notes. The Metropolitan Center for Independent Living estimates about 3,500 Minnesotans earn around $4 an hour, often for about 10 hours a week, in segregated settings ([MCIL](https://mcil-mn.org/minnesota-disability-employment-statistics/)). The Arc Minnesota backed a 2025 bill, SF 2149, to end the practice by 2028 and called it "archaic and discriminatory" ([The Arc Minnesota testimony, March 10, 2025](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2025/labor/20250311/labor_20250311_sf2149-testimony-The-Arc-Minnesota.pdf)). Letters to a 2024 Senate committee came from parents and providers on the side of ending it. One provider wrote that opponents say the change would ruin people's lives, and a parent who supported the bill wrote that for people in day programs, work should be a choice, not a requirement ([Senate committee letters, March 2024](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2024/human/20240318/Human_20240318_SF4399-Combined-Letters.pdf)). As checked for this page, the state minimum wage statute contains no phase-out date ([Minn. Stat. 177.24](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/177.24)).

**Community living.** Minnesota's Olmstead Plan, named for the 1999 U.S. Supreme Court decision and required by the Jensen settlement, sets goals for disabled Minnesotans to live integrated lives in communities of their choosing. A revised plan was being drafted in 2026, and MCIL and ADA Minnesota submitted comments on education and employment ([MCIL](https://mcil-mn.org/mcil-ada-minnesota-submits-comments-on-education-and-employment-to-minnesota-olmstead-plan/)).

**When a program ends.** The Department of Human Services ended Housing Stabilization Services, a Medicaid program launched in 2020 to help older adults, people with disabilities and people with mental illness or addiction find and keep housing, after federal prosecutors charged eight people with stealing about $10 million from it ([Yahoo News/Pioneer Press, Oct. 31, 2025](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/minnesota-ends-medicaid-funded-housing-214200017.html)). DHS's commissioner and inspector general said fraud left no choice. A Republican legislator called the shutdown "too little, too late" and said it penalized "the people who needed it most" ([KAAL](https://www.kaaltv.com/news/minnesota-dhs-moves-to-terminate-housing-stabilization-services-program-amid-fraud-concerns/)). How many disabled Minnesotans lost help, and what replaced it, could not be confirmed.

**Everyday access.** Mainstream consumer life is adding accommodations. Mall of America's site, for example, has accessibility and sensory-friendly resource pages ([Mall of America](https://www.mallofamerica.com/about)).

**Questions this page leaves open.** Do survey counts match how people see themselves? Who in Minnesota decides that a person counts as disabled: a doctor, a county assessor, the census question, or the person? What does the label open, such as waivers or workplace accommodation, and what does it close, such as a full wage under 14(c)? How much disability among veterans, older adults and workers in physical jobs comes from service, aging or injury? No Minnesota source opened for this page answered these by group, and they should stay questions.

## 13. What is changing now

- **Federal immigration enforcement** reshaped daily life in the Twin Cities from December 2025 to February 2026, with more than 4,000 arrests reported, two deaths, school attendance drops and food shelf strain (sections 2, 9 and 10).
- **Political violence and grief.** The Hortman assassination (June 2025) and the Annunciation shooting (August 2025) put safety and guns at the center of civic life. Gun bills introduced after Annunciation stalled on tie votes ([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/annunciation-catholic-church-and-school-shooting/3)).
- **Fraud and trust in government.** Fraud cases in Medicaid-funded programs led to the end of Housing Stabilization Services, and the acting U.S. attorney estimated in July 2025 that fraud in the state could top $1 billion ([Yahoo News/Pioneer Press](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/minnesota-ends-medicaid-funded-housing-214200017.html)). Fraud became a central issue in the 2026 governor's race ([MultiState](https://www.multistate.us/elections/governor-race/minnesota-2026)).
- **New workplace rights.** Paid Leave began in 2026 (section 3) and remains contested.
- **Back to the office.** Large employers moved staff back three or four days a week in 2025 (section 3).
- **Cost of living.** Record food shelf use, rising uninsurance and high child care costs all point to strain on working and middle-income families (sections 2 and 11).
- **Faith.** The unaffiliated are now 29 percent of adults, and older denominations reported steep declines from 2000 to 2018 (section 5).
- **Fewer births, more elders.** Fertility has been below replacement since 2006 (section 7).
- **America at 250.** The 2026 semiquincentennial brought Capitol readings, MN250 road trips and hometown Fourth of July events (sections 5, 6 and 8).
- **Election.** Minnesota votes Nov. 3, 2026, in an open race for governor, with early voting running ahead of 2022 (sections 6 and 9).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **Showing up.** High voter turnout, the nation's highest youth turnout in 2024, and a volunteering rate third in the country (sections 3 and 9).
- **Inventing shared spaces.** America's first indoor mall, the country's best-known mega-mall, and a 12-day State Fair that draws nearly 2 million people (section 4).
- **Feeding the country.** The nation's top turkey producer, and home to General Mills, Hormel, Land O'Lakes and CHS (sections 3 and 4).
- **Work and care.** A labor force participation rate well above the national rate, job growth that outpaced the country in 2025-26, a large health care workforce, and union density above the national average (section 3).
- **Schools and youth.** A record graduation rate, high activity participation, and leadership in girls' hockey (sections 7, 8 and 10).
- **Culture.** From *Main Street* to Prince, Minnesotans have shaped how the country sees itself (section 8).
- **Public health gains.** Large drops in overdose deaths in 2024 (section 11).
