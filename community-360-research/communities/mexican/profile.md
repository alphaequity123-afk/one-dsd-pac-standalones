# Mexican in Minnesota
Checked: October 30, 2026

## 1. People and place

**How many.** Minnesota Compass, using the American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024, counts 231,993 Mexican Minnesotans, give or take 5,615. It places 60.8 percent in the Twin Cities and 39.2 percent in Greater Minnesota, each give or take 1.3 points ([Minnesota Compass, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/mexican)). The count rests on what people report in a survey, so it carries error, and it does not reach people the survey misses. The state Council on Latino Affairs reported about 200,000 Minnesotans tracing ancestry to Mexico in 2021 estimates, describing them as three in five in one place and two-thirds in another in the same report ([Minnesota Council on Latino Affairs, March 2023](https://mn.gov/mcla/assets/03_31_23%20-%20Demographic%20Report%20-%20MCLA_tcm1099-602196.pdf)). A recent state budget summary puts all Latino Minnesotans at about 381,000, or 6.6 percent of the state ([Minnesota Management and Budget, 2026](https://mn.gov/mmb-stat/documents/budget/research-and-data/2026/summary-of-agencies-programs-activities/latino-affairs-council.pdf)).

**Who the numbers may leave out.** Mexican officials have long said census counts run low. In 2005 the first consul put the census figure at about 100,000 and his own estimate at about 140,000 ([Post Bulletin, June 21, 2005](https://www.postbulletin.com/news/mexican-consulate-opens-in-st-paul)). In 2015 a deputy consul set a census figure of about 200,000 against his own estimate of about 300,000 ([Star Tribune, via LatinoLEAD, posted March 3, 2021](https://www.latinoleadmn.org/post/mexican-consul-in-st-paul-strives-to-build-links-partnerships)). These are officials' estimates, and no current one was found.

**Young, and mostly born here.** In the Compass profile, 36.8 percent are 17 or younger and 4.2 percent are 65 or older. Foreign-born residents are 27.5 percent, give or take 1.3 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/mexican)). So nearly three in four Mexican Minnesotans were born in the United States, which matters for any picture of this community as newly arrived.

**Where in the Twin Cities.** St. Paul's West Side is the oldest center. MNopedia calls the Lower West Side, or "the Flats," the heart of the state's early Mexican community ([MNopedia, modified Jan. 14, 2026](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/minnesotanos-latino-journeys-minnesota)). In Minneapolis, sources point to Sagrado Corazón on Pleasant Avenue and the shops of East Lake Street (sections 4 and 5). A Women's Foundation of Minnesota writer notes that many Minneapolis taqueria workers trace their roots to Morelos ([Women's Foundation of Minnesota, April 10, 2023](https://www.wfmn.org/axochiapan-connecting-communities-and-women-led-movements-from-mexico-to-minnesota)). Consulate staff in 2015 named Morelos and Puebla as common home states ([Star Tribune, via LatinoLEAD](https://www.latinoleadmn.org/post/mexican-consul-in-st-paul-strives-to-build-links-partnerships)).

**Where in Greater Minnesota.** The Council on Latino Affairs reports that the Latino population grew by more than 5,000 between 1980 and 2020 in each of Nobles, Rice and Kandiyohi counties, all of which had fewer than 1,000 Latino residents in 1980. Those are all-Latino figures, not Mexican only ([MCLA, March 2023](https://mn.gov/mcla/assets/03_31_23%20-%20Demographic%20Report%20-%20MCLA_tcm1099-602196.pdf)). Town by town, the roots differ:

- **Willmar.** A University of Minnesota scholar reported in 1994 that about 90 percent of Willmar's Chicano and Latino residents were Tejanos, mostly from Texas's Rio Grande Valley, who had settled out of the migrant stream. She called it the state's third-largest Chicano and Latino community at that time ([Green, Julian Samora Research Institute, May 1994](https://jsri.msu.edu/upload/working-papers/wp19.pdf)). No current breakdown was found.
- **Austin.** In 2010 the census counted about 2,500 Latino residents, while local officials and advocates estimated up to 5,000. MPR News reported that many came from Oaxaca, with a sizable Guatemalan community too ([MPR News, Aug. 9, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/08/09/austin-at-a-crossroads-part2)).
- **Northfield.** Migration from Maltrata, Veracruz, began around 1991. St. Dominic's parish reported more than 1,500 Hispanic residents, about two-thirds from Maltrata and nearby ([Northfield News, July 27, 2017, reposted by a St. Olaf scholar](https://pages.stolaf.edu/medinavi/2017/08/08/portraits-maltrata-northfields-sister-to-the-south/)). Sahan Journal put the city's Latino population above 1,700 in 2020 ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 23, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/northfield-minnesota-latino-community-city-council-school-board/)).
- **Worthington.** Immigration here is not mainly Mexican. A 2019 report described the largest Latino group as mostly Indigenous Guatemalans from San Marcos, in a town of about 13,000 with some 62 nationalities ([Workday Magazine, July 9, 2019](https://workdaymagazine.org/on-the-front-lines-of-trumps-immigration-war-in-the-u-s-heartland/)). Efrain Patino's Mexican groceries and restaurant are there (section 4).
- **The Red River Valley.** Moorhead and nearby towns drew South Texas families to the sugar beet fields for decades, as a 1990 documentary recorded ([MPR, June 20, 1990](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1990/06/20/season-of-discontent-migrants-in-the-red-river-valley)).

**How people came.** Railroads, beet fields and packinghouses recruited first (section 3). Between 1920 and 1930, more than 200,000 Mexicans legally entered the United States, and by 1928 more than 7,000 Mexican workers were in Minnesota's beet fields each year ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/minnesotanos-latino-journeys-minnesota)). In the 1890s, sugar beets brought the first Mexicans to the Willmar area, and the 1900 census counted 24 Mexicans in the state ([Green, 1994](https://jsri.msu.edu/upload/working-papers/wp19.pdf)). A foundation profile of the Minnesota Latino Museum gives the earliest documented arrival it cites as Luis Garzón of Oaxaca, who came in 1886 and settled on the West Side Flats ([Saint Paul & Minnesota Foundation, undated, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://spmcf.org/blog/minnesota-latino-museum-saint-paul-west-side)). That same year a 56-member Mexican military band played in Minneapolis for 41 days and led a Mexican Independence Day parade on Sept. 16 ([MPR News, Aug. 21, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/08/21/mn-shortlist-aug-21-aug-27)).

**Hard chapters.** During the Depression, Mexicans were pushed out of beet work and many moved to St. Paul ([Green, 1994](https://jsri.msu.edu/upload/working-papers/wp19.pdf)). Sources differ on the forced removals. MNopedia's timeline says at least 15 percent of St. Paul's Mexican settlement was repatriated in 1931 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/minnesotanos-latino-journeys-minnesota)), while a union history says St. Paul deported 324 Latinos in 1934, some of them U.S. citizens ([Union Advocate history, on Workday Magazine](https://workdaymagazine.org/latina-packinghouse-workers/)). Later, the floods of 1952 and 1957 and the Riverview Industrial District emptied the Flats. The city completed the project in 1964, and the promised jobs did not arrive in the numbers pledged ([MNopedia, modified Dec. 18, 2025](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/chicano-movement-westside-st-paul)).

**Later arrivals.** Workers came under the Bracero Program from 1942 to 1964, several thousand of them to Minnesota and the Midwest. In the early 2000s, an estimated 20,000 to 30,000 migrants a year came to the state for farm and food plant work; that figure is not Mexican only ([MNopedia, Migrant Workers, modified April 11, 2025](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/migrant-workers)). More than a third of the people of Axochiapan, Morelos, were reported to have left for the United States, and many Minneapolis residents come from there ([Diario Las Américas, Feb. 4, 2020](https://www.diariolasamericas.com/eeuu/mexicanos-minneapolis-mantienen-vivas-sus-tradiciones-n4192355)).

**Generations and languages.** Among Mexican Minnesotans 5 and older, 58.6 percent speak a language other than English at home and 23.4 percent speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/mexican)). Compass does not name the languages. Oaxacan roots in Austin raise the question of who speaks Mixtec, Zapotec or another Indigenous language at home; no Minnesota count was found. People use many names for themselves. Sources here use Mexican, Mexican American, Chicano, Tejano, Latino and Latine, and these do not all mean the same thing ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/chicano-movement-westside-st-paul); [Green, 1994](https://jsri.msu.edu/upload/working-papers/wp19.pdf)).

## 2. All walks of life

No one number describes this community. Figures below are from the Compass 2020 to 2024 profile unless noted, in 2024 dollars ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/mexican)).

- **Comfortable and well-off households.** Median household income is $71,697, give or take $3,694. About a third of households, 32.8 percent, take in $100,000 or more. No published list of the wealthiest Mexican Minnesotan families was found, and no wealth figure either.
- **Professionals and owners.** Among adults 25 and older, 20.6 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more, including 6.0 percent with a graduate or professional degree. Public examples include the Frías family, whose Boca Chica has fed St. Paul since 1964 ([Visit Saint Paul, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.visitsaintpaul.com/directory/boca-chica-restaurante/)); Milissa Silva, chief executive of El Burrito Mercado, which employed 95 people before the 2026 surge ([Sahan Journal, April 29, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/cinco-de-mayo-st-paul-west-side-latino-business-recovery/)); Gustavo and Kate Romero of Nixta and Oro ([Explore Minnesota, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.exploreminnesota.com/food-drink/best-mexican-food-minneapolis-st-paul)); Victoria Gonzalez Rocha of Manny's Tortas, named a 2024 notable Hispanic leader ([Twin Cities Business](https://tcbmag.com/notable/victoria-gonzalez-rocha/)); Ricardo Espinoza of La Michoacana Purépecha, from Michoacán, whose shops reached about 10 locations ([Neighborhood Development Center, about 2025](https://www.ndc-mn.org/news-events/spotlighting-ricardo-espinoza-founder-of-la-michoacana/)); and Efrain Patino of Worthington, who owns groceries in three states ([Sioux Falls Business, April 8, 2025](https://siouxfalls.business/?p=167029)).
- **Middle-income and working families.** Owner-occupied homes are 53.1 percent of households. Employment among adults 18 to 64 is 78.2 percent: 82.0 percent for men and 73.8 percent for women. About 32 percent of households earn $35,000 to $74,999, and 14.8 percent more earn $75,000 to $99,999.
- **People in poverty and under strain.** Twelve percent of people live below the poverty line, give or take 1.4 points. That includes 14.5 percent of children and 20.3 percent of people 65 and older, though the older figure has a wide margin of 6.2 points. About a fifth of households, 20.3 percent, take in under $35,000. Nearly half of renting households, 48.3 percent, spend too much of their income on housing. Without health insurance: 15.5 percent. Without a high school diploma among adults 25 and older: 26.6 percent.

**Over time.** Compass reported 37 percent of adults without a diploma in its 2015 to 2019 figures ([Minnesota Compass, Oct. 11, 2021](https://www.mncompass.org/trends/insights/2020-06-23-Minnesota's-Latinos-in-numbers)), against 26.6 percent now. Survey years and margins differ, so this shows a direction rather than an exact change. A hard history sits behind today's numbers too: a 1946 survey found Mexican St. Paul residents living in the poorest conditions of any group in the city ([MNopedia, Migrant Workers](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/migrant-workers)).

## 3. Work and the economy

**Sugar beets.** Growers recruited Mexican and Tejano workers, called *betabeleros*, from the early 1900s, and American Crystal Sugar became a major employer ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/minnesotanos-latino-journeys-minnesota)). Families came for spring planting and fall harvest, in company housing that was often run down, then went south or wintered in the Twin Cities ([MNopedia, American Crystal Sugar](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/american-crystal-sugar-company)). Most came from Texas. A 1927 national survey ranked the Burlington Railroad boxcars housing workers in Inver Grove Heights the worst in the nation, and until a 1937 federal law, children commonly worked the fields ([MNopedia, Migrant Workers](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/migrant-workers)). In 1990, an MPR documentary still followed South Texas families to the valley, with low pay, scarce housing and open prejudice in Moorhead-area towns ([MPR, June 20, 1990](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1990/06/20/season-of-discontent-migrants-in-the-red-river-valley)).

**Canning.** In 1943, about 350 Mexican nationals were in the beet fields, and more than 1,000 worked for Minnesota Valley Canning (later Green Giant) and Fairmont Canning ([Green, 1994](https://jsri.msu.edu/upload/working-papers/wp19.pdf)).

**Packing in South St. Paul.** Armour, Swift and Cudahy drew West Side workers. Latinas entered the plants during World War II, mainly in processing departments. A union history recalls the United Packinghouse Workers backing plant-wide seniority and integrated locker rooms, a three-month strike by about 10,000 workers in 1948, and one woman's pay rising from 50 to 75 cents an hour after a union meeting ([Union Advocate history, on Workday Magazine](https://workdaymagazine.org/latina-packinghouse-workers/)).

**Turkey in Willmar.** Jennie-O reportedly had an unwritten policy in the 1960s of wanting to hire Mexicans. By 1994 it employed about 4,000 people, a large majority Chicano or Latino, and remained non-union. The same paper reports residents' complaint that the state's job office steered Mexicans to the plants instead of mechanical or office jobs for which they were qualified ([Green, 1994](https://jsri.msu.edu/upload/working-papers/wp19.pdf)). Current figures were not found.

**Pork in Austin.** After the 1985 to 1986 Hormel strike, sources differ on who replaced strikers. MNopedia's account says most of about 540 non-union hires in January 1986 were Mexican migrant workers ([MinnPost/MNopedia, updated Feb. 6, 2024](https://www.minnpost.com/mnopedia/2019/08/the-1985-hormel-strike-was-one-of-minnesotas-most-contentious-labor-disputes/)). A local labor historian told FOX 9 that all replacement workers were white, that most quit within about five years, and that Hormel then recruited from Mexico and Central America ([FOX 9, Sept. 14, 2023](https://www.fox9.com/news/how-hormels-1985-strike-change-austin-minn)). Quality Pork Processors took over slaughter at lower pay ([WNYC Studios and The Atlantic, Feb. 17, 2022](https://wnycstudios.org/podcasts/experiment/episodes/spam-factory-meatpacking-autoimmune-disorder)). Union officials said in 2010 that up to 90 percent of its 1,200 workers were Latino ([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/08/09/austin-at-a-crossroads-part2)).

**Pork in Worthington.** On Dec. 12, 2006, federal agents raided Swift plants in six states, arresting nearly 1,300 workers, including at Worthington. A year later, a union official and others described fear and mistrust that had not lifted, and a second raid arrested 20 more ([MPR News, Dec. 11, 2007](https://www.mprnews.org/2007/12/11/swiftanniversary/)). JBS bought the plant soon after and in 2019 was the town's largest employer, with an estimated 1,800 workers ([Workday Magazine, 2019](https://workdaymagazine.org/on-the-front-lines-of-trumps-immigration-war-in-the-u-s-heartland/)).

**Trade and today's organizing.** Minnesota exports to Mexico reached $2.24 billion in 2014, and Mexico was the state's second-largest goods market then ([Star Tribune, via LatinoLEAD](https://www.latinoleadmn.org/post/mexican-consul-in-st-paul-strives-to-build-links-partnerships)). Farmworkers in southern Minnesota formed Centro Campesino in 1998 ([MNopedia, Migrant Workers](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/migrant-workers)). COPAL runs a Workers' Center and Radio Jornalera, part of a network of day laborers' radio stations ([COPAL, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.copalmn.org/); [Radio Jornalera, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://radiojornaleramn.org/)). No occupation table for Mexican Minnesotans turned up, and no current count of union members.

**Entrepreneurs.** Willmar's Latino and Somali business directory listed nearly 35 firms in 2009, and its director said they had grown from retail and restaurants to insurance, legal and medical practices ([MPR News, July 13, 2009](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2009/07/13/willmar_directory)). The Latino Economic Development Center in St. Paul lends and advises, and describes itself as the only Latino-focused community lender in Minnesota and the Upper Midwest ([LEDC, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://ledcmn.org/)). The Latino Chamber of Commerce Minnesota began in 2016 after the Hispanic Chamber of Commerce of Minnesota ceased operating ([Twin Cities Business](https://tcbmag.com/tcb-100-people/john-pacheco-jr/); [Latino Chamber, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.latinochambermn.com/)). No current count of Mexican-owned firms was found.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

A fuller list goes with this page, and many places on it could not be confirmed open. The picture below uses places with current signs of operation. It covers Mexican and wider Latino commerce; several owners' Mexican roots are not stated, and the list says so.

**St. Paul's West Side and District del Sol.** El Burrito Mercado, a market and restaurant serving since 1979 at 175 Cesar Chavez St., is a three-generation business that cut hours and staff in 2026 ([Sahan Journal, April 29, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/cinco-de-mayo-st-paul-west-side-latino-business-recovery/); [El Burrito Mercado, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.elburritomercado.com/); [Explore Minnesota](https://www.exploreminnesota.com/food-drink/best-mexican-food-minneapolis-st-paul)). Boca Chica, opened in 1964 with 28 seats, posted 2026 menus ([Boca Chica, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.bocachicarestaurant.com/)). Its owner has credited West Side Mexican American families with the deep-fried "West Side taco" that spread to St. Paul bars ([Racket, March 8, 2022](https://racketmn.com/lets-eat-some-st-paul-bar-tacos)). A new neighborhood look, with a sunset logo for District del Sol, was unveiled in 2026 ([St. Paul Publishing, August 2026](https://www.stpaulpublishing.com/new-west-side-brand-unveiled-vote-soon-to-rename-cesar-chavez-street/)).

**East Lake Street, Minneapolis.** Mercado Central opened in July 1999 with 47 small businesses and now holds 35 tenants. Its co-founder, Juan Linares, moved from Mexico City to Detroit Lakes and then Minneapolis, and the market reported losing 85 percent of revenue in January 2026 ([Sahan Journal, April 28, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/mercado-central-lake-street-minneapolis-loan-forgiven-immigration/); [Twin Cities Business, March 30, 2026](https://tcbmag.com/tcb-show/the-story-of-mercado-central-and-25-years-in-the-heart-of-south-minneapolis/)). Tortillería La Perla has a restaurant inside ([MPR News, Oct. 6, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/06/minneapolis-shops-invite-customers-for-latino-business-week-amid-ice-surge)). Plaza Mexico, at Lake and Fifth, had closed stalls in December 2025 after employees were detained ([WCCO, Dec. 12, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/twin-cities-latino-somali-businesses-struggling-ice-operation-metro-surge)). Manny's Tortas has run at Midtown Global Market since 1999 ([Manny's Tortas, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.mannystortas.com/); [Explore Minnesota](https://www.exploreminnesota.com/food-drink/best-mexican-food-minneapolis-st-paul)). Marsu Pizzeria and Taqueria was still open in April 2026 with a staff of four ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=83245)). Colonial Market closed its Lake Street store on June 14, 2026, but kept Bloomington and north Minneapolis open ([Sahan Journal, June 26, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=85331)).

**Elsewhere.** Oro by Nixta and Nixta are in Northeast Minneapolis, and Oro was one of 10 James Beard nominees for Best New Restaurant in 2024 ([Nixta, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.nixtampls.com/oro); [KSTP, 2024](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/local-news/local-restaurant-chefs-among-james-beard-finalists/)). In Worthington, Efrain Patino opened the El Mexicano grocery in 2011 and a restaurant in 2014 ([Sioux Falls Business](https://siouxfalls.business/?p=167029)).

Media and business groups are in sections 3 and 8.

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

**Catholic life, with deep roots.** The Guild of Catholic Women's report led to Minnesota's first Mexican mission and the state's first Spanish-language Mass in 1931 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/minnesotanos-latino-journeys-minnesota)). Our Lady of Guadalupe, now at 401 Concord St. in St. Paul, moved there when the Flats were demolished in 1961 and offers Mass in English and Spanish ([Archdiocese of Saint Paul and Minneapolis, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.archspm.org/?p=5890)). It was named the archdiocese's first and only diocesan shrine in December 2019 ([The Catholic Spirit, Dec. 13, 2019](https://thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/st-paul-parish-named-diocesan-shrine-to-our-lady-of-guadalupe/)). The Guadalupanas and the Congregation of the Sacred Heart were among the West Side's early groups ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/minnesotanos-latino-journeys-minnesota)).

**Other Catholic communities.** Sagrado Corazón de Jesús, a chaplaincy at Incarnation in south Minneapolis, lists four Spanish Masses across Saturday and Sunday ([Archdiocese, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.archspm.org/location/sagrado-corazon-de-jesus-catholic-church)). Families from Axochiapan hold a January feast for San Pablo there, with some 1,200 taking part (section 6). Queen of Angels in Austin grew from one monthly Spanish Mass to three services by 2010 ([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/08/09/austin-at-a-crossroads-part2)). St. Dominic in Northfield and the parish in Maltrata became sister parishes in 2007 ([Northfield News, reposted](https://pages.stolaf.edu/medinavi/2017/08/08/portraits-maltrata-northfields-sister-to-the-south/)). In Burnsville, Church of the Risen Savior held a Guadalupe evening with hundreds in 2014 ([MPR News, Dec. 17, 2014](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2014/12/17/photos-our-lady-guadalupe)). In Willmar, local churches were among the first to hold Spanish Masses and run a shared children's camp in the 1980s ([Green, 1994](https://jsri.msu.edu/upload/working-papers/wp19.pdf)).

**Not only Catholic.** About 300 people marked Guadalupe in 2003 at Misión El Santo Niño Jesús, an Episcopal Hispanic church in St. Paul, with mariachi and Aztec dancers ([MPR, Dec. 12, 2003](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/2003/12/12/mexican-minnesotans-celebrate-our-lady-of-guadalupe)). Whether that congregation continues was not confirmed. Mexica dance groups such as Kalpulli KetzalCoatlicue appear at public celebrations ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/cinco-de-mayo-saint-paul-west-side-parade/)); what the dance means to its dancers, as ceremony or heritage, could not be established.

**What could not be measured.** No figures were found for the share of Mexican Minnesotans who are Catholic, evangelical, of another faith or of no faith. No named Mexican Protestant or Pentecostal congregation could be confirmed, so none is listed, and that gap should not be read as absence.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

**Cinco de Mayo.** It marks a Mexican military victory in 1862, and Sahan Journal notes it does not carry much weight in Mexico itself ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/cinco-de-mayo-saint-paul-west-side-parade/)). On the West Side it has been held since 1985 ([Explore Minnesota, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.exploreminnesota.com/event/cinco-de-mayo-west-side-st-paul/2682)). The 2026 parade ran May 2 on Cesar Chavez Street from Wabasha to Ada, with a car show May 1 ([West Side Fiestas, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://westsidefiestas.com/); [Explore Minnesota](https://www.exploreminnesota.com/event/cinco-de-mayo-west-side-st-paul/2682)). About 30,000 came in 2025 ([Sahan Journal, April 29, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=83245)). In 2026 organizers debated whether to hold it, began planning again only about three weeks out, and went ahead in a smaller footprint; thousands came ([MPR News, May 2, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/05/02/st-pauls-cinco-de-mayo-parade-draws-crowds-after-winter-of-fear); [MPR News, April 30, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/04/30/west-st-paul-cinco-de-mayo-after-challenging-winter); [MN Daily, May 4, 2026](https://mndaily.com/city/thousands-gather-on-st-pauls-west-side-for-cinco-de-mayo-celebration/05/04/2026/eicmndaily-com/)).

**Mexican Independence Day (Sept. 16).** St. Paul's first Fiestas Patrias in 1939 paraded from the West Side downtown, with food from the Guadalupanas ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/minnesotanos-latino-journeys-minnesota)). West Side Fiestas set its 2026 celebration for Sept. 12, with a car show, music, vendors and a march ([West Side Fiestas](https://westsidefiestas.com/)).

**Our Lady of Guadalupe (Dec. 12).** It honors the 1531 appearance reported by Juan Diego ([MPR, 2003](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/2003/12/12/mexican-minnesotans-celebrate-our-lady-of-guadalupe)). The St. Paul shrine holds an annual one-mile procession, and about 700 attended its Mass on the feast's eve in 2019 ([The Catholic Spirit, 2019](https://thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/st-paul-parish-named-diocesan-shrine-to-our-lady-of-guadalupe/)). The 2026 schedule could not be found.

**Día de los Muertos (Nov. 1 and 2).** The 14th Festival de las Calaveras is Oct. 31, 2026, at the Hook and Ladder in Minneapolis, with the Mexican Consulate among its partners ([Opendate listing, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://app.opendate.io/e/festival-de-las-calaveras-2026-october-31-2026-718151)). CLUES holds its free day at Midtown Global Market on Nov. 1, 2026 ([Midtown Global Market, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://midtownglobalmarket.org/new-events/2026/10/31/dia-de-muertos)). Meet Minneapolis lists ten related events this year, including El Colegio High School's and the first ofrenda at Hennepin History Museum ([Meet Minneapolis, checked Oct. 30, 2026](http://www.minneapolis.org/calendar/holidays/dia-de-los-muertos/)). Altarista Monica Vega has built on her family's roots in Michoacán at the Weisman ([Weisman Art Museum, 2024](https://wam.umn.edu/dia-de-los-muertos-community-ofrenda)).

**The feast of San Pablo (around Jan. 25).** Axochiapan's patron saint is honored at Incarnation/Sagrado Corazón with Masses, a snowy street procession, dance and tamales with mole ([Diario Las Américas, 2020](https://www.diariolasamericas.com/eeuu/mexicanos-minneapolis-mantienen-vivas-sus-tradiciones-n4192355)). No 2026 report was found.

**Hispanic Heritage Month.** CLUES's Fiesta Latina was set for Sept. 19, 2026, at CHS Field ([CLUES, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.clues.org/)). Rochester's Latino Fest took place the same day, drawing hundreds in the rain ([KTTC, Sept. 19, 2026](https://www.kttc.com/2026/09/19/hundreds-celebrate-latino-fest-2026-downtown-rochester/)). Minneapolis held its third Latino Business Week through Oct. 11 ([MPR News, Oct. 6, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/06/minneapolis-shops-invite-customers-for-latino-business-week-amid-ice-surge)).

**Farmworkers Day (March 31).** Minnesota lawmakers repealed Cesar Chavez Day in 2026 after abuse allegations, and the governor proclaimed Farmworkers Day that year ([FOX 9, March 31, 2026](https://fox9.com/news/minnesota-recognizes-farmworkers-day-following-cesar-chavez-fallout-march-2026)).

**Christian seasons and home traditions.** Lent, Holy Week, Christmas, Las Posadas and Día de los Reyes appear on the calendar, but no Minnesota report of a public Posadas, Via Crucis or Reyes celebration in a Mexican community could be confirmed, so none is described here. That does not mean they do not happen, only that none could be documented. No Minnesota Guelaguetza was found either.

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Households.** The average household has 2.99 people, and 9.3 percent of Mexican Minnesotans are under 5. Men slightly outnumber women, 52.3 to 47.7 percent, and adults 65 and older are few, just 4.2 percent ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/mexican)). Sources do not say how many households hold three generations.

**Keeping ties across a border.** One organizer of the San Pablo feast said, in Spanish, that he wants his children and grandchildren to remember it ([Diario Las Américas](https://www.diariolasamericas.com/eeuu/mexicanos-minneapolis-mantienen-vivas-sus-tradiciones-n4192355)). Nearly all the women a Women's Foundation team met in Axochiapan had family in Minnesota, and returning the body of a relative who dies here can cost about $10,000 ([Women's Foundation of Minnesota, 2023](https://www.wfmn.org/axochiapan-connecting-communities-and-women-led-movements-from-mexico-to-minnesota)).

**Food.** Corn masa, tamales, mole, pozole, menudo and tortas turn up across the sources ([Explore Minnesota](https://www.exploreminnesota.com/food-drink/best-mexican-food-minneapolis-st-paul); [Diario Las Américas](https://www.diariolasamericas.com/eeuu/mexicanos-minneapolis-mantienen-vivas-sus-tradiciones-n4192355)). A 2011 MPR piece described a Frogtown Mexican grocery and meat market whose owner knew the writer by name ([MPR News, April 2011](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2011/04/14/astrain)).

**Milestones.** Los Alegres Bailadores performs at weddings and quinceañeras as well as public events ([Los Alegres Bailadores, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://losalegresbailadores.com/)). Beyond that, no Minnesota source was found on courtship, marriage, childrearing or care of elders in Mexican families, so this page does not describe them. CLUES lists Latino Elder Programs without detail on its home page ([CLUES](https://www.clues.org/)).

**The winter of 2025 to 2026 at home.** Many families stayed home, Bob Cruz said, and neighbors watched businesses and delivered food ([MN Daily, May 4, 2026](https://mndaily.com/city/thousands-gather-on-st-pauls-west-side-for-cinco-de-mayo-celebration/05/04/2026/eicmndaily-com/); [MPR News, April 30, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/04/30/west-st-paul-cinco-de-mayo-after-challenging-winter)).

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Museums and history.** The Minnesota Latino Museum, led by Aaron Johnson-Ortiz, has existed since 2015 and is described as the first museum in Minnesota for Mexican and Latin artists. It seeks a home in Harriet Island Regional Park, and its alebrijes filled Raspberry Island, 17 figures up to 16 feet tall ([Saint Paul & Minnesota Foundation](https://spmcf.org/blog/minnesota-latino-museum-saint-paul-west-side)). With Hennepin History Museum it opened "140 Years of Mexican & Latino Music in Minnesota" on Aug. 20, 2026, running through February 2028 ([MPR News, Aug. 21, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/08/21/mn-shortlist-aug-21-aug-27)). The Minnesota Historical Society holds the Mexican American Community in St. Paul Collections, oral histories and the newspaper *La Voz* ([MNHS guide, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://libguides.mnhs.org/mexican)).

**Dance and theater.** Los Alegres Bailadores, led by Rebecca Moran Cusick, has been active more than 45 years and paused classes in the 2026 winter ([Los Alegres Bailadores](https://losalegresbailadores.com/); [MN Daily, 2026](https://mndaily.com/city/thousands-gather-on-st-pauls-west-side-for-cinco-de-mayo-celebration/05/04/2026/eicmndaily-com/)). Kalpulli KetzalCoatlicue, Kalpulli Yaocenoxtli, Mexica Yolotl and Comparsa San Lucas Evangelista have danced at the celebration ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/cinco-de-mayo-saint-paul-west-side-parade/)). Teatro del Pueblo on West Page Street now offers the *Coco's Barrio* podcast ([Teatro del Pueblo, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://teatrodelpueblo.org/)). Centro Cultural Chicano, founded in 1974, is now Centro Tyrone Guzman ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/place/centro-cultural-chicano-minneapolis)). CLUES runs a St. Paul gallery, with "To Be Minnesotane: Ser y Estar" up through Nov. 6, 2026 ([CLUES](https://www.clues.org/)).

**Media.** La Raza 95.7 FM carries daily Spanish programming from Santamaria Broadcasting ([La Raza, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://laraza1400.com/)). La Prensa de Minnesota, started in 1991 by Mario Duarte, who came from El Salvador, posted a story dated Oct. 30, 2026 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/la-prensa-de-minnesota); [La Prensa, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.laprensademn.com/)). Radio Jornalera broadcasts online from East Lake Street ([Radio Jornalera](https://radiojornaleramn.org/)). La Voz Latina runs with the St. Paul Voice ([St. Paul Voice and La Voz Latina, Sept. 18, 2026](https://www.newsbreak.com/st-paul-voice-and-la-voz-latina-314811571/4894343305702-cesar-chavez-street-renaming-delayed)).

**Sports.** West Side Boosters, founded in 1970, runs youth teams; about 80 percent of its coaches played there as children, by the count of Santino Franco, co-chair of West Side Fiestas ([MPR News, April 30, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/04/30/west-st-paul-cinco-de-mayo-after-challenging-winter)). The parade ends at De La O Football Field ([Explore Minnesota](https://www.exploreminnesota.com/event/cinco-de-mayo-west-side-st-paul/2682)). The Timberwolves schedule a Noche Latina on Nov. 2, 2026 ([Meet Minneapolis](http://www.minneapolis.org/calendar/holidays/dia-de-los-muertos/)). No named Mexican Minnesotan professional athletes could be confirmed.

## 9. Civic and political life

**Firsts.** Conrado Vega became the state's first Latino legislator in 1977, and Minnesota created the Chicano Latino Affairs Council in 1978 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/minnesotanos-latino-journeys-minnesota)). Alondra Cano became the first Mexican American on the Minneapolis City Council after the 2013 election ([MPR News, November 2013](https://www.mprnews.org/story/cities/2013/11/voters-bring-more-racial-ethnic-diversity-to-minneapolis-city-council)). In Northfield in 2020, George Zuccolotto and Claudia Gonzalez-George were the first Latinos elected ([Sahan Journal, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/northfield-minnesota-latino-community-city-council-school-board/)). Jason Chavez, described by the city as the first LGBTQ+ Latinx council member, chairs the Minneapolis council's Public Health, Safety & Equity Committee ([City of Minneapolis, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/government/city-council/members/ward-9/about-jason-chavez)). No current list of Mexican American state lawmakers was found.

**The Chicano movement.** Sister Mary Giovanni Gourhan proposed the Guadalupe Area Plan in 1966. A St. Paul Brown Berets chapter formed in 1969. On Oct. 26, 1971, students led by Ramona Rosales occupied Morrill Hall at the University of Minnesota ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/chicano-movement-westside-st-paul)).

**The Mexican Consulate in St. Paul.** It opened on the East Side in June 2005, so that people need not drive to Chicago. Consul General Nathan Wolf and community leader Pablo Tapia were among those named ([Post Bulletin, June 21, 2005](https://www.postbulletin.com/news/mexican-consulate-opens-in-st-paul)). Its early work centered on the *matrícula consular* identity card and helping nationals in legal trouble reach a translator and lawyer ([MPR, May 24, 2005](https://news.minnesota.publicradio.org/features/2005/05/20_tonessb_consulate/)). Alejandra María Gabriela Bologna Zubikarai has been consul since Aug. 18, 2025, at 797 East 7th St., with a district of Minnesota, the Dakotas and 19 Wisconsin counties. Departments handle protection, documents, community and education affairs, a health window, and economic and cultural promotion ([Consulate of Mexico, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://consulmex.sre.gob.mx/saintpaul/index.php/el-con)).

**Organizations.** COPAL, begun in 2018, is raising $20 million for a Latino Center for Community Engagement and promotes the vote ([COPAL](https://www.copalmn.org/)). The Minnesota Latino Leadership Alliance pressed lawmakers in February 2026 on the STAR Act and small business relief ([Hola América News, Feb. 18, 2026](https://holaamericanews.com/minnesota-latino-leaders-press-lawmakers-to-protect-immigrants-after-ice-occupation/)). COPAL's agenda also defends driver's licenses regardless of immigration status and the North Star Promise ([COPAL, Feb. 17, 2026](https://copalmn.org/copal-launches-2026-legislative-agenda-with-gathering-at-the-minnesota-capitol/)). West Side Community Organization serves as the planning council for District 3 ([WSCO, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.wsco.org/)).

**Debates inside the community.** The biggest open one is the street (section 13). Northfield's Zuccolotto observed in 2020 that many Latino voters are Catholic and hold some conservative views ([Sahan Journal, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/northfield-minnesota-latino-community-city-council-school-board/)). That is one elected official's observation, not a measurement, and no Minnesota poll of Mexican voters was found.

## 10. Education

**Attainment.** Among adults 25 and older, 73.4 percent finished high school and 20.6 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/mexican)).

**Community-rooted schools.** The Guadalupe Area Plan, known as GAP, began from Our Lady of Guadalupe in the 1960s and was still serving St. Paul students in 2019 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/chicano-movement-westside-st-paul)). The St. Paul charter long known as Academia Cesar Chavez, at 1801 Lacrosse Ave., now appears on its website as Academia In Lak'ech, with the motto "you are my other me," and its old web address leads there, though the site does not explain the change ([Academia In Lak'ech, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.academiainlakech.org/)). In March 2026 the school called the allegations against Chávez "profoundly concerning" ([Racket, March 20, 2026](https://racketmn.com/after-cesar-chavez-fallout-maybe-we-just-stop-naming-things-after-people)), and MPR reported a St. Paul charter school had removed his name ([MPR News, July 28, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/07/28/cesar-chavez-street-st-paul-renaming-feedback-deadline)). El Colegio High School in Minneapolis lists a Día de los Muertos for 2026 ([Meet Minneapolis](http://www.minneapolis.org/calendar/holidays/dia-de-los-muertos/)).

**Higher education.** Student protests led to the University of Minnesota's Department of Chicano-Latino Studies, and St. Cloud State added a minor in 1995 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/minnesotanos-latino-journeys-minnesota)). First classes ran in fall 1972 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/chicano-movement-westside-st-paul)). LEDC offers a Latino Scholarship Fund ([LEDC](https://ledcmn.org/)). Scholars who have written on this history include Dionicio Valdés and Jim Norris ([MNHS guide](https://libguides.mnhs.org/mexican)).

**A rural comparison.** In 2018 to 2019, Northfield's Latino students graduated at 89 percent, against just over half in neighboring Faribault ([Sahan Journal, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/northfield-minnesota-latino-community-city-council-school-board/)). These are all-Latino figures, and the reasons were not established.

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Community-built care.** La Clínica began on the West Side in 1969 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/minnesotanos-latino-journeys-minnesota)). West Side Community Health Services has been home to a Somali, Latino and Hmong health partnership ([Minnesota Department of Health report, 2014](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/2014/mandated/140404.pdf)). CLUES, founded in 1981 and now led by Liliana Letran-Garcia, runs the Abriendo Caminos behavioral health clinic and has offices in St. Paul, Minneapolis, Willmar and Austin ([CLUES](https://www.clues.org/)). In 2015 the consulate described referring patients to a partner clinic ([Star Tribune, via LatinoLEAD](https://www.latinoleadmn.org/post/mexican-consul-in-st-paul-strives-to-build-links-partnerships)); today it lists a health window ([Consulate of Mexico](https://consulmex.sre.gob.mx/saintpaul/index.php/el-con)).

**Coverage.** Among Mexican Minnesotans, 15.5 percent have no health insurance ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/mexican)).

**Workplace injury and illness.** Around 2007, workers at the Quality Pork Processors head table in Austin developed weakness and nerve problems from breathing mist made of pig brain matter. Counts range from 15 to 21 cases, depending on the source; many were Spanish-speaking and some came from Mexico. The company stopped using the machine and no new cases were reported ([WNYC Studios and The Atlantic, Feb. 17, 2022](https://wnycstudios.org/podcasts/experiment/episodes/spam-factory-meatpacking-autoimmune-disorder)).

**Traditional practice.** No Minnesota source was found on *curanderas*, herbal practice or how Mexican families use them alongside clinics. No Minnesota rate of diabetes or other conditions for Mexican-origin adults was found.

**Stress in 2026.** Fear reached ordinary errands, MPR News reported ([MPR News, May 2, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/05/02/st-pauls-cinco-de-mayo-parade-draws-crowds-after-winter-of-fear)). No measure of the effect on health was found.

## 12. Disability

**What the state's numbers say.** Compass reports 8.1 percent of Mexican Minnesotans with a disability, give or take 0.9, and 7.2 percent of those under 65 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/mexican)). This is what people report on a survey. The number invites questions rather than answers: the population is young, words may not translate, and people may hesitate to name a condition to a government survey. No source has settled which of these matters, if any.

**No Mexican-specific study was found.** Minnesota evidence comes under "Hispanic" or "Latino," so it covers Mexican families without describing them alone.

**Autism among children.** The University of Minnesota's tracking found 1 in 28 eight-year-olds with autism in 2022, in parts of Anoka, Hennepin and Ramsey counties, and Hispanic prevalence 1.4 times the rate for white children. The project does not examine causes ([Institute on Community Integration, 2025](https://ici.umn.edu/news/new-autism-prevalence-findings-in-minnesota)). Why the local number runs this way remains an open question, and it should not be read as a trait of any people.

**What Latino parents said.** A state study interviewed 17 Latino parents, mostly in focus groups, alongside Somali and Hmong parents. They described long waits, language barriers and insurance costs, with concerns about unauthorized immigration status adding to the difficulty even though their children were likely born here. They asked for coverage for undocumented families. The summary says Latino parents linked autism to vaccines, yet the body describes one parent who was unsure ([Minnesota Department of Health, Feb. 15, 2014](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/2014/mandated/140404.pdf)). The group is small and the study is over a decade old.

**Who uses disability services.** A report for the Minnesota Department of Human Services studied Medicaid records from 2019. Among people with a recorded disability, 32 percent of Hispanic people used a disability waiver, compared with 46 percent of white people, and 52 percent of Hispanic people used neither a waiver nor personal care help. Among older adults, 60 percent of Hispanic people used neither ([Arling and others, March 26, 2021](https://developtoolmn.org/dhs/assets/racial-and-ethnic-comparison-report-quantatitive_tcm1053-663962.pdf)). A companion report gathered views from a community board. One member said that in the Latino community "it is hard to speak about disability," and noted that being undocumented makes seeking help harder ([Shippee and others, Feb. 12, 2021](https://www.developtoolmn.org/dhs/assets/stakeholder-feedback-report-dhs-qualitative_tcm1053-663963.pdf)). That is one voice. The reports do not establish why the gap exists.

**Questions worth asking, since the sources leave them open.**

- Who decides what counts as disability in a given family, and in what language? Does a label from a school or clinic open services, or close doors, for a family worried about immigration records?
- Does a parent's immigration status limit help for a U.S.-born child in practice, whatever the rules say?
- What did the work itself produce? The Austin plant illness in section 11 is one documented case of harm caused on the job. Injury rates in packing and field work for Mexican Minnesotans could not be found.
- What do older adults need, given a 20.3 percent poverty rate among those 65 and up? No source answers this.

**Help named in Spanish.** PACER Center and Family Voices of Minnesota began Latino parent groups in 2024, with a state grant, and tip sheets in Spanish ([PACER, July 17, 2024](https://www.pacer.org/?p=8448)). The state and the Institute on Community Integration released a 17-minute Spanish film on autism in 2018 ([ICI, 2018](https://ici.umn.edu/products/oSoIi1YoR92u22wV3xMs-A)). No culturally specific Mexican or Latino disability organization in Minnesota could be confirmed, and no public Mexican Minnesotan disability advocate could be named.

## 13. What is changing now

**The surge and its aftermath.** The federal immigration operation known as Operation Metro Surge escalated in Minnesota in December 2025. WCCO reported 1,694 ICE arrests in the state from Jan. 20 to Oct. 15, 2025, and more than 400 once the escalated operation began ([WCCO, Dec. 12, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/twin-cities-latino-somali-businesses-struggling-ice-operation-metro-surge)). St. Paul estimated $129 million in small business losses and $118 million in lost immigrant income for January and February 2026; Minneapolis estimated at least $203 million in one month ([Sahan Journal, April 29, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=83245)). Lake Street's counts differ by measure: the council said over half of immigrant-owned businesses were closed at some point, while fewer than five of about 1,000 corridor businesses had closed for good by June ([Sahan Journal, April 29, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=83245); [Sahan Journal, June 26, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=85331)). A Lake Street survey found 96 percent of businesses hurt ([Lake Street Council, March 2026](https://www.visitlakestreet.com/business-blog/ice-outspring-forward)). By October, a Mercado Central owner said some had recovered and others were just trying to keep up ([MPR News, Oct. 6, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/06/minneapolis-shops-invite-customers-for-latino-business-week-amid-ice-surge)). The West Side organized too: nearly 120 people came to an April gathering for affected families and trained observers ([WSCO](https://www.wsco.org/)). How many of those arrested were Mexican nationals could not be found.

**César Chávez, and a vote days from now.** After a March 2026 New York Times investigation reported sexual abuse allegations against Chávez, Minnesota ended Cesar Chavez Day ([FOX 9, March 31, 2026](https://fox9.com/news/minnesota-recognizes-farmworkers-day-following-cesar-chavez-fallout-march-2026)). On the West Side, part of Concord Street had become Cesar Chavez Street in 2004, and a facilitator said that was done without public input ([MPR News, July 28, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/07/28/cesar-chavez-street-st-paul-renaming-feedback-deadline)). Listening sessions produced five names: Del Sol, El Barrio, El Rio Vista, La Raza and West Side ([St. Paul Voice and La Voz Latina, Sept. 18, 2026](https://www.newsbreak.com/st-paul-voice-and-la-voz-latina-314811571/4894343305702-cesar-chavez-street-renaming-delayed)). Voting set for August was called off after people asked for Concord Street on the ballot. The committee reconvened in October and left the ballot unchanged, with no write-ins. The vote is Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026, 8 a.m. to 8 p.m., for residents and owners in ZIP code 55107, age 16 and up ([WSCO, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.wsco.org/streetrenaming)). Views differ. A longtime resident wants the old name back. Another said Chávez never represented all Mexican Americans. WSCO's director calls the effort "a form of collective self-determination" ([MPR News, July 28, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/07/28/cesar-chavez-street-st-paul-renaming-feedback-deadline); [MPR News, July 16, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/07/16/st-paul-neighborhood-begins-process-to-rename-csar-chavez-street)). The winning name still goes to the city.

**Generations.** Most Mexican Minnesotans are U.S.-born, and the foreign-born share has moved little: about 30 percent in a 2019 analysis ([Sahan Journal, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesotas-racial-ethnic-diversity-grows-even-as-immigration-slows/)) and 27.5 percent now ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/mexican)).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

Mexican workers planted and cut the beets, canned the vegetables and packed the meat in much of Minnesota's food economy, across a century and more ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/minnesotanos-latino-journeys-minnesota); [Green, 1994](https://jsri.msu.edu/upload/working-papers/wp19.pdf)). Families whose neighborhood was flooded and then cleared kept a church, started a school program and a clinic, sustained a youth sports league for more than five decades and grew a parade that drew about 30,000 in 2025 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/chicano-movement-westside-st-paul); [MPR News, April 30, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/04/30/west-st-paul-cinco-de-mayo-after-challenging-winter); [Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=83245)). University students won a department. Northfield gained a sister parish and new city leaders ([Northfield News, reposted](https://pages.stolaf.edu/medinavi/2017/08/08/portraits-maltrata-northfields-sister-to-the-south/); [Sahan Journal, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/northfield-minnesota-latino-community-city-council-school-board/)). A Minneapolis restaurant built on corn masa reached the James Beard finals ([KSTP, 2024](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/local-news/local-restaurant-chefs-among-james-beard-finalists/)). In the winter of 2026, neighbors watched over each other's stores and grocers carried food to people afraid to leave home ([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/04/30/west-st-paul-cinco-de-mayo-after-challenging-winter); [WCCO](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/twin-cities-latino-somali-businesses-struggling-ice-operation-metro-surge)), and then they held the parade.
