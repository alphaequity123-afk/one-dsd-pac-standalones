# Indian in Minnesota
Checked: October 31, 2026

This page is about Minnesotans with roots in India. It does not cover Native American communities. Where a source speaks of "South Asians," it is counting a wider group that includes people from Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Sri Lanka and elsewhere, and the text says so.

## 1. People and place

**How many.** Minnesota Compass, using the American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024, counts 51,618 Indian Minnesotans, give or take 3,878 ([Minnesota Compass, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/indian)). The state demographer's chart for the single year 2024 shows 50,093 Asian Indian residents, second to Hmong among Asian Pacific groups in Minnesota ([Susan Brower, State Demographer, June 27, 2026](https://mn.gov/capm/assets/SusanBrower_StateOfAsianPacificMinnesotans_June2026_tcm1051-759113.pdf)). The state's own 2026 economic chartbook, using 2019 to 2023 survey data, counts 44,000, give or take 4,600, because it places people who belong to more than one cultural group in a separate "multiple groups" category ([The Economic Status of Minnesotans 2026, Minnesota State Demographic Center](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). All three are estimates. They ask slightly different questions, so they should not be read as a change over time.

**How it grew.** Preeti Mathur's MNopedia history gives 3,670 people in 1980, the first year the federal census asked about Asian Indians, about 8,234 in 1990, and 33,031 in 2010 ([MNopedia, last modified April 16, 2025](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/ganges-ten-thousand-lakes-immigration-india-minnesota)). In the 2026 chartbook, 12,100 of the 27,700 foreign-born Asian Indian Minnesotans ages 16 to 64 had been in the United States 10 years or less, among the largest shares of newer arrivals of any group ([Economic Status 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). The state demographer's June 2026 talk says recent Asian Pacific migration to Minnesota has been led by people coming from India and China ([Brower, June 2026](https://mn.gov/capm/assets/SusanBrower_StateOfAsianPacificMinnesotans_June2026_tcm1051-759113.pdf)).

**Where.** Compass places 90.0 percent in the Twin Cities area and 10.0 percent in Greater Minnesota, each give or take 1.9 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/indian)). The India Association of Minnesota, drawing on 2019 to 2023 census tables, lists Plymouth (5,379), Eden Prairie (4,921), Minneapolis (4,703), Eagan (2,663) and Maple Grove (2,476) as the largest city counts, with Eden Prairie the largest share of its city at 7.78 percent among those five. These are the association's figures; the census table behind them was not checked here ([India Association of Minnesota, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://iamn.org/?p=391)). Outside the metro, the clearest organized presence on record is around Rochester, where the Indian Cultural Association of Minnesota has held Diwali shows at the Mayo Civic Center and now lists Hindi and Tamil schools and a Diwali program in Byron ([KAAL](https://www.kaaltv.com/news/diwali/); [ICAM, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://icamn.org/)).

**Young, and mostly born abroad.** Compass reports 26.6 percent are 17 or younger and 4.7 percent are 65 or older, and 64.4 percent, give or take 2.9, were born outside the United States ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/indian)). The chartbook, with its narrower group, puts the foreign-born at 32,500 of 44,000 and the median age at 34, against 38 for all Minnesotans. About one in five moved in the past year, which the report links to the large share of new international arrivals ([Economic Status 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). A second generation is here, with 8,000 born in Minnesota in the chartbook's count, and the India Association's history work has included projects on first-generation immigrants (finished 1994) and on their children (finished 1998) ([IAM Oral History, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://iamn.org/iam-oral-history/)).

**How people came.** MNopedia dates the first known Indian who moved to Minnesota for good to 1904, when Hira Singh, a Sikh from Lahore, enrolled at the University of Minnesota, though its own chronology gives that title to Mohan Singh Sekhon, who arrived in 1931. The university was the door for decades: 94 Indian students in 1944 and 1,607 in 1964, by MNopedia's count. The 1965 immigration law ended national-origin quotas, and after the Immigration Act of 1990 created the H-1B visa, software work tied to Y2K brought "several hundreds" of H-1B holders to Minnesota firms, some of whom later became permanent residents ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/ganges-ten-thousand-lakes-immigration-india-minnesota)). Zafar Siddiqui, an information technology specialist and advocate, told Sahan Journal he came from Hyderabad in 1991 for a master's degree and later moved to Minnesota for an IBM job ([Sahan Journal, Sept. 25, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-indian-asian-minnesota-us-census-data-income-poverty/)). Not everyone came straight from India: the India Association's 2024 oral history project covers Guyanese East Indians of Minnesota ([IAM Oral History](https://iamn.org/iam-oral-history/)).

**Languages and regions.** Compass reports 68.7 percent of those 5 and older speak a language other than English at home and 11.7 percent speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/indian)). CBS reported in 2022 that Hindu Society of Minnesota members alone speak 24 languages and 1,700 dialects ([WCCO, Nov. 2, 2022](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/hindu-society-of-minnesota-stresses-past-and-future-at-one-of-largest-u-s-temples/)). There is no single Indian Minnesotan experience, and the community's own structure shows it. The India Association's partner list includes separate groups for Bengali, Gujarati, Malayalee, Marathi, Punjabi, Odia, Kannada, Tamil and Telugu speakers, a Telangana association, and a Bihar and Jharkhand group ([IAM partners, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://iamn.org/partners/)). SEWA-AIFW, the main culturally specific service group, cited a 2015 to 2016 state education report showing 8,173 Minnesota students spoke a South Asian language at home, 97 percent of them in the seven-county metro ([SEWA-AIFW letter to the Minnesota House, March 21, 2022](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/CDQo7-e4AU_2I5wkRAQ6bA.pdf)).

**Neighbors in the wider South Asian picture.** The state demographer's 2024 chart shows smaller Pakistani (3,996), Bangladeshi (3,816), Nepalese (3,675) and Sri Lankan (1,527) groups, each its own community ([Brower, June 2026](https://mn.gov/capm/assets/SusanBrower_StateOfAsianPacificMinnesotans_June2026_tcm1051-759113.pdf)). SEWA-AIFW's 2022 letter gives "local estimates" of 80,000 to 92,000 South Asians in Minnesota. That is the organization's own estimate, well above the survey counts, and the letter does not show how it was reached ([SEWA-AIFW letter](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/CDQo7-e4AU_2I5wkRAQ6bA.pdf)).

## 2. All walks of life

The averages here are high, and they hide a lot. A reader should hold both of these truths at once.

**The state's most formally educated group, with high earnings.** Among Asian Indian adults ages 25 to 64, 84 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more, the highest share of any cultural group the state tracks. Median earnings for full-time workers are $106,200, give or take $7,200, against $61,900 for all Minnesotans, and median household income is $130,200, give or take $9,000, against $82,700 (2023 dollars, 2019 to 2023) ([Economic Status 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). Compass, in 2024 dollars for 2020 to 2024, gives median household income of $133,600, give or take $9,635 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/indian)).

**Wealth and prominence.** One documented story of a fortune built here is Mahendra Nath's. Twin Cities Business's 2002 Hall of Fame entry says an immigrant arrived "with $800," opened a shop, and three decades later held 138 quick-service eateries, two hotels and over 1,000 rental units ([Twin Cities Business, 2002](https://tcbmag.com/2002-minnesota-business-hall-of-fame/)). MNopedia names him among the community's business figures ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/ganges-ten-thousand-lakes-immigration-india-minnesota)). Normandale Community College's donor page says he was born and raised in India, and that he and his wife built Nath Companies, a family-owned Bloomington business he leads, from the ground up ([Normandale Foundation, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.normandale.edu/why-normandale/foundation/donor-profiles.html)). The firm says it has been hands-on since 1977 and shows hotels, apartments and a shopping mall on its site ([Nath Companies, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.nathcompanies.com/)). In public life, Neel Kashkari leads the Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis ([Minneapolis Fed, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/people/neel-kashkari)), and Srilata Zaheer led the Carlson School of Management from 2012 to 2023 and chaired the Minneapolis Fed's board from 2020 to 2023 ([Carlson School, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://carlsonschool.umn.edu/faculty/sri-zaheer)). MNopedia also names 3M scientists Sam and Sumita Mitra and Mayo Clinic physician Amit Sood ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/ganges-ten-thousand-lakes-immigration-india-minnesota)).

**Salaried and middle-income households.** About nine in ten employed Asian Indian Minnesotans work full time, the highest share of any group, and 8,700 of 17,200 households have two or more earners while 7,800 have one ([Economic Status 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). Home ownership is lower than the state's, not higher: 10,700 of 17,900 households own, about 60 percent, against about 72 percent statewide (same report). Compass puts ownership at 61.8 percent, give or take 4.6 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/indian)). How much of the gap comes from recent arrival, age or visa status is a fair question; no source I could open measured it.

**Family businesses and the work behind them.** Not every story here runs through a corporate office. Sachin Patel, a Twin Cities physician and writer, described growing up in Shakopee in the 1990s in "a closely knit community of Indian American operators" of small motels around the Twin Cities, where his parents, his sister and he lived on site and cleaned rooms. He wrote that many guests were sent by county social service agencies, and that South Asian owners' motels are often small and in places few people want to live. That is one man's account in an opinion column, not a count of motel owners ([Carleton College reprint of his Star Tribune column, April 6, 2026](https://www.carleton.edu/news/stories/sachin-patel-00-publishes-opinion-piece-in-star-tribune/)). Restaurant families show the same pattern of long hours and slow growth. KROC News reported that brothers Tirlochn and Paramjit Mand came from India in 1992, followed an older brother into the restaurant trade, opened India Palace in Eden Prairie in 2003, and opened Ityhaas in Rochester on January 7, 2026 ([KROC News, Jan. 8, 2026](https://krocnews.com/new-indian-restaurant-rochester-barlow-plaza/)). Sasi Nimmigadda said he started Hyderabad Indian Grill because no restaurant near him served the biryani he missed; Volume One reported in June 2026 that the chain has seven Minnesota locations ([Volume One, June 9, 2026](https://volumeone.org/articles/2026/06/09/392141-hyderabad-indian-grill-aims-for-august-opening-in-eau-claire-first-wisconsin-location)). Jyotiee Kistner, a former software designer from Pune, sold street food from a farmers market tent in 2018 and a food truck in 2021 before opening Muddy Tiger in Edina in 2023 ([Edina Magazine, August 2023](https://edinamag.com/muddy-tiger-roars-onto-the-restaurant-scene/)). Three families opened Fresh India in Woodbury in 2021 as what Woodbury Magazine called a women- and minority-owned grocery ([Woodbury Magazine, April 2021](https://woodburymag.com/?p=3008)).

**People in poverty and close to it.** The chartbook counts about 3,000 people below the poverty line, give or take 1,200, and another 1,500, give or take 700, just above it, out of 43,500. About 500 children are in poverty, give or take 500, so that number is very uncertain. About 1,400 households have no vehicle, and about 700 have no earner ([Economic Status 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). Compass reports poverty at 5.5 percent, give or take 2.1 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/indian)). SEWA-AIFW told the Minnesota House in 2022 that it served 796 seniors across the metro in 2019 to 2020 through a program for low-income, vulnerable elders, helping with food support, rides and language ([SEWA-AIFW letter](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/CDQo7-e4AU_2I5wkRAQ6bA.pdf)). The same letter's South Asian seniors are not all from India. The organization's founder, Raj Chaudhary, has said the work began with domestic violence ([American Immigration Council, Feb. 28, 2022](https://www.americanimmigrationcouncil.org/story/indian-researcher-starts-non-profit-to-serve-south-asian-community-in-minneapolis/)).

**A gap worth asking about.** Among Asian Indian women ages 16 to 64, 11,000 of 15,100 are in the labor force. Among men, it is 16,000 of 17,400 ([Economic Status 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). The report does not explain this. SEWA-AIFW notes that immigration status in the community runs "from spousal visas to U.S. Citizenship" ([SEWA-AIFW letter](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/CDQo7-e4AU_2I5wkRAQ6bA.pdf)), which raises a question about work permission and choice that the numbers alone cannot answer.

**What pride and obligation sound like.** Tanwi Prigge, then India Association president, told Sahan Journal in 2023 that "We work very hard," and also said the community has gained "on the backs of Black Americans" through civil rights victories, and that it owes support to underserved communities ([Sahan Journal, Sept. 25, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-indian-asian-minnesota-us-census-data-income-poverty/)). That is one leader's view, not a survey.

## 3. Work and the economy

**Who works.** Labor force participation for ages 16 to 64 is 83 percent, against 82 percent statewide, and about 800 of 27,000 in the labor force were unemployed in 2019 to 2023. Compass reports 83.3 percent of adults 18 to 64 employed ([Economic Status 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf); [Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/indian)).

**H-1B workers and the employers who need them.** Sahan Journal reported that the Census Bureau estimated about 32,000 Minnesota residents were born in India in 2023, and that the bureau does not track how many came on H-1B visas. Nationally, Indian nationals have been over 70 percent of H-1B recipients over the past decade, by federal data the article cites. Among the state's largest H-1B employers it named Target, Best Buy, the University of Minnesota, Mayo Clinic and U.S. Bank, which received between 128 and 598 approvals that year. It also found schools relying on them: Superintendent Brenda Lewis of Fridley Public Schools said the district had about 70 H-1B teachers, mostly in special education. The article does not say where those teachers are from ([Sahan Journal, Oct. 23, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=76030)). See section 13 for what has changed.

**Business ownership.** MNopedia gives an estimate of 2,404 Indian American businesses with about $740 million in combined sales, $140 million in annual payroll and about 4,000 employees. It does not give the year of that estimate, so it should be treated as dated ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/ganges-ten-thousand-lakes-immigration-india-minnesota)).

**An organized business community.** The India Chamber of Commerce MN tells would-be sponsors that they will be "at the heart of Minnesota's Asian Indian business ecosystem." Its board lists founders who lead Sambatek, a Minnetonka engineering firm; Vajraasys; and US Dairy and Foods, along with executives at Medtronic, Solventum and Sleep Number ([ICOCMN board, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.icocmn.org/ourboard); [sponsorship page](https://www.icocmn.org/corporatepartnership)). The University of Minnesota's supplier directory lists Sambatek as a minority-owned, Asian-owned civil engineering, surveying and planning firm ([UMN Office for Supplier Diversity, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://osd.umn.edu/business/sambatek)). The chamber's Economic Forum on October 22, 2026, in Plymouth featured Dr. Prathibha Varkey, president of Mayo Clinic Health System, who the chamber says began her training at Christian Medical College in Vellore, India ([Economic Forum 2026](https://www.icocmn.org/economic-forum-2026)). Its first Pinnacle Gala was held March 20, 2026, in Minneapolis ([Pinnacle Gala 2026](https://www.icocmn.org/pinnaclegala2026)).

**Work outside the professions.** No Minnesota source found counts Indian Minnesotans in service, retail, trucking, warehouse or hotel jobs, and no source on unions was found. What exists is closer to the ground. Patel's column describes family-run motels where parents and children did the cleaning, and says that during the federal immigration operation of the winter of 2025 to 2026 some owners helped their Latino housekeepers get safely to and from work ([Carleton College reprint, April 6, 2026](https://www.carleton.edu/news/stories/sachin-patel-00-publishes-opinion-piece-in-star-tribune/)). Restaurant openings and expansions, such as Ityhaas in Rochester and the Hyderabad Indian Grill chain, mean kitchen and counter jobs, though the sources do not say who fills them ([KROC News](https://krocnews.com/new-indian-restaurant-rochester-barlow-plaza/); [Volume One](https://volumeone.org/articles/2026/06/09/392141-hyderabad-indian-grill-aims-for-august-opening-in-eau-claire-first-wisconsin-location)). The chartbook's figures on households without a vehicle or an earner, in section 2, are another reminder that the high averages do not describe everyone.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

**A chamber of commerce.** The India Chamber of Commerce MN, on Plymouth Boulevard, listed its Economic Forum for October 22, 2026, at the Plymouth Community Center. Its site also lists a start-up pitch competition, a women's leadership program, a mentorship program and a gala ([India Chamber of Commerce MN, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.icocmn.org/)).

**No single main street, but clusters.** No source describes a named "Little India" district. Directory listings cluster along Central Avenue Northeast and Hilltop in the north metro, Prairie Center Drive and Joiner Way in Eden Prairie, Plymouth Boulevard, and Granada Avenue in Apple Valley ([LocalFiles grocers](https://localfiles.com/indian/minneapolis/yellow_pages/grocers/list_1.htm); [LocalFiles restaurants](https://localfiles.com/indian/minneapolis/yellow_pages/restaurants/list_1.htm)). LocalFiles, a long-running directory for Indian readers, is the closest thing to a community yellow pages, listing 72 restaurants and 29 grocers. It shows no date for its listings, so a listing there does not prove a place is open today.

**Groceries confirmed with a current sign.** India Bazaar (Plymouth and Eagan) advertised a Diwali sale running October 22 to November 8, 2026, and says it has served the Twin Cities since 2013 ([LocalFiles](https://localfiles.com/indian/minneapolis/); [India Bazaar](https://www.indiabazaarmn.com/)). Pooja Grocers advertised a Diwali sale for October 16 to November 9, 2026. MNopedia dates it to 2009 at another address on 45th Avenue Northeast, and the directory now lists it on Central Avenue Northeast ([LocalFiles](https://localfiles.com/indian/minneapolis/); [MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/ganges-ten-thousand-lakes-immigration-india-minnesota)). A local food writer's posts confirm India Market in Eden Prairie in February 2025 and the new Spice Village restaurant and grocery in Apple Valley, opened summer 2025 ([My Annoying Opinions, South Asian markets](https://myannoyingopinions.com/tag/market-south-asian/)).

Fresh India in Woodbury, a vegetarian grocery and eatery, catered the Hindu Society of Minnesota's Diwali Mela on October 18, 2025 ([Hindu Society Diwali page](https://www.hindutemplemn.org/diwali); [Eventbrite](https://www.eventbrite.com/e/diwali-tickets-1704302818609)). Meet Minneapolis, the city's tourism bureau, listed India Bazaar and Little India International Market on Central Avenue Northeast on a Diwali page last updated October 12, 2026 ([Meet Minneapolis, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.minneapolis.org/calendar/holidays/diwali/)). A Texas grocery chain, Desi Brothers, won Maple Grove's approval for a store in 2021, but its website now describes only Texas delivery, so this page does not count it as open here ([CCX Media, June 22, 2021](https://ccxmedia.org/news/maple-grove-city-council-approves-specialty-indian-grocery-store); [Desi Brothers](https://desibrothers.com/)).

**Restaurants.** Godavari in Eden Prairie was serving as of a post dated October 22, 2026, and the same writer calls it a favorite in the metro, a personal view ([My Annoying Opinions](https://myannoyingopinions.com/tag/twin-cities-indian/); [Aug. 20, 2025](https://myannoyingopinions.com/2025/08/20/godavari-summer-2025/)). MNopedia calls Tandoor, opened in Northeast Minneapolis in 1982, the longest-running Indian restaurant in the state ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/ganges-ten-thousand-lakes-immigration-india-minnesota)); no current page for it was found. Several restaurants show 2026 signs of life. Raag on 50th, at 50th and France, posted a Diwali buffet and bazaar for October 24 and 25, 2026 ([Raag on 50th, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.raagon50th.com/)). Hyderabad Indian Grill has seven Minnesota locations, and its owner also runs Dosa South Indian Grill in Bloomington ([Volume One, June 9, 2026](https://volumeone.org/articles/2026/06/09/392141-hyderabad-indian-grill-aims-for-august-opening-in-eau-claire-first-wisconsin-location)). Pizza Karma, with three locations, and Bombay Pizza Kitchen in Plymouth and Eden Prairie blend Indian flavors into pizza ([Pizza Karma](https://www.pizzakarma.com/); [Bombay Pizza Kitchen](https://bombaypizza.kitchen/contact/)). Meet Minneapolis's 2026 Diwali page lists Curry Corner, Dancing Ganesha, Darbar India Grill, Gandhi Mahal, India Palace Uptown, Namaste Cafe and Spice and Tonic in Minneapolis ([Meet Minneapolis](https://www.minneapolis.org/calendar/holidays/diwali/)). In Rochester, Ityhaas opened in January 2026 ([KROC News](https://krocnews.com/new-indian-restaurant-rochester-barlow-plaza/)). Not every listing is current: Malabari Kitchen's own site still says it closed because of COVID-19 ([Malabari Kitchen](https://www.malabarikitchen.com/)). Of 94 places in the separate business list, 31 show a current sign of operation and 63 could not be confirmed; most of the unconfirmed are restaurants and groceries known only from an undated directory.

**Professional firms.** LocalFiles lists realtors, a mortgage broker, an insurance and retirement planner, a counseling practice, photographers, salons, threading bars, makeup and henna artists, boutiques and home caterers, most without dates ([LocalFiles](https://localfiles.com/indian/minneapolis/)). Beyond the chamber's leadership and Sambatek (section 3), no Indian-owned law, accounting, medical, dental, travel or media firm could be confirmed from a page that ties it to the community.

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

No Minnesota count of Indian residents by faith was found. As national background only, a Pew Research Center survey of 7,006 Asian American adults, taken July 2022 to January 2023 in six languages including Hindi, found Indian Americans nationwide to be 48 percent Hindu, 15 percent Christian, 8 percent Muslim and 8 percent Sikh. It does not describe Minnesota, and the local mix may differ ([Pew Research Center, Oct. 11, 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2023/10/11/religion-among-asian-americans/)). MNopedia names Hindu, Sikh, Muslim, Jain and Christian institutions side by side ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/ganges-ten-thousand-lakes-immigration-india-minnesota)). The India Association calls itself non-religious ([IAM, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://iamn.org/about/)). No Minnesota source documents Indian Minnesotans of no faith, though Pew found 32 percent of Asian Americans nationally were religiously unaffiliated, up from 26 percent in 2012 ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2023/10/11/religion-among-asian-americans/)).

**Hindu.** The Hindu Society of Minnesota's Hindu Temple of Minnesota, at 10530 Temple Drive in Maple Grove, is the largest. Local Hindus bought a former Episcopal church in 1978. The Maple Grove temple, 43,000 square feet on 80 acres, began construction in 2003, came into use in 2006 and had its grand opening in June 2009, when MPR News reported its cost at more than $6 million. Then-president Mythili Chari told MPR the trustees chose to include as many strains of worship as possible, calling it "a microcosm of India, itself" ([MPR News, June 26, 2009](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2009/06/26/minnesotas-largest-hindu-temple-gets-grand-ceremony)). MPR also reported that in 2006 two young men vandalized the shrines and later did community service at the temple and joined its community. CBS reported 60 festivals a year and members from India, Bhutan and Nepal, some driving hours through several states ([WCCO, Nov. 2, 2022](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/hindu-society-of-minnesota-stresses-past-and-future-at-one-of-largest-u-s-temples/)). The temple's site now lists 21 deities and programs including a temple school, a youth group and senior services ([Hindu Society of Minnesota, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.hindutemplemn.org/about)). Other Hindu places named in the sources include S.V. Temple on Metro Boulevard in Edina, where SEWA holds a clinic ([SEWA-AIFW](https://www.sewa-aifw.org/)), Geeta Ashram ([Sulekha](https://events.sulekha.com/geeta-ashram-diwali-celebration-2026_event-in_eden-prairie-mn_402138)), Milan Mandir in Farmington ([LocalFiles events](http://www.localfiles.com/indian/minneapolis/classifieds/events/list_1.htm)), ISKCON Minnesota, whose site shows daily programs and a Sunday feast ([ISKCON Minnesota, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://iskconminnesota.org/)), BAPS Shri Swaminarayan Mandir in Brooklyn Center and Sri Saibaba Mandir near Chaska ([HinduTone, Oct. 5, 2026](https://hindutone.com/festivals/dussehra-2026-minneapolis/)). Rochester has the Hindu Samaj Temple ([KAAL](https://www.kaaltv.com/news/diwali/)). The Science of Spirituality center meets partly at the Maple Grove temple ([SOS Minnesota, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.sosmn.org/)).

**Sikh.** The Sikh Society of Minnesota worships at 9000 West Bloomington Freeway ([SEWA-AIFW](https://www.sewa-aifw.org/)) and describes itself as the only gurdwara in the state ([Sikh Society of Minnesota, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnsikhs.com/)). A 2010 report said it paid $600,000 for an 8,000-square-foot former church after outgrowing a former McDonald's, with nearly 300 followers at that time ([American Turban, Dec. 2, 2010](https://americanturban.com/2010/12/02/minnesotas-sikhs-have-a-new-home/)). Services are Friday evenings and Sunday mornings, followed by langar, the shared meal, and guests are welcome with heads covered and shoes off ([Sikh Society visitors page](https://www.mnsikhs.com/visitors.html)). It runs a Punjabi school and a medical clinic ([Sikh Society](https://mnsikhs.com/)). The society describes the gurdwara as open to people "from all faiths, backgrounds, and walks of life," and is run by an 11-member elected board; Manjeet Singh is listed as president ([Sikh Society, about, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://mnsikhs.com/about.html)). Its 2026 calendar lists an evening program each month for Sangrand, the start of the month in the Punjabi calendar, plus Gurpurabs and Bandi Chor Divas ([Sikh Society events](https://www.mnsikhs.com/events.html)).

**Muslim.** Indian Muslims are present but thinly documented as such. A 2010 AMPERS radio segment said most of Minnesota's roughly 150,000 Muslims come from African and Arab countries and the Indian subcontinent; that estimate is old and does not separate India from Pakistan or Bangladesh ([AMPERS MN90, Oct. 27, 2010](https://ampers.org/mn90-minnesotas-muslims-celebrating-together/)). MNopedia lists an Islamic Center among the institutions Indian Minnesotans have built or joined ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/ganges-ten-thousand-lakes-immigration-india-minnesota)). The Islamic Center of Minnesota in Fridley, started by Muslim students in 1969, runs Al-Amal School ([GiveMN, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Islamic-Center-Of-Minnesota)). The Northwest Islamic Community Center in Plymouth drew an estimated 3,000 people for Eid prayers on April 21, 2023 ([CCX Media, April 21, 2023](https://ccxmedia.org/news/muslims-celebrate-eid-in-plymouth-building-renovation-planned/)). Neither source says how many worshippers have Indian roots, and no Minnesota mosque was found that a source describes as Indian-rooted. Minnesota's large Eid gatherings, such as at the Minneapolis Convention Center in June 2023, are shared by many communities ([WCCO, June 28, 2023](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/minnesota/news/tens-of-thousands-of-muslims-celebrate-eid-al-adha-at-minneapolis-convention-center/)).

**Jain.** The Jain Center of Minnesota worships at a Jain Mandir inside the Hindu Temple of Minnesota campus and calls itself more than 100 families. Its site lists a Paathshaala and a sports club, and its proposed 2026 schedule includes both Paryushan and Das Lakshan ([Jain Center of Minnesota, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://jaincentermn.org/); [2026 events](https://jaincentermn.org/2026-events)).

**Christian.** Kerala's ancient Christian traditions have at least three Minnesota congregations. St. Alphonsa Syro-Malabar Catholic Church was made a mission parish of the Chicago-based St. Thomas Syro-Malabar Diocese in November 2009, and on July 27, 2024, rededicated its own church building at 651 Virginia Street in St. Paul; it holds Sunday Mass at 11 a.m. and runs a Sunday school, choir, youth group and cultural academy ([Archdiocese of Saint Paul and Minneapolis, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.archspm.org/location/st-alphonsa-syro-malabar-catholic-church-eastern-rite); [St. Alphonsa, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://stalphonsamn.org/)). An older directory still shows it at the Church of St. Mary ([GCatholic, updated Dec. 21, 2025](https://gcatholic.org/churches/usa-08/16973)). A second Syro-Malabar mission, dating to 1999, meets at the Church of St. Augustine in St. Cloud ([GCatholic](https://gcatholic.org/churches/usa-08/16972)). St. Gregorios Malankara Orthodox Church, at 1010 East 58th Street in south Minneapolis, was declared a parish of its church's Diocese of South-West America effective June 29, 2020, and runs a Saturday Sunday school ([St. Gregorios, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.sgocminnesota.org/); [about](https://www.sgocminnesota.org/about)). The parish's own history page recounts how Kerala's Syrian Christians split over centuries into Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant branches, which helps explain why Minnesota has several separate congregations. The Mar Thoma Church's North American diocese lists a Minneapolis congregation served by an assistant vicar of the Chicago parish, and announced a bishop's visit to it on June 6, 2026 ([Mar Thoma Diocese, clergy and parishes](https://marthomana.org/clergy-and-parishes/); [episcopal visit](https://marthomana.org/event-item/episcopal-visit-minneapolis-mtc/)). No Telugu, Tamil or Pentecostal Indian congregation in Minnesota could be confirmed.

**Older roots.** Paramahansa Yogananda lectured in Minnesota in the 1920s, and MNopedia links a 1927 Minneapolis visit to a Self-Realization Fellowship temple ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/ganges-ten-thousand-lakes-immigration-india-minnesota)).

**Differences inside, including caste.** Region, language and faith divide this community in ways visible in its many associations. Caste is harder to see, and Minnesota evidence is thin. What exists comes from the University of Minnesota. Its policy library says caste discrimination is prohibited as part of its ban on discrimination by race, national origin and religion, and defines castes as "rigidly hierarchical social classes" assigned at birth, found "across South Asian countries and religions" ([University of Minnesota policy FAQ, printed Oct. 29, 2026](https://policy.umn.edu/opportunity/discrimination-faq07)). In September 2022 the Humphrey School of Public Affairs added caste to its diversity criteria, the fourth university unit to do so after the Department of Gender, Women and Sexuality Studies, the English department's creative writing program and a College of Biological Sciences graduate program. The step was welcomed by Equality Labs, a Dalit civil rights group, which quoted an unnamed graduate student who described facing caste oppression ([The News Minute, Sept. 7, 2022](https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/first-public-affairs-school-us-adds-caste-its-non-discrimination-policy-167625); [American Bazaar, Sept. 7, 2022](https://americanbazaaronline.com/2022/09/07/minnesota-public-affairs-school-adds-caste-to-non-discrimination-policy-450862/)). These sources describe university policy and one advocacy group's account. They do not measure how often caste discrimination happens in Minnesota, and no Minnesota source was found giving the views of Indian Minnesotans who disagree with such policies. This page draws no conclusion about any person's caste or about how caste shapes life here.

## 7. Family, home and daily life

The chartbook counts 17,900 households: 4,600 of one person, 8,800 of two or three, and 4,600 of four or more. About 7,800 include children ([Economic Status 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). Compass gives an average household size of 2.55 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/indian)). About 1,800 people in the chartbook are 65 or older, a small share. SEWA-AIFW holds a Monday senior gathering for South Asian elders, with yoga, lunch and chai ([SEWA-AIFW](https://www.sewa-aifw.org/)). Its letter says the senior program aims to let elders communicate in their preferred language, and describes trips to Asian grocery stores so elders can get familiar foods ([SEWA-AIFW letter](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/CDQo7-e4AU_2I5wkRAQ6bA.pdf)).

**Food and dress.** At festivals described by Sahan Journal, people eat dosas, pani puri and dhokla, sip mango lassi, and learn to drape a saree ([Sahan Journal, Oct. 21, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=75984); [Aug. 11, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/india-fest-saturday-st-paul-minnesota-state-capitol-grounds/)). Home food businesses and saree boutiques also appear in the LocalFiles directory ([LocalFiles](https://localfiles.com/indian/minneapolis/)).

**Marriage and family difficulty.** MNopedia records that the first Hindu wedding here, in 1961, officiated by Padmakar K. Dixit, needed a special act of the Legislature ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/ganges-ten-thousand-lakes-immigration-india-minnesota)). SEWA's letter says family values are "being challenged related to the concept of 'shame,'" and that language is part of why survivors of domestic violence need a safe place. That is the organization's account of its work, not a measure of how common violence is ([SEWA-AIFW letter](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/CDQo7-e4AU_2I5wkRAQ6bA.pdf)). No Minnesota source was found on arranged marriage, intermarriage, or LGBTQ life in this community.

## 9. Civic and political life

**Elected service.** Satveer Chaudhary of Fridley, elected to the Minnesota House in 1996 and later the Senate, served until 2010 and is recorded as the first known Asian American member of the Legislature ([Legislative Reference Library, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/legdb/fulldetail?id=10096)). No current state legislator or statewide official of Indian descent was found. At the city level, PG Narayanan, a past president of the India Association of Minnesota, was appointed to the Eden Prairie City Council in 2018, elected in 2020, and reelected in November 2024; he was sworn in for a term running through 2028. Eden Prairie Local News described him in 2024 as the council's first Asian American member ([Eden Prairie Local News, July 29, 2024](https://eplocalnews.org/2024/07/29/narayanan-announces-reelection-campaign-bid-for-city-council/); [Jan. 8, 2025](https://www.eplocalnews.org/2025/01/08/narayanan-toomey-sworn-in-for-new-terms-on-the-eden-prairie-city-council)). City council and school board seats across the state were not surveyed, so others may serve.

**Government service.** Gopal Khanna became Minnesota's first chief information officer in 2005 and left in December 2010 ([MPR News, Sept. 14, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/09/14/states-top-it-official-stepping-down)). Governor Tim Walz proclaimed August 15, 2023, "India Day," saying Indian Minnesotans work as educators, business owners, health care providers, technology entrepreneurs and artists ([Siasat, Aug. 16, 2023](https://www.siasat.com/minnesota-governor-proclaims-august-15-as-india-day-2668471/)).

**Both parties.** MNopedia records an Asian Indian American Republicans of Minnesota group formed in 1992 and a Minnesota Asian Indian Democratic Association formed in 1994 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/ganges-ten-thousand-lakes-immigration-india-minnesota)). Neither could be confirmed as active today. The India Chamber invited members to a "Chai, Politics & Conversations" evening with Congressman Tom Emmer in Eden Prairie on November 23, 2026; its notice names trade with India and work visa policy among the issues at stake for business owners ([India Chamber, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.icocmn.org/event-details/chai-politics-conversations-with-tom-emmer)). Its March 2026 gala featured speakers from both parties and the governor's office ([Pinnacle Gala 2026](https://www.icocmn.org/pinnaclegala2026)).

**Organizations.** The India Association, founded 1973, calls itself non-political and non-partisan, and works as an umbrella for regional groups. Its current president is Mangala Acharya ([IAM](https://iamn.org/about/)). Immigration and health equity are among the issues its government relations committee lists ([IAM government relations](https://iamn.org/initiatives/government-relations/)). It started South Asians Against Racial Injustice with partner groups, and that page lists Black History Month, Juneteenth and other observances as focus areas ([SAARI](https://iamn.org/initiatives/saari/)). It has raised money for disaster relief, by its count $275,000 after the 2001 Gujarat earthquake and over $180,000 during COVID ([IAM](https://iamn.org/about/)). Its annual meeting is November 15, 2026, in South St. Paul, and it is taking 2027 board applications ([IAM home](https://iamn.org/)).

**Debates inside.** Little open debate is documented. One leader's comments stand out: Prigge spoke in 2023 of an obligation to other communities ([Sahan Journal, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/hmong-indian-asian-minnesota-us-census-data-income-poverty/)), and said she thinks Indian American culture "now has become Bollywood" ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 11, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/india-fest-saturday-st-paul-minnesota-state-capitol-grounds/)). Patel's 2026 column is itself a challenge from inside the community: he wrote that his family's motel community had stayed "largely disengaged from civic participation" and silent while others were targeted, until enforcement reached South Asians too ([Carleton College reprint, April 6, 2026](https://www.carleton.edu/news/stories/sachin-patel-00-publishes-opinion-piece-in-star-tribune/)). That is one writer's view. On caste, see section 5. No Minnesota reporting was found on arguments among residents over Indian politics or Hindu nationalism, though these may exist.

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Coverage.** Compass reports 2.6 percent without health insurance, give or take 1.1 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/indian)).

**A Minnesota community survey.** SEWA-AIFW and the University of Minnesota's Center for Applied Research and Educational Improvement surveyed over 1,154 self-identified South Asian adults in Minnesota, recruited through personal networks rather than at random, in a report dated February 2014. It reported diabetes at 12 percent against 7 percent in the general population, and fewer wellness checks. Sixteen percent were dissatisfied that providers did not understand South Asian diet, genetic tendencies, family support or religion ([Project SAHAT, SEWA-AIFW](https://www.sewa-aifw.org/project-sahat-download)). It is now over a decade old, covers more than Indians, and does not represent everyone. SEWA's 2022 letter also mentions a later survey of 1,000 South Asians with Bethel students, but its results were not found ([SEWA-AIFW letter](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/CDQo7-e4AU_2I5wkRAQ6bA.pdf)).

**Community care.** SEWA-AIFW, in Brooklyn Center, holds free monthly clinics at S.V. Temple in Edina, the Sikh Society in Bloomington and the Hindu temple in Maple Grove, and keeps a 24-hour crisis line at (952) 912-9100 ([SEWA-AIFW](https://www.sewa-aifw.org/)). Its founder said in 2022 there is a strong need for culturally specific mental health and children's services, and that city materials are translated into Spanish, Vietnamese and Chinese but not South Asian languages ([American Immigration Council](https://www.americanimmigrationcouncil.org/story/indian-researcher-starts-non-profit-to-serve-south-asian-community-in-minneapolis/)). Its research page describes a pilot on adverse childhood experiences without giving results ([SEWA research](https://www.sewa-aifw.org/research)). Its reach figures vary: its 2022 letter says over 23,000 individuals in 2020 to 2021, while the American Immigration Council profile says over 82,000 a year.

**Traditional and modern practices.** The Hindu temple offers yoga and meditation, and Science of Spirituality offers meditation classes ([Hindu Society](https://www.hindutemplemn.org/about); [SOS Minnesota](https://www.sosmn.org/)). LocalFiles lists a counseling practice ([LocalFiles](https://localfiles.com/indian/minneapolis/)). No Minnesota source was found on Ayurveda practitioners or on how families combine it with other care.

## 12. Disability

**What the Minnesota numbers show.** Compass reports that 4.8 percent of Indian Minnesotans for whom disability status was determined have a disability, give or take 1.4 points, out of 51,545 people, and 4.5 percent of those under 65, give or take 1.4 (2020 to 2024) ([Minnesota Compass, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/indian)). The chartbook counts about 1,300 people ages 18 to 64, give or take 500: about 1,000 ages 18 to 44 and 300 ages 45 to 64. That is about 4 percent of the 31,700 in that age range, against about 330,000 of about 3.4 million Minnesotans, or close to 10 percent. The measure is six survey questions about serious difficulty with vision, hearing, walking, thinking, self-care or living independently ([Economic Status 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). These survey estimates are the only state-level disability figures found for Indian residents. No Minnesota Department of Human Services, Department of Education or Department of Health report was found that breaks out Indian residents, so service use and diagnosis rates for Indian families here are unknown.

**Questions a low number raises, none of them settled here.**
- How much of it is age? This is a young group with few elders.
- How much reflects who could migrate? People coming through work and student visas may differ from the wider population in ways these tables cannot show.
- How much is the question itself? The survey asks about "serious difficulty." Would a parent answering for a child, or a person answering in a second language, describe the same thing the same way?
- Who decides what counts? A school, a doctor, a family and a visa form may each draw a different line. What does a label open, such as school services, and what might a family fear it closes?
- What about Medicaid and waiver use? I found no Minnesota DHS or Department of Education figures for Indian residents at all, so I cannot say how many use these services.

**A Minnesota program.** SEWA-AIFW's SAPNA supports neurodivergent children and families with parent groups, social skills groups, sensory-friendly events and academic and behavior help, plus a podcast. Its name is spelled out two ways on the same page: South Asian Parents Network and Advocacy, and South Asian Persons of Neurodiverse Abilities. The page says autism, ADHD and dyslexia "are not simply problems to be fixed," and that neurodivergent people may sometimes be misjudged as lazy or disobedient in South Asian communities. That is an organization's framing, not a study ([SEWA neurodiversity](https://www.sewa-aifw.org/neurodiversity); [SEWA-AIFW](https://www.sewa-aifw.org/)).

**Not from Minnesota.** Ritu Goel, a psychiatrist in Long Beach, California, argued in STAT in June 2026 that stigma is the main barrier to diagnosis for South Asian children, more than language, insurance or geography. This is one clinician's opinion, from elsewhere, and describes no Minnesota family ([STAT, June 15, 2026](https://www.statnews.com/2026/06/15/autism-diagnosis-south-asian-american-families-stigma/)).

**A connection worth noting.** Fridley's superintendent said most of the district's H-1B teachers work in special education ([Sahan Journal, Oct. 23, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=76030)). The article does not say where those teachers are from, so this is a question about how visa rules may reach special education, not a finding about Indian teachers.

## 13. What is changing now

**Visa rules are the main story.** In September 2025 the president ordered a $100,000 payment for new H-1B petitions from abroad; it did not apply to extensions or to people already here changing status. Fridley's superintendent paused hiring a dozen international employees and said, "We can't afford that." Sahan Journal also spoke with an Eagan resident on a student work permit and an Eden Prairie resident already on H-1B, who was not affected ([Sahan Journal, Oct. 23, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=76030)). On June 8, 2026, Judge Leo Sorokin in Boston struck the fee down in a case brought by 20 states, as reported the next day, and the White House said it expected reversal on appeal ([Outlook Business, June 9, 2026](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/trumps-100000-h-1b-fee-ruled-unlawful-heres-what-it-means-for-indian-applicants)). Where that appeal stands today was not confirmed today.

**Green cards.** On October 8, 2026, the White House suspended several tech and outsourcing firms, including Microsoft, Infosys, Tata Consultancy Services and Wipro, from a program that sponsors green cards. Al Jazeera reported that Indian nationals are about 79 percent of the backlog, and that India's foreign ministry called Vice President Vance's "indentured servants" comparison "deeply offensive" ([Al Jazeera, Oct. 9, 2026](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/9/indentured-servants-us-green-card-move-will-hit-thousands-of-it-workers)).

**People leaving.** The American Bazaar, citing Xpheno data through The Economic Times, reports about 12,700 Indian tech workers left the United States by September 2026. This is a national figure from one firm's count ([American Bazaar, Oct. 19, 2026](https://americanbazaaronline.com/2026/10/19/over-12000-indian-tech-professionals-leave-us-as-visa-uncertainty-layoffs-mount-490368/)). I found no Minnesota figure.

**Recent local signs.** Spice Village opened in Apple Valley in summer 2025, and the Tamil Sangam's airport mural went up the same year ([My Annoying Opinions](https://myannoyingopinions.com/tag/twin-cities-indian/); [Minnesota Tamil Sangam](https://www.mntamilsangam.org/)). The India Association lists a "Know Your Rights" resource on its about page, without details ([IAM](https://iamn.org/about/)).

**Immigration enforcement in Minnesota.** Truthout reported that the federal operation called Operation Metro Surge brought some 4,000 ICE and Border Patrol agents to Minnesota from December 2025 through February 2026 ([Truthout, June 27, 2026](https://truthout.org/articles/minnesota-immigrants-are-still-facing-the-fallout-of-trumps-ice-invasion/)). In an April 2026 Star Tribune column, Sachin Patel wrote that agents stayed in Indian American families' motels while targeting motel staff and residents, that some owners who refused agents rooms had franchise licenses revoked and received federal subpoenas, and that "South Asians with H-1B work visas were being searched and detained." These are one writer's statements in an opinion piece; no news report or count of affected Indian residents was found to confirm or measure them ([Carleton College reprint, April 6, 2026](https://www.carleton.edu/news/stories/sachin-patel-00-publishes-opinion-piece-in-star-tribune/)).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

In a few decades this community has built temples, a gurdwara, schools, dance companies and service groups, and many of them carry the work of serving others. Community groups run language schools from Como Park to Hopkins. A temple on former farmland was built to hold many strains of Hindu worship, and it hosts a free monthly health clinic ([MPR News, 2009](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2009/06/26/minnesotas-largest-hindu-temple-gets-grand-ceremony); [SEWA-AIFW](https://www.sewa-aifw.org/)). A Sikh congregation shares its community meal with visitors ([Sikh Society](https://www.mnsikhs.com/visitors.html)). Kerala Christian families built their own church in St. Paul in 2024 and a parish in south Minneapolis, keeping Syro-Malabar and Malankara Orthodox worship ([Archdiocese of Saint Paul and Minneapolis](https://www.archspm.org/location/st-alphonsa-syro-malabar-catholic-church-eastern-rite); [St. Gregorios](https://www.sgocminnesota.org/)). Family restaurants have grown from a farmers market tent to a storefront, and in one case to seven Minnesota locations ([Edina Magazine](https://edinamag.com/muddy-tiger-roars-onto-the-restaurant-scene/); [Volume One](https://volumeone.org/articles/2026/06/09/392141-hyderabad-indian-grill-aims-for-august-opening-in-eau-claire-first-wisconsin-location)). A 1973 cultural club grew into a free festival at the State Capitol ([IAM](https://iamn.org/about/); [IndiaFest](https://iamn.org/event/indiafest-2026/)). Ragamala takes classical dance to Worthington, Brainerd and Austin ([Ragamala](https://www.ragamaladance.org/upcoming)). Sumita Mitra's science at 3M, Kashkari's and Kocherlakota's leadership at the Minneapolis Fed and Dixit's work at the university are threads of a larger contribution in science, medicine, business and public life ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/ganges-ten-thousand-lakes-immigration-india-minnesota)). And the state demographer reports that nearly 30 percent of recent foreign-born labor force growth came from Asian Pacific groups, led by arrivals from India and China ([Brower, June 2026](https://mn.gov/capm/assets/SusanBrower_StateOfAsianPacificMinnesotans_June2026_tcm1051-759113.pdf)).

## What could not be established

Nothing here should be read as saying a thing does not exist because it is not on this page.

- **Businesses.** Of 94 businesses listed, 63 could not be confirmed open. Most of them are restaurants and groceries known only from LocalFiles, a community directory that shows no dates, and from small-business ads on it (salons, threading bars, realtors, photographers, caterers). For many, no business website or dated article could be found; some sites have lapsed or now show unrelated content. Several restaurants are confirmed only by Meet Minneapolis's 2026 Diwali list, not by their own pages. The thinnest types remain law, accounting, medical, dental, tax, insurance, travel and media firms, hotel and motel owners, and salons: none could be tied to the community and confirmed open from a dated page. No Patel Brothers or Desi Brothers store in Minnesota could be confirmed.
- **2026 festival dates not found.** The Hindu Society's Woodbury Diwali Mela (its page still shows 2025), the Gujarati Samaj's 2026 Garba, any 2026 Minnesota Vaisakhi, Holi, Onam or Bonalu event. Kerala's 2026 Thiruvonam date itself is reported differently by different pages.
- **Indian-organized Eid, and mosques.** No Eid event organized by an Indian Minnesotan group, and no mosque that a source describes as Indian-rooted, was found. The Anjuman-e-Asghari Shia community in Brooklyn Park has Khoja roots, but no available page describes its members' origins; Harvard's Pluralism Project and Carleton's Global Religions in Minnesota pages would not open.
- **Christian congregations.** Syro-Malabar, Malankara Orthodox and Mar Thoma congregations were found. Telugu, Tamil, Pentecostal and other Indian Christian congregations could not be confirmed, and no membership counts were found for any of them.
- **Faith counts.** No Minnesota count by faith. Only a national survey is cited, as background.
- **Disability.** No Minnesota Department of Human Services, Department of Education or Minnesota Department of Health figure for Indian residents specifically. The University of Minnesota's autism surveillance site and digital conservancy would not open, and the Zechella and Raval study of Indian immigrant parents could not be opened.
- **Working-class Indian Minnesotans.** No Minnesota count of Indian workers in service, retail, trucking, warehouse or hotel jobs, and nothing on unions. Evidence on family motels comes from one opinion column. Whether undocumented Indian residents live in Minnesota, and how many, was not found in any Minnesota source.
- **Officials.** No current state legislator of Indian descent was found; city councils and school boards statewide were not surveyed. Ashwin Madia, the 2008 DFL nominee for Congress, is left off because no opened page states his family background.
- **Caste.** Only university policy and one advocacy group's account were found. No Minnesota survey or reporting on how caste affects residents, and no Minnesota source giving opposing community views.
- **Immigration enforcement.** No news report or count of Indian residents affected by the 2025 to 2026 federal operation; the only account is an opinion column.
- **Disagreements among counts.** The India Association's page gives 69.8 percent foreign-born and 63.4 percent home ownership for 2019 to 2023 while citing Compass. Compass's newer figures are used here; which earlier figure is right could not be checked. A [Minnesota Compass article of May 30, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-asian-communities) gives a lower ownership rate of 54 percent and no data year, and is not used above.
- **Pages that would not open.** Migration Policy Institute's state profile, the BAPS Minneapolis page, Carleton's Global Religions in Minnesota, Harvard's Pluralism Project, the South Asian American Digital Archive, the Minnesota Malayalee Association, the Council on Asian Pacific Minnesotans news page, Eden Prairie's city council page, and Time and Date's holiday pages. The Telugu Association's site showed only a title, and the Minnesota Punjabi Society's event page was an unreadable image.
