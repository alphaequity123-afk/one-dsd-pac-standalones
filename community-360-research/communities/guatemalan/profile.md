# Guatemalan Minnesotans in Minnesota

## 1. People and place

**How many.** Minnesota Compass, using the American Community Survey (ACS) for 2020 to 2024, counts 17,306 Guatemalan Minnesotans, give or take 2,627. It places 49.1 percent in the Twin Cities and 50.9 percent in Greater Minnesota, each give or take 7.9 points, so the true split could lean either way ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/guatemalan)). The Census Bureau's narrower count of people who name Guatemalan as their Hispanic origin (ACS table B03001) is lower: 13,725, give or take 1,359, for the same 2020 to 2024 period ([Census Reporter, ACS 2020-2024](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=04000US27,050|04000US27)). The single-year 2024 survey puts that same origin count at 16,875, give or take 3,539 ([Census Reporter, ACS 2024 1-year](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_1yr?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=04000US27)). The figures differ because they use different definitions and samples; all are estimates with wide margins. People born in Guatemala and living in Minnesota number about 6,837, give or take 706, in the five-year data, and about 9,348, give or take 2,097, in the 2024 single-year data ([Census Reporter, ACS 2020-2024 table B05006](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US27); [Census Reporter, ACS 2024 1-year table B05006](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US27)). None of these counts separates Maya people from ladino (non-Indigenous, Spanish-speaking) Guatemalans.

**Young, and many born here.** In the Compass profile, 46.1 percent are 17 or younger, 13.9 percent are under 5, and only 6.0 percent are 45 to 64; the 65-and-older share is suppressed because the sample is too small. Foreign-born residents are 43.7 percent, give or take 4.5 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/guatemalan)). In plain terms, a majority of Guatemalan Minnesotans were born in the United States, and the community is one of children, young parents and working-age adults.

**Where.** Census estimates for 2020 to 2024 show the largest Guatemalan-origin counts by county in Hennepin (2,716), Nobles (1,942), Dakota (1,066), Ramsey (955), Kandiyohi (676), Mower (661), Watonwan (559), Anoka (465) and Martin (452), each with a large margin of error. Nobles County stands out: Guatemalans make up about 8.8 percent of its residents, and Watonwan about 5 percent ([Census Reporter, counties](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=04000US27,050|04000US27)). By city, the largest estimates are Worthington (1,714, about 12 percent of the city), Minneapolis (1,077), Willmar (657), Austin (636), St. Paul (610), Fairmont (419) and St. James (338, about 7 percent) ([Census Reporter, places](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=160|04000US27)). Smaller pockets appear in Lake City, Faribault, Evansville and Park Rapids in local reporting (sections 3, 4 and 13).

**Worthington.** The southwest Minnesota meatpacking town is the clearest center of Guatemalan life in the state. A 2019 Workday Magazine report said Latinos were the town's largest ethnic group and that most of them were Indigenous Guatemalans, largely from the San Marcos department, an area hit hard during the civil war ([Workday Magazine, July 9, 2019](https://workdaymagazine.org/on-the-front-lines-of-trumps-immigration-war-in-the-u-s-heartland/)). A December 2024 Minnesota Star Tribune report said many in town have ties to San Marcos and that nearly 40 languages are spoken at home by students ([Star Tribune via Citrus County Chronicle, Dec. 2024](https://www.chronicleonline.com/18-years-ago-federal-agents-raided-this-minnesota-meatpacking-town-residents-fear-round-2/article_a0f1c7bd-8d77-559c-bd72-53481afaa973.html)). At the March 2026 opening of the Nobles County Historical Society's immigration exhibit, board member Jerry Fiola called Worthington the center of Mam speakers in the United States; that is his statement, not a count ([The Globe, March 22, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/nobles-county-historical-society-soft-launches-first-permanent-exhibit)).

**How people came.** Several streams show up in Minnesota sources. Some people fled the 1960 to 1996 civil war. A Worthington man who arrived in 1992 at 16 said he left to escape a violent war ([The Globe, Sept. 30, 2017](https://www.dglobe.com/news/deportation-to-guatemala-looms-as-gabriel-tomas-fate)). Another Worthington resident wrote that he came on a wartime work permit and that his asylum case was closed after the December 1996 peace accord ([The Globe, Nov. 2, 2014](https://www.dglobe.com/news/column-the-experience-of-one-guatemalan-immigrant)). Others came for work after the war: Eugenio Lopez's parents came at 18 and 17 in 1999 as migrant pickers, and JBS drew them to Worthington ([The Globe, Aug. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/eugenio-lopez-passionate-about-local-politics)). More recent arrivals include teenagers who came alone or with siblings, spent time in federal custody and then joined relatives, as Lake City's 2025 English-learner graduates described ([Post Bulletin, May 28, 2025](https://www.postbulletin.com/news/local/from-guatemala-to-lake-city-students-share-stories-of-their-journeys-from-central-america-to-graduation)). St. James oral histories collected from 2017 to 2024 include a Guatemalan woman who arrived around 2005 by way of Houston and a K'iche'-speaking woman who came in 2015 to join her sister ([University of Minnesota Morris, Uniting Cultures collection](https://digitalcommons.morris.umn.edu/unitingcultures/)). A separate group are Guatemalan-born adoptees raised by Minnesota families; a public podcast follows one Tz'utujil Maya adoptee raised in St. Paul and notes that more than 50,000 children were adopted from Guatemala before the country shut down international adoptions in 2008 ([Defining Diego, Apple Podcasts](https://podcasts.apple.com/mn/podcast/defining-diego/id1607121653); [Alexandria Echo Press, 2006](https://www.echopress.com/news/from-guatemala-with-love)).

**Generations and languages.** Among Guatemalan Minnesotans 5 and older, 60.2 percent speak a language other than English at home and 34.3 percent speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/guatemalan)). The Census does not report Mayan languages separately, and none appears among Minnesota's 12 most common home languages for English learners in 2024-25 ([Minnesota Department of Education, English Learners in Minnesota 2024-25](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/other/261381.pdf)). Minnesota sources name Mam in Worthington ([The Globe, March 22, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/nobles-county-historical-society-soft-launches-first-permanent-exhibit)) and among a St. Paul father who could not read the Spanish immigration papers he was given ([West Central Tribune, Aug. 3, 2017](https://www.wctrib.com/news/deportation-expected-for-st-paul-father-of-two-who-fled-guatemala-as-a-teen)), and K'iche' in Faribault and St. James ([Faribault Daily News, 2022](https://www.southernminn.com/faribault_daily_news/news/siblings-open-guatemalan-grocery-clothing-store/article_b7c65650-fc9a-11ec-91f6-0beede47c292.html); [UMN Morris](https://digitalcommons.morris.umn.edu/unitingcultures/)). Second-generation young people often interpret for parents. "We start interpreting for our parents at a very, very young age," Eugenio Lopez told The Globe ([The Globe, Aug. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/eugenio-lopez-passionate-about-local-politics)).

> **Content note:** This passage describes the genocide against Maya communities and the deaths and disappearances of Guatemala's civil war. Readers may pause or skip to section 2, All walks of life.

**Background, labeled as such.** Nationally, Pew counted about 1.8 million people of Guatemalan origin in 2021, with a median age of 26.6 ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/us-hispanics-facts-on-guatemalan-origin-latinos/)). Guatemala's independence from Spain is dated to the Act of Independence of Central America on September 15, 1821 ([Office Holidays](https://www.officeholidays.com/holidays/guatemala/guatemala-independence-day)). The United Nations-backed Historical Clarification Commission, presenting its report in 1999, said testimony from some 9,000 witnesses showed a government policy of genocide against Maya communities ([UN press briefing, March 1, 1999](https://press.un.org/en/1999/19990301.guate.brf.html)), and attributed the clear majority of more than 200,000 deaths and disappearances in the 36-year conflict to the military ([Duke Today, Feb. 2000](https://today.duke.edu/2000/02/guatemala211.html)).

## 2. All walks of life

**Income and poverty.** Compass reports a median household income of $59,000, give or take $9,755, in 2024 dollars, across 3,248 households. Income is spread out: 20.2 percent of households earn under $35,000, while 22.0 percent earn $100,000 or more. Poverty stands at 12.8 percent, and another 12.4 percent live between 100 and 149 percent of the poverty line ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/guatemalan)). Those margins are wide; small shifts in the sample move the numbers.

**Homes.** Just under half of households own their home (47.7 percent), and 52.3 percent rent. Among renters, 49.5 percent are cost-burdened, compared with 20.3 percent of owners; the median gross rent is $1,206 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/guatemalan)). In Worthington, Lucas López and María Ramírez bought their first home a few blocks from St. Mary's Church on his pork plant wages, the Associated Press reported in 2025 ([AP via KSAT, Jan. 17, 2025](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2025/01/17/in-guatemala-and-minnesota-holy-feast-brings-migrant-families-hope-and-pride-amid-crackdown-fears/)).

**Education.** Among adults 25 and older, 38.4 percent did not finish high school, 24.3 percent have a diploma or GED, 22.1 percent have some college or an associate degree, and 15.1 percent hold a bachelor's degree or higher ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/guatemalan)). Lake City graduates explained that public school in rural Guatemala often ends at sixth grade, when many young people must start earning for their families ([Post Bulletin, May 28, 2025](https://www.postbulletin.com/news/local/from-guatemala-to-lake-city-students-share-stories-of-their-journeys-from-central-america-to-graduation)).

**Business owners and professionals.** Minnesota reporting shows owners and professionals as well as plant workers. In Worthington, Guatemalan-born Ana Cuadros owns Panaderia Mi Tierra, which she bought in 2022 after working at the store since 2006 ([The Globe, July 15, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/american-dream-from-factory-worker-to-owner-of-panaderia-mi-tierra)). Neftali Ramirez Lopez and Maynor Lopez Chavez built Tortilleria El Maná into a shop and food trailer ([The Globe, July 22, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/tortilleria-el-mana-goes-mobile-with-food-trailer)). Humberto Chavez Perez, the son of a San Marcos businessman, left JBS in May 2026 to open La Eterna Primavera ([The Globe, May 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/la-eterna-primavera-to-open-friday-in-worthington)). The Quila siblings, who describe themselves as K'iche' Maya, opened Tienda Maya Quiche in Faribault in 2022 ([Faribault Daily News, 2022](https://www.southernminn.com/faribault_daily_news/news/siblings-open-guatemalan-grocery-clothing-store/article_b7c65650-fc9a-11ec-91f6-0beede47c292.html)). Vinicio Perez runs Tienda Chapin 2 in Park Rapids, sister to Tienda Chapin in Willmar ([Park Rapids Enterprise, Oct. 1, 2025](https://www.parkrapidsenterprise.com/business/new-guatemalan-grocery-store-sells-freshly-imported-produce)). Eugenio Lopez, born to Guatemalan parents, works as an executive assistant and payment specialist at an immigration law office and was a 2024 Democratic National Convention delegate ([The Globe, Aug. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/eugenio-lopez-passionate-about-local-politics)). Eligio Ramirez Sandoval, who came in 2007, drives a school bus in Worthington ([The Globe/MPR News, Aug. 31, 2024](https://www.dglobe.com/news/minnesota/how-a-guatemalan-immigrant-and-a-4th-generation-farmer-see-immigration-issues-in-worthington)). In Rochester, three Guatemalan immigrants worked as teachers at a Spanish immersion day care until July 2025 ([The Globe/Post Bulletin, July 2, 2025](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/spanish-immersion-day-care-in-rochester-feel-the-effects-of-trump-immigration-crackdown)). No Minnesota source found names Guatemalan Minnesotans among the state's wealthiest families or senior corporate leaders.

**Hardship.** More than one in four Guatemalan Minnesotans (27.0 percent, give or take 6.7 points) had no health insurance in 2020 to 2024 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/guatemalan)). Worthington's free clinic for the uninsured most often treats hypertension and diabetes ([The Globe, Dec. 13, 2022](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/free-health-clinic-for-uninsured-gets-new-home-help-from-sanford-health-cooperative)). Families also carry obligations abroad: a Worthington choir member told AP that relatives in San Marcos depend on remittances because wages there hover around $6 a day ([AP via KSAT, Jan. 17, 2025](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2025/01/17/in-guatemala-and-minnesota-holy-feast-brings-migrant-families-hope-and-pride-amid-crackdown-fears/)).

## 3. Work and the economy

**Employment.** Compass reports that 77.0 percent of working-age adults are employed: 86.1 percent of men and 66.5 percent of women ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/guatemalan)). Nearly 73 percent of workers drive to work, and 35.3 percent travel 30 minutes or more each way ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/guatemalan)). AP described a Worthington couple who drive 45 minutes each way to a pork plant ([AP via KSAT, Jan. 17, 2025](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2025/01/17/in-guatemala-and-minnesota-holy-feast-brings-migrant-families-hope-and-pride-amid-crackdown-fears/)).

**Meatpacking.** The JBS pork plant, formerly Swift, is Worthington's largest employer. The Minneapolis Fed reported more than 2,200 workers there in 2018, with high turnover, about 15 hires a week and a starting wage of about $15 an hour ([Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis, 2018](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2018/spotlight-on-worthington-a-global-community-in-rural-minnesota)). A 2019 report put the plant at about 1,800 employees ([Workday Magazine, 2019](https://workdaymagazine.org/on-the-front-lines-of-trumps-immigration-war-in-the-u-s-heartland/)). AP reported that Lucas López wakes at 4:20 a.m. daily for his pork plant job ([AP via KSAT](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2025/01/17/in-guatemala-and-minnesota-holy-feast-brings-migrant-families-hope-and-pride-amid-crackdown-fears/)). In Willmar, Jennie-O is among the town's biggest employers ([CBS Minnesota, Jan. 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/willmar-ice-federal-agents-restaurant-workers-detained/)), but no source found breaks down any Minnesota plant's workforce by Guatemalan origin.

**Dairy and hog farms.** A University of Minnesota-led worker health and safety project trained 360 immigrant workers on 19 Minnesota dairy farms; 12 percent came from Guatemala, 74 percent from Mexico, and 42 percent said they had been injured at work at some point ([National Academies presentation, Sept. 29, 2025, citing Vázquez et al., Journal of Dairy Science 2025](https://www.nationalacademies.org/cdn/materials/a002da7b-5ac6-47c5-a2f4-53c10557d2fb)). A Guatemalan man near Evansville milked cows on a family farm from 2013 until his 2017 detention ([Alexandria Echo Press, Oct. 20, 2017](https://www.echopress.com/news/from-guatemala-to-evansville-man-facing-possible-deportation-started-a-family-in-minnesota)). A Worthington man deported in 2017 had worked for years on a local pig farm ([The Globe, Sept. 30, 2017](https://www.dglobe.com/news/deportation-to-guatemala-looms-as-gabriel-tomas-fate)).

**Other work.** Minnesota reporting also shows Guatemalan workers in landscaping in the Twin Cities ([West Central Tribune, Aug. 3, 2017](https://www.wctrib.com/news/deportation-expected-for-st-paul-father-of-two-who-fled-guatemala-as-a-teen)), school bus driving ([The Globe/MPR, 2024](https://www.dglobe.com/news/minnesota/how-a-guatemalan-immigrant-and-a-4th-generation-farmer-see-immigration-issues-in-worthington)), early childhood teaching ([The Globe/Post Bulletin, 2025](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/spanish-immersion-day-care-in-rochester-feel-the-effects-of-trump-immigration-crackdown)) and legal office work ([The Globe, Aug. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/eugenio-lopez-passionate-about-local-politics)).

**Labor history.** On December 12, 2006, federal agents raided the Swift plant in Worthington as one of six Swift plants hit that day, arresting nearly 1,300 people nationwide in what the Star Tribune called the largest one-day mass raid in U.S. history ([Star Tribune via Citrus County Chronicle](https://www.chronicleonline.com/18-years-ago-federal-agents-raided-this-minnesota-meatpacking-town-residents-fear-round-2/article_a0f1c7bd-8d77-559c-bd72-53481afaa973.html)). The raid still shapes memory: Eugenio Lopez, then 3, recalled his parents telling him they had no status ([same source](https://www.chronicleonline.com/18-years-ago-federal-agents-raided-this-minnesota-meatpacking-town-residents-fear-round-2/article_a0f1c7bd-8d77-559c-bd72-53481afaa973.html)). In 2022 volunteers from the local union helped run a Guatemalan mobile consulate in Worthington ([Center for Rural Affairs, June 28, 2022](https://www.cfra.org/blog/guatemalan-consulate-chicago-offers-services-rural-minnesota)). No Minnesota source found reports Guatemalan membership counts or leadership in a specific union local.

**Entrepreneurship.** Guatemalan-owned food businesses in Worthington sell beyond their town: Tortilleria El Maná takes its trailer to Turkey Valley Farms in Marshall and Premium Iowa Pork in Luverne, and customers come from Sioux Falls, Mitchell and Mankato ([The Globe, July 22, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/tortilleria-el-mana-goes-mobile-with-food-trailer)). A Worthington banker told the Minneapolis Fed in 2018 that it was "not uncommon for a 12-year-old" to help parents apply for a loan ([Minneapolis Fed](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2018/spotlight-on-worthington-a-global-community-in-rural-minnesota)).

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

Most named Guatemalan-owned businesses found are in Greater Minnesota, especially Worthington's downtown on 10th Street and Oxford Street. No Twin Cities business could be confirmed as Guatemalan-owned from a current published source, even though about half of Guatemalan Minnesotans live in the metro; this is a gap in the record, not proof that none exist. The full list, with status and evidence, is in businesses.json.

- **Panaderia Mi Tierra**, 424 10th St., Worthington: bakery and grocery owned by Guatemalan-born Ana Cuadros, open 6 a.m. to 9:30 p.m. daily ([The Globe, July 15, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/american-dream-from-factory-worker-to-owner-of-panaderia-mi-tierra)).
- **Tortilleria El Maná**, 1506 Oxford St., Worthington: tortillas and Guatemalan dishes, plus a food trailer ([The Globe, July 22, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/tortilleria-el-mana-goes-mobile-with-food-trailer)).
- **La Eterna Primavera**, inside Corky's Corner at Oxford and McMillan streets, Worthington: tortas, tacos and flautas from a San Marcos-born owner, set to open May 29, 2026; later operation not confirmed ([The Globe, May 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/la-eterna-primavera-to-open-friday-in-worthington)).
- **Tienda Chapin 2**, 203 Henrietta Ave., Park Rapids, and its sister **Tienda Chapin** in Willmar: Central American groceries including jocote, epazote and Guatemalan breads ([Park Rapids Enterprise, Oct. 1, 2025](https://www.parkrapidsenterprise.com/business/new-guatemalan-grocery-store-sells-freshly-imported-produce)).
- **Tienda Maya Quiche**, 712 Fourth St./Highway 60, Faribault: K'iche' family store with groceries, handmade clothing and pottery, opened in 2022; current operation could not be confirmed ([Faribault Daily News, 2022](https://www.southernminn.com/faribault_daily_news/news/siblings-open-guatemalan-grocery-clothing-store/article_b7c65650-fc9a-11ec-91f6-0beede47c292.html)).
- **Tienda Guatemala**, Worthington: listed in 2010 as carrying specifically Guatemalan goods; current status unknown ([The Globe, April 4, 2010](https://www.dglobe.com/business/shoppers-grab-your-carts-local-food-offerings-range-from-exotic-to-down-home)).

Businesses run by others also serve the community. Tienda Telvis II at 423 10th St., owned by a Mexican-American family, sells religious goods, quinceañera items and money transfers; in 2007 its owners said Guatemala and Mexico were their most popular airline ticket destinations ([The Globe, June 15, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/the-american-dream-family-business-telvis-open-for-over-two-decades); [The Globe, Oct. 28, 2007](https://www.dglobe.com/news/family-opens-its-second-business-in-worthington)). A 2024 Star Tribune report mentioned a Worthington shop selling traje, traditional Guatemalan dress, without naming it ([Star Tribune via Citrus County Chronicle](https://www.chronicleonline.com/18-years-ago-federal-agents-raided-this-minnesota-meatpacking-town-residents-fear-round-2/article_a0f1c7bd-8d77-559c-bd72-53481afaa973.html)). At Worthington's 2026 International Festival, K & W Innovations sold Guatemalan and Mexican raspados ([The Globe, July 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/international-festival-food-vendors-offer-up-wide-variety-of-treats-to-tempt-your-tastebuds)).

**Consulate.** Guatemala's consulate in Omaha, Nebraska, opened under a 2023 plan to serve Iowa, Nebraska, the Dakotas and Minnesota ([AGN, May 18, 2023](https://agn.gt/guatemala-abrira-consulados-en-nashville-y-omaha-estados-unidos/); [Living in Guatemala directory](https://livinginguatemala.com/consulates/omaha/)). Before that, the Chicago consulate served Minnesota and sent mobile teams: in May 2022 it processed papers for 759 people in Worthington over three days ([Center for Rural Affairs, June 28, 2022](https://www.cfra.org/blog/guatemalan-consulate-chicago-offers-services-rural-minnesota)), and a 2012 visit drew 2,500 to 3,000 people, some from as far as Colorado, according to the Rev. Jim Callahan ([The Globe, Feb. 24, 2013](https://www.dglobe.com/news/in-local-visit-guatemalan-consulate-assists-nationals)). No source found shows a Guatemalan consulate office in Minnesota itself.

**Media.** No Minnesota-based Guatemalan media outlet was found. A Guatemalan radio station, Emisoras Unidas, connected two Guatemalans living in Minnesota who met at U.S. Bank Stadium for Guatemala's 2025 Gold Cup match ([Emisoras Unidas, July 1, 2025](https://emisorasunidas.com/deportes/2025/07/01/radio-emisoras-unidas-une-a-guatemaltecos-en-minnesota-durante-la-copa-oro/)). Worthington residents said in 2026 that Facebook groups spread warnings and resource news quickly ([The Globe, Jan. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/resident-shares-fears-about-ice-presence-in-southwest-minnesota)).

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

**Catholic life in Worthington.** St. Mary's Catholic Church is the most documented religious home. It holds Spanish Masses at 7 p.m. Saturday and 11 a.m. Sunday ([St. Mary's Catholic Church](https://www.stmarysworthington.org/)). AP reported in 2024 that St. Mary's had 10 Latino choirs; one, Nueva Inspiración, is directed by Dagoberto Mendez, who came in 2000, and features his children and other Guatemalans ([AP via WSLS, Oct. 31, 2024](https://www.wsls.com/news/national/2024/10/31/in-amharic-karen-and-spanish-worship-is-like-home-for-migrants-in-heartland-town/)). In January 2025, parishioners from San Marcos held what AP called their biggest celebration yet of the Black Christ of Esquipulas, with a replica statue and two marimbas brought from Guatemala after raising nearly $40,000 through taco and tamale sales ([AP via KSAT, Jan. 17, 2025](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2025/01/17/in-guatemala-and-minnesota-holy-feast-brings-migrant-families-hope-and-pride-amid-crackdown-fears/)). The parish declared itself a sanctuary for undocumented immigrants in December 2016 ([The Globe, Dec. 9, 2016](https://www.dglobe.com/news/st-marys-proclaims-itself-as-official-sanctuary-for-undocumented-immigrants)). It has long hosted consulate visits and gave birth to a free clinic (sections 4 and 11).

**Faith in daily decisions.** People quoted in Minnesota reporting often speak of faith. The Quila siblings in Faribault said they ask God before every major step ([Faribault Daily News, 2022](https://www.southernminn.com/faribault_daily_news/news/siblings-open-guatemalan-grocery-clothing-store/article_b7c65650-fc9a-11ec-91f6-0beede47c292.html)). A Worthington traje seamstress said in 2024 that if she were deported, "we have to accept that that's what God wants" ([Star Tribune via Citrus County Chronicle](https://www.chronicleonline.com/18-years-ago-federal-agents-raided-this-minnesota-meatpacking-town-residents-fear-round-2/article_a0f1c7bd-8d77-559c-bd72-53481afaa973.html)). Eugenio Lopez credits his Catholic upbringing and confirmation name, Francis of Assisi, for his urge to help others ([The Globe, Aug. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/eugenio-lopez-passionate-about-local-politics)). These are individual voices, not a measure of the whole community.

**Differences within.** Evangelical and Pentecostal congregations are widespread in Guatemala and among Guatemalans elsewhere, but no Minnesota source was found naming a Guatemalan evangelical or Pentecostal congregation, a Maya spiritual practitioner (ajq'ij), or the share of Guatemalan Minnesotans with no religion. These remain unknown here.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

- **Black Christ of Esquipulas (El Señor de Esquipulas).** The feast day is January 15. In Worthington, St. Mary's held Mass, a barefoot offering dance in traje, marimba music, a procession float and a free taco lunch on Sunday, January 12, 2025 ([AP via KSAT, Jan. 17, 2025](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2025/01/17/in-guatemala-and-minnesota-holy-feast-brings-migrant-families-hope-and-pride-amid-crackdown-fears/)). No 2026 date was found.
- **Holy Week and Easter.** St. Mary's held Holy Thursday services in English at 3 p.m. and Spanish at 5 p.m. on April 2, 2026, and Easter Masses in both languages on April 5, 2026 ([St. Mary's events page](https://www.stmarysworthington.org/events)). No Minnesota source was found describing Guatemalan-style processions or sawdust carpets (alfombras).
- **Independence Day, September 15.** Guatemala and its neighbors mark independence from Spain on this date ([Office Holidays](https://www.officeholidays.com/holidays/guatemala/guatemala-independence-day)). No Minnesota celebration organized specifically by Guatemalans was found.
- **Hispanic Heritage Month, September 15 to October 15.** Worthington High School's Sueños Club hosted a community fiesta on October 2, 2026, at the 10th Street Pavilion ([The Globe, Oct. 5, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/hs-suenos-club-celebrates-hispanic-heritage-with-community-fiesta)).
- **Worthington International Festival.** The 32nd festival ran July 10 and 11, 2026, downtown, with a parade of flags and food vendors including Guatemalan raspados ([The Globe, July 13, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/international-festival-boasts-music-dance-and-worldly-cultures); [The Globe, July 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/international-festival-food-vendors-offer-up-wide-variety-of-treats-to-tempt-your-tastebuds)).
- **Willmar's May festival.** The Latino Community Association of Willmar's Cinco de Mayo celebration on May 16, 2026, included Guatemalan food; a founding member named Guatemalans among the cultures represented ([West Central Tribune, May 16, 2026](https://www.wctrib.com/news/local/cinco-de-mayo-event-in-downtown-willmar-shows-off-cultures)).
- **All Saints' Day, Christmas and others.** All Saints' Day (November 1), Christmas Eve and Christmas are public holidays in Guatemala ([Office Holidays, 2026](https://www.officeholidays.com/countries/guatemala/2026)). No Minnesota source described how Guatemalan families here mark All Saints', Día de los Muertos, Las Posadas or Día de los Reyes, so the calendar lists these without Minnesota detail.

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Households.** Guatemalan households average 3.52 people. Married couples with children under 18 make up 27.9 percent of households, single mothers with children 15.3 percent, other family households 15.6 percent, and people living alone 13.1 percent ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/guatemalan)).

**Families across borders.** Separation is a recurring theme. A Worthington woman told The Globe in 2011 that her mother left her with grandparents at age 6 to work in southwest Minnesota and send money home ([The Globe, Sept. 14, 2011](https://www.dglobe.com/news/guatemalan-seeks-american-dream)). In 2013, Worthington's Abuelos y Nietos Juntos project, coordinated by Lisa Kremer, took 14 U.S.-born children to San Marcos to meet grandparents and, in two cases, siblings they had never seen, because their parents could not safely travel ([Fox News Latino, Nov. 21, 2013](https://www.foxnews.com/lifestyle/minnesota-woman-helps-children-of-deported-guatemalans-reconnect-with-family.amp)). The trip became the film Abrazos, screened in Worthington in 2021 ([The Globe, Sept. 18, 2021](https://www.dglobe.com/community/abrazos-screening-event-features-immigration-law-q-a-local-families)). In 2026 a Worthington woman with a work permit said her parents returned to their home country out of fear of arrest, leaving her and her siblings here ([The Globe, Jan. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/resident-shares-fears-about-ice-presence-in-southwest-minnesota)).

**Food.** Minnesota sources mention tortillas made fresh twice a day ([The Globe, July 22, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/tortilleria-el-mana-goes-mobile-with-food-trailer)), tamales and tacos sold to fund the Esquipulas statue ([AP via KSAT](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2025/01/17/in-guatemala-and-minnesota-holy-feast-brings-migrant-families-hope-and-pride-amid-crackdown-fears/)), Guatemalan breads such as pan galleta and milhojas, and fruits such as jocote ([Park Rapids Enterprise, 2025](https://www.parkrapidsenterprise.com/business/new-guatemalan-grocery-store-sells-freshly-imported-produce)), and a family pot for cooking black beans ([The Globe, 2011](https://www.dglobe.com/news/guatemalan-seeks-american-dream)).

**Dress.** Women and girls wore sequined, embroidered huipiles and skirts with ribbons in their hair for the 2025 Esquipulas Mass ([AP via KSAT](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2025/01/17/in-guatemala-and-minnesota-holy-feast-brings-migrant-families-hope-and-pride-amid-crackdown-fears/)). Tienda Maya Quiche sold clothing handmade by K'iche' weavers, one piece taking about a month ([Faribault Daily News, 2022](https://www.southernminn.com/faribault_daily_news/news/siblings-open-guatemalan-grocery-clothing-store/article_b7c65650-fc9a-11ec-91f6-0beede47c292.html)).

**Childhood and family events.** Worthington businesses report steady demand for baptisms, weddings and quinceañeras ([The Globe, June 15, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/the-american-dream-family-business-telvis-open-for-over-two-decades)). Children commonly interpret for parents (section 1). No Minnesota source was found on Guatemalan elders' care or aging; with the 65-and-older count suppressed, older adults are few and hard to measure ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/guatemalan)).

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Music.** Marimba is the instrument most tied to Guatemalan celebrations in Minnesota reporting. Worthington players include Antulio Juarez, who learned by ear from his grandfather in the highlands ([AP via KSAT](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2025/01/17/in-guatemala-and-minnesota-holy-feast-brings-migrant-families-hope-and-pride-amid-crackdown-fears/)). Spanish-language church choirs with trumpets, saxophone and güira lead worship at St. Mary's ([AP via WSLS, 2024](https://www.wsls.com/news/national/2024/10/31/in-amharic-karen-and-spanish-worship-is-like-home-for-migrants-in-heartland-town/)).

**Film and history.** Guatemalan filmmaker Luis Argueta, who does not live in Minnesota, has screened films about immigration raids at Minnesota West in Worthington and filmed the 2013 Abuelos y Nietos trip ([The Globe, March 2, 2019](https://www.dglobe.com/news/guatemalan-filmmakers-the-u-turn-documentary-showing-march-7-at-minnesota-west); [Fox News Latino, 2013](https://www.foxnews.com/lifestyle/minnesota-woman-helps-children-of-deported-guatemalans-reconnect-with-family.amp)). The Nobles County Historical Society's permanent exhibit "Nobles County: A Second Home for Immigrants," opened to the public April 11, 2026, includes the story and artifacts of Guatemalan contributor Eligio Ramirez Sandoval, who spoke part of his remarks in Mam ([The Globe, March 22, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/nobles-county-historical-society-soft-launches-first-permanent-exhibit)). Minnesota West has hosted Guatemalan cultural nights with weaving, tortilla making and a style show ([The Globe, 2009](https://www.dglobe.com/news/brief-mw-guatemala-event-is-wednesday)), and a Guatemalan presenter shared traditional dress and cooking at a 2026 culture camp in Adrian ([The Globe, Aug. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/kids-learn-about-different-cultures-at-ncics-culture-camp)). In St. James, community meals called Culture through Cuisine featured Guatemalan food alongside Scandinavian, Mexican and Salvadoran dishes ([Mankato Free Press, Jan. 14, 2019](https://www.mankatofreepress.com/news/immigrant-stories-vary-but-share-a-common-thread/article_101f555a-17a6-11e9-ab1b-2710cce824d0.html)).

**Visual art.** The 2023 book Latin Art in Minnesota profiles 12 artists with roots in Mexico, Cuba, Chile, Venezuela and Tejana and Chicano cultures; none is identified as Guatemalan ([Minneapolis Institute of Art store](https://shop.artsmia.org/products/latin-art-in-minnesota)). No Guatemalan Minnesotan visual artist, writer or musician with a current public profile was confirmed.

**Sports.** Soccer is the sport most mentioned. A Lake City graduate found friends only after joining the school soccer team ([Post Bulletin, 2025](https://www.postbulletin.com/news/local/from-guatemala-to-lake-city-students-share-stories-of-their-journeys-from-central-america-to-graduation)). Guatemalan fans in Minnesota watched their national team's 2025 Gold Cup match at U.S. Bank Stadium ([Emisoras Unidas, 2025](https://emisorasunidas.com/deportes/2025/07/01/radio-emisoras-unidas-une-a-guatemaltecos-en-minnesota-durante-la-copa-oro/)). No Guatemalan amateur league in Minnesota was found.

## 9. Civic and political life

**Candidates.** Eugenio Lopez, the son of Guatemalan immigrants, ran for an open Worthington City Council seat in fall 2024 and drew nearly 20 percent of the vote ([Star Tribune via Citrus County Chronicle, Dec. 2024](https://www.chronicleonline.com/18-years-ago-federal-agents-raided-this-minnesota-meatpacking-town-residents-fear-round-2/article_a0f1c7bd-8d77-559c-bd72-53481afaa973.html)). In 2026 he filed for the District 518 school board, where three candidates filed for three open seats, The Globe reported in August. He said every person of color who had run in Worthington had lost ([The Globe, Aug. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/eugenio-lopez-passionate-about-local-politics)). No Guatemalan American state legislator or statewide official in Minnesota was found.

**Organizations.** No Minnesota organization founded by and for Guatemalans or Maya people was found. Groups that serve them include CLUES, which has offices in St. Paul, Minneapolis, Willmar and Austin and served more than 30,000 people in 2025 ([CLUES](https://clues.org/)); the Latino Community Association of Willmar ([West Central Tribune, May 2026](https://www.wctrib.com/news/local/cinco-de-mayo-event-in-downtown-willmar-shows-off-cultures)); Familias Juntas in Worthington ([The Globe, 2021](https://www.dglobe.com/community/abrazos-screening-event-features-immigration-law-q-a-local-families)); Unidos Minnesota, which organized for driver's licenses in Worthington ([Workday Magazine, 2019](https://workdaymagazine.org/on-the-front-lines-of-trumps-immigration-war-in-the-u-s-heartland/)); and the Nobles County Integration Collaborative ([The Globe, Aug. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/kids-learn-about-different-cultures-at-ncics-culture-camp)).

**Debates inside the community.** Views on enforcement differ. A Worthington seamstress said some immigrants welcomed a tougher stance on cartels even while fearing deportation ([Star Tribune via Citrus County Chronicle](https://www.chronicleonline.com/18-years-ago-federal-agents-raided-this-minnesota-meatpacking-town-residents-fear-round-2/article_a0f1c7bd-8d77-559c-bd72-53481afaa973.html)). Eugenio Lopez said Guatemala has grown safer and more developed over the 13 years he has visited ([The Globe, Aug. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/eugenio-lopez-passionate-about-local-politics)), while advocates for a Burnsville woman said in 2026 that her family had no safe home to return to ([West Central Tribune, April 24, 2026](https://www.wctrib.com/news/moorhead/local-leaders-call-for-release-of-minnesota-woman-suffering-medical-emergency-in-immigration-detention)). Workday Magazine's 2019 report quoted organizer Jessica Lee Velasco saying newcomers felt pushed to "water down who you are" to belong ([Workday Magazine](https://workdaymagazine.org/on-the-front-lines-of-trumps-immigration-war-in-the-u-s-heartland/)).

## 10. Education

**Schools.** Worthington Public Schools enrolled 1,412 English learners in 2025, up from 1,265 in 2020, among the 15 Minnesota districts with the most English learners; Austin enrolled 1,423 ([MDE, English Learners in Minnesota 2024-25](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/other/261381.pdf)). AP reported in 2025 that students of color are more than 80 percent of Worthington's K-12 enrollment ([AP via KSAT](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2025/01/17/in-guatemala-and-minnesota-holy-feast-brings-migrant-families-hope-and-pride-amid-crackdown-fears/)). Eugenio Lopez said he campaigned as a student for the 2019 referendum that built a new intermediate school ([The Globe, Aug. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/eugenio-lopez-passionate-about-local-politics)).

**Newcomer students.** Lake City's English-learner graduates of 2025, several of them from Guatemala, arrived as teens, some with a sixth-grade education, and worked while in school; one planned to earn a commercial driver's license at Minnesota State College Southeast ([Post Bulletin, May 28, 2025](https://www.postbulletin.com/news/local/from-guatemala-to-lake-city-students-share-stories-of-their-journeys-from-central-america-to-graduation)).

**Higher education.** Minnesota West Community and Technical College in Worthington has been a regular venue for Guatemalan cultural events ([The Globe, 2009](https://www.dglobe.com/news/brief-mw-guatemala-event-is-wednesday); [The Globe, 2019](https://www.dglobe.com/news/guatemalan-filmmakers-the-u-turn-documentary-showing-march-7-at-minnesota-west)). The University of Minnesota Morris archive holds St. James oral histories with Guatemalan narrators ([UMN Morris](https://digitalcommons.morris.umn.edu/unitingcultures/)). No Mayan-language school program, Guatemalan student association, or Guatemalan Minnesotan scholar was confirmed.

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Coverage and access.** The 27.0 percent uninsured rate is the clearest health fact in the data ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/guatemalan)). The Rev. Jim Callahan started the Our Lady of Guadalupe Free Clinic in Worthington in 2011, he said, because immigrants were using the emergency room for care. He also said many people were still affected by the 2006 raid, describing "a lot of after-effects, you know, PTSD" ([Star Tribune via Citrus County Chronicle](https://www.chronicleonline.com/18-years-ago-federal-agents-raided-this-minnesota-meatpacking-town-residents-fear-round-2/article_a0f1c7bd-8d77-559c-bd72-53481afaa973.html)). That is his observation, not a diagnosis. The clinic, now called OLGFC, moved in 2022 to 616 11th St.; Sanford Health Cooperative nurses, a pharmacist and an interpreter see patients each Wednesday, and Saturday specialty clinics have served more than 100 patients at a time ([The Globe, Dec. 13, 2022](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/free-health-clinic-for-uninsured-gets-new-home-help-from-sanford-health-cooperative); [OLGFC](https://olgfc.org/)).

**Language in care.** Interpreters are central to the clinic's work ([The Globe, 2022](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/free-health-clinic-for-uninsured-gets-new-home-help-from-sanford-health-cooperative)). No Minnesota source found reports whether health systems provide Mam or K'iche' interpreters, or how often.

**Fear and care.** In January 2026, a Worthington resident said many people stopped leaving home or going to work after immigration agents arrived, sending U.S.-born children on errands instead ([The Globe, Jan. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/resident-shares-fears-about-ice-presence-in-southwest-minnesota)). A young Burnsville woman originally from Guatemala was detained in February 2026 days before a scheduled surgery and released in June after about four months in El Paso ([Park Rapids Enterprise, June 4, 2026](https://www.parkrapidsenterprise.com/news/minnesota/ice-releases-burnsville-asylum-seeker-with-life-threatening-condition)).

**Traditional practice.** No Minnesota source was found on Maya healers, midwives, sweat baths (temazcal) or herbal practice among Guatemalan Minnesotans.

## 12. Disability

**What the numbers say.** Compass reports that 5.4 percent of Guatemalan Minnesotans, give or take 2.3 points, have a disability, and 5.5 percent of those under 65 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/guatemalan)). The low figure invites questions rather than conclusions. The population is very young, which lowers any disability rate. The survey's disability questions are asked in English or Spanish, so it is unknown how well they reach Mam or K'iche' speakers. Are people who fear contact with government less likely to answer at all? No source established which of these, if any, matters.

**What produced impairment.** Minnesota evidence points to work as one source of injury. In the University of Minnesota-led dairy project, 42 percent of 360 immigrant workers, 12 percent of them from Guatemala, said they had been injured at work at some point ([National Academies presentation, 2025](https://www.nationalacademies.org/cdn/materials/a002da7b-5ac6-47c5-a2f4-53c10557d2fb)). Meatpacking, where many Guatemalan Minnesotans work, is physically hard, with high turnover ([Minneapolis Fed, 2018](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2018/spotlight-on-worthington-a-global-community-in-rural-minnesota)). Chronic conditions such as hypertension and diabetes are the most common reasons for visits to Worthington's free clinic, which serves people of many origins ([The Globe, 2022](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/free-health-clinic-for-uninsured-gets-new-home-help-from-sanford-health-cooperative)).

**What a label opens or closes.** One Minnesota court case shows disability shaping an immigration decision. In 2017 a Guatemalan dairy worker near Evansville and his wife testified in immigration court that returning would harm their son, who has autism, and that the family would be shunned there; the judge questioned why the family would go with him ([The Globe/Forum News Service, Nov. 10, 2017](https://www.dglobe.com/news/central-minn-farm-worker-from-guatemala-makes-his-case-to-stay-in-u-s)). An immigration lawyer said in 2017 that children's medical problems had once sometimes won families a humanitarian reprieve, but that prosecutors had stopped using that discretion ([West Central Tribune, Aug. 3, 2017](https://www.wctrib.com/news/deportation-expected-for-st-paul-father-of-two-who-fled-guatemala-as-a-teen)).

**Language as access.** The case of a St. Paul father who was given immigration papers in Spanish, though he spoke Mam and could not read, shows how a language mismatch alone can close doors ([West Central Tribune, 2017](https://www.wctrib.com/news/deportation-expected-for-st-paul-father-of-two-who-fled-guatemala-as-a-teen)). Whether Minnesota disability, special education or waiver services have reached Mam- and K'iche'-speaking families, and how families themselves define disability, could not be established from any Minnesota source.

## 13. What is changing now

**Operation Metro Surge.** Federal immigration agents surged into Minnesota from December 2025. MPR News and APM Reports, analyzing federal data released in March 2026, found at least 3,789 arrests between December 1 and March 10; most of those arrested came from Latin America, led by Ecuador, with many from Mexico, Guatemala and Honduras, and fewer than one-quarter had a criminal conviction ([MPR News via Detroit Lakes Online, March 31, 2026](https://www.dl-online.com/news/minnesota/newly-released-federal-data-shows-majority-of-ice-arrestees-in-minn-had-no-criminal-record)). The report did not give a separate count for Guatemala.

**Greater Minnesota felt it too.** Agents arrived in Worthington in January 2026, and residents described staying home ([The Globe, Jan. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/resident-shares-fears-about-ice-presence-in-southwest-minnesota)). In Willmar, arrests in January closed restaurants ([CBS Minnesota, Jan. 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/willmar-ice-federal-agents-restaurant-workers-detained/)), and a Latino Community Association leader said in May that people were still afraid to come out ([West Central Tribune, May 16, 2026](https://www.wctrib.com/news/local/cinco-de-mayo-event-in-downtown-willmar-shows-off-cultures)). On January 16, 2026, a 19-year-old Guatemalan man was arrested after an incident with agents in Virginia, Minnesota; prosecutors dropped the criminal case, but he remained in immigration custody ([The Globe/Duluth News Tribune, Jan. 27, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/minnesota/feds-drop-case-against-guatemalan-man-arrested-on-iron-range)).

**Work permits and jobs.** In July 2025, three Guatemalan teachers lost their jobs at a Rochester day care when a federal parole program ended ([The Globe/Post Bulletin, July 2, 2025](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/spanish-immersion-day-care-in-rochester-feel-the-effects-of-trump-immigration-crackdown)).

**Generational change.** U.S.-born young adults are stepping into civic roles. Eugenio Lopez's 2026 school board bid is one example ([The Globe, Aug. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/eugenio-lopez-passionate-about-local-politics)). New businesses keep appearing, such as La Eterna Primavera, announced to open in May 2026 ([The Globe, May 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/la-eterna-primavera-to-open-friday-in-worthington)), and Tienda Chapin 2 in Park Rapids in August 2025 ([Park Rapids Enterprise, 2025](https://www.parkrapidsenterprise.com/business/new-guatemalan-grocery-store-sells-freshly-imported-produce)). Families also keep returning to Guatemala to visit when they can: Lopez's family has made several trips ([The Globe, Aug. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/eugenio-lopez-passionate-about-local-politics)).

**Remembering.** Nobles County's new immigration exhibit, built over about six years with nearly $400,000 in support through the Minnesota Historical Society, places Guatemalan stories in the county's permanent history ([The Globe, March 22, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/nobles-county-historical-society-soft-launches-first-permanent-exhibit)).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **Rural renewal.** The Minneapolis Fed found that immigration drove Worthington's growth while the native-born population of the city and county fell ([Minneapolis Fed, 2018](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2018/spotlight-on-worthington-a-global-community-in-rural-minnesota)). Indigenous Guatemalans, most of the town's Latino residents by one 2019 account, are central to that story ([Workday Magazine](https://workdaymagazine.org/on-the-front-lines-of-trumps-immigration-war-in-the-u-s-heartland/)).
- **Food and farm labor.** Guatemalan workers help run Minnesota's pork plants and dairy farms (section 3).
- **Small business.** Bakeries, tortillerias, groceries and restaurants in Worthington, Willmar, Park Rapids and Faribault (section 4).
- **Faith and organizing.** Parish choirs, the self-funded Esquipulas feast and the community effort behind it ([AP via KSAT](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2025/01/17/in-guatemala-and-minnesota-holy-feast-brings-migrant-families-hope-and-pride-amid-crackdown-fears/)).
- **Language heritage.** Mam and K'iche' speakers keep living Maya languages in Minnesota towns (section 1).
- **Bridge-building.** A second generation that interprets, campaigns and runs for office (section 9).
