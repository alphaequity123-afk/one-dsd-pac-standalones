# Corrections to the existing Guatemalan Minnesota page
Checked: October 31, 2026, against https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/guatemalan

The page also omits the uninsured rate, which Compass reports as 27.0 percent (give or take 6.7). The page says it has no uninsured rate; one now exists.

## Claims that are wrong or need correcting

- **"CLUES is a Twin Cities door that does not automatically reach a dairy county" / "CLUES ... offers Twin Cities services, without automatic reach into Greater Minnesota."** CLUES's own site says it has offices in St. Paul, Minneapolis, Willmar and Austin, and served more than 30,000 people across those places and statewide in 2025 ([CLUES, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://clues.org/)). Willmar and Austin both have Guatemalan populations ([Census Reporter, ACS 2020-2024](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=160|04000US27)). The point that a metro office may not reach every rural county still holds, but CLUES does have Greater Minnesota offices.
- **Place list "Worthington, Willmar, Austin, Albert Lea."** Census estimates support Worthington (about 1,714), Willmar (about 657) and Austin (about 636). Albert Lea does not appear among the top 25 Minnesota cities by Guatemalan-origin count, and Freeborn County is not among the top 20 counties. Places that do appear and are missing from the page include Fairmont (about 419), St. James (about 338, about 7 percent of the city), and Nobles, Watonwan, Kandiyohi, Mower and Martin counties ([Census Reporter, places](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=160|04000US27); [Census Reporter, counties](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=04000US27,050|04000US27)). All have wide margins of error. Albert Lea should not be named as a Guatemalan center without a source.
- **Consular services.** The page does not name a consulate. Since a 2023 decision, Minnesota falls under the Guatemalan Consulate General in Omaha, Nebraska, not Chicago ([AGN, May 18, 2023](https://agn.gt/guatemala-abrira-consulados-en-nashville-y-omaha-estados-unidos/); [Living in Guatemala directory, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://livinginguatemala.com/consulates/omaha/)). Earlier Minnesota sources refer to Chicago ([Center for Rural Affairs, 2022](https://www.cfra.org/blog/guatemalan-consulate-chicago-offers-services-rural-minnesota)).

## Claims that could not be supported from a Minnesota source

- **"K'iche', Mam, Q'eqchi', Kaqchikel and other Mayan languages are spoken in this state."** Mam (Worthington; a St. Paul father) and K'iche' (Faribault; St. James) are documented in Minnesota sources ([The Globe, March 22, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/nobles-county-historical-society-soft-launches-first-permanent-exhibit); [West Central Tribune, Aug. 3, 2017](https://www.wctrib.com/news/deportation-expected-for-st-paul-father-of-two-who-fled-guatemala-as-a-teen); [Faribault Daily News, 2022](https://www.southernminn.com/faribault_daily_news/news/siblings-open-guatemalan-grocery-clothing-store/article_b7c65650-fc9a-11ec-91f6-0beede47c292.html); [UMN Morris oral histories](https://digitalcommons.morris.umn.edu/unitingcultures/)). Tz'utujil appears for one adoptee ([Defining Diego](https://podcasts.apple.com/mn/podcast/defining-diego/id1607121653)). No Minnesota source was found for Q'eqchi' or Kaqchikel speakers. The page should name only languages it can support or say "other Mayan languages may also be spoken."
- **"Mam dairy worker," "plant and dairy towns in southern and western Minnesota."** Guatemalan dairy and meatpacking work is documented (12 percent of 360 workers in a University of Minnesota-led dairy project came from Guatemala, and Worthington's JBS plant employs many Guatemalans), but no source ties Mam speakers specifically to dairy work ([National Academies presentation, 2025](https://www.nationalacademies.org/cdn/materials/a002da7b-5ac6-47c5-a2f4-53c10557d2fb)).
- **"Catholic, evangelical, and Pentecostal streams are all large in Guatemalan Minnesota."** Catholic life is well documented in Worthington ([AP via WSLS, 2024](https://www.wsls.com/news/national/2024/10/31/in-amharic-karen-and-spanish-worship-is-like-home-for-migrants-in-heartland-town/)). No Minnesota source was found naming a Guatemalan evangelical or Pentecostal congregation or measuring their size.
- **"Maya calendar dates (Cholq'ij and related observances) live in some rooms," "costumbre lives in some homes."** No Minnesota source was found.
- **"Leagues and pickup games are social infrastructure in plant towns."** No Minnesota source on Guatemalan soccer leagues was found.
- **"CECLC is a department door."** The Minnesota DHS page for this council could not be accessed, so the name and role could not be checked.
- **"Worker centers" as partners in Greater Minnesota.** No Guatemalan-serving worker center in Greater Minnesota was found; the organizations found are CLUES, the Latino Community Association of Willmar, Familias Juntas, Unidos Minnesota and the Nobles County Integration Collaborative.

## Claims checked and supported

- Independence on September 15, 1821, shared with other Central American countries ([Office Holidays](https://www.officeholidays.com/holidays/guatemala/guatemala-independence-day)).
- Civil war from 1960 to 1996 ([Workday Magazine, 2019](https://workdaymagazine.org/on-the-front-lines-of-trumps-immigration-war-in-the-u-s-heartland/)). The page says "historical-record bodies named genocide"; the UN-backed Historical Clarification Commission said in 1999 that testimony showed a governmental policy of genocide ([UN, March 1, 1999](https://press.un.org/en/1999/19990301.guate.brf.html)).
- Children interpreting for parents is described by a Guatemalan American in Worthington ([The Globe, Aug. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/eugenio-lopez-passionate-about-local-politics)).
- A Spanish interpreter may not work for Mayan-language speakers: a Mam-speaking St. Paul father was given immigration papers in Spanish he could not read ([West Central Tribune, 2017](https://www.wctrib.com/news/deportation-expected-for-st-paul-father-of-two-who-fled-guatemala-as-a-teen)).
- Enforcement keeping people home from work and appointments is documented in Worthington in January 2026 ([The Globe, Jan. 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/resident-shares-fears-about-ice-presence-in-southwest-minnesota)).
- Remittances as an obligation are documented ([AP via KSAT, 2025](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2025/01/17/in-guatemala-and-minnesota-holy-feast-brings-migrant-families-hope-and-pride-amid-crackdown-fears/)).

## Missing from the page

- Worthington's role as the best-documented center, its San Marcos ties and the 2006 Swift raid.
- The Black Christ of Esquipulas feast at St. Mary's in Worthington, the community's best-documented Minnesota celebration.
- Named Guatemalan-owned businesses (Panaderia Mi Tierra, Tortilleria El Maná, Tienda Chapin in Willmar and Park Rapids, Tienda Maya Quiche in Faribault).
- Guatemalan adoptees raised in Minnesota.
