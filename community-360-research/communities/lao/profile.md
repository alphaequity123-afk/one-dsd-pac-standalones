# Lao in Minnesota
Checked: October 31, 2026

This page is about ethnic Lao Minnesotans, the lowland Lao people of Laos and their descendants. Laos is home to many peoples. Hmong, Khmu, Iu Mien, Tai Dam and others also came to Minnesota from Laos, and the Hmong community has its own page. Bryan Thao Worra, the Lao Minnesotan poet laureate, told MPR News in 2025 that "Laos isn't monolithic" and spoke of more than 160 ethnic groups affected by the war ([MPR News, Apr. 30, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2025/04/30/literary-project-shares-history-of-lao-minnesotans)). Names can mislead. The Lao Family Community of Minnesota, for example, has long organized the St. Paul Hmong New Year ([MPR News archive, Nov. 27, 2004](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/2004/11/27/dao-bay-of-the-lao-family-community-on)).

## 1. People and place

**How many.** Minnesota Compass, using the American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024, counts 14,918 Lao (non-Hmong) Minnesotans, give or take 2,177. It places 69.0 percent in the Twin Cities and 31.0 percent in Greater Minnesota, each give or take 6.6 points ([Minnesota Compass, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lao-non-hmong)). Pew Research Center, counting only people who name Laotian alone and no other origin in the 2021 to 2023 survey, reports about 9,000 in Minnesota. That places Minnesota third, after California (45,000) and Texas (13,000), out of about 180,000 nationally ([Pew Research Center, May 1, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-laotians-in-the-u-s/)). The two counts differ because they ask different questions; Pew notes that 28 percent of Laotian Americans also name another race or origin, and those people drop out of its count. Both are survey estimates with wide margins.

Other figures circulate. The American Immigration Council wrote in 2022 of "13,000+" Lao rebuilding their lives in Minnesota ([American Immigration Council, 2022](https://www.americanimmigrationcouncil.org/story/lao-refugee-becomes-engaged-community-leader-in-minneapolis/)). Sahtu Press put the estimate at 12,000 in 2015 ([Sahtu Press, 2015](https://sahtu.press/articles/national-lao-american-symposium-and-writers-summit-2015)). A 2020 news report quoted the International Institute of Minnesota as counting 15,000 Laotians in the Twin Cities metro ([Forum News Service, Feb. 2020](https://www.inforum.com/news/mccollum-trump-administration-set-to-deport-minnesota-hmong-lao-refugees)). Community leaders have long said Minnesota has the third-largest Lao population in the country: Chanida Phaengdara Potter in 2013 ([MPR News, Apr. 14, 2013](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2013/04/14/lao-new-year-unites-groups-to-celebrate-together-for-first-time-in-minn)) and Bryan Thao Worra in 2025 ([MPR News, Apr. 30, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2025/04/30/literary-project-shares-history-of-lao-minnesotans)). Pew's ranking agrees.

**Age, sex and birthplace.** In the Compass profile, 23.2 percent are 17 or younger and 12.1 percent are 65 or older. Women and girls are 55.1 percent. Foreign-born residents are 47.1 percent, give or take 5.6 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lao-non-hmong)). Nationally, Pew puts the median age of Laotian Americans at 40.6, with immigrants at 52.5 and the U.S.-born at 24.0 ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-laotians-in-the-u-s/)). In other words, the community holds both an aging first generation and a young U.S.-born one.

**How people came.** In January 1980, MPR News reported nearly 7,000 refugees from Indochina in Minnesota, most from Laos and Vietnam, 2,000 of whom had arrived in 1979. About 60 percent lived in the Twin Cities. All had been matched with volunteer sponsors through Lutheran Social Service, Catholic Charities of Saint Paul, the International Institute and Church World Service. One Lao refugee interviewed, a former military officer who had interpreted for U.S. advisors, said he and about 3,000 other officers were airlifted to Thailand on May 14, 1975, and waited a year in camps. A state official noted refugees moving here from other states to join family ([MPR News archive, Jan. 7, 1980](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1980/01/07/laotian-refugees-in-minnesota)). By September 1988, the State Refugee Program Office counted about 5,656 Laotians among 35,684 Southeast Asian refugees in Minnesota ([Older Refugees Program Report for DHS, Feb. 1989](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/2016/other/160681.pdf)). That 1989 report said Lao elders came from both rural and urban backgrounds: many had been wet-rice farmers in remote villages, while some had run businesses such as rice stores. The Lao Assistance Center of Minnesota was founded in 1983 by ethnic Lao to meet urgent resettlement needs ([Lao Assistance Center, About](https://www.laocenter.org/about)). The American Immigration Council notes that the federal refugee office had not listed Minneapolis among 24 cities where it expected Lao refugees to thrive ([American Immigration Council](https://www.americanimmigrationcouncil.org/story/lao-refugee-becomes-engaged-community-leader-in-minneapolis/)).

**Where.** Sources place Lao life in North Minneapolis, where the Lao Assistance Center has its office ([Lao Assistance Center](https://www.laocenter.org/)); in the northwest suburbs, including Brooklyn Park, Brooklyn Center and Crystal ([Siengkane Lao MN handout, 2025](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2025/envlegacy/20250325/envlegacy_20250325_SF2543-Siengkane-Lao-handout.pdf); [MPR News, 2013](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2013/04/14/lao-new-year-unites-groups-to-celebrate-together-for-first-time-in-minn)); in Dakota County, where a Lao mutual assistance association worked from 1982 ([1989 DHS report](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/2016/other/160681.pdf)); and in Greater Minnesota. In the southwest, the Lao temple near Worthington drew people in 2015 from Worthington, Jackson, Mountain Lake and Magnolia, as well as Sioux Falls and Sibley, Iowa ([Worthington Daily Globe via Grand Forks Herald, 2015](https://www.grandforksherald.com/lifestyle/a-buddhist-temple-thrives-in-rural-minnesota)). Registered Lao temples and churches are listed in Cedar, Farmington, Forest Lake, Robbinsdale, Waite Park and Warroad ([ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/search?q=lao)). A 2026 deportation case involved a Rochester family ([FOX 9, Aug. 7, 2026](https://www.fox9.com/news/mn-clemency-review-commission-considering-case-man-facing-deportaion-aug-7-2026)). No source was found that maps Lao Minnesotans by neighborhood or county.

**Languages.** Among Lao Minnesotans 5 and older, 67.5 percent speak a language other than English at home and 31.7 percent speak English less than "very well"; 32.5 percent speak only English ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lao-non-hmong)). Nationally, Pew finds 91 percent of U.S.-born Laotian Americans proficient in English, against 45 percent of immigrants ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-laotians-in-the-u-s/)). In schools, Lao does not appear among the 12 most common home languages of English learners in 2024-25; the twelfth language on that list had 649 students ([MDE, English Learners in Minnesota 2024-25](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/other/261381.pdf)). The SEAD Project says it helps diaspora language learners "reconnect with language so that they can communicate with our elders" ([University of Minnesota, May 13, 2026](https://cla.umn.edu/liberal-arts-engagement-hub/news-events/story/southeast-asian-diaspora-project-honors-50-years-history-through-archival-storytelling)).

## 2. All walks of life

Lao Minnesotans are spread across every income level. All figures below are from the Compass 2020 to 2024 profile, in 2024 dollars, and carry wide margins of error because the group is small ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lao-non-hmong)).

- **Comfortable and well-off households.** Median household income is $82,225, give or take $12,527. Of 4,733 households, 39.2 percent have incomes of $100,000 or more.
- **Middle-income and working families.** Another 28.1 percent earn $50,000 to $74,999 and 13.6 percent earn $75,000 to $99,999. Among adults 18 to 64, 80.9 percent are employed. Homeownership is 62.3 percent, give or take 9.7 points.
- **Families under strain.** About 9.9 percent of households earn under $35,000. The poverty rate is 7.7 percent, give or take 5.3 points, and another 17.9 percent live between 100 and 199 percent of the poverty line. Among renters, 44.3 percent are cost-burdened. About 7.2 percent of households have no vehicle, and 6.1 percent of people lack health insurance.
- **Education.** Among adults 25 and older, 21.1 percent did not finish high school, 30.7 percent stopped at a diploma or GED, 32.5 percent have some college or an associate degree, and 15.7 percent hold a bachelor's degree or higher, including 5.7 percent with a graduate or professional degree.

Nationally, Pew reports a median household income of $79,400 for Laotian-headed households in 2023, compared with $105,600 for Asian-headed households overall, and a 10 percent poverty rate ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-laotians-in-the-u-s/)).

**Success, in public view.** Chef Ann Ahmed runs Gai Noi and Khâluna in Minneapolis. Gai Noi is on a 2026 list of the Twin Cities' 50 best restaurants, which credits Ahmed with working "to redefine the food of Laos globally"; the same list includes Soul Lao in St. Paul, owned by Sabrina Boualaphanh and Eric Phothisanh ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine, 2026](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/the-best-restaurants-in-the-twin-cities-2026/)). Nia Rasavong, who came from a Thai refugee camp at age six and grew up in Eagan, co-owns Ruam Mit Thai + Lao Cuisine in downtown St. Paul ([Power 104.7 FM, July 2024](https://www.power1047.fm/post/ruam-mit-offers-food-culture-and-connection)). Sunny Chanthanouvong has led the Lao Assistance Center since 2003 and is a Bush Leadership Fellow ([American Immigration Council](https://www.americanimmigrationcouncil.org/story/lao-refugee-becomes-engaged-community-leader-in-minneapolis/)). Playwright Saymoukda Duangphouxay Vongsay was named a Bush Leadership Fellow in 2022 ([University of Minnesota CCAPS, 2022](https://ccaps.umn.edu/story/jai-khang-heart-hard)). In Worthington, a 2015 profile described temple leaders who were a longtime JBS employee and union officer and a former local business owner who raised five daughters, all of whom went on to higher education ([Grand Forks Herald, 2015](https://www.grandforksherald.com/lifestyle/a-buddhist-temple-thrives-in-rural-minnesota)).

**Hardship, in public view.** The Lao Assistance Center says it serves more than 5,000 low-income, limited-English individuals, families and elders, and has run housing stability programs for more than 15 years ([Lao Assistance Center, About](https://www.laocenter.org/about)). Lao Culture Dance Fashion MN says many people at its events live near or below the poverty line and face language barriers ([MRAC, May 22, 2024](https://mrac.org/2024/05/22/grantee-spotlight-lao-culture-dance-fashion-mn/)). In 1980, a Lao refugee worker described a community limited to low-paying factory jobs because of language ([MPR News archive, 1980](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1980/01/07/laotian-refugees-in-minnesota)). In 2015, elder advocate Banlang Phommasouvanh said older Lao Minnesotans who "worked hard all those years" now "don't know where to go" in retirement ([Diverse Elders Coalition, Aug. 10, 2015](https://diverseelders.org/2015/08/10/the-mongoose-and-the-bird-of-paradise-storytelling-to-help-older-lao-americans-thrive-in-minnesota/)). In 2026, deportations separated some families (section 13).

## 3. Work and the economy

**Factory and plant work.** Early jobs were mostly in factories. A Lao resettlement worker said in 1980 that refugees could find only "factory jobs that are not paying well" ([MPR News archive, 1980](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1980/01/07/laotian-refugees-in-minnesota)). In Worthington in 2015, the Lao temple president was a longtime employee of the JBS meat plant and one of five vice presidents of United Food and Commercial Workers Local 1161. He said JBS gave the temple an annual cash donation and meat for events, and the union was "very supportive." The temple's advisory board chair worked at Bedford Industries ([Grand Forks Herald, 2015](https://www.grandforksherald.com/lifestyle/a-buddhist-temple-thrives-in-rural-minnesota)).

**Public and professional work.** A man born in Laos who was pardoned in 2026 had worked for decades in the Minneapolis engineering department, according to the governor's office as reported by KSTP ([KSTP, May 4, 2026](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/minnesota-board-pardons-laos-refugee-day-before-possible-deportation/)); his family lives in Brooklyn Park ([MPR News, Apr. 29, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/04/29/at-ricky-chandee-ice-detention-deportation-future-uncertain)).

**Workforce programs.** The Lao Assistance Center began employment programs in 1990. It says its most structured workforce programs, from 1999 to 2003, served "thousands of Lao Minnesotans," with financial literacy added later. Its staff list in 2026 includes a program manager for a Department of Employment and Economic Development (DEED) program ([Lao Assistance Center, About](https://www.laocenter.org/about)).

**Getting to work.** Of 8,110 Lao workers, 86.9 percent drive or ride in a car, and 42.0 percent travel 30 minutes or more each way ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lao-non-hmong)).

**Small business.** In 1984, Minnesota's refugee coordinator estimated about 70 refugee-owned businesses statewide, mostly restaurants and groceries, with import shops, beauty parlors and car repair shops too ([MPR News archive, Nov. 16, 1984](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1984/11/16/southeast-asian-refugee-businesses-growing-in-the-twin-cities)). Today the most visible Lao businesses are restaurants (section 4). Ruam Mit's owner says she hires many kitchen and front-of-house workers who do not speak English, because "you have to give them a chance" ([Power 104.7 FM, 2024](https://www.power1047.fm/post/ruam-mit-offers-food-culture-and-connection)). No union history specific to Lao workers beyond the Worthington example was found.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

Lao food in the Twin Cities was long hidden inside Thai restaurants. In 2017, MPR News noted that there were more Minnesotans from Laos than from Thailand or Vietnam, yet "hardly any Lao eateries"; cook and YouTube creator Christina Tia explained that diners could order dishes "Lao style" at many Thai restaurants ([MPR News, Mar. 8, 2017](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2017/03/08/appetites-where-is-the-lao-food-in-the-twin-cities)). By 2024, writer Sanaphay Rattanavong described Lao food as having reached "culinary critical mass" in the Twin Cities ([Joysauce, Oct. 1, 2024](https://joysauce.com/why-lao-food-is-booming-in-the-twin-cities/)).

Confirmed open as of October 2026:

- **Gai Noi**, 1610 Harmon Place, Loring Park, Minneapolis: a Lao restaurant by Chef Ann Ahmed, open daily for lunch and dinner, walk-in only ([Gai Noi](https://www.gainoimpls.com/)). It is on the 2026 list of the Twin Cities' 50 best restaurants ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/the-best-restaurants-in-the-twin-cities-2026/)).
- **Khâluna**, 4000 Lyndale Avenue South, Minneapolis: Ahmed's Southeast Asian restaurant, whose name means "please" in Lao and whose logo border draws on the Lao sinh skirt ([Khâluna](https://khaluna.com/)).
- **Soul Lao**, Sibley Plaza, Highland Park, St. Paul: a Lao restaurant that began as a food truck in 2017; customers can also order to eat at Wandering Leaf Brewing next door ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine, 2026](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/the-best-restaurants-in-the-twin-cities-2026/); [My Annoying Opinions, Oct. 29, 2025](https://myannoyingopinions.com/2025/10/29/soul-lao-st-paul-mn/)).

Could not confirm open: Ruam Mit Thai + Lao Cuisine at 367 Wabasha St. N. in downtown St. Paul, founded in 1989 and reopened downtown in 2024 ([Visit Saint Paul](https://www.visitsaintpaul.com/directory/ruam-mit/); [Power 104.7 FM, 2024](https://www.power1047.fm/post/ruam-mit-offers-food-culture-and-connection)); Lat14 Asian Eatery in Golden Valley, whose website loads but shows no current hours ([Lat14](https://www.lat14.com/)); and Top Asian Food in Worthington, owned by one Thai and one Lao family in 2017 ([MPR News, Feb. 27, 2017](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2017/02/27/worthington-minn-minority-trump-future)). The New Orient Market on Nicollet Avenue was described in 2024 as a key source of Southeast Asian ingredients for Lao cooks; its ownership was not stated ([Joysauce, 2024](https://joysauce.com/why-lao-food-is-booming-in-the-twin-cities/)).

The list is short. No Lao-owned grocery, law, accounting, insurance, tax, travel or salon business, no Lao chamber of commerce, and no Lao-language newspaper or broadcast outlet based in Minnesota could be confirmed. Lao book publisher Sahtu Press, founded in 2013 with Minnesota poet Bryan Thao Worra among its founders, says all its books are printed in Minnesota ([Sahtu Press, 2024](https://sahtu.press/articles/q7arce0k1hic2yopfa1ckda4g2su41)).

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

**Theravada Buddhism.** Sahtu Press writes that "many traditional Wat Lao Theravada Buddhist temples have served the community here over the decades" ([Sahtu Press, 2024](https://sahtu.press/articles/q7arce0k1hic2yopfa1ckda4g2su41)). Federal nonprofit records list several Minnesota Lao temples. Their filings show registration and finances, not how active each one is ([ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/search?q=lao)):

- **Wat Lao Xayabhoummaram**, Cedar, the largest by filings: $531,896 in revenue in 2024, with three unpaid officers and a stated purpose of housing, feeding and clothing monks so they can teach ([Lucido, 990 data](https://lucidodata.com/en/organizations/wat-lao-xayabhoummaram-472753522)).
- **Wat Lao Minnesota Lao Buddhist Temple**, Farmington, tax-exempt since May 2005 ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/363423583)), and **Wat Lao Buddhakhodome of SW MN**, also listed in Farmington ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/search?q=lao)).
- **Lao Temple Vipassana Center of Minnesota**, Forest Lake, tax-exempt since January 2012 ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/900602632)).
- **Wat Lao Buddhasamakidham of Warroad**, listed in far northern Minnesota ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/search?q=lao)).
- **Lao Temple Siri (or Sri) Buddharam**, rural Nobles County near Worthington, established in 2004 as Worthington's first Buddhist temple. In 2015 two monks lived there, about 150 people came to monthly services, and Nobles County had changed its zoning to allow a church in an agricultural area. Members said they wanted a temple because the Twin Cities were far and it was hard to get a monk for ceremonies ([Grand Forks Herald, 2015](https://www.grandforksherald.com/lifestyle/a-buddhist-temple-thrives-in-rural-minnesota); [The Globe, Apr. 25, 2022](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/locals-hope-to-turn-a-rural-worthington-acreage-into-a-temple-of-their-own)).

The temple is also where life's passages are marked. In Worthington in 2015, a family held a service at the temple 100 days after a death, followed by an evening gathering of about 500 people with a Laotian band from Minneapolis ([Grand Forks Herald, 2015](https://www.grandforksherald.com/lifestyle/a-buddhist-temple-thrives-in-rural-minnesota)). Elder advocate Banlang Phommasouvanh described the same "100 days of the dead tradition" in the Twin Cities ([Diverse Elders Coalition, 2015](https://diverseelders.org/2015/08/10/the-mongoose-and-the-bird-of-paradise-storytelling-to-help-older-lao-americans-thrive-in-minnesota/)). One temple leader said "some people go to the temple every weekend, and some do their own private worship at home" ([Grand Forks Herald, 2015](https://www.grandforksherald.com/lifestyle/a-buddhist-temple-thrives-in-rural-minnesota)).

**Christianity.** Many refugees were sponsored by churches, and Lao congregations followed. Lao Culture Dance Fashion MN grew out of youth programs at a Lao Lutheran Church ([MRAC, 2024](https://mrac.org/2024/05/22/grantee-spotlight-lao-culture-dance-fashion-mn/)). Nonprofit records list a Lao Evangelical Lutheran Church in Robbinsdale, Reborn Lao Lutheran Church in Waite Park, a Lutheran Laotian Ministry in St. Cloud and a Laotian Church of Peace ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/search?q=lao)). The Converge Baptist network lists the Laotian Church of Peace at 4800 Douglas Drive North ([Converge](https://converge.org/church/laotian-church-of-peace/)). A Lao congregation in the Christian Reformed Church left that denomination in 2020 ([CRCNA](https://crcna.org/churches/6608)). In 1989, a Dakota County Lao elders program worked closely with Peace Reform Church in Eagan ([1989 DHS report](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/2016/other/160681.pdf)). One Worthington temple leader had come to the United States in 1980 under the sponsorship of a Baptist family ([Grand Forks Herald, 2015](https://www.grandforksherald.com/lifestyle/a-buddhist-temple-thrives-in-rural-minnesota)).

**Differences and gaps.** No Minnesota source found gives the share of Lao Minnesotans who are Buddhist, Christian, of another faith or of no faith. Spirit practices alongside Buddhism are mentioned on the existing page, but no Minnesota source was found that documents them.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

**Lao New Year (Pi Mai Lao, Boun Pi Mai).** The traditional new year falls in mid-April. A Worthington temple leader said in 2015 that "April 15 is the actual date" but that the community would celebrate on May 2 at the Brewster American Legion Hall, with free food and traditional music ([Grand Forks Herald, 2015](https://www.grandforksherald.com/lifestyle/a-buddhist-temple-thrives-in-rural-minnesota)). In the Twin Cities, New Year was long hosted by the temples. In 2013, Chanida Phaengdara Potter said there had been years without a celebration and ceremonies "in gyms," and that younger leaders had brought more than 20 organizations together for a second annual community-wide celebration in Crystal, with a pageant ([MPR News, Apr. 14, 2013](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2013/04/14/lao-new-year-unites-groups-to-celebrate-together-for-first-time-in-minn)). The United Senior Lao American Association applied for a state history grant for "Lao New Year 2025 and 50th Commemoration of Lao Diaspora" ([Minnesota Historical Society applicant list, 2024](https://www.mnhs.org/sites/default/files/2024-11/fy25-sea50-hrac-applicant-list.pdf)). No 2026 Minnesota Lao New Year announcement was found.

**Boun Pha Vet (King Vessantara festival).** Lao Culture Dance Fashion MN uses state arts grants to support a "King Pavade Heritage Day," teaching youth the story of King Vessantara (Pha Vet) through dance. It also names a Miss Nang Sangkhan pageant and fashion shows as part of Lao cultural life here ([MRAC, 2024](https://mrac.org/2024/05/22/grantee-spotlight-lao-culture-dance-fashion-mn/)).

**Buddhist holy days.** Visakha Bucha, Makha Bucha and the rains retreat (Khao Phansa) are Theravada holy days on the Lao calendar. In 2015, the Worthington temple held about four large celebrations a year and an annual summer concert with outdoor games, but the article does not say which holy days they mark ([Grand Forks Herald, 2015](https://www.grandforksherald.com/lifestyle/a-buddhist-temple-thrives-in-rural-minnesota)). No source was found documenting how Minnesota temples mark each holy day or the That Luang festival.

**Lao Artists Heritage Month.** Former Governor Mark Dayton issued a "Lao Artists Heritage Month" proclamation honoring Saymoukda Duangphouxay Vongsay ([University of Minnesota CCAPS, 2022](https://ccaps.umn.edu/story/jai-khang-heart-hard)). Sahtu Press said the Laomerica 50 exhibit, which opened August 1, 2024, at the Minneapolis Central Library, began "during Lao American Artists Heritage Month" ([Sahtu Press, 2024](https://sahtu.press/articles/q7arce0k1hic2yopfa1ckda4g2su41)). The year of the proclamation was not found.

**Shared Southeast Asian events.** The SEAD Project's Colors of Southeast Asia Fest marked 50 years of Southeast Asian life in Minnesota on June 7, 2025, at Centennial Park in Brooklyn Center ([Hoodline, May 2025](https://hoodline.com/2025/05/minnesota-marks-50th-anniversary-of-colors-of-southeast-asia-fest-with-woven-theme-in-brooklyn-center/)), and SEAD lists a 2026 festival ([SEAD Project](https://www.theseadproject.org/)). SEAD's 15th-anniversary gala is set for November 21, 2026, at North Garden Theater in St. Paul ([SEAD Project](https://www.theseadproject.org/ror2026)). Lao Culture Dance Fashion MN also organizes a World Refugee Day festival ([MRAC, 2024](https://mrac.org/2024/05/22/grantee-spotlight-lao-culture-dance-fashion-mn/)).

**Lao National Day (December 2)** marks the founding of the current government in 1975, the year many Lao refugees fled. No Minnesota observance was found.

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Households.** Compass counts 4,733 Lao households with an average of 3.37 people. Married couples without children under 18 are 20.9 percent of households, married couples with children 10.6 percent, single mothers with children 15.9 percent, other family households 15.1 percent, people living alone 12.0 percent, and unmarried couples 21.8 percent. Margins are wide ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lao-non-hmong)). Nationally, 60 percent of Laotian immigrant adults are married, compared with 25 percent of U.S.-born adults, who are much younger ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-laotians-in-the-u-s/)).

**Elders.** A 1989 state report said that in Laos elders lived with the extended family and counseled their children, but that in Minnesota children "don't always have enough time or money" to provide for them ([1989 DHS report](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/2016/other/160681.pdf)). The United Senior Lao American Association, founded by elder leaders, helped older people travel to temples, visit the sick and dying, and pass on culture, food and dance. Its members piloted a skit in which elders and children speak to each other in Lao poetry ([Diverse Elders Coalition, 2015](https://diverseelders.org/2015/08/10/the-mongoose-and-the-bird-of-paradise-storytelling-to-help-older-lao-americans-thrive-in-minnesota/)).

**Food.** Sticky rice, eaten by hand from bamboo baskets, is the base of the table. Padaek, a thick fermented fish sauce, sets Lao dishes apart from Thai ones, and staples include larb, green papaya salad (tam mak hoong), khao poon noodle soup and sai oua sausage. Meals are often family style, and a common greeting is "Have you eaten yet?" ([Joysauce, 2024](https://joysauce.com/why-lao-food-is-booming-in-the-twin-cities/)). Writer Sanaphay Rattanavong recalled that growing up Lao in the 1980s and 1990s meant a lunch that "stands out" ([Joysauce, 2024](https://joysauce.com/why-lao-food-is-booming-in-the-twin-cities/)). Chanida Phaengdara Potter called Lao people "the original foodies" ([MPR News, Jan. 23, 2015](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2015/01/23/chanida-phaengdara-potter)). Gai Noi's website even teaches diners how to eat sticky rice ([Gai Noi](https://www.gainoimpls.com/)).

**Dress.** Traditional silks, including the sinh skirt and handwoven shawls, are worn for dance, pageants and festivals ([MRAC, 2024](https://mrac.org/2024/05/22/grantee-spotlight-lao-culture-dance-fashion-mn/); [Khâluna](https://khaluna.com/)). Sahtu Press credits a Minnesota family with helping preserve Lao weaving here ([Sahtu Press, 2024](https://sahtu.press/articles/q7arce0k1hic2yopfa1ckda4g2su41)).

**Childhood and growing up.** Nia Rasavong recalled being teased at school in Eagan as one of very few minority students, and the closeness of a family of daughters around her mother's cooking ([Power 104.7 FM, 2024](https://www.power1047.fm/post/ruam-mit-offers-food-culture-and-connection)). Chanida Phaengdara Potter's blog Little Laos on the Prairie, started in 2011, began with posts such as "10 Ways to be Lao in Minnesota" ([MPR News, 2015](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2015/01/23/chanida-phaengdara-potter)).

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Literature.** Bryan Thao Worra received a National Endowment for the Arts poetry fellowship in 2009 ([NEA](https://www.arts.gov/impact/literary-arts/creative-writing-fellows/bryan-thao-worra)). In 2025, he was leading a SEALit Center and Lao Assistance Center project, funded by the Minnesota Historical Society, that uses poetry and prose to record 50 years of Lao refugee stories. He noted that fewer than 50 books had been written by the community "in our own words" ([MPR News, Apr. 30, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2025/04/30/literary-project-shares-history-of-lao-minnesotans)). The SatJaDham Lao Literary Project held its sixth conference at the University of Minnesota in 2001, and Minneapolis hosted the first two Lao American Writers Summits, in 2010 and 2015 ([Sahtu Press, 2024](https://sahtu.press/articles/q7arce0k1hic2yopfa1ckda4g2su41)). In April 2015, the first National Lao American Symposium met at the University of Minnesota's Urban Research and Outreach Center ([Sahtu Press, 2015](https://sahtu.press/articles/national-lao-american-symposium-and-writers-summit-2015)).

**Theater.** Saymoukda Duangphouxay Vongsay, born in a refugee camp in Nong Khai, Thailand, became a Mellon Foundation playwright in residence at Theater Mu in 2020 ([Theater Mu](https://www.theatermu.org/news/theater-mu-and-twin-cities-based-artist-saymoukda-duangphouxay-vongsay-receive-prestigious-mellon-grant)). Her play Kung Fu Zombies Vs. Cannibals has been staged around the country, and she received a Sally Award from the Ordway in 2019 ([CCAPS, 2022](https://ccaps.umn.edu/story/jai-khang-heart-hard)). The two sources give her arrival in Minnesota as 1984 and 1985.

**Exhibits.** Laomerica 50: Democracy and Diaspora showed more than 90 objects at Minneapolis Central Library from August 1 to September 30, 2024, including a Lao-keyboard typewriter used in the refugee camps ([Sahtu Press, 2024](https://sahtu.press/articles/q7arce0k1hic2yopfa1ckda4g2su41)). Laomagination, a 20-year retrospective of Lao speculative art, ran in North Minneapolis in 2019 ([Sahtu Press, 2019](https://sahtu.press/articles/laomagination-20-year-retrospective-a-success)). Refuge of the InvisibLao opened at Vine Arts Center in 2015 ([MPR News, 2015](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2015/01/23/chanida-phaengdara-potter)).

**Dance and traditional arts.** Lao Culture Dance Fashion MN, founded in 2018 by Birdy Niphone Phommaras, trains youth in Lao dance; she first trained 15 Lao students from Roosevelt High School in 1991 ([MRAC, 2024](https://mrac.org/2024/05/22/grantee-spotlight-lao-culture-dance-fashion-mn/)). For Siengkane Lao MN, based in Brooklyn Park, the 2023 Legislature appropriated $50,000 in each of the next two fiscal years from the Legacy arts fund "to create cultural arts projects and to preserve traditional performances" ([Laws of Minnesota 2023, ch. 40](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/laws/2023/0/Session+Law/Chapter/40/)). The Lao Assistance Center started a Lao Culture Camp in 2017 and earlier taught Lao language and traditional dance ([Lao Assistance Center, About](https://www.laocenter.org/about)). The SEAD Project, founded in 2011 and based in Minneapolis, runs heritage language, storytelling and leadership programs for Hmong, Khmer, Lao and Vietnamese communities ([SEAD Project, About](https://www.theseadproject.org/about)).

**Media.** No Lao-language newspaper, radio or TV station based in Minnesota was found. Christina Tia's Lao cooking channel on YouTube, begun in 2013, is credited with helping young Lao Americans reconnect with their food ([Joysauce, 2024](https://joysauce.com/why-lao-food-is-booming-in-the-twin-cities/)).

**Sports.** The Lao Advancement Organization of America lists a Lao Minnesota Soccer Organization among its health programs ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Lao-Advancement-Organization-Of-America)). No current Minnesota Lao tournament could be confirmed.

## 9. Civic and political life

**Organizations.** The Lao Assistance Center of Minnesota (founded 1983, North Minneapolis) offers food help, system navigation, housing stability, health programs and civic engagement, including outreach on the Blue Line Extension light rail project ([Lao Assistance Center, What We Do](https://www.laocenter.org/what-we-do)). Since April 21, 2026, Hennepin County navigation staff have held appointments at its office ([Lao Assistance Center, Resources](https://www.laocenter.org/resources)). It held a community gathering on June 17, 2026, to hear ideas about the future of its services ([Lao Assistance Center, Events](https://www.laocenter.org/event-calendar)). The Lao Advancement Organization of America, tax-exempt since 1995, reported $352,475 in revenue in 2024 ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/411651825)) and describes youth, elder, citizenship and English programs ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Lao-Advancement-Organization-Of-America)). Other groups include Siengkane Lao MN, Lao Culture Dance Fashion MN, the United Senior Lao American Association and the University of Minnesota's Lao Student Association.

**Public voice.** In 2025, MPR News described Bryan Thao Worra as chair of the community board of the Council on Asian Pacific Minnesotans ([MPR News, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2025/04/30/literary-project-shares-history-of-lao-minnesotans)). Siengkane Lao MN made its case for Legacy funding to a Senate committee in March 2025 ([Senate handout, 2025](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2025/envlegacy/20250325/envlegacy_20250325_SF2543-Siengkane-Lao-handout.pdf)). In 2026, Representatives Lee and Xiong carried HF 3887, which would give $160,000 in fiscal 2027 for Lao dance and fashion education; the bill page lists no action after its referral to the Legacy Finance committee on March 2, 2026 ([Revisor of Statutes](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/bills/94/2026/0/HF/3887/?body=house)). Minnesota law lets veterans of the Secret War in Laos be buried in state veterans cemeteries ([Minn. Stat. 197.236](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/197.236)).

**Elected officials.** No Lao American elected official in Minnesota was found.

**Debates.** The sharpest current issue is deportation (section 13). In 2020, Rep. Betty McCollum opposed a planned repatriation deal with Laos after the Coalition of Asian American Leaders warned that about 700 Minnesotans could be at risk ([Forum News Service, 2020](https://www.inforum.com/news/mccollum-trump-administration-set-to-deport-minnesota-hmong-lao-refugees); [CAAL, Feb. 7, 2020](https://caalmn.org/2020/02/07/lao-hmong-deportation/)). A second recurring theme is visibility. Bryan Thao Worra has said the community does not see itself in history books ([MPR News, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2025/04/30/literary-project-shares-history-of-lao-minnesotans)), and Minnesota Compass argues that grouping all Asian Minnesotans together hides differences among communities such as Lao and Hmong ([Minnesota Compass, May 30, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-asian-communities)).

## 10. Education

Among Lao Minnesotans 25 and older, 78.9 percent finished high school and 15.7 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lao-non-hmong)). Nationally, Laotian immigrants are less likely than the U.S.-born to hold a bachelor's degree (16 percent against 24 percent) ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-laotians-in-the-u-s/)).

Heritage education happens mostly outside school. The Lao Assistance Center ran tutoring, homework help and Lao language classes from its early years, and youth leadership and a culture camp today ([Lao Assistance Center, About](https://www.laocenter.org/about)). The Lao Advancement Organization of America lists homework help, Lao language and dance instruction, an introduction to higher education and an urban 4-H program ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Lao-Advancement-Organization-Of-America)). The SEAD Project offers heritage language workshops ([University of Minnesota, 2026](https://cla.umn.edu/liberal-arts-engagement-hub/news-events/story/southeast-asian-diaspora-project-honors-50-years-history-through-archival-storytelling)).

At the University of Minnesota Twin Cities, the Lao Student Association says it aims to help students "embrace their identity with pride" ([GopherLink](https://gopherlink.umn.edu/0167/home/)), and the Asian American Studies Program lists it among partner groups ([University of Minnesota](https://cla.umn.edu/asianamerican/undergraduate/organizations)). The university's Immigration History Research Center co-hosted the 2015 national Lao American symposium ([Sahtu Press, 2015](https://sahtu.press/articles/national-lao-american-symposium-and-writers-summit-2015)). No Minnesota school with a Lao language program was found.

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

The Lao Assistance Center began health work in 2005, after community forums named tobacco use and mental health as pressing concerns. It now offers culturally specific tobacco cessation, in-language mental health outreach and navigation, breast and cervical cancer screening, hepatitis B awareness, immunizations, colon cancer education, overdose prevention, problem gambling education and Long COVID support ([Lao Assistance Center, About](https://www.laocenter.org/about); [What We Do](https://www.laocenter.org/what-we-do)). It hosted a mobile mammogram day on May 20, 2026 ([Lao Assistance Center, Events](https://www.laocenter.org/event-calendar)).

About 6.1 percent of Lao Minnesotans lack health insurance ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lao-non-hmong)). Language is a barrier that sources raise again and again. A Diverse Elders Coalition fact sheet for Southeast Asian Americans nationally, which does not give a Lao-only figure, reports limited English proficiency of 40 to 50 percent, up to 90 percent among elders, and links war trauma and deportations to distrust of formal services ([Diverse Elders Coalition, Jan. 2023](https://www.caregiving.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/DEC-FactSheet-SEA-American-0123.pdf)). The 1989 state report said social workers saw "serious mental health problems" among Southeast Asian refugee elders ([1989 DHS report](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/2016/other/160681.pdf)). In 2026, the managing director of ROOTS Laos described a "severe mental health crisis" among people deported to Laos ([MPR News, Apr. 29, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/04/29/at-ricky-chandee-ice-detention-deportation-future-uncertain)). No Minnesota source was found documenting traditional Lao healing practices here.

## 12. Disability

**Minnesota numbers.** Compass reports that 10.2 percent of Lao Minnesotans have a disability, give or take 3.3 points, and 7.1 percent of those under 65, give or take 2.8 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lao-non-hmong)). With margins this wide, these survey figures cannot show whether Lao Minnesotans differ from other groups.

**What else exists.** No Minnesota study, Minnesota Department of Health or Department of Human Services report, or University of Minnesota thesis focused on disability among ethnic Lao Minnesotans was found. The Lao Assistance Center's program list does not name disability services ([Lao Assistance Center](https://www.laocenter.org/what-we-do)). The Lao Advancement Organization of America, which says it serves all generations, describes health and isolation-reduction programs but not disability services ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Lao-Advancement-Organization-Of-America)). In the state's English learner report, Lao is not among the languages broken out ([MDE, 2024-25](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/other/261381.pdf)). The national Diverse Elders Coalition survey of Southeast Asian American family caregivers found 51 percent said they were the only person available to give care ([Diverse Elders Coalition, Jan. 2023](https://www.caregiving.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/DEC-FactSheet-SEA-American-0123.pdf)).

**Questions the evidence raises, not answers.**
- Who names disability in a Lao family, and in which language? No Minnesota source gives Lao words for disability.
- Elders who did years of factory and plant work (section 3) and refugees who lived through war and camps are both present. Sources describe this history but do not link it to any rate of impairment here.
- The state lets veterans of the Secret War in Laos be buried in state veterans cemeteries ([Minn. Stat. 197.236](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/197.236)). Whether Lao veterans can reach other veterans' services was not found.
- For people facing deportation, health needs can become a legal question. In August 2026, a 52-year-old Rochester-area man facing removal to Laos told FOX 9 that losing access to his medications "can be a death sentence," and a Mayo Clinic doctor wrote in support of his pardon request ([FOX 9, Aug. 7, 2026](https://www.fox9.com/news/mn-clemency-review-commission-considering-case-man-facing-deportaion-aug-7-2026)).

## 13. What is changing now

**Deportation.** For years, deportations to Laos were rare. Since 1998, the United States had deported 219 people to Laos, five of them in 2019, according to a 2020 coalition statement that also reported a verbal U.S.-Laos agreement in 2019 ([CAAL, 2020](https://caalmn.org/2020/02/07/lao-hmong-deportation/)). In March 2025, Sahan Journal reported that Laos had no repatriation agreement and that ICE reported no deportations there between October 2023 and September 2024. It added that Hmong and other minority groups are counted as Lao nationals too ([Sahan Journal, Mar. 28, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/trump-deportations-southeast-asian-hmong-laos-thailand-vietnam-cambodia/)). That changed in 2025. A coalition of ManForward, the Southeast Asian Freedom Network, the Immigrant Law Center of Minnesota and Transforming Generations said 2025 saw "the highest level of Hmong and Lao deportations in the last 15 years," and SEAFN offers a 24-hour line in Lao, Hmong, Khmer and Vietnamese ([MPR News, Feb. 24, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/02/24/southeast-asian-minnesotans-deportation-and-detention)). MPR News, citing Deportation Data Project figures, reported that about 90 people born in Laos and arrested in Minnesota were detained by ICE between December 1 and mid-March, at least 11 were released, and 10 people were deported to Laos in October and November 2025. These figures count everyone born in Laos, not only ethnic Lao ([MPR News, Apr. 29, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/04/29/at-ricky-chandee-ice-detention-deportation-future-uncertain)).

Two cases drew statewide attention. On May 4, 2026, the Board of Pardons met in an emergency session and pardoned a man born in Laos, convicted of assault in 1993 at age 18, a day before his possible deportation ([KSTP, May 4, 2026](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/minnesota-board-pardons-laos-refugee-day-before-possible-deportation/)). His wife lives in Brooklyn Park ([MPR News, Apr. 29, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/04/29/at-ricky-chandee-ice-detention-deportation-future-uncertain)). In August 2026, the Clemency Review Commission considered the Rochester-area case described in section 12 ([FOX 9, 2026](https://www.fox9.com/news/mn-clemency-review-commission-considering-case-man-facing-deportaion-aug-7-2026)). Family members told MPR News of group chats of detainees' wives and of rumors about flights ([MPR News, Apr. 29, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/04/29/at-ricky-chandee-ice-detention-deportation-future-uncertain)).

**Fifty years.** 2025 marked 50 years since 1975. Lao groups sought Minnesota Historical Society grants for projects such as "Lao Americans: Our Stories at 50" ([MNHS applicant list](https://www.mnhs.org/sites/default/files/2024-11/fy25-sea50-hrac-applicant-list.pdf)), and Bryan Thao Worra planned exhibits in early 2026 ([MPR News, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2025/04/30/literary-project-shares-history-of-lao-minnesotans)).

**Generations.** Fewer than half of Lao Minnesotans (47.1 percent) were born abroad in the 2020-2024 data, and a third speak only English at home ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lao-non-hmong)). Youth camps, dance classes and the college student association all describe their work as connecting younger generations with Lao roots ([Lao Assistance Center](https://www.laocenter.org/about); [MRAC, 2024](https://mrac.org/2024/05/22/grantee-spotlight-lao-culture-dance-fashion-mn/); [GopherLink](https://gopherlink.umn.edu/0167/home/)).

**Food and visibility.** Khâluna has drawn national acclaim ([Joysauce, 2024](https://joysauce.com/why-lao-food-is-booming-in-the-twin-cities/)), and Gai Noi and Soul Lao appear on a 2026 Twin Cities best-restaurant list ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine, 2026](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/the-best-restaurants-in-the-twin-cities-2026/)).

**Institutions in transition.** The Lao Assistance Center asked the community in June 2026 how its services should change ([Lao Assistance Center, Events](https://www.laocenter.org/event-calendar)). The website of the Lao Arts and Culture Collective, linked from the existing page, was not working on October 31, 2026.

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **Institution building.** Ethnic Lao refugees founded the Lao Assistance Center in 1983, and it has served Lao Minnesotans for more than 40 years ([Lao Assistance Center](https://www.laocenter.org/about)).
- **Greater Minnesota.** Nearly a third of Lao Minnesotans live outside the Twin Cities ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lao-non-hmong)), and southwestern families built Worthington's first Buddhist temple ([The Globe, 2022](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/locals-hope-to-turn-a-rural-worthington-acreage-into-a-temple-of-their-own)).
- **Work and ownership.** Most working-age adults are employed and most households own their homes ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lao-non-hmong)).
- **Arts and letters.** Minnesota has been a national center of Lao American writing, theater and exhibition, from the first Lao American Writers Summit to Laomerica 50 ([Sahtu Press, 2024](https://sahtu.press/articles/q7arce0k1hic2yopfa1ckda4g2su41)).
- **Food.** Lao chefs have helped make Lao food recognized in its own name ([Joysauce, 2024](https://joysauce.com/why-lao-food-is-booming-in-the-twin-cities/)).
- **Caring across generations.** Elders teach youth through story and poetry, and youth groups seek out elders ([Diverse Elders Coalition, 2015](https://diverseelders.org/2015/08/10/the-mongoose-and-the-bird-of-paradise-storytelling-to-help-older-lao-americans-thrive-in-minnesota/)).
