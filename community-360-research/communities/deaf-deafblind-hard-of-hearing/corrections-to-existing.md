# Corrections to the existing Deaf, DeafBlind and Hard of Hearing page

Page: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/deaf-deafblind-hard-of-hearing
Checked: October 31, 2026. Each item gives the claim as stated, what the opened source says, the link, and the date checked.

## Errors, outdated claims, or claims that should change

1. **Claim (Hard of hearing and late-deafened experience):** "We found no Minnesota figure with a usable denominator."
   **Source says:** The Minnesota State Demographic Center (Feb. 2026), using 2023 ACS one-year data, counts about 203,500 Minnesotans with a hearing disability ("deaf or serious difficulty hearing") within a state disability total of about 667,700 (11.5 percent of the population), with counts by age (about 132,000 are 65 or older). Minnesota Compass (July 14, 2026, 2024 ACS) reports 13 percent of Minnesotans 65+ with hearing limitations. These are self-reported and do not identify signers, but they are Minnesota figures with denominators. The national NIDCD figure the page uses can be kept as background.
   **Links:** https://mn.gov/admin/assets/accessible%20MN_MinnesotansWithDisabilities_tcm36-734001.pdf ; https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/featured-trend/few-things-know-about-minnesotans-disabilities . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

2. **Claim (Disability and access, opening paragraph):** "We did not find ... anything on faith or traditional healing among them."
   **Source says:** Minnesota sources on faith life exist: Bread of Life Deaf Lutheran in Minneapolis (founded 1956, worships in ASL); the Archdiocese of St. Paul and Minneapolis's deaf ministry at Our Lady of Mount Carmel (about 50 of 170 members deaf or hard of hearing in 2019); and the Minnesota Deaf Muslim Community's 2024 Deaf Iftar with Tarawih prayer. Nothing was found on traditional healing, so that half of the sentence stands.
   **Links:** https://www.givemn.org/organization/Bread-Of-Life-Deaf-Lutheran-Church ; https://thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/blind-father-deaf-daughter-share-lectoring-ministry/ ; https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/03/31/photos-deaf-community-celebrates-ramadans-end-with-iftar . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

3. **Claim (Deaf immigrants and refugees):** "We found no U.S. or Minnesota study, and culture-specific sources for Minnesota's Somali, Hmong, Karen, Latino and other Deaf residents are missing."
   **Source says:** Still no Minnesota study was found, but Minnesota evidence exists and could be cited: 11 percent of students with a deaf or hard of hearing primary label (2024-25) also receive English learner services (MDE, 2026); 12 percent are Hispanic or Latino and 11 percent Asian. The Commission's 2025-2030 Collaborative Plan (Wilder Research) records professionals saying Somali, Hmong and Spanish-speaking families face added barriers. The Minnesota Deaf Muslim Community is an organized group serving Deaf Muslims.
   **Links:** https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261028.pdf ; https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf ; https://www.tptoriginals.org/muslim-shero-valerie-shirley-empowers-the-deaf-community/ . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

4. **Claim (Who to involve):** "Minnesota's Deaf and Hard of Hearing Services Division as a resource."
   **Source says:** The statute now names it the Deaf, DeafBlind, and Hard of Hearing State Services Division (the page uses the current name elsewhere). The older name should be updated for consistency.
   **Link:** https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/256C.233 . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

5. **Claim (Metro Deaf School):** "founded in 1992."
   **Source says:** The governor's 2024 proclamation says it was "established in 1992" as the second charter school to open in the United States; the school's own history page says it has led "since before the doors opened in 1993." Suggest "established 1992, opened 1993."
   **Links:** https://mn.gov/governor/assets/04.01.24%20Deaf%20History%20Month_SIgned_tcm1055-620392.pdf ; https://www.mdsmn.org/our-story . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

6. **Claim (Minnesota State Academy for the Deaf):** Lists only Noyes Hall (1975) on the National Register.
   **Source says:** The 2024 proclamation says MSAD "has two buildings listed in the National Register of Historic Places: the Administration and Girl's Dormitory and Noyes Hall." Not an error, but incomplete.
   **Link:** https://mn.gov/governor/assets/04.01.24%20Deaf%20History%20Month_SIgned_tcm1055-620392.pdf . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

7. **Claim (Charles Thompson Memorial Hall, in Olof Hanson profile):** MNopedia calls it the first U.S. social club designed exclusively for deaf people; the Council on Disability calls it the nation's first Deaf club.
   **Source says:** Both confirmed. The hall's own site goes further, calling it "the first clubhouse for the deaf in the world." The page may want to note the hall's own claim as its own, and keep MNopedia's narrower wording as the reference.
   **Links:** https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/structure/charles-thompson-memorial-hall-st-paul ; https://charlesthompsonhall.org/ . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

8. **Claim (DHS division):** "Its governing law, section 256C.233, dates from 1996."
   **Source says:** Correct for that section, but DHS's 2023 biennial report says the Legislature created the Deaf and Hard of Hearing Services Act (sections 256C.21 to 256C.26) and the division in 1980. Suggest adding the 1980 origin.
   **Links:** https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/256C.233 ; https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2023/Mandated/231530.pdf . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

## Claims checked and supported

- Commission created 1985 (first special session), renamed 1993 and 2008; ten at-large members plus up to five advisory-committee members; at least 50 percent of voting members deaf, deafblind or hard of hearing; at least one parent or guardian (Minn. Stat. 256C.28; Legislative Reference Library, checked Oct. 31, 2026).
- Section 256C.233 history begins in 1996 and was last amended in 2024 (revisor history line), and it requires culturally and linguistically affirmative mental health services for visual-language users.
- Minnesota State Academy for the Deaf opened in 1863 in a rented store building; signing always permitted; bilingual ASL-English approach; 1993 student call for a Deaf administrator (MPR News, Sept. 12, 2023).
- Blanche Wilkins graduated in 1893, the first Black Deaf woman to graduate from the Minnesota school, and was refused admission to Gallaudet because of race (MSAD Alumni Spotlight, checked Oct. 31, 2026).
- Petra Fandrem Howard became chief of the Labor Bureau for the Deaf in 1915 and was first woman president of the Minnesota Association of the Deaf, 1941-46 (RIT Libraries).
- Nic Zapko Day declared March 9, 2021 (WCCO/CBS Minnesota, March 9, 2021).
- Bread of Life Deaf Lutheran founded 1956 in the Seward and Longfellow area (GiveMN).
- Minnesota DeafBlind Association since 1979, only DeafBlind-led 501(c)(3) in Minnesota (MDBA site).
- DeafBlind Services Minnesota is part of Vision Loss Resources, Little Canada (VLR site).
- Minnesota DeafBlind Project, Arden Hills, free help from birth up to age 22 (project site).
- Lifetrack merged into Lutheran Social Service of Minnesota in July 2021 (DHS 2023 report).
- HLAA Twin Cities has a current October 2026 newsletter and Zoom meetings (chapter site).
- MRID was founded in 1971 with the support of MADC (MRID site).
- Governor's April 2024 proclamation names the St. Paul/Minneapolis Black Deaf Advocates and the other listed groups.
- The Protactile origin in Seattle in 2007 is supported by the Christian Science Monitor (Jan. 15, 2020).
