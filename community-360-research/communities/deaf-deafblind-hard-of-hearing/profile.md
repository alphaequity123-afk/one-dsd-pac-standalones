# Deaf, DeafBlind and Hard of Hearing Minnesotans

This page describes three overlapping groups. Deaf people who use American Sign Language (ASL) share a language and a culture with its own clubs, schools, churches, humor and history. DeafBlind people live with combined hearing and vision loss; some use tactile signing or Protactile, a touch-based language, and their needs and leadership are their own. Hard of hearing and late-deafened people, the largest group by far, mostly use spoken English with hearing aids, cochlear implants, captions or assistive listening, and many never learn to sign. Minnesota's state bodies name all three in their titles and laws ([Minn. Stat. 256C.28](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/256C.28)). Where a fact applies to only one group, this page says so.

## 1. People and place

**How many.** No single count exists, because the available numbers measure different things.

- The American Community Survey (ACS) asks whether a person is "deaf or has serious difficulty hearing." The Minnesota State Demographic Center, using 2023 ACS one-year data, counts about 203,500 Minnesotans with a hearing disability, out of about 667,700 with any disability (11.5 percent of the state population). By age, that is about 2,000 children under 5, 5,000 ages 5 to 17, 64,000 ages 18 to 64 and 132,000 ages 65 and older ([State Demographic Center, Feb. 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/accessible%20MN_MinnesotansWithDisabilities_tcm36-734001.pdf)). These are self-reported survey estimates. They do not say who signs, who identifies as Deaf, or who is DeafBlind.
- Minnesota Compass, using 2024 ACS data, reports that 13 percent of Minnesotans 65 and older report hearing limitations ([Minnesota Compass, July 14, 2026](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/featured-trend/few-things-know-about-minnesotans-disabilities)).
- State officials use a much broader figure. The Commission of the Deaf, DeafBlind and Hard of Hearing has described its constituency as "20 percent of Minnesotans," as House Session Daily quoted in 2018 ([Minnesota House Session Daily, 2018](https://www.house.mn.gov/SessionDaily/Story/13317)), and a 2025 state vocational rehabilitation training says "1 in 5 Minnesotans experience some type of hearing loss" ([DEED Vocational Rehabilitation Services, Jan. 2025](https://mn.gov/deed/assets/january-2025-vr-community-learning-connections_tcm1045-672408.pdf)). Neither source gives its method. The ACS counts only "serious difficulty," while the 20 percent figure counts any hearing loss, so the two describe different groups.
- No count was found of Minnesotans who use ASL as a main language, or of DeafBlind adults statewide. For children, the Minnesota Department of Education (MDE) counted 2,552 children and youth from birth to 22 whose primary disability was deaf or hard of hearing in 2024-25, and 125 whose primary disability was DeafBlind, plus about 250 more who met eligibility for both hearing and vision services but had a different primary label ([MDE biennial report, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261028.pdf)). A national census counted 379 DeafBlind children and youth in Minnesota in 2019 ([Wilder Research, June 2021](https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/pdf-file/MNCDHH_SupportingDeafBlindChildrenAndYouth_Report_6-21.pdf)).

**Where.** Of the 2,162 K-12 students with deaf or hard of hearing as a primary disability in 2024-25, 1,193 were in the Twin Cities region. The highest share of any region was in southeastern Minnesota (Region 10), where 0.39 percent of students carried that label, against 0.26 percent statewide ([MDE, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261028.pdf)). The report does not explain the southeastern figure. Faribault, home of the state academy since 1863, is the community's historic center outside the metro ([MPR News, Sept. 12, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2023/09/12/how-deaf-education-has-changed-in-minnesota-over-160-years)), and one parent in a 2025 focus group said families move to their town "just to be near the deaf school" ([Commission Collaborative Plan, Aug. 2025](https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf)). In the Twin Cities, community life gathers at Charles Thompson Memorial Hall on Marshall Avenue in St. Paul, Metro Deaf School in St. Paul, and congregations in Minneapolis and St. Paul (sections 4, 5 and 10). The state's regional services for these Minnesotans were based in Duluth, Hibbing, Mankato, Moorhead, Rochester, St. Cloud and St. Paul in 2021-22 ([DHS biennial report, Aug. 2023](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2023/Mandated/231530.pdf)).

**How people come to the community.** Most people do not inherit Deaf culture from parents. The state education report notes that most deaf and hard of hearing children are born to families who use spoken language and do not know sign language ([MDE, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261028.pdf)). Parents in 2025 focus groups described meeting Deaf mentors, other families and Deaf schools as their way in (section 7). Hearing loss also arrives with age: of the roughly 203,500 Minnesotans the ACS counts, about 132,000 are 65 or older ([State Demographic Center, 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/accessible%20MN_MinnesotansWithDisabilities_tcm36-734001.pdf)). Some families are Deaf across generations; one St. Paul archdiocesan parish story in 2019 described a Bloomington family with four generations of Deaf members ([The Catholic Spirit, Feb. 5, 2019](https://thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/blind-father-deaf-daughter-share-lectoring-ministry/)).

**Languages.** ASL is the language of Deaf community life, schools such as the state academy and Metro Deaf School, and many churches and clubs. Protactile, which began among DeafBlind people in Seattle in 2007 ([Christian Science Monitor, Jan. 15, 2020](https://www.csmonitor.com/USA/Society/2020/0115/In-one-revolutionary-language-a-community-taps-the-power-of-touch-video)), is taught in Minnesota; state grants paid for Protactile training for DeafBlind people, interpreters, interveners and support service providers in 2021 and 2022 ([DHS, 2023](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2023/Mandated/231530.pdf)). Deaf Minnesotans from immigrant families may use another country's sign language or come to ASL late. Eleven percent of students with a deaf or hard of hearing primary label also received English learner services in 2024-25 ([MDE, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261028.pdf)). No Minnesota study of Black ASL, regional signing or home sign systems was found.

**Diversity within.** Among students with a deaf or hard of hearing label in 2024-25, 61 percent were white, 12 percent Hispanic or Latino, 11 percent Asian, 9 percent Black, 6 percent two or more races, and 1 percent American Indian. Among DeafBlind students, 56 percent were white, 16 percent Black, 11 percent Asian and 10 percent Hispanic or Latino ([MDE, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261028.pdf)). Community organizations reflect this: the governor's 2024 Deaf History Month proclamation names the St. Paul/Minneapolis Black Deaf Advocates, the Minnesota Deaf Muslim Community, Deaf Women of Minnesota and Minnesota Deaf Queers among the groups serving the community ([Governor's proclamation, April 2024](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/04.01.24%20Deaf%20History%20Month_SIgned_tcm1055-620392.pdf)).

## 2. All walks of life

No Minnesota source gives income or wealth for Deaf signers as a group. The closest measures are ACS figures for people who report a hearing disability, which mix lifelong Deaf signers with many older adults who lost hearing late. Read them with that limit in mind.

- **People who are doing well.** Among the six disability types the ACS measures, Minnesotans with a hearing disability have the lowest poverty rate: 12 percent of adults with a hearing difficulty lived below the poverty line in 2023, against 19 percent of all adults with a disability and 8 percent of people without one ([State Demographic Center, Feb. 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/accessible%20MN_MinnesotansWithDisabilities_tcm36-734001.pdf)). Among working-age adults with a hearing difficulty, 51 percent worked full-time, year-round in 2023, the highest share of any disability type (same source).
- **Professionals and leaders.** Deaf Minnesotans have long held professional roles. Olof Hanson, a graduate of the Faribault school whom the academy's director called "a famous architect in town," designed Charles Thompson Memorial Hall ([MPR News, Sept. 12, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2023/09/12/how-deaf-education-has-changed-in-minnesota-over-160-years)); Petra Fandrem Howard ran the state's Labor Bureau for the Deaf from 1915 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/structure/charles-thompson-memorial-hall-st-paul); [RIT Libraries](https://infoguides.rit.edu/deafsuffragists/howard)). Today, the state academy's director told MPR News in 2023 that the superintendent, both principals at the deaf school, the dorm director and all of its teachers were Deaf ([MPR News, Sept. 12, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2023/09/12/how-deaf-education-has-changed-in-minnesota-over-160-years)). Bobbi Cordano, a former Minnesota assistant attorney general and Wilder Foundation vice president from St. Paul, became president of Gallaudet University in 2015 ([Minnesota Council on Disability, April 14, 2022](https://www.disability.state.mn.us/2022/04/14/a-perspective-on-deaf-history-in-minnesota/)).
- **Wealth and philanthropy in the community's history.** Charles Thompson, son of a wealthy St. Paul banker, and his wife Margaret Brooks Thompson, both Deaf, opened their properties to Deaf gatherings. After his death Margaret used her inheritance to build the St. Paul hall and placed it, with a $45,000 trust, under a board so that it would stay free to all Deaf Minnesotans ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/structure/charles-thompson-memorial-hall-st-paul); [Governor's proclamation, April 2024](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/04.01.24%20Deaf%20History%20Month_SIgned_tcm1055-620392.pdf)).
- **Working families and small business owners.** The Minnesota Association of Deaf Citizens keeps a short directory of Deaf-owned businesses, including a barbecue caterer, a baker and a woodworker (section 4) ([MADC Deaf Business Directory](https://www.minndeaf.org/deaf-business-directory)). Young Deaf and hard of hearing workers coming out of state vocational rehabilitation between 2021 and 2023 most often went into food preparation and serving, transportation and material moving, and production jobs, at an average of $16.84 an hour and 28.6 hours a week across 84 participants ([Commission Collaborative Plan, Aug. 2025](https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf)).
- **Hardship.** One in four working-age adults with a hearing difficulty did not work at all in 2023 ([State Demographic Center](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/accessible%20MN_MinnesotansWithDisabilities_tcm36-734001.pdf)). DHS writes that barriers to communication can leave gaps in language and in the "fund of information" people gain from everyday conversation, making it harder to navigate daily life ([DHS, 2023](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2023/Mandated/231530.pdf)); ThinkSelf, a St. Paul nonprofit, offers adult classes in reading, writing, sign language, math and technology and advocacy for Deaf, DeafBlind and hard of hearing survivors of domestic violence and crime ([ThinkSelf](https://thinkself.org/)). The Minnesota Deaf Muslim Community, co-founded by ASL teacher Valerie Shirley, hosted a 2024 Deaf Iftar in St. Paul that paired a fast-breaking meal with a presentation on culturally and linguistically appropriate services ([TPT Originals](https://www.tptoriginals.org/muslim-shero-valerie-shirley-empowers-the-deaf-community/); [MPR News, March 31, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/03/31/photos-deaf-community-celebrates-ramadans-end-with-iftar)). Deaf people in jails have described being cut off from phones, meals and announcements; a 2024 settlement required the state Department of Corrections to send announcements to the pagers given to deaf and hard of hearing inmates ([MPR News, July 10, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2024/07/10/understanding-the-rights-of-deaf-and-hard-of-hearing-people-in-prison-and-jail)).
- **Older adults on fixed incomes.** About 132,000 of the Minnesotans with a hearing disability are 65 or older ([State Demographic Center](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/accessible%20MN_MinnesotansWithDisabilities_tcm36-734001.pdf)). Charles Thompson Hall prices its senior Halloween lunch at $12 and runs weekly coffee mornings, and the 2024 proclamation lists a Minnesota Deaf Senior Citizens group ([Charles Thompson Hall events](https://charlesthompsonhall.org/events/); [Governor's proclamation, 2024](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/04.01.24%20Deaf%20History%20Month_SIgned_tcm1055-620392.pdf)).

## 3. Work and the economy

**A long labor history.** Minnesota created a Labor Bureau for the Deaf in 1915, and its first chief, Petra Fandrem Howard, spent more than 40 years in employment services for Deaf workers ([RIT Libraries](https://infoguides.rit.edu/deafsuffragists/howard)). The state academy once trained students for printing, woodworking and mechanical trades; its school psychologist told MPR News in 2023 that it now teaches technology, coding and robotics ([MPR News, Sept. 12, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2023/09/12/how-deaf-education-has-changed-in-minnesota-over-160-years)).

**Who works now.** In 2023, among Minnesotans ages 18 to 64 with a hearing difficulty, 51 percent worked full-time and year-round, 23 percent worked less than that, and 25 percent did not work; the Demographic Center calls the hearing group the most likely of any disability type to have worked in the past year ([State Demographic Center, Feb. 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/accessible%20MN_MinnesotansWithDisabilities_tcm36-734001.pdf)). These figures include many people with mild or late hearing loss; no Minnesota employment figure for ASL users alone was found.

**Young workers.** The state Vocational Rehabilitation Services program served 165 students with deaf or hard of hearing as a primary or secondary disability in 2023, up from 148 in 2021. In 2023, 72 percent of them received at least one pre-employment transition service, and half took part in work-based learning. Among VR students who graduated in 2024, 52 continued with VR after graduation and 20 enrolled in college or graduate school ([Commission Collaborative Plan, Aug. 2025](https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf)).

**The access economy.** A sizable part of the community's economy is communication access itself: ASL interpreters, Certified Deaf Interpreters, captioners, Deaf mentors, interveners and support service providers. ASL Interpreting Services in Golden Valley says it contracts with more than 300 interpreters and has a Deaf Advisory Board ([ASLIS](https://www.aslis.com/)). Keystone Interpreting worked with Deaf interpreters Nic Zapko and JP Beldon on the governor's pandemic briefings, where Deaf interpreters signed to the public while hearing interpreters fed them the English ([WCCO/CBS Minnesota, 2020](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/asl-interpreters-praised-for-delivering-key-messages-to-deaf-community-during-pandemic/)). The state paid ASLIS $290,966 a year in 2021 and 2022 to build interpreting capacity in Greater Minnesota ([DHS, Aug. 2023](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2023/Mandated/231530.pdf)). Workforce shortages run through state reports: a 2026 education report calls the interpreter shortage "critical" and describes the Commission's Interpreting Forward 2030 plan ([MDE, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261028.pdf)), and professionals in 2025 described "one interpreter for three schools" and DeafBlind students with no intervener ([Commission Collaborative Plan, 2025](https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf)).

**Training the workforce.** St. Catherine University in St. Paul says it is the only interpreter education program in Minnesota accredited by the Commission on Collegiate Interpreter Education ([St. Catherine University](https://www.stkate.edu/accreditation/program-accreditation/american-sign-language-ac)). Saint Paul College offers a Sign Language Interpreter/Transliterator associate degree ([Saint Paul College](https://www.saintpaul.edu/programs/sign-language-interpreter-transliterator/program-details/)), and M State in Moorhead offers an ASL pre-interpreting program ([M State](https://www.minnesota.edu/degrees/m254)). The Minnesota Registry of Interpreters for the Deaf, founded in 1971 with support from the Minnesota Association of Deaf Citizens, is the interpreters' professional body ([MRID](https://www.mrid.org/)).

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

Commercial life in this community is small and scattered rather than gathered in a district. The center of gravity is a clubhouse, not a shopping street.

- **Charles Thompson Memorial Hall (St. Paul Deaf Club)**, 1824 Marshall Ave., St. Paul. Dedicated Nov. 5, 1916, it says it is "owned by the Deaf community and managed by dedicated Deaf volunteers." It runs Coffee and ASL every Friday from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m., Friday board game nights, bingo, Vikings tailgating parties and holiday parties, and rents its space ([Charles Thompson Hall](https://charlesthompsonhall.org/); [Mission page](https://charlesthompsonhall.org/mission-and-vision/)). MNopedia notes it has struggled at times to maintain the building and attract younger members ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/structure/charles-thompson-memorial-hall-st-paul)); the hall's site is raising money for a renovation and elevator project ([Charles Thompson Hall](https://charlesthompsonhall.org/)).
- **Deaf-owned businesses.** The Minnesota Association of Deaf Citizens lists three: Pam's Delicious BBQ (Pam Burry), ILY by Tricio (Patrick Vellia) and TPleski Woodworking (Todd Pleski), a self-taught woodworker from the Duluth-Superior area who makes wood and resin pieces and lists Nov. 21 and Nov. 29, 2026 craft shows ([MADC directory](https://www.minndeaf.org/deaf-business-directory); [TPleski Woodworking](https://www.tpleskiwoodworking.com/)). MPR News photographed a DeafBlind baker, Patrick Tricio, at the 2024 Deaf Iftar ([MPR News, March 31, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/03/31/photos-deaf-community-celebrates-ramadans-end-with-iftar)). ThinkSelf's website notes it was built by a Deaf-owned firm ([ThinkSelf](https://thinkself.org/)).
- **Interpreting and captioning firms.** ASL Interpreting Services (Golden Valley) and Keystone Interpreting Solutions are the best documented; ownership was not established for either. DeafBlind Services Minnesota is a limited liability company of Vision Loss Resources that employs support service providers and interveners ([Vision Loss Resources](https://visionlossresources.org/programs/dbsm/)).
- **Media.** The Thompson Hall Newsletter, started in 1957, became the Minnesota Deaf Newsletter in 2002 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/structure/charles-thompson-memorial-hall-st-paul)); its current status was not confirmed. The Hearing Loss Association's Twin Cities chapter publishes a monthly newsletter, with an October 2026 issue posted ([HLAA Twin Cities](https://www.hlaatc.org/)). State grants paid TPT, KAAL-TV in Rochester, KBJR-TV in Duluth and Lakeland Public Television in Bemidji to caption live local news in 2021-22 ([DHS, 2023](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2023/Mandated/231530.pdf)).

No Minnesota chamber of commerce or business association for Deaf owners was found, and no Deaf-owned restaurant or shop with a storefront could be confirmed open in 2026.

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

Faith life in ASL has a long history in the Twin Cities, built largely by Deaf people who wanted worship and study in their own language. No Minnesota survey of religious affiliation among Deaf, DeafBlind or hard of hearing people was found, so nothing can be said about how many practice any faith or none.

- **Bread of Life Deaf Lutheran (BOLD), Minneapolis.** Founded in 1956 in the Seward and Longfellow area with support from hearing Lutherans at Bethany on Franklin Avenue, at a time, the congregation writes, when Deaf people were "actively discouraged" from signing. It worships and teaches in ASL, says it serves the Deaf, DeafBlind, hard of hearing, CODA and interpreting community, runs a Zoom Bible study, and described itself in its fundraising appeal as "in a season of transition" ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Bread-Of-Life-Deaf-Lutheran-Church)).
- **Catholic deaf ministry, Our Lady of Mount Carmel, northeast Minneapolis.** The parish is home to the Archdiocese of St. Paul and Minneapolis's ministry to deaf and hard of hearing people. In 2019, about 50 of its 170 members were deaf or hard of hearing, coming from across the Twin Cities and from New Prague, Monticello and western Wisconsin; the 10:30 a.m. Sunday Mass used signed readings and a signed choir, with potlucks, a book and movie club and monthly meetings for Deaf elders ([The Catholic Spirit, Feb. 5, 2019](https://thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/blind-father-deaf-daughter-share-lectoring-ministry/)). The parish's own website was unavailable, so current schedules were not confirmed.
- **Deaf-led evangelical congregations.** Emory Dively, who ran for the Minnesota House in 2006, was then pastor of the Twin Cities Deaf Assembly of God Church ([MPR News, July 12, 2006](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2006/07/12/candidate-wants-to-be-first-deaf-lawmaker-in-minnesota-history)). Current service information for Deaf-specific evangelical churches in the metro was not confirmed.
- **Muslim community.** The Minnesota Deaf Muslim Community held a Deaf Iftar at the Wilder Foundation in St. Paul on March 30, 2024, with a keynote on the tenets of Islam and community members praying Tarawih together ([MPR News, March 31, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/03/31/photos-deaf-community-celebrates-ramadans-end-with-iftar)).

Differences inside the community show up in faith settings too. The 2019 Catholic Spirit story quoted a young hard of hearing woman who said well-meaning people had urged her to pray for healing of her deafness, while she now embraces being deaf and ASL ([The Catholic Spirit, 2019](https://thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/blind-father-deaf-daughter-share-lectoring-ministry/)). No Minnesota source on Jewish, Buddhist, Hindu or other faith communities for Deaf signers, or on people of no faith, was found.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

- **Deaf History Month.** Governor Tim Walz proclaimed April 2024 Deaf History Month in Minnesota, naming Olof Hanson, Agatha Tiegel Hanson, Cadwallader Washburn, Gerald "Bummy" Burstein, Doug Bahl and Blanche Wilkins Williams, plus Thompson Hall, the state academy and Metro Deaf School ([Governor's proclamation, April 2024](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/04.01.24%20Deaf%20History%20Month_SIgned_tcm1055-620392.pdf)). The state Council on Disability marks the month as well ([Minnesota Council on Disability, April 14, 2022](https://www.disability.state.mn.us/2022/04/14/a-perspective-on-deaf-history-in-minnesota/)). Some national sources use March 13 to April 15 instead; no Minnesota source using those dates was found.
- **International Week of Deaf People and International Day of Sign Languages.** The United Nations marks Sept. 23 as the International Day of Sign Languages, the date the World Federation of the Deaf was founded in 1951; the 2026 International Week ran Sept. 21 to 27 ([United Nations](https://www.un.org/en/observances/sign-languages-day)). No Minnesota event tied to these dates was confirmed.
- **MADC Deaf Awareness Day and conference.** The Minnesota Association of Deaf Citizens holds an annual Deaf Awareness Day; the 2025 edition was Saturday, Nov. 1, 2025, at the Crystal Community Center, where the Minnesota Disability Law Center tabled ([Mid-Minnesota Legal Aid](https://mylegalaid.org/events/location/4800-douglas-dr-n-crystal-mn-55429/)), and MADC held its 66th conference the same day, electing a 2025-2027 board ([MADC](https://www.minndeaf.org/)). Bread of Life members recall meeting the congregation at Deaf Awareness Day ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Bread-Of-Life-Deaf-Lutheran-Church)). No 2026 date was found.
- **Charles Thompson Memorial Hall's 110th anniversary.** The hall was dedicated Nov. 5, 1916. Its 110th anniversary celebration is set for Nov. 13 and 14, 2026: historical tours and an Italian dinner at the hall on Friday, then a semi-formal banquet and gala on Saturday at TST Creative Catering's Ideal Hall ([Charles Thompson Hall](https://charlesthompsonhall.org/event/110th/)).
- **DeafBlind Awareness Week.** Held the last week of June, around Helen Keller's June 27 birthday, since a 1984 presidential proclamation ([Helen Keller Services](https://www.helenkeller.org/hknc/deafblind-awareness-week/)). No Minnesota event was confirmed.
- **Walk4Hearing.** The Hearing Loss Association of America opened its 2026 Walk4Hearing series with the Twin Cities walk on May 16, 2026 ([HLAA press release, May 2026](https://digital-release.wowktv.com/business/press-releases/cision/20260507PH52754/people-nationwide-lace-up-to-go-the-distance-for-hearing-health-at-walk4hearing-events)). Eden Prairie hearing-aid maker Starkey sent more than 100 employees, friends and family to the 2025 Twin Cities walk ([Starkey release via HearingTracker, June 26, 2025](https://www.hearingtracker.com/press-releases/starkey-cares-teams-up-with-hlaa-to-advocate-for-hearing-health-at-hlaa-s-walk4hearing)). This is a hard of hearing community event.
- **Nic Zapko Day.** Gov. Walz declared March 9, 2021, Nic Zapko Day to honor the Deaf interpreter of his pandemic briefings, choosing her birthday ([WCCO/CBS Minnesota, March 9, 2021](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/gov-tim-walz-announces-nic-zapko-day-in-minnesota/)). No later renewal was found.
- **Religious and seasonal gatherings.** The Deaf Iftar in Ramadan (2024), Thompson Hall's Halloween parties for children, seniors and adults on Oct. 31, 2026, and its New Year's Eve parties are regular social markers ([MPR News, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/03/31/photos-deaf-community-celebrates-ramadans-end-with-iftar); [Thompson Hall events](https://charlesthompsonhall.org/events/)).

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Most Deaf children have hearing parents.** The state education report notes that most deaf and hard of hearing children are born into families that use spoken language and do not know sign language, and that many enter school without a full first language ([MDE, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261028.pdf)). An interpreter quoted in 2019 put the share of deaf people born to deaf parents at about 10 percent ([The Catholic Spirit, 2019](https://thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/blind-father-deaf-daughter-share-lectoring-ministry/)). Hearing children of Deaf adults, called CODAs, are part of community life; the director of the Minnesota Council on Disability wrote in 2022 about growing up a CODA at Deaf camps and MADC meetings at Thompson Hall ([Minnesota Council on Disability, 2022](https://www.disability.state.mn.us/2022/04/14/a-perspective-on-deaf-history-in-minnesota/)).

**From birth.** In 2023, 99 percent of Minnesota newborns were screened for hearing. Of 59,955 screened, 1,134 were referred for diagnostic testing and 143 were found to have permanent hearing loss; for 43 percent of those referred, the state had no record that diagnosis was completed. Of babies with permanent loss, 62 percent were enrolled in early intervention, 8 percent were not, and 30 percent were unknown. In February 2023 Minnesota became the first state to screen every newborn for congenital cytomegalovirus, which MDH calls the most common cause of nongenetic hearing loss in childhood ([Commission Collaborative Plan, Aug. 2025, citing MDH](https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf)).

**What families say.** In 2025 focus groups, parents praised Deaf mentors, Minnesota Hands and Voices, Help Me Grow and a Department of Health binder, and named other families as their best resource. They also described being their own case managers, long drives in Greater Minnesota, child care centers turning away Deaf+ children, and virtual therapy that did not work for toddlers ([Commission Collaborative Plan, 2025](https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf)). The Deaf Mentor Family Program, run by Lutheran Social Service after its 2021 merger with Lifetrack, had 15 families on its waiting list in mid-2022 ([DHS, 2023](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2023/Mandated/231530.pdf)). Both the state academy and Metro Deaf School offer family ASL classes ([MSAD](https://msad.msa.state.mn.us/about-us); [Metro Deaf School](https://www.mdsmn.org/)).

**Daily life and technology.** The state academy's director told MPR News in 2023 that the room-sized TTYs of the past have given way to phone apps ([MPR News, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2023/09/12/how-deaf-education-has-changed-in-minnesota-over-160-years)). The state's equipment distribution program lent specialized phone equipment to 3,342 people in state fiscal year 2022 ([DHS, 2023](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2023/Mandated/231530.pdf)).

**Gathering and food.** Social life centers on events where signing is the norm: Friday coffee and card games at Thompson Hall, potluck lunches, Vikings tailgates with themed food specials, monthly bingo and holiday parties ([Charles Thompson Hall events](https://charlesthompsonhall.org/events/)). A 2023 day camp at Fort Snelling State Park run by Wilderness Inquiry used ASL as its primary language; its DeafBlind coordinator said Minnesota had lost several similar camps over the years ([MPR News, Oct. 9, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/10/09/a-new-camp-makes-the-outdoors-accessible-for-deaf-deafblind-hardofhearing-kids)).

**Aging.** Hearing loss is overwhelmingly an experience of later life in the survey numbers (section 1). Groups organized around it include the Minnesota Deaf Senior Citizens and the HLAA Twin Cities chapter, which meets on Zoom with captions, generally on the third Saturday from September to May, and runs a monthly book club ([HLAA Twin Cities](https://www.hlaatc.org/); [Governor's proclamation, 2024](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/04.01.24%20Deaf%20History%20Month_SIgned_tcm1055-620392.pdf)). The Catholic deaf ministry held monthly meetings for elderly deaf parishioners in 2019 ([The Catholic Spirit, 2019](https://thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/blind-father-deaf-daughter-share-lectoring-ministry/)).

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

- **Literature.** John Lee Clark, a DeafBlind poet and essayist born Deaf into an ASL-using family, is a leading voice of the Protactile movement. His collection *How to Communicate* (W. W. Norton, 2022) was a finalist for the 2023 Minnesota Book Award and the National Book Award in poetry; his essay collection *Touch the Future* argues for Protactile language and "co-navigation" ([MPR News, May 1, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2023/05/01/st-paul-poet-john-lee-clark-explores-change-miscommunication-in-how-to-communicate); [Academy of American Poets](https://poets.org/poet/john-lee-clark); [John Lee Clark](https://johnleeclark.com/)). His own site says he lives in St. Paul with his partner, the ASL Deaf artist Adrean Clark; other profiles place the family in Hopkins ([United States Artists](https://www.unitedstatesartists.org/artists/john-lee-clark)).
- **Film.** Daniel Durant grew up in Duluth and graduated from the Minnesota State Academy for the Deaf in 2008; he played Leo in *CODA*, the first film with a largely Deaf cast to win the Academy Award for best picture, in 2022 ([Faribault Daily News, 2022](https://www.southernminn.com/faribault_daily_news/community/msad-grads-film-won-oscar/article_7534e9e0-b074-11ec-8dbd-4f1a69ca8683.html); [MPR News, Aug. 13, 2021](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2021/08/13/duluth-actor-gets-his-biggest-stage-yet-in-new-movie-coda)).
- **Performance and visual art.** The University of Minnesota's College of Education and Human Development and its ASL program drew more than 300 Twin Cities Deaf community members each to a March 2025 evening with Visual Vernacular artist Justin Perez and a September 2025 evening with actor CJ Jones ([UMN CEHD Connect, Dec. 11, 2025](https://connect.cehd.umn.edu/removing-barriers-and-building-community)). Public performances with ASL interpretation are common, such as BareBones Puppets' Halloween show in Minneapolis, which offered ASL on its Sunday performance in 2026 ([MPR News, Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/30/minnesota-halloween-events-barebones-ballet-and-haunted-houses)). No currently active Deaf-led theater company in Minnesota was confirmed.
- **Clubs and heritage.** Thompson Hall's large windows and high ceilings were designed so signs could be seen; it once had a bowling alley and showed captioned films on a stage curtain ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/structure/charles-thompson-memorial-hall-st-paul)). The Minnesota Digital Library holds 173 historical photos and documents from Thompson Hall, MADC and the state academy's alumni museum, and the Commission hosts oral-visual history interviews ([Governor's proclamation, 2024](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/04.01.24%20Deaf%20History%20Month_SIgned_tcm1055-620392.pdf)). The state academy's director helped write its 150th anniversary book and keeps a campus museum ([MPR News, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2023/09/12/how-deaf-education-has-changed-in-minnesota-over-160-years)).
- **Sports.** The academy's teams compete as the MSAD Trojans in athletics and fine arts and host regional tournaments ([MSAD Athletics](https://athletics.msa.state.mn.us/)); Metro Deaf School's teams are the Cheetahs ([Metro Deaf School](https://www.mdsmn.org/)). Thompson Hall's Vikings Fan Club holds tailgate parties through the 2026 season ([Charles Thompson Hall](https://charlesthompsonhall.org/)). Historically, the Thompson Hall newsletter covered "local deaf sports teams" ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/structure/charles-thompson-memorial-hall-st-paul)).
- **Interpreters as public figures.** Deaf interpreter Nic Zapko became widely known during the governor's 2020 and 2021 pandemic briefings ([WCCO/CBS Minnesota, May 12, 2020](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/asl-interpreters-praised-for-delivering-key-messages-to-deaf-community-during-pandemic/)).

## 9. Civic and political life

**A state commission led by the people it serves.** Minnesota created what began as the Hearing Impaired Council in 1985; it was renamed in 1993 and again in 2008, and is now the Commission of the Deaf, DeafBlind and Hard of Hearing ([Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/agencies/detail?AgencyID=1930)). By law it is "the principal agency of the state" to advocate for equal access for these Minnesotans, and at least half its voting members must be deaf, deafblind or hard of hearing, with at least one parent or guardian ([Minn. Stat. 256C.28](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/256C.28)). Separately, the Department of Human Services division, created under a 1980 law, runs regional services, ASL mental health care and equipment programs and funds grants ([DHS, 2023](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2023/Mandated/231530.pdf)). In 2025 the Legislature appropriated $2,886,000 a year for Deaf, DeafBlind and hard of hearing grants for fiscal years 2026 and 2027, and $17.8 million for the State Academies in fiscal year 2026 ([Laws 2025, 1st Spec. Sess., ch. 9](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/laws/2025/1/Session+Law/Chapter/9/); [ch. 10](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/laws/2025/1/Session+Law/Chapter/10/)).

**Community organizations.** The Minnesota Association of Deaf Citizens, founded in 1885, works to preserve and promote "the civil, human, and American Sign Language" rights of Deaf, DeafBlind and hard of hearing Minnesotans and held its 66th conference on Nov. 1, 2025 ([MADC](https://www.minndeaf.org/)). The Minnesota DeafBlind Association, founded in 1979, calls itself the only DeafBlind-led nonprofit in the state ([MDBA](https://www.mndeafblind.org/about-1)). Deaf Equity, a volunteer nonprofit, runs events such as Read and Sign ([Deaf Equity](https://www.deafequitymn.org/event)). The 2024 proclamation also names the St. Paul/Minneapolis Black Deaf Advocates, the Minnesota Deaf Muslim Community, Deaf Women of Minnesota, Minnesota Deaf Queers, Minnesota Deaf Senior Citizens and ThinkSelf ([Governor's proclamation, 2024](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/04.01.24%20Deaf%20History%20Month_SIgned_tcm1055-620392.pdf)).

**Running for office.** In 2006 Emory Dively, a Republican and pastor of the Twin Cities Deaf Assembly of God, ran for the Minnesota House in District 64B, saying he would be the first deaf legislator in the state and nation ([MPR News, July 12, 2006](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2006/07/12/candidate-wants-to-be-first-deaf-lawmaker-in-minnesota-history)). He did not win ([Minnesota Council on Disability, 2022](https://www.disability.state.mn.us/2022/04/14/a-perspective-on-deaf-history-in-minnesota/)). No Deaf member of the current Legislature was identified.

**Debates inside the community.**
- *How to prevent language deprivation.* When Rep. Brian Daniels brought a bill, HF 612, that would have used Child Protective Services to address deaf children's lack of language access, the Commission wrote that it "enthusiastically" shared the goal but opposed the mechanism, citing educators, mental health professionals and parents who feared child welfare systems mishandle families with deaf members ([Commission memo to House committee](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/q1aChQYzmU6DSiKHQcF3yQ.pdf)). In 2025 the Commission's government relations director called the lack of an accessible language in infancy "a public health crisis" and backed $150,000 a year for Metro Deaf School's Birth-3 program, which it called the only center-based ASL immersion program for families in the metro ([Commission memo, May 23, 2025](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/joint/2025/wg_ed/20250602/wg_ed_20250602_SF2255-Commission-DDBHH-support-2025-0523.pdf)). Whether that line survived in the final 2025 education law was not confirmed.
- *Who leads.* In 1993, students at the Faribault academy called for the hearing administrator to resign and for a Deaf administrator, echoing Gallaudet's 1988 Deaf President Now protest; the academy's director said in 2023 that the protest led to more Deaf leadership ([MPR News, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2023/09/12/how-deaf-education-has-changed-in-minnesota-over-160-years)).
- *Deaf interpreters.* WCCO reported in 2020 on "a growing movement" to put Deaf interpreters in front, with hearing interpreters in support ([WCCO/CBS Minnesota, May 12, 2020](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/asl-interpreters-praised-for-delivering-key-messages-to-deaf-community-during-pandemic/)).

**Rights in practice.** The Minnesota Disability Law Center represented two Stillwater prison inmates whose 2024 settlement required the Department of Corrections to use pagers for announcements; a Deaf man, Choua Yang, described being left without phone or interpreter access in county jails and settled suits with Washington County in 2016 and Hennepin County in 2022 ([MPR News, July 10, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2024/07/10/understanding-the-rights-of-deaf-and-hard-of-hearing-people-in-prison-and-jail)).

## 10. Education

**Minnesota State Academy for the Deaf (Faribault).** Opened in 1863 in a rented store building; its director told MPR News that it has always allowed signing and now uses an ASL-English bilingual approach, and its school psychologist said technology, coding and robotics have replaced the old trades ([MPR News, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2023/09/12/how-deaf-education-has-changed-in-minnesota-over-160-years)). Two of its buildings are on the National Register of Historic Places ([Governor's proclamation, 2024](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/04.01.24%20Deaf%20History%20Month_SIgned_tcm1055-620392.pdf)). The State Academies, which also include the academy for the blind, received $17,838,000 for fiscal year 2026 ([Laws 2025, 1st Spec. Sess., ch. 10](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/laws/2025/1/Session+Law/Chapter/10/)).

**Metro Deaf School (St. Paul).** A free public charter for students from birth to 22, established in 1992 as the second charter school to open in the United States ([Governor's proclamation, 2024](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/04.01.24%20Deaf%20History%20Month_SIgned_tcm1055-620392.pdf)); its doors opened in 1993 and it teaches Deaf, DeafBlind and hard of hearing students with an ASL/English bilingual approach ([Metro Deaf School](https://www.mdsmn.org/our-story)).

**Most students are in neighborhood schools.** In 2024-25, 65 percent of students with a deaf or hard of hearing primary label spent less than 21 percent of the day outside regular classrooms, and 7 percent were in separate facilities. DeafBlind students were far more often in separate settings: 41 percent in separate classrooms most of the day and 28 percent in separate facilities. The state had 231 teachers of the deaf and hard of hearing and 43 oral/aural teachers ([MDE, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261028.pdf)).

**Outcomes.** MCA math proficiency for students with a deaf or hard of hearing label rose from 29 percent in 2021 to 34 percent in 2025 (statewide: 45 percent); reading held at 36 to 38 percent. The four-year graduation rate was 78 percent for the class of 2024 (general education 87 percent; all special education 67 percent), and the seven-year rate, 91 percent for the class of 2021, matches general education ([MDE, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261028.pdf)). The Commission's 2025 plan, using a broader group that includes DeafBlind students, reported a four-year rate of 74 percent for 2023-24 ([Commission Collaborative Plan, 2025](https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf)). MDE cautions that its tests are "neither accessible nor normed" for these students ([MDE, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261028.pdf)).

**Higher education and teacher training.** The University of Minnesota houses the state's teacher licensure program for deaf and hard of hearing education and an ASL program; a college task force formed in 2023 after an interpreter request went unfilled, and the college now provides interpreters automatically at commencement and other core events ([UMN CEHD Connect, Dec. 11, 2025](https://connect.cehd.umn.edu/removing-barriers-and-building-community)). The University of Minnesota Duluth moved to end its deaf studies minor in 2025, prompting student protests; the university said 56 students had declared it ([MPR News, April 9, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/04/09/university-of-minnesota-duluth-students-protest-after-university-cuts-deaf-studies-program)). Interpreter training is in section 3. ThinkSelf teaches Deaf adults reading, writing, math, sign language and technology ([ThinkSelf](https://thinkself.org/)), and the state funded programs for Deaf, DeafBlind and hard of hearing adults at $70,000 a year for 2026 and 2027 ([Laws 2025, 1st Spec. Sess., ch. 10](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/laws/2025/1/Session+Law/Chapter/10/)).

**DeafBlind students.** The federally funded Minnesota DeafBlind Project, based at BrightWorks in Arden Hills, gives free help to families and school teams of children with combined hearing and vision loss from birth to 22 and trains interveners ([Minnesota DeafBlind Project](https://dbproject.mn.org/)).

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Mental health in ASL.** State law requires the DHS division to provide "culturally and linguistically affirmative" mental health services for people who use ASL, another sign language or a tactile form of a visual language ([Minn. Stat. 256C.233](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/256C.233)). Its mental health program served 126 people in state fiscal year 2021 and 134 in 2022. Grants that year went to providers including Regions Hospital's Health and Wellness Program (94 people served in 2022), Therapeutic Services Agency for children and youth, People Incorporated, LifeConnect Health, Volunteers of America's Vona Center for Mental Health, and the Metro Educational Cooperative Service Unit for psychological assessments, which had 15 children waiting in 2021 ([DHS, 2023](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2023/Mandated/231530.pdf)). In 2025, professionals told the Commission that "language access isn't built into the behavioral health system" and that referrals had risen ([Commission Collaborative Plan, 2025](https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf)).

**Getting care.** The DHS division's regional advisory committees named better access to interpreters and real-time captioning, "particularly in healthcare settings," as a top need in both 2021 and 2022 ([DHS, 2023](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2023/Mandated/231530.pdf)). A state-funded training program run by ASLIS aimed to increase interpreters' confidence in medical settings (same source).

**Hearing health.** Newborn hearing and cytomegalovirus screening are described in section 7. Hearing-aid maker Starkey is based in Eden Prairie ([HearingTracker, June 26, 2025](https://www.hearingtracker.com/press-releases/starkey-cares-teams-up-with-hlaa-to-advocate-for-hearing-health-at-hlaa-s-walk4hearing)). The HLAA Twin Cities chapter's November 2026 meeting featured an audiologist on talking with families about hearing loss ([HLAA Twin Cities](https://www.hlaatc.org/)).

**Safety.** ThinkSelf's advocates support Deaf, DeafBlind and hard of hearing people who have experienced domestic abuse, sexual violence or crime, with a hotline monitored on weekdays ([ThinkSelf](https://thinkself.org/)).

## 12. Disability

**Who defines it.** Whether "disability" is the right word is contested, and Minnesota law holds two framings at once. The state requires "culturally and linguistically affirmative" services built around language and culture, yet places them in the Department of Human Services ([Minn. Stat. 256C.233](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/256C.233)). In schools, a child is counted under one "primary disability," so a DeafBlind child whose main label is something else does not show up as DeafBlind: MDE counted 125 DeafBlind students but about 250 more who qualified for both hearing and vision services ([MDE, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261028.pdf)). The state's advisory committee has asked, without success so far, that districts report secondary and tertiary disabilities (same source).

**DeafBlind experience is distinct.** A 2021 Wilder Research study for the Commission, based on 45 interviews and a 22-member advisory group, stresses that only a small share of DeafBlind people are completely deaf and blind; many have some hearing or vision. It counted 379 DeafBlind children and youth in Minnesota in 2019, using a National Center on Deaf-Blindness census, and found that the state "lacks a cohesive system" for them, that families coordinate their own services, and that social isolation is a common risk. One parent said it was hard to find a DeafBlind community because there are so many "variants" of the experience. The study notes that a definition of "family and community intervener" was added to state law in 2019 ([Wilder Research, June 2021](https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/pdf-file/MNCDHH_SupportingDeafBlindChildrenAndYouth_Report_6-21.pdf)). MDE estimates that about 80 percent of students with combined hearing and vision loss have additional disabilities and are emergent communicators ([MDE, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261028.pdf)). For adults, DeafBlind Services Minnesota provided support service providers to 52 adults a year in 2021 and 2022, and intervener services to 36 children and their families; a consumer-directed DeafBlind program served 38 to 39 people a year ([DHS, 2023](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2023/Mandated/231530.pdf)). The Protactile movement, which began among DeafBlind people in Seattle in 2007 ([Christian Science Monitor, Jan. 15, 2020](https://www.csmonitor.com/USA/Society/2020/0115/In-one-revolutionary-language-a-community-taps-the-power-of-touch-video)), moves away from dependence on sighted interpreters toward direct tactile communication; researchers describe groups who mostly were born sighted, learned ASL as children and lost sight over decades ([Edwards and Brentari, Frontiers in Psychology, 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.579992/full)). Minnesota writer John Lee Clark has written against "access" that only recreates a hearing-sighted experience ([John Lee Clark](https://johnleeclark.com/)).

**Hard of hearing experience is distinct.** Professionals told the Commission in 2025 that hard of hearing children "don't qualify for DHH services, but they're still struggling," that mild or late losses are caught late, and that it is hard "to navigate both worlds—especially if you're hard of hearing and don't sign" ([Commission Collaborative Plan, 2025](https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf)). Between 2019 and 2023, an average of 78 Minnesota children a year were reported with late-onset hearing loss after passing an earlier screening (same source, citing MDH).

**Deaf+ and DeafDisabled.** Nationally, MDE notes, an estimated 35 to 50 percent of deaf and hard of hearing students have additional disabilities ([MDE, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261028.pdf)). Families of Deaf+ children described being "too deaf for one service, too complex for another" ([Commission Collaborative Plan, 2025](https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf)).

**What a label opens.** In Minnesota's disability waiver rate system, a person who meets defined deaf or hard of hearing criteria (for example, a person with a developmental disability whose assessment shows severe hearing loss and limited expressive communication) gets a $2.50-per-hour customization added to direct-care costs ([Minn. Stat. 256B.4914, subd. 12](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/256B.4914)). A school label opens teachers of the deaf, interpreters and interveners; adult services open support service providers, ASL mental health care and phone equipment (sections 10 and 11).

**What produced an impairment.** Records point to many causes rather than one: congenital hearing loss found at birth, congenital cytomegalovirus (the most common nongenetic cause in childhood, per MDH), late-onset childhood loss, aging, and illness, as with actor CJ Jones, who became Deaf after spinal meningitis at age 7 ([Commission Collaborative Plan, 2025](https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf); [State Demographic Center, 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/accessible%20MN_MinnesotansWithDisabilities_tcm36-734001.pdf); [UMN CEHD Connect, Dec. 11, 2025](https://connect.cehd.umn.edu/removing-barriers-and-building-community)). Why southeastern Minnesota has the highest share of students with a deaf or hard of hearing label, and why Black students make up a larger share of DeafBlind students (16 percent) than of deaf and hard of hearing students (9 percent), are open questions; no source explained either ([MDE, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261028.pdf)).

**Questions worth asking a person, drawn from these sources.** Which language works best (ASL, Protactile, another sign language, spoken English with captions, writing)? Was language available early in life? Is a Deaf interpreter needed alongside a hearing one? For DeafBlind people, who navigates and how is information conveyed by touch? For hard of hearing people, is assistive listening or real-time captioning the right fit?

## 13. What is changing now

- **Interpreter and teacher shortages.** MDE's 2026 report describes a "critical shortage of sign language interpreters," limited preparation pipelines, isolation for staff who serve several schools, and a state DHH education specialist position posted in November 2025; it recommends funded mentoring and apprenticeship pathways ([MDE, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261028.pdf)). Professionals told the Commission in 2025 that some teachers of deaf students work on emergency licenses ([Commission Collaborative Plan, 2025](https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf)).
- **A new five-year plan.** The Commission's 2025-2030 Collaborative Plan, built with Wilder Research from interviews, focus groups and a May 8, 2025 summit, sets six priorities: family resources from identification through transition, mental health, the Collaborative Experience Conference, data, in-state postsecondary options, and early language acquisition ([Commission Collaborative Plan, Aug. 2025](https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf)).
- **Newborn screening and early language.** Since 2023 every newborn is screened for cytomegalovirus; by June 30, 2025, 413 cases had been found, 30 with sensorineural hearing loss ([Commission Collaborative Plan, 2025](https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf)). The Newborn Hearing Screening Advisory Committee, whose law was amended in 2025, must include a representative of a consumer organization representing culturally deaf persons and at least two members who are deaf or hard of hearing ([Laws 2025, 1st Spec. Sess., ch. 3](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/laws/2025/1/Session+Law/Chapter/3/)).
- **Higher education cuts and gains.** UMD moved to end its deaf studies minor in 2025 ([MPR News, April 9, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/04/09/university-of-minnesota-duluth-students-protest-after-university-cuts-deaf-studies-program)), while the Twin Cities campus's College of Education and Human Development began providing interpreters automatically at core events ([UMN CEHD Connect, Dec. 11, 2025](https://connect.cehd.umn.edu/removing-barriers-and-building-community)).
- **Generational change at the clubs.** Thompson Hall turns 110 in November 2026 while raising money for renovations and an elevator ([Charles Thompson Hall](https://charlesthompsonhall.org/)); MNopedia notes its struggle to attract younger members ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/structure/charles-thompson-memorial-hall-st-paul)). Bread of Life described itself as in a season of transition ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Bread-Of-Life-Deaf-Lutheran-Church)).
- **Immigrant and multilingual families.** One professional told the Commission in 2025 that Somali, Hmong and Spanish-speaking families "get half the services and twice the confusion" ([Commission Collaborative Plan, 2025](https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf)). That is one interviewee's view, but it fits the 11 percent of students with a deaf or hard of hearing label who also receive English learner services ([MDE, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/261028.pdf)).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **Institutions built by Deaf people.** Thompson Hall, opened in 1916 and described by MNopedia as the first U.S. social club designed exclusively for deaf people, is still owned by the community and run by Deaf volunteers ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/structure/charles-thompson-memorial-hall-st-paul); [Charles Thompson Hall](https://charlesthompsonhall.org/mission-and-vision/)). MADC has worked since 1885, and the state's interpreter registry was founded in 1971 with its support ([MRID](https://www.mrid.org/)).
- **Firsts in schooling.** Metro Deaf School was among the nation's first charter schools ([Governor's proclamation, 2024](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/04.01.24%20Deaf%20History%20Month_SIgned_tcm1055-620392.pdf)); Minnesota was the first state to screen all newborns for cytomegalovirus ([Commission Collaborative Plan, 2025](https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf)).
- **Public access for everyone.** Deaf interpreters at the governor's briefings carried emergency information to all Minnesotans during the pandemic ([WCCO/CBS Minnesota, 2020](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/asl-interpreters-praised-for-delivering-key-messages-to-deaf-community-during-pandemic/)), and state-funded live captioning of local news in Duluth, Rochester, Bemidji and the Twin Cities serves any viewer who needs it ([DHS, 2023](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2023/Mandated/231530.pdf)).
- **Art and ideas.** John Lee Clark's poetry and Protactile writing, and Daniel Durant's screen work, have reached national audiences (section 8).
- **Mutual support.** Parents in 2025 said the state's Deaf, DeafBlind and hard of hearing communities are welcoming and quick to share what they know ([Commission Collaborative Plan, 2025](https://mn.gov/deaf-commission/assets/MNCommission_CollaborativePlanReport_8-2025_tcm1063-706624.pdf)).

## What could not be established

- **How many Minnesotans use ASL, identify as culturally Deaf, or are DeafBlind adults.** No Minnesota count exists for any of these. Survey figures cover "serious difficulty hearing," and the state's "20 percent" figure has no published method.
- **Income, wealth, homeownership and education for Deaf signers specifically.** The only Minnesota numbers mix signers with the much larger group of older adults with hearing loss.
- **Deaf-owned storefront businesses.** No Deaf-owned restaurant, shop or professional firm with a public storefront was confirmed open in 2026. Ownership of the main interpreting agencies was not established, and two businesses in the Deaf Business Directory could not be confirmed active. No Deaf business association or chamber was found.
- **Current faith life.** Current service times for the Catholic deaf ministry at Our Lady of Mount Carmel could not be confirmed because the parish site would not open; current information on Deaf evangelical congregations was not found. No source covered Jewish, Buddhist, Hindu or other Deaf faith communities, traditional healing practices, or people of no faith.
