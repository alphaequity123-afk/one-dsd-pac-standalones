# Ecuadorians in Minnesota

## 1. People and place

**How many.** Minnesota Compass, using the American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024, counts 19,484 Ecuadorian Minnesotans, give or take 3,601. It places 95.9 percent in the Twin Cities and 4.1 percent in Greater Minnesota, each give or take 1.9 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/ecuadorian)). A narrower Census count, of people who name Ecuadorian as their Hispanic origin, gives 15,993 (give or take 1,980) for the same five years, and 17,722 (give or take 4,752) for 2024 alone ([Census Bureau table B03001 via Census Reporter, 2020-2024](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=04000US27,16000US2743000,16000US2712700,16000US2758000); [2024 one-year](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_1yr?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=04000US27,05000US27053)). The counts differ because they ask different questions, and the margins are wide. Some published estimates have run higher: The Catholic Spirit wrote in 2013, without naming a source, that nearly 40,000 Ecuadorian immigrants lived in Minnesota ([Catholic Spirit, March 15, 2013](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/st-marys-health-clinics-partner-with-ecuadorian-consulate/)), a figure no census count supports. CBS Minnesota, citing the census, used "more than 15,000" in 2024 ([WCCO, Sept. 24, 2024](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/ecuadorian-volleyball-picnics-twin-cities/)). COPAL, a Latino organizing group, said in 2024 that Minnesota had the 10th-largest Ecuadorian population in the country; that is the group's own statement ([COPAL, 2024](https://copalmn.org/community-mobilization-leads-to-passage-of-temporary-protected-status-for-ecuador-resolution-in-columbia-heights/)).

**Born in Ecuador.** About 9,676 Minnesotans (give or take 1,207) were born in Ecuador, in the 2020 to 2024 survey. Among Latin American birthplaces in Minnesota, that is second only to Mexico (63,133), ahead of El Salvador (8,255) and Guatemala (6,837) ([Census table B05006 via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US27)). Compass puts the foreign-born share at 53.3 percent, give or take 6.1 points, so roughly half the community was born in the United States ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/ecuadorian)).

**Young.** Compass counts 45.6 percent age 17 or younger and 2.1 percent age 65 or older. More than one in ten (10.7 percent) are under 5 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/ecuadorian)).

**Where.** The community is concentrated in Northeast Minneapolis and next-door Columbia Heights ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 11, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-ecuadorian-community-trump-immigration/)), with Central Avenue as its main street ([Northeaster, July 21, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuadorians-make-northeast-metro-area-home/)). Smaller clusters appear in south Minneapolis, where Sahan Journal describes Ecuadorians playing soccer and selling street food at Powderhorn Park and an Ecuadorian bakery on Lake Street ([Sahan Journal, March 18, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/)). Meet Minneapolis also names Powderhorn Park as a gathering place for Ecuadorian and other South American residents ([Meet Minneapolis](https://www.minneapolis.org/latinx-community/two-day-itinerary/)). Outside the metro the numbers are very small: the same census table shows 118 people of Ecuadorian origin in St. Cloud, 87 in Rochester and 56 in Austin, each with a margin as large as or larger than the estimate ([St. Cloud](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=16000US2756896); [Rochester](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=16000US2754880); [Austin](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=16000US2702908)).

**How people came.** The Northeaster, a neighborhood paper, traces the community to a handful of families from Cuenca, in southern Ecuador, in the late 1990s. It says many left after El Niño storms damaged farming and craft work in the south, and that the opening of an Ecuadorian consulate on Central Avenue Northeast in 2005 drew more people to the area ([Northeaster, July 21, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuadorians-make-northeast-metro-area-home/)). The Archdiocese of Saint Paul and Minneapolis says Ss. Cyril and Methodius parish in Northeast has been mostly Hispanic since 1997, "with many new Spanish speaking immigrants from Ecuador" ([Archdiocese](https://www.archspm.org/location/ss-cyril-and-methodius-catholic-church/)). One volleyball organizer told WCCO he has played in Minnesota since he arrived in 1996 ([WCCO](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/ecuadorian-volleyball-picnics-twin-cities/)). By 2004, a University of Minnesota staff member, Juan Moreno, had spent about ten years petitioning Ecuador to open a consulate here; when he was named honorary consul that year, he called Ecuadorians the second-largest Latin American group in Minnesota, and said the consulate would open on Lake Street or near Central Avenue, "where there are the most Ecuadorians" ([Minnesota Daily, Oct. 12, 2004](https://mndaily.com/uncategorized/u-employee-head-ecuadorian-consulate/10/12/2004/snoadmin/)). Bertha Orellana, a Columbia Heights firefighter, came from Cuenca in 2009, when, she said, the community was fairly small ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 11, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-ecuadorian-community-trump-immigration/)).

**A newer wave.** A second, larger movement began after 2021. Sahan Journal reports that conditions in Ecuador worsened during the pandemic and that gangs tied to the cocaine trade made extortion and violence common, leading to a mass departure from 2022 on ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 11, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-ecuadorian-community-trump-immigration/)). Many crossed through the Darién Gap in Panama. Some came to Minnesota on Texas-chartered buses or on travel from New York; the archdiocese's Latino ministry director said in late 2023 that buses were arriving weekly with three to five Ecuadorian families ([Catholic Spirit, Dec. 7, 2023](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/many-ecuadorian-immigrants-are-placing-their-hope-in-minnesota/)). Asylum cases from Ecuador in Minnesota's immigration court rose from 192 in 2019 to 1,920 in 2024, according to Syracuse University data cited by MPR News ([MPR News, May 1, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/05/01/nonprofit-copal-calls-on-state-leaders-to-support-temporary-protected-status-for-ecuador)). By November 2025, Ecuadorians had 11,921 pending cases at Fort Snelling Immigration Court, more than twice the number for Mexico ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-ecuadorian-community-trump-immigration/)). Compass shows 3.1 percent of Ecuadorian Minnesotans lived outside the United States one year earlier ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/ecuadorian)).

**Regions and peoples.** Ecuadorians here come from the coast, the highlands and the Amazon. A Sabor Cuencano manager told the Northeaster in 2026 that Northeast is no longer just "Little Cuenca" because people now come "from the Coast and Sierra and Oriente" ([Northeaster, July 21, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuadorians-make-northeast-metro-area-home/)). Some families are Indigenous. Organizer Diego Guaman told Sahan Journal that his mother came in 1995 partly because her children faced discrimination in Ecuador as Indigenous people ([Sahan Journal, March 18, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/)). Afro-Ecuadorian bomba from the Chota Valley was taught in Minneapolis at the 2026 Minnesota International Dance Festival ([MNIDF program](https://mnidancefestival.org/festival-program)).

**Languages.** Among Ecuadorian Minnesotans 5 and older, 76.8 percent speak a language other than English at home and 49.1 percent speak English less than "very well" (margins 8.8 and 7.4 points) ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/ecuadorian)). The survey does not say which languages. Spanish is the main one in the sources, but El Colegio, a south Minneapolis charter high school, told Sahan Journal in 2024 that it offers language services and preservation classes for students who speak Quichua (Kichwa) ([Sahan Journal, March 18, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/)). No count of Kichwa or other Indigenous-language speakers in Minnesota was found.

## 2. All walks of life

All figures here are from the Compass 2020 to 2024 profile, in 2024 dollars, unless noted ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/ecuadorian)). The community is small enough that margins are wide; read these as rough shapes.

- **Comfortable households.** About 26.9 percent of households earn $100,000 or more (give or take 8.8 points). Median household income is $59,501, give or take $12,718. Households are large, averaging 3.92 people, and half of households (50.0 percent) are married couples with children.
- **Owners, professionals and business people.** Homeowners make up 64.8 percent of households, even with so many recent arrivals (margin 12.3 points). Among adults 25 and older, 19.8 percent have a bachelor's degree or more, and 11.1 percent hold a graduate or professional degree. Visible careers include restaurant owners Marcos Pinguil of Chimborazo ([Chimborazo](https://chimborazorestaurant.com/home/)) and Juan Yunga of La Mesa, who cooked in Twin Cities restaurants for about 20 years before opening his own in 2018 ([Racket, June 21, 2024](https://racketmn.com/bryn-mawrs-la-mesa-transports-diners-to-a-tiny-tucked-away-slice-of-ecuador)); firefighter and market co-owner Bertha Orellana ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-ecuadorian-community-trump-immigration/)); and artist Victor Yepez, who studied sculpture at Central University in Quito and works as a teaching artist ([Northeaster, Oct. 4, 2022](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuador-meets-afghanistan-in-one-of-latest-murals-completed/)).
- **Working families.** Among adults 18 to 64, 76.8 percent are employed: 84.0 percent of men and 68.4 percent of women. Half (50.0 percent) of people live at or above twice the poverty line; another 30.1 percent live between the poverty line and twice that line.
- **Hardship.** Poverty is 19.9 percent, with a very wide margin of 10.3 points, and 22.4 percent among children. Without health insurance: 24.0 percent. More than half of households (51.7 percent) spend too much of their income on housing, including 78.8 percent of renters. Among adults 25 and older, 42.3 percent did not finish high school. One in ten households (9.5 percent) has no vehicle.

Behind the numbers are very different lives: a firefighter who arrived in 2009 and now co-owns a market ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 11, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-ecuadorian-community-trump-immigration/)), a bakery owner whose family business has run on Central Avenue for a decade ([Northeaster, Aug. 3, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuadorians-bring-street-food-vending-to-central/)), and a father who crossed the Darién Gap with his wife and daughter in 2023 and waited hours in the cold for a parish food shelf to open ([Catholic Spirit, Dec. 7, 2023](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/many-ecuadorian-immigrants-are-placing-their-hope-in-minnesota/)). At the Camden Collective food share in north Minneapolis, the director said in 2024 that about 65 percent of people served the year before spoke Spanish, and about three-fourths of those were from Ecuador; many volunteers were recent Ecuadorian arrivals who wanted to give back ([Sahan Journal, March 18, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/)). No source on household wealth for Ecuadorian Minnesotans was found.

## 3. Work and the economy

Compass counts 8,292 Ecuadorian workers 16 and older. Most (78.3 percent) drive to work, and 8.5 percent take public transit ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/ecuadorian)). No occupation or industry table for Ecuadorian Minnesotans was found.

What reporting shows: recent arrivals work in cleaning, dishwashing, construction and other manual labor, often before they have work permits, according to organizer Diego Guaman ([Sahan Journal, March 18, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/)). Bertha Orellana said many customers of her market hold two jobs and support relatives in Ecuador, and that the new Ecuadorian markets all offer money transfer services ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 11, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-ecuadorian-community-trump-immigration/)).

**Women and work.** Claudia Lainez, who directs COPAL's Workers Center, told the Northeaster that lack of affordable childcare keeps most women from jobs: of ten men seeking work, about nine find it; of ten women, maybe one. As many did in Ecuador, she said, women sell fruit and snacks on corners and in parks while caring for children ([Northeaster, July 21, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuadorians-make-northeast-metro-area-home/)). She also named smaller hurdles, such as applying for jobs online and moving from daily to hourly pay.

**Street vending.** Fruit vendors became common in Minneapolis parks and medians starting in 2023. MPR News reported in 2024 that many were asylum seekers from Ecuador without work permits, and that the city had held classes on food licensing at Ss. Cyril and Methodius church ([MPR News, Sept. 24, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2024/09/24/minneapolis-weighs-permit-system-unlicensed-fruit-vendors)). An updated sidewalk cart ordinance led by council members Jason Chavez and Aurin Chowdhury passed in April 2026. One cevichocho vendor on Central Avenue said she earns about $150 a day with her two children beside her ([Northeaster, Aug. 3, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuadorians-bring-street-food-vending-to-central/)).

**Organizing.** COPAL, founded in 2018, runs a workers center and says most of the families it serves are Ecuadorian ([Northeaster, July 21, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuadorians-make-northeast-metro-area-home/)). No source on Ecuadorian union membership in Minnesota was found.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

**Central Avenue.** Northeast Minneapolis's Central Avenue is the commercial center. The city named the stretch from about 18th to 26th Avenues a cultural district in 2022 ([Northeaster, April 5, 2024](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/central-ave-labeled-as-cultural-district-amidst-concerns-of-franchise-takeover/)). Sabor Cuencano's manager recalled only three Ecuadorian-related businesses when he arrived in 2010; he counted more than a dozen by 2026 and estimated that about 90 percent of his customers are Ecuadorian ([Northeaster, Aug. 3, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuadorians-bring-street-food-vending-to-central/)).

**Restaurants and bakeries.**
- **Chimborazo**, 2851 Central Ave. NE, serves food of Ecuador and the Andean highlands. Owner and chef Marcos Pinguil came from the Cañar highlands by way of the coast. A second location at 508 Lexington Pkwy. S. in St. Paul opened in 2024, and the business runs a food truck ([Chimborazo](https://chimborazorestaurant.com/home/); [St. Paul](https://chimborazorestaurant.com/home/stpaul/); [Racket](https://racketmn.com/hello-to-a-new-chimborazo-fool-me-once-flees-and-more-april-restaurant-news)). The Minneapolis restaurant had a basement fire in June 2026; no one was hurt and the owners said they were working to restore the space ([Northeaster, June 29, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/in-business-7-1-26/)). Whether it has reopened could not be confirmed.
- **Sabor Cuencano**, 2552 Central Ave. NE, breakfast and Ecuadorian fast food, with current hours on its own site ([Sabor Cuencano](https://www.saborcuencanomn.com/)).
- **Panadería Ecuatoriana Charito**, 2409 Central Ave. NE, the Northeast branch of a family bakery, operating for about ten years ([Northeaster, Aug. 3, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuadorians-bring-street-food-vending-to-central/)).
- **La Mesa**, 230 Cedar Lake Road S. in Bryn Mawr, a bistro "with Latin roots" owned by Juan Yunga, from Cuenca, and Ann Carlson-Yunga ([La Mesa](https://www.lamesampls.com/)).
- **Chulla Vida**, announced in 2025 for 2405 Central Ave. NE with the promise of "a taste of Ecuador and more" ([Northeaster, March 13, 2025](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/in-business-3-12-25/)). No current source confirmed it is operating.
- **Los Andes Latin Bistro**, a family-owned Latin American restaurant on West Lake Street, opened a second location at 2300 University Ave. NE in 2026 and hosted EcuaFiesta in August 2026 ([Los Andes](https://losandesmn.com/); [Northeaster, May 19, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/in-business-5-20-26/); [Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/event/ecuafiesta-2026/)).

**Markets and shops.** El Paraíso, a supermarket and restaurant on Central Avenue co-owned by Bertha Orellana, opened in summer 2025, stocking chocho beans, Andean fruits, ají peppers and frozen cuy (guinea pig). La Cuencanita Market and La Casita Ecuatoriana opened in south Minneapolis about the same time ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 11, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-ecuadorian-community-trump-immigration/)). Killary Crafts in Columbia Heights, owned by Juan Cazho and Jeanneth Guaman, sold traditional clothing and handmade beadwork in 2024 ([Sahan Journal, March 18, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/)). Mirasol Express, run by the Quizhpi family at 2801 Central Ave. NE, was an Ecuadorian market in 2021 ([Northeaster, June 3, 2021](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ethnic-grocers-evoke-feelings-of-home/)).

**Street food.** Cevichocho stands on Central Avenue are part of daily life; Sahan Journal described about a dozen customers at one stand on a fall afternoon in 2025 ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-ecuadorian-community-trump-immigration/)).

**Tensions and support.** Some established owners are uneasy. The owner of Panadería Charito told the Northeaster in 2026 that he disagrees with unlicensed street vendors because his business pays taxes and staff and competes with them ([Northeaster, Aug. 3, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuadorians-bring-street-food-vending-to-central/)). After Operation Metro Surge hurt business, the city gave grants in April 2026, including $20,500 to the Audubon Neighborhood Association for a Central Avenue Latino Business Directory and events showcasing Ecuadorian culture, food and entrepreneurship ([Northeaster, May 19, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ne-organizations-receive-100000-in-city-grants/)).

This is a short list. No Ecuadorian-owned law, accounting, insurance, tax, travel or media business in Minnesota could be confirmed, and no Ecuadorian chamber of commerce was found (see "What could not be established").

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

**Catholic parishes.** Ss. Cyril and Methodius, founded by Slovak Catholics on 2nd Street NE, is the best-documented Ecuadorian parish. The archdiocese says it has been predominantly Hispanic since 1997 ([Archdiocese](https://www.archspm.org/location/ss-cyril-and-methodius-catholic-church/)). Father Fernando Ortega, who ministers there, said in 2023 that it has the largest Ecuadorian congregation ([Catholic Spirit, Dec. 7, 2023](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/many-ecuadorian-immigrants-are-placing-their-hope-in-minnesota/)). Its summer festival features Ecuadorian food, soccer and volleyball tournaments and traditional dance, and a 2019 listing showed Sunday Mass at 9 a.m. in English and 10:30 a.m. in Spanish ([Northeaster, 2019](https://www.mynortheaster.com/event/saints-cyril-and-methodius-church-festival/)). In south Minneapolis, Sagrado Corazón de Jesús has held a Mass and procession for the Virgen del Cisne, a Marian devotion from southern Ecuador ([Catholic Spirit, Aug. 30, 2010](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/local/overheard/ecuadorian-catholics-display-devotion-to-mary/)), and the Cosecha del Corazón food center at Incarnation parish in south Minneapolis served many recent Ecuadorian arrivals in 2023 ([Catholic Spirit, Dec. 7, 2023](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/many-ecuadorian-immigrants-are-placing-their-hope-in-minnesota/)). In Richfield, a volunteer at Assumption parish praised the faith of Ecuadorian newcomers ([same](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/many-ecuadorian-immigrants-are-placing-their-hope-in-minnesota/)). St. John Paul II Catholic School, at Holy Cross parish, enrolls many Ecuadorian children ([Northeaster, Sept. 22, 2017](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/three-new-priests-serving-holy-cross-catholic-parish/)).

**Practice.** Parishes are also places for food, fundraising and help: Ss. Cyril's Christmas brunch has served Ecuadorian hominy ([Northeaster, 2019](https://www.mynortheaster.com/event/christmas-brunch-at-st-cyrils/)), and the city used the church to teach vendor licensing ([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2024/09/24/minneapolis-weighs-permit-system-unlicensed-fruit-vendors)).

**Other faiths.** No Minnesota source was found on Ecuadorian evangelical or Pentecostal congregations, on Indigenous Andean spiritual practice here, or on Ecuadorians with no religion. No survey of religious affiliation for Ecuadorian Minnesotans exists in what was found.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

- **Independence Day (August 10).** Ecuador marks the 1809 call for independence in Quito as a national holiday ([Teleamazonas, 2026](https://www.teleamazonas.com/actualidad/noticias/ecuador/proximos-feriados-ecuador-2026-agosto-diciembre-124862/)). In Minnesota, families celebrate on a nearby Sunday. In 2017, dancers of the group Indi Samay performed in a restaurant parking lot at 40th and Central in Columbia Heights; the group's president said they chose Sunday because August 10 fell on a workday ([Northeaster, Aug. 21, 2017](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/families-celebrate-ecuadors-independence-with-music-food-and-dance/)). **EcuaFiesta 2026** was held Sunday, Aug. 2, 2026, 11 a.m. to 5 p.m., at Los Andes Latin Bistro, 2300 University Ave. NE, with music, folk dance, an ecuavóley tournament, food and vendors ([Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/event/ecuafiesta-2026/)).
- **Virgen del Cisne (around August 15).** A Marian feast from southern Ecuador. In 2010 Sagrado Corazón de Jesús held an Aug. 15 Mass, changed the statue's robe, and led a three-block procession, after a day of games in a nearby park ([Catholic Spirit](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/local/overheard/ecuadorian-catholics-display-devotion-to-mary/)). No current-year announcement was found.
- **Parish festival at Ss. Cyril and Methodius (August).** Held on a weekend in August in many years (Aug. 17-18, 2019; Aug. 21, 2021; Aug. 19, 2023), with Ecuadorian food and tournaments ([2019](https://www.mynortheaster.com/event/saints-cyril-and-methodius-church-festival/); [2021](https://www.mynortheaster.com/event/sts-cyril-and-methodius-church-festival/); [2023](https://www.mynortheaster.com/event/ss-cyril-methodius-church-festival/)). No 2026 date was found.
- **Carnaval.** The Monday and Tuesday before Ash Wednesday (Feb. 16 and 17 in 2026 in Ecuador) ([El Universo](https://www.eluniverso.com/noticias/ecuador/los-dias-exactos-de-carnaval-2026-nota/)). Ecuadorian students at El Colegio hosted Carnaval on Feb. 9, 2024, spraying soap and glitter outside in the cold ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/)).
- **Holy Week and Easter, Christmas, Three Kings Day.** Major Catholic observances; parishes named above hold Spanish Masses. No Minnesota source described a specifically Ecuadorian Holy Week, Christmas procession (Pase del Niño) or Three Kings event.
- **Día de los Difuntos (November 2) and Cuenca's Independence (November 3).** National holidays in Ecuador ([Teleamazonas](https://www.teleamazonas.com/actualidad/noticias/ecuador/proximos-feriados-ecuador-2026-agosto-diciembre-124862/)). No Minnesota celebration was found, despite the many families from Cuenca.
- **Hispanic Heritage Month (September 15 to October 15).** No Ecuadorian-specific Heritage Month event was found, but during the 2026 month the Minnesota International Dance Festival chose Ecuador as its guest country, with workshops Sept. 28 to Oct. 2 and performances Oct. 2 and 3 at the Southern Theater, featuring Away Runakuna, Daniela Estafania and the Ayazamana Dance Group ([DanceMN](https://dancemn.org/submissions/minnesota-international-dance-festival-2026-celebrate-ecuador/); [MNIDF](https://mnidancefestival.org/festival-program)).
- **Summer volleyball picnics.** Not a holiday but a long tradition: every summer weekend since the 1990s, families gather to play and eat, WCCO reported ([WCCO, 2024](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/ecuadorian-volleyball-picnics-twin-cities/)).

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Households.** Ecuadorian households average 3.92 people. Half are married couples with children under 18; 7.8 percent are single mothers with children; 8.2 percent are people living alone ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/ecuadorian)).

**Generations.** Stephanie Puma of Columbia Heights told WCCO her family is now in its third generation in Minnesota, and that she met her husband at one of the volleyball picnics. Organizer Nixon Orellana said families try to speak Spanish with their children "so we don't want to lose that" ([WCCO, Sept. 24, 2024](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/ecuadorian-volleyball-picnics-twin-cities/)).

**Separated families.** Many recent arrivals came to join relatives already here ([Catholic Spirit, 2022](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/catholic-elementary-school-in-northeast-minneapolis-greets-newly-arrived-ecuadorians-ukrainians/)). The principal of St. John Paul II school named family reunification as a key goal, describing how arrivals at different times shake up family roles ([Northeaster, March 25, 2023](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/john-paul-ii-sees-immigrant-influx/)). One mother told Sahan Journal in 2024 she had come with her 5-year-old son to rejoin her partner, who had since been deported ([Sahan Journal, March 18, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/)).

**Food.** Sources describe llapingachos (potato cakes), hornado (roast pork), cevichochos (lupini beans in citrus sauce), humitas, guaguas de pan, colada morada, chaulafán, and cuy ([Racket](https://racketmn.com/bryn-mawrs-la-mesa-transports-diners-to-a-tiny-tucked-away-slice-of-ecuador); [Northeaster, Aug. 3, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuadorians-bring-street-food-vending-to-central/); [Sahan Journal, Nov. 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-ecuadorian-community-trump-immigration/)).

**Winter and newcomers.** A Catholic school counselor said children from Ecuador often need help understanding winter and getting a warm coat, and families ask about sledding and the Winter Carnival ([Catholic Spirit, 2022](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/catholic-elementary-school-in-northeast-minneapolis-greets-newly-arrived-ecuadorians-ukrainians/)).

**Aging.** Only 2.1 percent are 65 or older, and Compass suppresses most older age and poverty rows because the numbers are too small ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/ecuadorian)). One TPS Collective member who has lived in Columbia Heights for more than 30 years said he has not been able to retire ([COPAL, 2024](https://copalmn.org/community-mobilization-leads-to-passage-of-temporary-protected-status-for-ecuador-resolution-in-columbia-heights/)). No other Minnesota source on Ecuadorian elders was found.

No Minnesota sources on dress, marriage customs or childhood traditions beyond those above were found.

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Dance and music.** Indi Samay, a dance group that began with one family, performed traditional dances such as "Matrimonial" in 2017 ([Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/families-celebrate-ecuadors-independence-with-music-food-and-dance/)). Away Runakuna Dance Group performed at Logan Park's holiday event in December 2025 ([Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/event/lights-of-logan-3/)) and taught sanjuanito, Inti Raymi, Shuar and Carnaval dances at the 2026 international dance festival ([MNIDF](https://mnidancefestival.org/festival-program)). Ecuadorian dancers performed at Columbia Heights' 2021 centennial cultural festival ([Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/event/heights-cultural-festival/)).

**Visual art.** Victor Yepez made a mosaic mural for a Central Avenue business in 2022 as part of the Mural Central project, and had completed a festival mural in Austin, Minnesota ([Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuador-meets-afghanistan-in-one-of-latest-murals-completed/)). Chimborazo decorates with Otavalo textiles and plays Quichua music ([Chimborazo](https://chimborazorestaurant.com/home/)).

**Sports.** Ecuavóley, a three-player volleyball played with a soccer ball and a higher net, is central. After consulting the Ecuadorian community, the Minneapolis Park and Recreation Board rebuilt Audubon Park in Northeast with courts striped for ecuavóley; they opened in late October 2025 ([Northeaster, July 9, 2025](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/renovations-are-now-underway-at-audubon-park/); [Northeaster, Nov. 7, 2025](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/items-of-note-11-5-25/)). Liga Northeast, a futsal and soccer league started in 2005 by Jose and Bertha Arias after a tournament at St. Cyril's, began with Ecuadorian players and by 2022 had more than 1,000 players of many backgrounds, playing Sundays at Edison High School and Northeast Middle School ([Northeaster, Feb. 1, 2022](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/atletico-san-miguel-penarol-tie-2-2-in-futsal/)).

**Media.** No Ecuadorian-specific outlet in Minnesota was found. Northeast's community paper, the Northeaster, covers the community regularly, and Sahan Journal publishes in Spanish.

## 9. Civic and political life

**Consulate.** Ecuador's consulate is on Central Avenue Northeast ([FOX 9, Jan. 2026](https://www.fox9.com/news/ice-agent-consulate-ecuador-minneapolis-attempted-incursion-jan-27-2026)). The Northeaster dates its opening to 2005 ([Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuadorians-make-northeast-metro-area-home/)). On Jan. 27, 2026, staff blocked an ICE agent from entering. Ecuador's foreign ministry called it an "attempted incursion" and sent a protest note to the U.S. Embassy in Quito; one employee said on video that agents had followed people there ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/immigration/ice-agent-blocked-ecuador-consulate-minneapolis-trump-immigration-rcna256284)).

**Temporary Protected Status campaign.** COPAL organized a TPS Collective of Ecuadorian members. In spring 2024 the Columbia Heights and St. Paul councils voted unanimously for resolutions urging the federal government to designate Ecuador for TPS, and Minneapolis followed, working with the consulate. COPAL's organizing director said Ecuadorian member-leaders met weekly and that, since the decision belongs to the executive branch, the campaign was "all about education and public pressure" ([COPAL, Columbia Heights](https://copalmn.org/community-mobilization-leads-to-passage-of-temporary-protected-status-for-ecuador-resolution-in-columbia-heights/); [COPAL, St. Paul](https://copalmn.org/community-mobilization-leads-to-passage-of-temporary-protected-status-for-ecuador-resolution-in-st-paul/); [COPAL](https://copalmn.org/minneapolis-st-paul-and-columbia-heights-city-councils-with-copal-mn-and-the-consulate-of-ecuador-passed-a-resolution-callging-on-federal-officials-to-designate-ecuador-for-tps/)). U.S. Rep. Ilhan Omar co-authored a TPS bill ([Sahan Journal, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/)). As of November 2025, TPS had never been granted to Ecuador ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-ecuadorian-community-trump-immigration/)).

**Organizations.** COPAL (Communities Organizing Latine Power and Action); Movimiento Comunitario Minnesota, which brought food and immigration information to shelters and hotels ([Sahan Journal, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/)); and parishes are the most visible. Diego Guaman, who came in 2011 sponsored by his mother and attended Wellstone International High School, has organized for new arrivals ([same](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/)).

**Officials who work with the community.** Minneapolis council member Jason Chavez, who described himself as the son of Mexican immigrants, led the city's TPS resolution and the street-vendor ordinance ([MPR News, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/05/01/nonprofit-copal-calls-on-state-leaders-to-support-temporary-protected-status-for-ecuador); [Northeaster, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuadorians-bring-street-food-vending-to-central/)). No Ecuadorian American elected official in Minnesota was found.

**Debates inside the community.** Established business owners and newer street vendors disagree about licensing and competition. Older residents and newcomers also differ in how they came: Guaman's mother, for example, became a permanent resident and sponsored her children, while many recent arrivals are in asylum proceedings ([Northeaster, Aug. 3, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuadorians-bring-street-food-vending-to-central/); [Sahan Journal, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/)).

## 10. Education

**Attainment.** Among Ecuadorian adults 25 and older, 57.7 percent finished high school and 19.8 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more, with 11.1 percent holding a graduate degree ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/ecuadorian)). With 42.3 percent lacking a diploma and 11.1 percent holding a graduate degree, schooling in the community varies widely.

**Schools.** CBS Minnesota reported in December 2024 that 49 percent of Columbia Heights Public Schools students were English learners; the principal of Valley View Elementary said most of his new students come from Ecuador. His school offers a six-week parent course, a food and clothing shelf, free medical and dental care, and help applying for Driver's Licenses for All, and a district leader said the district goes without about $1 million in state funding because aid is based on the prior year's count ([CBS Minnesota, Dec. 20, 2024](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/columbia-heights-migrant-students/)). St. John Paul II Catholic School in Northeast built a "Newcomer Experience" in 2022; most of its new arrivals come from Ecuador, and more than 75 percent of its roughly 180 students are not native English speakers ([Northeaster, March 25, 2023](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/john-paul-ii-sees-immigrant-influx/); [Catholic Spirit, Dec. 5, 2022](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/catholic-elementary-school-in-northeast-minneapolis-greets-newly-arrived-ecuadorians-ukrainians/)). In 2024, 44 of 98 students at El Colegio charter high school were from Ecuador. The school created a position to evaluate credits earned in Ecuador and offers Quichua classes ([Sahan Journal, March 18, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/)). Organizer Diego Guaman said he works with students so they do not miss class for work.

**During the 2026 enforcement surge.** Columbia Heights' superintendent said about 800 of 3,400 students stopped attending in person, and the district expanded online learning from about 25 students to 800 ([Northeaster, Feb. 17, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/citys-schools-reeling-from-ice-presence/)).

**College.** An Augsburg University student from Ecuador with a pending asylum case was detained in August 2025 and later left the country, his attorney told Sahan Journal ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-ecuadorian-community-trump-immigration/)). No Ecuadorian student association or Ecuadorian scholar at a Minnesota college was found.

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Coverage.** 24.0 percent of Ecuadorian Minnesotans have no health insurance (give or take 7.2 points) ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/ecuadorian)). Minnesota ended MinnesotaCare eligibility for undocumented noncitizens 18 and older on Jan. 1, 2026, after a 68-65 House vote in June 2025 ([Minnesota House Session Daily](https://www.house.mn.gov/sessiondaily/Story/18830)). How many Ecuadorian adults this affected is not known.

**Earlier efforts.** In 2013 the consulate and St. Mary's Health Clinics, a ministry of the Sisters of St. Joseph of Carondelet, signed what the consul general called the first agreement of its kind in the United States, setting up a health resource center, Centro Vida y Salud, inside the consulate for screenings and referrals to free care. A mother interviewed then said hospitals first ask for an insurance card, which her family did not have ([Catholic Spirit, March 15, 2013](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/st-marys-health-clinics-partner-with-ecuadorian-consulate/)). Whether the center still operates was not found.

**Where people get help now.** School-based clinics in Columbia Heights ([CBS Minnesota](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/columbia-heights-migrant-students/)), parish food centers and the Camden Collective food share ([Catholic Spirit](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/many-ecuadorian-immigrants-are-placing-their-hope-in-minnesota/); [Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/)) appear in reporting. During the 2026 surge, neighborhood volunteers delivered food to families afraid to leave home ([Northeaster, Feb. 17, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/citys-schools-reeling-from-ice-presence/)).

**Stress and trauma.** Educators at St. John Paul II said most new students have experienced trauma on the journey or in separation ([Northeaster, 2023](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/john-paul-ii-sees-immigrant-influx/)). El Colegio staff said some students' families were robbed and assaulted after arriving on Lake Street ([Sahan Journal, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/)). Columbia Heights' superintendent said children came home sick from detention centers and worried about their trauma ([Northeaster, Feb. 17, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/citys-schools-reeling-from-ice-presence/)).

No Minnesota clinic tied specifically to the Ecuadorian community, and no Minnesota source on traditional Andean healing practice, was found.

## 12. Disability

**The only Ecuadorian-specific Minnesota number.** Compass reports 6.1 percent of Ecuadorian Minnesotans with a disability (give or take 2.6 points), and 4.9 percent of those under 65 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/ecuadorian)). The community is very young and the margin is wide. What lies behind the figure is an open question: the age structure, how the survey questions land in translation, reluctance to answer a government survey, or real difference could each play a part, and no source settles it.

**Latino-wide evidence.** A study for the Minnesota Department of Human Services by NORC at the University of Chicago, which included Latinx Minnesotans, found that people with limited English struggle with the lack of in-language materials and interpreters during the assessment for home and community-based waiver services. It also described stigma around asking for help with mental health or toileting, and distrust that can lead people to understate need ([NORC for DHS](https://www.norc.org/content/dam/norc-org/pdf2024/FINAL_HEARD%20Community-Facing%20Findings.pdf)). Those findings are for Latinx people generally, not Ecuadorians.

**Questions the evidence raises.**
- Who names a disability first? For children, it is often schools. Columbia Heights, El Colegio and St. John Paul II all describe many new Ecuadorian students who arrived speaking little English, placed below their age because of credit gaps, or carrying trauma from the journey ([CBS Minnesota](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/columbia-heights-migrant-students/); [Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/); [Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/john-paul-ii-sees-immigrant-influx/)). How schools tell language learning, trauma and disability apart for these children is not documented in what was found.
- What does a label open or close? Public coverage depends on immigration status: undocumented adults lost MinnesotaCare in 2026 ([Session Daily](https://www.house.mn.gov/sessiondaily/Story/18830)).
- What produced an impairment? Sources describe journeys through the Darién Gap, assaults, and detention. Whether these have led to lasting injury or disability among Ecuadorian Minnesotans is not documented.
- Which language? A Spanish interpreter may not serve a Kichwa-speaking family. No count of Kichwa speakers or of qualified Kichwa interpreters in Minnesota was found.

No Minnesota organization focused on Ecuadorian people with disabilities, and no Minnesota Department of Health, Department of Education or University of Minnesota study naming Ecuadorians, was found.

## 13. What is changing now

**Operation Metro Surge (winter 2025-26).** NBC News reported that some 3,000 ICE and Border Patrol agents had been sent to Minnesota by January 2026 ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/immigration/ice-agent-blocked-ecuador-consulate-minneapolis-trump-immigration-rcna256284)). Columbia Heights became what the Northeaster called an "epicenter." On Jan. 20, 2026, ICE detained an Ecuadorian father and his 5-year-old son in Columbia Heights and sent them to a family detention center in Dilley, Texas. Their attorney said they had entered at Brownsville in 2024 using the CBP One app and were following the asylum process; the Department of Homeland Security said the father ran and left the child ([KSTP, Jan. 22, 2026](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/lawyer-father-5-year-old-son-detained-in-columbia-heights-were-legal-asylum-seekers/)). The pair were later released temporarily, and the superintendent said at least seven school-age children and 23 adults had been detained in Columbia Heights by mid-February ([Northeaster, Feb. 17, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/citys-schools-reeling-from-ice-presence/)). The consulate incident followed a week later ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/immigration/ice-agent-blocked-ecuador-consulate-minneapolis-trump-immigration-rcna256284)).

**Legal limbo.** Immigration attorney Graham Ojala-Barbour told Sahan Journal in late 2025 that most Ecuadorians are fleeing generalized gang violence, which rarely meets asylum rules, and that judges can now dismiss such claims without a full hearing. Many hold work permits, he said, but ICE now argues people who did not enter with a visa are not eligible for bond. A family reunification parole program begun in late 2023 was ended ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 11, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-ecuadorian-community-trump-immigration/)).

**Still growing.** At the same time, the Northeaster reported new businesses, ecuavóley courts and a new EcuaFiesta in 2026 ([Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuadorians-make-northeast-metro-area-home/); [Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/event/ecuafiesta-2026/)). Recent arrivals told Sahan Journal they like Minnesota; one vendor said, "It's relaxed here" ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-ecuadorian-community-trump-immigration/)).

**Disagreements.** Inside the community, people disagree about unlicensed vending and about competition between older and newer businesses ([Northeaster, Aug. 3, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/ecuadorians-bring-street-food-vending-to-central/)). One young man told Sahan Journal he left Ecuador because of discrimination over his sexuality ([Sahan Journal, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/)); no Minnesota source on LGBTQ Ecuadorians more broadly was found.

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **Building Northeast.** Ecuadorian families helped refill an old Slovak parish and a Polish-heritage Catholic school, and filled storefronts along Central Avenue ([Archdiocese](https://www.archspm.org/location/ss-cyril-and-methodius-catholic-church/); [Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/john-paul-ii-sees-immigrant-influx/)).
- **Mutual aid.** At Camden Collective, most volunteers who speak only Spanish were recent Ecuadorian arrivals who first came for food ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ecuadorians-asylum-minnesota/)). Volleyball picnics have welcomed newcomers for almost 30 years ([WCCO](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/ecuadorian-volleyball-picnics-twin-cities/)).
- **Civic voice.** COPAL credits its Ecuadorian TPS Collective with driving resolutions in three city councils in 2024 ([COPAL](https://copalmn.org/minneapolis-st-paul-and-columbia-heights-city-councils-with-copal-mn-and-the-consulate-of-ecuador-passed-a-resolution-callging-on-federal-officials-to-designate-ecuador-for-tps/)).
- **Shaping public space.** Ecuavóley courts at Audubon Park, built after the park board consulted the Ecuadorian community, are now part of a city park ([Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/items-of-note-11-5-25/)).
- **Food and art.** Chimborazo, La Mesa, Sabor Cuencano, Charito and dance groups like Away Runakuna bring Andean and coastal traditions to wide audiences ([Meet Minneapolis](https://www.minneapolis.org/latinx-community/two-day-itinerary/); [MNIDF](https://mnidancefestival.org/festival-program)).
- **Schools.** A Columbia Heights principal called the newcomers "a gift," noting that English learners in the district graduate at nearly 83 percent ([CBS Minnesota](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/columbia-heights-migrant-students/)).
