# Corrections to the existing Salvadoran Minnesota page

Page: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/salvadoran

## Outdated

1. **Claim:** All Compass figures, labeled ACS 2019 to 2023: 17,571 people; 76.7 percent Twin Cities and 23.3 percent Greater Minnesota; 48.5 percent age 17 or younger; 43.9 percent foreign-born; 79.1 percent language other than English; 39.0 percent English less than "very well"; 62.1 percent high school or higher; 17.0 percent bachelor's or higher; median household income about $79,911; poverty 5.9 percent; owner-occupied 55.9 percent; household size 3.79; disability 3.4 percent.
   **Source says:** Compass now reports 2020 to 2024: 17,444 (plus or minus 3,014); 73.2 percent Twin Cities and 26.8 percent Greater Minnesota; 46.5 percent 17 or younger; 43.0 percent foreign-born; 76.8 percent language other than English; 34.8 percent English less than "very well"; 65.3 percent high school or higher; 18.7 percent bachelor's or higher; median household income $88,051 (plus or minus $23,118) in 2024 dollars; poverty 5.6 percent; owner-occupied 61.6 percent; household size 3.64; disability 3.3 percent. The page's repeated phrase "Greater Minnesota at 23.3 percent" should become 26.8 percent.
   **Link:** https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran .
2. **Claim:** "Compass suppresses the 65+ figure in this vintage, you should not invent one" (repeated several times).
   **Source says:** The 2020 to 2024 profile now publishes 65 and older as 1.9 percent, plus or minus 1.3. Individual 65-74, 75-84 and 85+ rows remain suppressed.
   **Link:** https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran .
3. **Claim:** Health section: "Uninsured and delayed-care patterns sit here, so date Minnesota figures when you have them, and do not invent a rate."
   **Source says:** Compass does publish an uninsured rate: 19.3 percent of Salvadoran Minnesotans have no health insurance (plus or minus 5.4). It also shows 59.1 percent of renter households are cost-burdened. The page should use these dated figures.
   **Link:** https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran .
## Claims that should change or be qualified

4. **Claim:** "We did not find a Salvadoran association or hometown committee in Minnesota.. Organizing we could document runs through Latino-led agencies, legal services and the consulate."
   **Source says:** Still no Salvadoran-only association was found, but the page misses Greater Minnesota organizing with Salvadoran founders: Convivencia Hispana in St. James was formed in 2015 with help from Marta Zelaya, who fled El Salvador's war, and lists roots in Mexico, Honduras, Guatemala and El Salvador.
   **Links:** https://archive.strongtowns.org/journal/2019/10/31/we-watch-out-for-each-other ; https://www.latinoleadmn.org/post/greater-mn-story-convivencia-hispana .
5. **Claim:** COPAL is "Comunidades Organizando el Poder y la Acción Latina."
   **Source says:** COPAL's own site now uses "Communities Organizing Latine Power and Action." The Spanish name is what Twin Cities Business used in 2024. Founding year 2018 is confirmed.
   **Links:** https://www.copalmn.org/ ; https://tcbmag.com/notable/edwin-torres-desantiago/ .
6. **Claim:** La Prensa de Minnesota is "now part of LCN Media."
   **Source says:** MNopedia says the Duartes sold it in 2006 to El Cine Latino Communications Networks, which later sold it to Latino Communications Network, and that by 2018 it was distributed inside another Spanish-language paper. "LCN Media" was not confirmed. The paper's site was active with October 2026 stories.
   **Links:** https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/la-prensa-de-minnesota ; https://laprensademn.com/ .
7. **Claim:** Sandra Benítez's "mother was Puerto Rican."
   **Source says:** Not found in the pages opened. El Diario de Hoy names her parents as James Q. Ables, a U.S. Embassy diplomat, and Martha Benítez, and says the family moved to San Salvador in 1946; it does not give her mother's birthplace. Minnesota Writers on the Map says she adopted her mother's maiden name. El Diario de Hoy spells her birth name "Sandra Jeannette Ables"; the page has "Jeanette." Her death in Minneapolis on July 25, 2024, and her awards are confirmed.
   **Links:** https://www.elsalvador.com/h-vida/h-mujeres/cultura-escritora-estadounidense-sandra-benitez-y-su-conexion-con-el-salvador/1160683/2024/ ; https://mnwritersmap.org/sandra-benitez/ .
8. **Claim:** Counts "disagree across ACS vintages and definitions" (stated without numbers), and "MPR News reported in September 2026 that more than 14,000 Salvadorans live in Minnesota."
   **Source says:** Both are accurate, and the difference can now be explained: the Census Bureau's Hispanic-origin question counts 14,670 Salvadorans (plus or minus 1,712) for 2020 to 2024, while Compass's broader cultural-community method counts 17,444. The 2024 one-year estimate is 11,312 (plus or minus 3,384). Census also counts 8,255 Minnesotans born in El Salvador.
   **Links:** https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=04000US27,050%7C04000US27 ; https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US27 ; https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/09/08/what-the-end-of-tps-could-mean-for-salvadorans-in-minnesota .
9. **Claim:** "Do not assume Minneapolis.. Greater Minnesota towns with work and housing" (no towns named).
   **Source says:** Census counts by place show St. Paul (3,092) far ahead of Minneapolis (815), and Greater Minnesota concentrations in Worthington (527), Rochester (452) and smaller towns such as St. James, Madelia and Montevideo, all with wide margins. Naming St. Paul and these towns would make the page more useful.
   **Link:** https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=160%7C04000US27 .
## Not supported by any Minnesota source found

10. **Claims:** "voseo is ordinary in Salvadoran rooms"; "Catholic and a large evangelical / Pentecostal stream both live in Salvadoran Minnesota"; "Fútbol is the common public sport"; "Some people send money to relatives in El Salvador as an obligation"; "Cumbia, church music, and later pop are all here"; work "spans service, construction, food processing, restaurants, cleaning, childcare.. public employment."
    **Source says:** No Minnesota source opened addresses Salvadoran Spanish usage, religious affiliation, sports, remittances or music. Minnesota sources do document meat processing, landscaping on H-2B visas, food businesses, banking and communications work. These claims may be broadly true but should be marked as general background or removed.
   .
11. **Claim:** "A fiesta or a Romero commemoration is optional professional development if you are invited."
    **Source says:** No current Salvadoran fiesta or Romero commemoration in Minnesota was found. The most recent documented Minnesota Romero event was a March 2020 speaking tour by Marta Portillo, a catechist who worked with Romero, in southern Minnesota parishes.
    **Links:** https://files.constantcontact.com/0d30324c501/8c489f3d-7a22-4940-a7a7-dab33788681b.pdf ; https://www.mankatofreepress.com/news/local_news/monday-informer-speaker-to-discuss-el-salvador-romero/article_4337a350-5f0f-11ea-afd4-3b419bb8b6c3.html .
12. **Claim:** January 16 "is the Peace Accords."
    **Source says:** The accords were signed Jan. 16, 1992, but in January 2022 El Salvador's Legislative Assembly renamed the day the Day of the Victims of the Armed Conflict and repealed the peace-day decrees. The page should note the change.
    **Links:** https://peaceaccords.nd.edu/accord/chapultepec-peace-agreement ; https://www.elciudadano.com/en/bukele-talks-about-the-peace-agreements-and-unleashes-another-controversy-in-el-salvador/01/21/ .
