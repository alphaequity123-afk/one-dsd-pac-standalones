# Salvadorans in Minnesota

## 1. People and place

**How many.** Minnesota Compass counts 17,444 Salvadoran Minnesotans, give or take 3,014, using the American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024. It places 73.2 percent in the Twin Cities and 26.8 percent in Greater Minnesota, each give or take 5.9 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran)). Compass builds its cultural community counts from several self-reported answers, including ancestry, ethnic group, birthplace and parents' characteristics ([Minnesota Compass, Sept. 18, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-hispanic-and-latino-communities)). That is why it runs higher than the Census Bureau's own count of people who mark "Salvadoran" on the Hispanic-origin question: 14,670, give or take 1,712, in the same 2020 to 2024 survey ([Census Bureau ACS table B03001, via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=04000US27,050%7C04000US27)). The single-year 2024 estimate is 11,312, with a wide margin of 3,384 ([ACS 2024 one-year, via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_1yr?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=04000US27)). MPR News used "more than 14,000" in September 2026 ([MPR News, Sept. 8, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/09/08/what-the-end-of-tps-could-mean-for-salvadorans-in-minnesota)). All of these are survey estimates of a small group, so treat any one number as a range, not a head count.

**Born here and born there.** Compass puts the foreign-born share at 43.0 percent, give or take 6.5 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran)). The Census Bureau counts 8,255 Minnesotans born in El Salvador, give or take 1,154 ([ACS table B05006, via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US27)); Compass's immigrant table shows 7,829 ([Minnesota Compass, immigration](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/immigration)). In other words, a majority of Salvadoran Minnesotans were born in the United States.

**A very young community.** Compass reports 46.5 percent are 17 or younger and just 1.9 percent are 65 or older (give or take 1.3 points). Children under 5 alone are 11.8 percent. Women are 52.1 percent and men 47.9 percent ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran)).

**Where.** The Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 counts by county put the largest groups in Ramsey (3,468, give or take 1,102), Hennepin (3,071, give or take 807), Dakota (2,289, give or take 721) and Anoka (1,226, give or take 449) counties. Outside the metro, Nobles County, home of Worthington, is next at 619 (give or take 301), followed by Olmsted, Washington, St. Louis, Watonwan and Mower counties, each in the low hundreds with wide margins ([ACS B03001 by county, via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=04000US27,050%7C04000US27)). By city, St. Paul stands out at 3,092 (give or take 1,082), far ahead of Minneapolis at 815 (give or take 385). Bloomington, Worthington, Burnsville, South St. Paul, Brooklyn Center, Fridley, Richfield and Rochester each show roughly 450 to 550, and smaller southern Minnesota towns such as St. James, Madelia and Montevideo each show more than 100, all with margins nearly as large as the estimates ([ACS B03001 by place, via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=160%7C04000US27)).

**How people came.** Several streams show up in Minnesota sources:

- *The war years.* Mario Duarte and his family fled the civil war and settled on St. Paul's West Side in the late 1980s. MNopedia notes that few Central Americans lived in Minnesota then, so his daughter Lorena grew up within the larger Chicano and Mexican community ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/la-prensa-de-minnesota)). In 1982 a Salvadoran man using the name René Hurtado took sanctuary at St. Luke Presbyterian Church in Hennepin County while his deportation case played out in court and the national press ([openDemocracy, March 30, 2017](https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/minnesota-churches-face-tough-questions-in-offering-sanctuar/)).
- *Secondary migration from California.* Marta Zelaya fled the war with her two daughters, lived first in Los Angeles, and moved to St. James in 1994 after a friend described Minnesota as peaceful ([Strong Towns, Oct. 31, 2019](https://archive.strongtowns.org/journal/2019/10/31/we-watch-out-for-each-other)). Oral histories gathered in St. James from 2017 to 2024 include seven Salvadoran narrators, several of whom lived in Los Angeles or elsewhere in California before following family to Minnesota ([UMN Morris Digital Well](https://digitalcommons.morris.umn.edu/unitingcultures/)).
- *Temporary Protected Status.* El Salvador was designated for TPS in March 2001 after an earthquake; only people present by then could qualify. Mitchell Hamline law professor Ana Pottratz Acosta estimated in 2026 that 3,000 to 4,000 Minnesotans hold it, while noting she could find no Minnesota-specific figure ([MPR News, Sept. 8, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/09/08/what-the-end-of-tps-could-mean-for-salvadorans-in-minnesota)).
- *A newer wave.* The same professor described significant arrivals from El Salvador and its neighbors from about 2012 or 2013 through 2022 or 2023 ([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/09/08/what-the-end-of-tps-could-mean-for-salvadorans-in-minnesota)). Sahan Journal met recent arrivals from El Salvador at a 2024 driver's license class in Minnesota ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/drivers-license-for-all-minnesota-undocumented-trump-deportation/)).
- *Seasonal workers.* Eight Salvadorans came to Minneapolis on H-2B visas in 2024 to tend gardens for Biota Landscape, which had hired Salvadoran crews three years running ([El Diario de Hoy, 2024](https://www.elsalvador.com/h-noticias/h-nacional/manos-salvadorenas-embellecen-jardines-minneapolis/1183783/2024/)).

The numbers grew. In 2006 the owners of a new St. Paul bakery told MPR News they knew of only a few thousand Salvadorans in Minnesota ([MPR News, June 26, 2006](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2006/06/26/salvadorianbakery)), and a 2021 Compass article listed Salvadorans as the third-largest Latino cultural group in the state, after Mexican and Puerto Rican Minnesotans ([Minnesota Compass, Oct. 11, 2021](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/updated-minnesotas-latinos-numbers-overview-minnesota-compass-data)).

**Places of origin.** Families name specific Salvadoran places. The owners of Cabañas restaurant in Worthington named it for co-owner Luis Madrid's home department and said "we have a lot of people from Cabañas in Worthington"; one owner is from San Vicente ([The Globe, Sept. 15, 2023](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/new-salvadoran-restaurant-is-answer-to-owners-prayers)).

**Language.** Among Salvadoran Minnesotans 5 and older, 76.8 percent speak a language other than English at home and 34.8 percent speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran)). Compass does not say which languages; Spanish is the obvious majority language, but no Minnesota source counted speakers of Nahuat or other Indigenous languages among Salvadoran residents. Poet Lorena Duarte, who came as a child, called herself a "Minnesotana-Salvadoreña" and summed it up as loving "pupusas and Mac and Cheese" ([MPR News, Sept. 24, 2008](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2008/09/24/palabristas)).

## 2. All walks of life

No single picture fits. Figures below are from Compass for 2020 to 2024, in 2024 dollars, unless noted, and carry wide margins because the group is small ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran)).

- **Comfortable and well-off households.** Median household income is $88,051, give or take a very wide $23,118. Nearly half of households, 46.6 percent, take in $100,000 or more. A Compass analysis of 2018 to 2022 data found Salvadoran-headed households earned nearly $17,000 more than the Latino median of $63,400 ([Minnesota Compass, Sept. 18, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-hispanic-and-latino-communities)). Households are large, averaging 3.64 people, so household income is spread across more earners and dependents.
- **Professionals and owners.** Among adults 25 and older, 18.7 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more, including 6.6 percent with a graduate or professional degree. Visible careers include Rene Madrid, who has led U.S. Bank's Lake Street branch in Minneapolis for more than a decade ([Twin Cities Business](https://tcbmag.com/notable/rene-madrid/)); Edwin Torres DeSantiago, a vice president at the Minneapolis communications firm NewPublica ([Twin Cities Business, 2024](https://tcbmag.com/notable/edwin-torres-desantiago/)); Alejandro Soto Bonilla, a co-owner of a café near the University of Minnesota ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 17, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/sip-of-silk-cafe-minneapolis-university-of-minnesota/)); and a St. Paul homeowner who came at 16 in 1994 and in 2020 worked in information technology at Century College ([Sahan Journal, Sept. 17, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/immigration-tps-el-salvador-minnesota/)).
- **Middle-income and working families.** Owner-occupied homes are 61.6 percent of households (give or take 8.6). Employment among adults 18 to 64 is 84.7 percent, 87.9 percent for men and 81.4 percent for women, higher than the 72 to 80 percent range Compass reported across Latino groups in 2024 ([Minnesota Compass, Sept. 18, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-hispanic-and-latino-communities)). Another 21.3 percent of people live between the poverty line and twice that line.
- **People in poverty and under strain.** About 5.6 percent of people live below the poverty line (give or take 2.9). Among 18- to 24-year-olds the rate is 15.7 percent, though that margin is 9 points. Among renters, 59.1 percent spend 30 percent or more of income on housing, and 19.3 percent of all Salvadoran Minnesotans have no health insurance. Over a third of adults 25 and older, 34.7 percent, did not finish high school ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran)). In St. James, one oral history narrator described growing up poor with a single mother in El Salvador, and another described an orphaned childhood and work in coffee fields from a young age ([UMN Morris Digital Well](https://digitalcommons.morris.umn.edu/unitingcultures/)).

**Over time, with caution.** A 2021 Compass article by HACER staff, using an earlier survey period, reported a Salvadoran median household income of $33,000 and a foreign-born share of 54 percent ([Minnesota Compass, Oct. 11, 2021](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/updated-minnesotas-latinos-numbers-overview-minnesota-compass-data)). The jump to $88,051 is large. Some of it may be real; some may reflect sampling error in a small group and changes in how Compass assembles cultural community counts. The sources do not explain the change, so read it as a direction to check, not a settled fact.

**Hard to see in the averages.** Household income does not show immigration status, and status shapes work and wealth. Professor Pottratz Acosta noted that many long-term TPS holders have jobs and have bought homes, which is what would be at risk if the status ended ([MPR News, Sept. 8, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/09/08/what-the-end-of-tps-could-mean-for-salvadorans-in-minnesota)). No Minnesota figure for Salvadoran household wealth was found.

**For national context only.** Pew counted about 2.5 million people of Salvadoran origin in the United States in 2021, 53 percent foreign-born, with 13 percent of adults holding a bachelor's degree and 17 percent in poverty ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/us-hispanics-facts-on-salvadoran-origin-latinos/)). Minnesota's figures differ from these on poverty and income, which is another reason not to borrow national numbers.

## 3. Work and the economy

**Who works.** Compass counts 8,175 Salvadoran workers 16 and older. Most, 84.5 percent, drive or ride in a car to work, and about two-thirds travel 10 to 29 minutes ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran)). No Minnesota table of Salvadoran occupations or industries was found.

**Meat and food processing in southern Minnesota.** In St. James, Marta Zelaya found steady work at a local meat processing plant after arriving in 1994. Strong Towns describes waves of workers there: Mexicans first, then Salvadorans fleeing war, then a large Guatemalan population ([Strong Towns, Oct. 31, 2019](https://archive.strongtowns.org/journal/2019/10/31/we-watch-out-for-each-other)). Among St. James oral history narrators, one man described his meat processing job as better than farmwork in California, and another worked in agriculture and industrial processing ([UMN Morris Digital Well](https://digitalcommons.morris.umn.edu/unitingcultures/)). Worthington, which has one of the largest Salvadoran populations outside the metro, had a Swift packing plant where federal agents arrested 230 workers in a December 2006 immigration raid ([MPR News archive, Dec. 13, 2006](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/2006/12/13/workers-react-arrest-230-worthington)). The nationalities of those arrested are not given in the part read.

**Landscaping and seasonal visas.** Biota Landscape in Minneapolis brought eight Salvadoran workers on H-2B visas in 2024. One, a former television journalist from Santa Rosa de Lima, said he came to keep learning; the crew returned home in November ([El Diario de Hoy, 2024](https://www.elsalvador.com/h-noticias/h-nacional/manos-salvadorenas-embellecen-jardines-minneapolis/1183783/2024/)).

**Banking, communications and public life.** Rene Madrid has spent nearly 30 years in finance, works with the Latino Economic Development Center, and has mentored more than 65 high school students ([Twin Cities Business](https://tcbmag.com/notable/rene-madrid/)). The Latino Economic Development Center describes itself as an association of Latino entrepreneurs and leaders in Minnesota ([LEDC](https://www.ledcmn.org/)).

**Entrepreneurs.** Small food businesses are the most visible Salvadoran-owned firms in Minnesota sources (section 4). El Guanaco's co-owner told Sahan Journal in 2020 that the bakery employed 30 people at its St. Paul and Bloomington locations ([Sahan Journal, Sept. 17, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/immigration-tps-el-salvador-minnesota/)). Food-truck owners in Faribault and Worthington describe building a customer base town by town ([Faribault Daily News, 2025](https://www.southernminn.com/faribault_daily_news/news/salvadorian-food-truck-celebrates-6-years-in-business/article_2de906a4-f84a-11ef-b0e1-6fbbf13809ed.html); [The Globe, July 24, 2023](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/sandra-pineda-maria-sorto-bring-a-salvadoran-flavor-to-worthington)).

**Work authorization under pressure.** TPS carries a work permit. After DHS let the El Salvador designation pass its Sept. 9, 2026, expiration without a published decision, advocates reported Salvadoran TPS holders nationally losing jobs and having trouble renewing driver's licenses ([Alianza Americas, Oct. 9, 2026](https://www.alianzaamericas.org/post/congressman-glenn-ivey-d-md-impacted-families-legal-experts-and-advocates-sound-the-alarm-on-tps-legal-limbo-hitting-salvadoran-families)). The nursing home trade group AHCA/NCAL told its member employers not to assume these workers lost authorization on Sept. 9 ([AHCA/NCAL](https://www.ahcancal.org/News-and-Communications/Blog/Pages/USCIS-Confirms-Salvadoran-TPS-Protections-Remain-Valid-.aspx)). COPAL in Minneapolis urged employers not to act on rumors or outdated information ([COPAL, Sept. 10, 2026](https://copalmn.org/25-years-of-building-home-salvadoran-tps-holders-belong-in-the-communities-they-helped-build/)). No Minnesota count of affected workers was found.

**Unions and labor history.** No Minnesota source was found on Salvadoran union membership or labor organizing as a distinct group.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

A list accompanies this profile with what each source shows. Few Salvadoran businesses in Minnesota keep their own websites, and much of the coverage is several years old, so most could not be confirmed as open on the check date. The list is short; that reflects both a small community and thin online records.

**Bakery and restaurants in St. Paul.** El Guanaco Bakery and Cafe opened on East Seventh Street in 2006, owned by Oscar and Dolores Murcia, who had baked in El Salvador. MPR called it what was believed to be the state's first Salvadoran bakery, selling the Salvadoran quesadilla, a cheese-and-rice pound cake, and the banana upside-down cake called *volteado de guineo*. A customer from Red Wing said he planned to commute to buy the bread ([MPR News, June 26, 2006](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2006/06/26/salvadorianbakery)). By 2020 it had a second location in Bloomington ([Sahan Journal, Sept. 17, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/immigration-tps-el-salvador-minnesota/)). Mañana Restaurant at 828 East Seventh Street was owned by Rosario Diaz, who told Heavy Table in 2009 that she traveled to El Salvador two or three times a year to bring back ingredients and had a grinder custom-made there for chicharrón ([Heavy Table, 2009](https://heavytable.com/on-the-trail-of-the-perfect-pupusa/)). Current operating status for both could not be confirmed.

**Minneapolis.** Pupusería El Rincón Salvadoreño has served pupusas inside Mercado Central on East Lake Street ([Heavy Table, 2018](https://heavytable.com/east-lake-checklist-mercado-central/)), a market the Lake Street Council lists as 35 businesses ([Visit Lake Street](https://www.visitlakestreet.com/official-directory/mercado-central)). Pupuseria La Palmera in south Minneapolis was opened by Mauricio Prieto, a California native, with pupusas made by his Salvadoran mother, Ana ([Heavy Table](https://heavytable.com/pupusas-at-la-palmera/); [Mpls.St.Paul Magazine](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/pupusas-are-coming/)). El Centro, opened on East Lake Street by sisters Helen, Cici and Sara Villeda using family recipes ([Heavy Table](https://heavytable.com/el-centro-pupuseria-and-restaurant-on-east-lake-street-in-minneapolis/)), could not be confirmed as open. Abi's Cafe at 1532 East Lake Street lists Salvadoran breakfast and pupusa platters ([FoodBoss](https://www.foodboss.com/twin-cities-mn/abis-cafe-1532-e-lake-st-minneapolis-delivery)). Sip of Silk, a Salvadoran-Palestinian café at 333 Huron Boulevard near the University of Minnesota, opened Nov. 2, 2025, with a Salvadoran panini on the menu and hours until 10 or 11 p.m. ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 17, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/sip-of-silk-cafe-minneapolis-university-of-minnesota/)).

**Greater Minnesota.** In Worthington, Cabañas opened in July 2023 in the former A&W on East Avenue, serving pupusas and some Mexican dishes; an owner said there was no other Salvadoran restaurant between there and the Twin Cities or Sioux Falls ([The Globe, Sept. 15, 2023](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/new-salvadoran-restaurant-is-answer-to-owners-prayers)). The Sabor Salvadoreño food truck, run by Sandra Pineda and Maria Sorto since 2021, sold pupusas, fried yuca and horchata on weekends ([The Globe, July 24, 2023](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/sandra-pineda-maria-sorto-bring-a-salvadoran-flavor-to-worthington)). Taqueria Gomez in Worthington is run by a couple, one from Jalisco and one from El Salvador ([The Globe, story summary, June 27, 2023](https://www.dglobe.com/search?q=salvadoran)). Delicious Pupusas, Silvia Echeverria's family food truck, marked six years in 2025; it winters in Faribault and follows festivals and markets to Mankato, Rochester, Owatonna, Northfield and Red Wing ([Faribault Daily News, 2025](https://www.southernminn.com/faribault_daily_news/news/salvadorian-food-truck-celebrates-6-years-in-business/article_2de906a4-f84a-11ef-b0e1-6fbbf13809ed.html)). A Salvadoran woman in St. James told oral historians she runs a business there ([UMN Morris Digital Well](https://digitalcommons.morris.umn.edu/unitingcultures/)).

**Shared Latino commercial spaces.** Salvadoran food is often sold beside Mexican food. Bakery staff told MPR in 2006 that mixing the two loses what is special to each ([MPR News, 2006](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2006/06/26/salvadorianbakery)). Cabañas and Mañana both describe mixed menus.

**Media.** La Prensa de Minnesota, started in 1991 by Mario Duarte, a Salvadoran immigrant, was the state's first Spanish-language newspaper and was sold in 2006 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/la-prensa-de-minnesota)). It was still posting stories in October 2026 ([La Prensa de Minnesota](https://laprensademn.com/)).

**Consulate.** The Consulate General of El Salvador at 60 Plato Boulevard East, Suite 150, in St. Paul handles passports, the DUI identity card and civil documents by appointment. It was one of four new consular offices El Salvador opened, as reported in August 2022 ([Enlace Latino NC, Aug. 20, 2022](https://enlacelatinonc.org/en/Where-are-the-consulates-of-El-Salvador-in-the-United-States-located%3F/)).

No Salvadoran chamber of commerce, grocery, travel agency, law firm, accounting firm or remittance business in Minnesota could be confirmed.

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

No Minnesota survey of Salvadoran religious affiliation was found, so no share of Catholics, evangelicals or people of no faith can be given. What Minnesota sources do show is faith woven through public life.

**Catholic ties and the memory of Romero.** Archbishop Óscar Romero was assassinated while saying Mass on March 24, 1980; the United Nations now marks that date as the International Day for the Right to the Truth ([United Nations](https://www.un.org/en/observances/right-to-truth-day)). For his 40th anniversary in 2020, the Diocese of Winona-Rochester and Catholic Charities of Southern Minnesota brought Marta Portillo, a catechist who worked with Romero in the 1970s, to speak at parishes in Rochester, Mankato, Pipestone, Owatonna, Fairmont and Winona ([Catholic Charities of Southern Minnesota flyer, 2020](https://files.constantcontact.com/0d30324c501/8c489f3d-7a22-4940-a7a7-dab33788681b.pdf); [Mankato Free Press, March 9, 2020](https://www.mankatofreepress.com/news/local_news/monday-informer-speaker-to-discuss-el-salvador-romero/article_4337a350-5f0f-11ea-afd4-3b419bb8b6c3.html)). A Marta Portillo also told her story at a 2019 St. James book event, saying she moved to Madelia in the 1980s to escape the war; the sources do not say whether this is the same person ([Mankato Free Press, Jan. 14, 2019](https://www.mankatofreepress.com/news/immigrant-stories-vary-but-share-a-common-thread/article_101f555a-17a6-11e9-ab1b-2710cce824d0.html)). At the time of Romero's 2015 beatification, a St. Paul songwriter, Rob Hahn, wrote a song for him that he said was spreading among Romero's followers in El Salvador ([WCCO](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/st-paul-man-pens-song-in-honor-of-slain-archbishop/)). The Diocese of St. Cloud's magazine covered Romero's 2018 canonization and Salvadorans' protection programs ([The Central Minnesota Catholic](https://thecentralminnesotacatholic.org/tag/el-salvador/page/2/)), and a St. Cloud parishioner wrote of a 1992 church-sponsored trip to Tenancingo just after the war ended ([The Central Minnesota Catholic](https://thecentralminnesotacatholic.org/encountering-the-faith-of-the-salvadoran-church/)). Most of this reflects Minnesota Catholics' ties to El Salvador; how Salvadoran Minnesotans themselves mark Romero's day was not found.

**Building a Spanish-language parish life.** When Marta Zelaya arrived in St. James in 1994, the local church held Mass only in English. She found she could make changes in her church, and she and others asked for Spanish Masses ([Strong Towns, Oct. 31, 2019](https://archive.strongtowns.org/journal/2019/10/31/we-watch-out-for-each-other)).

**Sanctuary.** St. Luke Presbyterian Church in Hennepin County sheltered a Salvadoran former soldier in 1982 while he fought deportation ([openDemocracy, March 30, 2017](https://www.opendemocracy.net/en/minnesota-churches-face-tough-questions-in-offering-sanctuar/)).

**Lutheran links.** Augsburg University's Center for Global Education, founded in 1982 at the request of the Lutheran Church, kept offices in El Salvador and took students to meet Bishop Medardo Gómez of the Salvadoran Lutheran Church and to study Romero's legacy ([Augsburg University, Oct. 1, 2008](https://www.augsburg.edu/now/2008/10/01/travel-that-transforms/)).

**Faith in daily language.** The owners of Cabañas in Worthington described their restaurant as an answer to prayer ([The Globe, Sept. 15, 2023](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/new-salvadoran-restaurant-is-answer-to-owners-prayers)).

**Not found.** No Minnesota congregation was found that identifies as Salvadoran, whether Catholic, evangelical, Pentecostal or Lutheran. No Minnesota celebration of the August feast of the Divino Salvador del Mundo, the country's patron, was found (section 6). No source described Salvadoran Minnesotans of other faiths or no faith.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

These feed the calendar. Minnesota-specific Salvadoran events were hard to find; where none was found, that is said plainly.

- **Central American Independence Day, Sept. 15.** Marks the 1821 independence of El Salvador and its neighbors; the Census Bureau notes the date anchors the start of Hispanic Heritage Month ([Census Bureau, 2026](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/facts-for-features/2026/hispanic-heritage-month.html)). No Salvadoran-organized Sept. 15 event in Minnesota could be confirmed.
- **Hispanic Heritage Month, Sept. 15 to Oct. 15.** CLUES held its tenth Fiesta Latina on Saturday, Sept. 19, 2026, at CHS Field in St. Paul ([CLUES](https://clues.org/events/clues-fiesta-latina-2026-2/)). In St. James, the Multicultural Fiesta drew thousands to Memorial Park on Saturday, Sept. 13, 2025 ([Mankato Free Press, Sept. 14, 2025](https://www.mankatofreepress.com/news/local_news/fiesta-2025-st-james-hosts-multicultural-celebration/article_ce684ec6-d69f-43bf-bcdb-18a68ed58a0e.html)); organizers had first timed it to Mexican Independence Day ([Strong Towns, 2019](https://archive.strongtowns.org/journal/2019/10/31/we-watch-out-for-each-other)). Both are pan-Latino or multicultural, not Salvadoran events.
- **Saint Óscar Romero, March 24.** See section 5. The 2020 southern Minnesota presentations are the clearest Minnesota observance found.
- **Peace Accords, Jan. 16.** The accords ending the war were signed Jan. 16, 1992 ([Peace Accords Matrix, Notre Dame](https://peaceaccords.nd.edu/accord/chapultepec-peace-agreement)). In January 2022 El Salvador's Legislative Assembly renamed the date the Day of the Victims of the Armed Conflict and repealed the earlier peace-day decrees ([El Ciudadano, Jan. 21, 2022](https://www.elciudadano.com/en/bukele-talks-about-the-peace-agreements-and-unleashes-another-controversy-in-el-salvador/01/21/)). So the meaning of the day is itself contested. No Minnesota observance was found.
- **Divino Salvador del Mundo (Fiestas Agostinas).** The patron feast runs in San Salvador from late July to Aug. 6; in 2026 the main Mass was Aug. 6 ([Diario El Mundo, 2026](https://diario.elmundo.sv/nacionales/iglesia-catolica-anuncia-programa-de-fiestas-del-divino-salvador-del-mundo)). Diaspora Masses are held in places like Washington, D.C. ([Archdiocese of Washington](https://adw.org/event/mass-divine-savior/)), but none was found in Minnesota.
- **Christian calendar.** Holy Week, Easter and Christmas are on the calendar as for other Christian communities; no Salvadoran-specific Minnesota observance was found.

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Households.** Compass counts 4,013 Salvadoran households averaging 3.64 people. Married couples with children under 18 are 34.6 percent of households, married couples without children 9.7 percent, single mothers with children 13.0 percent, unmarried couples 14.1 percent, and people living alone 15.9 percent. Most people, 85.7 percent, lived at the same address a year earlier ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran)).

**Mixed-status families.** Minnesota reporting describes households that mix U.S. citizen children with parents on TPS. A St. Paul father on TPS said that if it ended, some families would face separation; El Guanaco's co-owner told Sahan Journal in 2020 that she and her husband had lived under TPS for 20 years and had grandchildren who are U.S. citizens ([Sahan Journal, Sept. 17, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/immigration-tps-el-salvador-minnesota/)). Professor Pottratz Acosta said several of her Salvadoran clients with TPS have a U.S. citizen child who can sponsor them for permanent residence ([MPR News, Sept. 8, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/09/08/what-the-end-of-tps-could-mean-for-salvadorans-in-minnesota)).

**Family networks.** Migration stories in St. James repeatedly mention help from relatives and family friends already in the United States, and one woman described coming alone to support her family in El Salvador ([UMN Morris Digital Well](https://digitalcommons.morris.umn.edu/unitingcultures/)). No Minnesota figure on remittances was found.

**Food.** Pupusas, the corn cakes filled with cheese, beans, chicharrón, squash or the flower bud loroco, are served with curtido, a pickled cabbage slaw, and a mild tomato sauce ([Heavy Table, 2009](https://heavytable.com/on-the-trail-of-the-perfect-pupusa/)). Minnesota cooks adapt: Rosario Diaz used Maseca flour and mozzarella in place of fresh-ground masa and quesillo ([Heavy Table](https://heavytable.com/on-the-trail-of-the-perfect-pupusa/)). Other foods in Minnesota sources include panes con pollo, the Salvadoran quesadilla cake, fried yuca, and horchata made with ground seeds of the morro (crescentia) tree, rice, cocoa and cinnamon ([Heavy Table, El Centro](https://heavytable.com/el-centro-pupuseria-and-restaurant-on-east-lake-street-in-minneapolis/); [MPR News, 2006](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2006/06/26/salvadorianbakery); [The Globe, 2023](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/sandra-pineda-maria-sorto-bring-a-salvadoran-flavor-to-worthington)).

**Childhood and aging.** With 46.5 percent of Salvadoran Minnesotans 17 or younger and 1.9 percent 65 or older ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran)), this is a community of young families. In St. James, Convivencia Hispana found isolated, depressed Latino elders and started a twice-monthly gathering called Our Golden Age ([Strong Towns, 2019](https://archive.strongtowns.org/journal/2019/10/31/we-watch-out-for-each-other)), paused during COVID-19 ([Latino LEAD MN, 2020](https://www.latinoleadmn.org/post/greater-mn-story-convivencia-hispana)).

**Not found.** No Minnesota source described Salvadoran marriage customs, dress, naming practice or home life in detail.

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Writers and performers.** Lorena Duarte was born in El Salvador, raised in Minnesota and graduated from Harvard in 1998. She edited La Prensa de Minnesota from 2001 to 2006, performed with Palabristas, which MPR called the first Latino spoken word group in Minnesota, won Many Voices playwriting fellowships at the Playwrights' Center, and was the 2007 MN Slam! champion. Her poems appear in *The Wandering Song: Central American Writing in the United States* and *Theatre Under My Skin: Contemporary Salvadoran Poetry*, and she served six years on the board of the Loft Literary Center ([Saint Paul Chamber Orchestra](https://content.thespco.org/people/lorena-duarte/); [MPR News, 2008](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2008/09/24/palabristas); [MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/la-prensa-de-minnesota)). Novelist Sandra Benítez, born in Washington, D.C., to a U.S. diplomat father and raised in San Salvador from 1946 until she was sent to U.S. schools at 14, set *Bitter Grounds* and *The Weight of All Things* in El Salvador. She won a Minnesota Fiction Award in 1994 and died in Minneapolis on July 25, 2024 ([El Diario de Hoy, 2024](https://www.elsalvador.com/h-vida/h-mujeres/cultura-escritora-estadounidense-sandra-benitez-y-su-conexion-con-el-salvador/1160683/2024/); [Minnesota Writers on the Map](https://mnwritersmap.org/sandra-benitez/)).

**Media.** Mario Duarte founded La Prensa de Minnesota in 1991 as a bilingual weekly. At first he was its only employee, laying it out and delivering it himself, and he mailed bundles to rural towns ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/la-prensa-de-minnesota)). Spanish-language outlets such as El Minnesota de Hoy serve all Latino readers ([El Minnesota de Hoy](https://www.elminnesotadehoy.com/)). No Salvadoran-specific Minnesota outlet was found.

**Oral history.** The Uniting Cultures collection at the University of Minnesota Morris preserves 2017 to 2024 interviews from St. James, seven with Salvadoran narrators, redacted to protect identities ([UMN Morris Digital Well](https://digitalcommons.morris.umn.edu/unitingcultures/)). A 2019 St. James book, *Your Story, My Story, Our Story*, included Salvadoran war refugees, and a cooking series there featured Salvadoran food alongside Mexican, Guatemalan and Scandinavian ([Mankato Free Press, Jan. 14, 2019](https://www.mankatofreepress.com/news/immigrant-stories-vary-but-share-a-common-thread/article_101f555a-17a6-11e9-ab1b-2710cce824d0.html)).

**Sports.** No Minnesota source on Salvadoran soccer leagues, teams or athletes was found.

**Not found.** No Salvadoran folk dance group, music ensemble, festival or cultural center in Minnesota was found.

## 9. Civic and political life

**Organizations that serve Salvadorans among others.** No Minnesota organization was found that serves Salvadorans alone. Much of the civic work runs through Latino-wide groups:

- *Convivencia Hispana* in St. James, a volunteer network with roots in Mexico, Honduras, Guatemala and El Salvador. Marta Zelaya, who fled El Salvador's war, helped form it in 2015. It gives scholarships, holds workshops on credit and home buying, pushed for more school interpreters and Latino seats on town committees, and helped run about 500 COVID-19 tests with the state health department ([Strong Towns, 2019](https://archive.strongtowns.org/journal/2019/10/31/we-watch-out-for-each-other); [Latino LEAD MN, 2020](https://www.latinoleadmn.org/post/greater-mn-story-convivencia-hispana)).
- *COPAL* (Communities Organizing Latine Power and Action), founded in 2018. Edwin Torres DeSantiago, born in El Salvador, sits on its board ([Twin Cities Business, 2024](https://tcbmag.com/notable/edwin-torres-desantiago/)). On Sept. 10, 2026, COPAL Executive Director Francisco Segovia said Salvadoran families "should not have to continually prove that they belong" ([COPAL](https://copalmn.org/25-years-of-building-home-salvadoran-tps-holders-belong-in-the-communities-they-helped-build/)).
- *CLUES*, founded in 1981 on St. Paul's West Side and now Minnesota's largest Latino-led nonprofit, with offices in St. Paul, Minneapolis, Willmar and Austin ([CLUES](https://clues.org/about/)).
- *Immigrant Law Center of Minnesota*, which lists TPS help for people in Minnesota and North Dakota ([ILCM](https://www.ilcm.org/)), and Mitchell Hamline's immigration clinic ([Mitchell Hamline](https://mitchellhamline.edu/clinics/immigration-clinic/)).

**The consulate as a public site.** About 500 people protested outside El Salvador's St. Paul consulate on April 16, 2025, demanding the return of Kilmar Abrego Garcia, a Maryland man wrongly deported to a Salvadoran prison ([MPR News, April 17, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/04/17/protesters-gather-el-salvador-consulate-st-paul)); FOX 9 reported that the Hands Off Coalition of Indivisible Twin Cities and Women's March MN planned a protest there as well ([FOX 9, April 16, 2025](https://www.fox9.com/video/1626129)). The sources do not say how many protesters were Salvadoran.

**Elected officials.** No Salvadoran-born or Salvadoran American elected official in Minnesota was found.

**Debates inside the community.** The clearest disagreement in the sources concerns El Salvador itself. President Nayib Bukele's government replaced the Jan. 16 peace commemoration with a day for victims, a move that drew marches in El Salvador ([El Ciudadano, 2022](https://www.elciudadano.com/en/bukele-talks-about-the-peace-agreements-and-unleashes-another-controversy-in-el-salvador/01/21/)), and his government's acceptance of U.S. deportees into its CECOT prison prompted the St. Paul protests above. Professor Pottratz Acosta speculated that friendly ties between the two governments may explain why TPS was extended in 2025 ([MPR News, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/09/08/what-the-end-of-tps-could-mean-for-salvadorans-in-minnesota)). How Salvadoran Minnesotans themselves divide on Bukele was not found in any Minnesota source.

## 10. Education

**Attainment.** Among Salvadoran Minnesotans 25 and older, 34.7 percent did not finish high school, 24.3 percent have a diploma or GED, 22.3 percent have some college or an associate degree, 12.1 percent a bachelor's degree, and 6.6 percent a graduate or professional degree. The bachelor's-or-higher share is 18.7 percent, give or take 5.9 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran)). Nationally, Pew found U.S.-born Salvadorans three times as likely as the foreign-born to hold a bachelor's degree, 27 versus 9 percent ([Pew, national background](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/us-hispanics-facts-on-salvadoran-origin-latinos/)); no Minnesota split by birthplace was found.

**Schools.** In St. James, the community education director said white students are now a minority in the schools ([Strong Towns, 2019](https://archive.strongtowns.org/journal/2019/10/31/we-watch-out-for-each-other)), and Convivencia Hispana pressed for more school interpreters and offers college scholarships ([Latino LEAD MN](https://www.latinoleadmn.org/post/greater-mn-story-convivencia-hispana)). No Minnesota school or program aimed specifically at Salvadoran students was found, and the state's enrollment data do not separate Salvadoran students from other Spanish speakers.

**Higher education.** One St. James narrator had a child at college in Mankato ([UMN Morris Digital Well](https://digitalcommons.morris.umn.edu/unitingcultures/)). At the University of Minnesota, historian Patrick McNamara studies El Salvador, migration and asylum ([UMN Experts](https://experts.umn.edu/en/persons/patrick-j-mcnamara/)). Augsburg's Center for Global Education has kept offices in El Salvador and led student seminars there, including in 2005 and 2007 ([Augsburg, 2008](https://www.augsburg.edu/now/2008/10/01/travel-that-transforms/)).

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Insurance.** Compass reports 19.3 percent of Salvadoran Minnesotans have no health insurance, give or take 5.4 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran)). It sits beside high employment; the sources do not explain why so many working people lack coverage.

**Housing stress.** Over a third of households, 35.4 percent, are cost-burdened, including 59.1 percent of renters ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran)).

**Fear as a health factor.** During Operation Metro Surge, which began in December 2025 and peaked in January 2026, at least 3,700 immigrants were detained in Minnesota, and volunteers from a Burnsville church were making about 900 home food deliveries a day by January to people short on money or afraid to leave home ([MPR News, May 7, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/05/07/minnesota-immigrant-families-struggle-after-ice-deportations)). DHS said one of the first 12 people arrested was from El Salvador ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ice-arrests-minneapolis-operation-metro-surge/)). The sources do not count how many Salvadorans were detained overall.

**Providers.** CLUES runs behavioral health, elder and family programs for Latinos ([CLUES](https://clues.org/about/)). Convivencia Hispana worked with the state health department on COVID-19 testing in St. James ([Latino LEAD MN](https://www.latinoleadmn.org/post/greater-mn-story-convivencia-hispana)). No clinic or provider tied specifically to the Salvadoran community, and no Minnesota source on traditional healing practices, was found.

## 12. Disability

**Minnesota numbers.** Compass reports 3.3 percent of Salvadoran Minnesotans have a disability (give or take 1.6), and 2.5 percent of those under 65 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran)). That is low, but it describes a very young population, with nearly half under 18.

**What Minnesota work exists.** No Minnesota study of disability among Salvadoran families was found. Nearby work is Latino-wide. In 2023 HACER and the Southeastern Minnesota Center for Independent Living held Spanish-language focus groups with Latinos with disabilities and their caregivers to identify unmet needs ([Minnesota Council on Disability, April 26, 2023](https://www.disability.state.mn.us/2023/04/26/viviendo-con-una-discapacidad-como-latina-en-mn/)). In 2024 PACER Center began DHS-funded Spanish-language parent support groups for families of children with autism and other disabilities ([PACER Center, July 2024](https://www.pacer.org/press-release/new-pacer-center-project-will-support-latino-and-somali-families-with-children-on-the-autism-spectrum/)). A University of Minnesota study of Latine families in early intervention is listed in the university repository, which could not be opened.

**Questions worth asking, not answers.**
- Who defines disability here? Compass's low rate may reflect youth, or the way survey questions land, or reluctance to report. The sources do not say which.
- What does a label open or close? For TPS holders and mixed-status families, applying for public programs may feel risky. Advocates reported in October 2026 that Salvadoran TPS holders were facing barriers to programs they are legally eligible for ([Alianza Americas, Oct. 20, 2026](https://www.alianzaamericas.org/post/alianza-americas-african-communities-together-casa-cusp-and-seiu-call-on-dhs-to-uphold-tps-protections-for-ukraine-sudan-and-el-salvador)). Whether that has kept Salvadoran Minnesotans from disability services is not documented.
- What produced an impairment? Physical work in meat processing and landscaping, and flight from war, appear in Minnesota migration stories ([UMN Morris Digital Well](https://digitalcommons.morris.umn.edu/unitingcultures/); [Strong Towns](https://archive.strongtowns.org/journal/2019/10/31/we-watch-out-for-each-other)). No source connects these to disability rates among Salvadoran Minnesotans, and any connection would be for a person to tell, not for staff to assume.
- Uninsurance near 20 percent ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran)) may mean conditions go undiagnosed. That is a question, not a finding.

**People and organizations.** No Salvadoran Minnesotan disability self-advocate or Salvadoran-led disability organization in Minnesota was found.

## 13. What is changing now

**TPS in limbo (as of Oct. 31, 2026).** El Salvador's TPS designation was set to expire Sept. 9, 2026. DHS let the date pass without a formal decision and said current holders keep protection and work authorization until it announces one ([National Immigration Project](https://nipnlg.org/work/resources/what-happening-salvadoran-tps)). A Telemundo report noted that by law, a designation without a timely decision is extended automatically for six months, though the government must publish that ([KTTC/Telemundo, Sept. 10, 2026](https://www.kttc.com/2026/09/10/el-departamento-de-seguridad-nacional-dice-que-el-tps-para-los-salvadoreos-sigue-vigente-hasta-nuevo-aviso/)). As of Oct. 9 and Oct. 20, advocates said no formal decision had appeared in the Federal Register and that holders were losing jobs and struggling to renew driver's licenses ([Alianza Americas, Oct. 9, 2026](https://www.alianzaamericas.org/post/congressman-glenn-ivey-d-md-impacted-families-legal-experts-and-advocates-sound-the-alarm-on-tps-legal-limbo-hitting-salvadoran-families); [Alianza Americas, Oct. 20, 2026](https://www.alianzaamericas.org/post/alianza-americas-african-communities-together-casa-cusp-and-seiu-call-on-dhs-to-uphold-tps-protections-for-ukraine-sudan-and-el-salvador)). In Minnesota, an estimated 3,000 to 4,000 people may be affected; there is no official state count ([MPR News, Sept. 8, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/09/08/what-the-end-of-tps-could-mean-for-salvadorans-in-minnesota)). This has happened before: the first Trump administration moved to end Salvadoran TPS in 2018, and a 2020 appeals ruling allowed it to proceed, yet the status continued, and Professor Pottratz Acosta noted that the Trump administration extended it again in March 2025 ([MPR News, Jan. 9, 2018](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2018/01/09/salvadoran-immigrants-in-minnesota-face-wrenching-decisions-as-special-protections-end); [Sahan Journal, Sept. 17, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/immigration-tps-el-salvador-minnesota/)).

**Enforcement.** Operation Metro Surge in winter 2025 to 2026 (section 11) reached Salvadoran residents along with others ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ice-arrests-minneapolis-operation-metro-surge/)). Twin Cities attorneys reported in December 2025 that naturalization interviews for green-card holders from El Salvador and other countries were being cancelled ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 30, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-citizenship-interviews-oath-ceremonies-cancelled/)).

**Ties to El Salvador's government.** Minnesotans protested at the St. Paul consulate in April 2025 over deportations to El Salvador's CECOT prison (section 9).

**New arrivals and new businesses.** Recent arrivals from El Salvador were taking driver's license classes in 2024 ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/drivers-license-for-all-minnesota-undocumented-trump-deportation/)), and a Salvadoran-Palestinian café opened near the University of Minnesota in late 2025 ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 17, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/sip-of-silk-cafe-minneapolis-university-of-minnesota/)).

**Generations.** A U.S.-born majority is growing up between languages; Lorena Duarte described the "Minnesota Latino experience" as pupusas at home and hotdish at the potluck ([MPR News, 2008](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2008/09/24/palabristas)). Rural towns are changing too: St. James's Multicultural Fiesta paused in 2023 for lack of volunteers and returned in 2024 and 2025 ([Mankato Free Press, Sept. 14, 2025](https://www.mankatofreepress.com/news/local_news/fiesta-2025-st-james-hosts-multicultural-celebration/article_ce684ec6-d69f-43bf-bcdb-18a68ed58a0e.html)).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **Work.** Employment among working-age Salvadoran Minnesotans is 84.7 percent ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran)). Strong Towns credits immigrants, Salvadorans among them, with sparing St. James the population loss other small towns have seen ([Strong Towns, 2019](https://archive.strongtowns.org/journal/2019/10/31/we-watch-out-for-each-other)).
- **Building institutions for everyone.** Mario Duarte's La Prensa gave Minnesota's Latinos their first Spanish-language paper ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/la-prensa-de-minnesota)). Marta Zelaya helped bring Spanish Masses to St. James and helped found Convivencia Hispana ([Strong Towns](https://archive.strongtowns.org/journal/2019/10/31/we-watch-out-for-each-other)).
- **Home ownership and roots.** About 62 percent of households own their homes ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/salvadoran)).
- **Food and small business.** From El Guanaco, which employed 30 people in 2020 ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/immigration-tps-el-salvador-minnesota/)), to food trucks that serve southern Minnesota towns ([Faribault Daily News, 2025](https://www.southernminn.com/faribault_daily_news/news/salvadorian-food-truck-celebrates-6-years-in-business/article_2de906a4-f84a-11ef-b0e1-6fbbf13809ed.html)), Salvadoran cooks have put pupusas on the Minnesota map.
- **Mentoring and letters.** Rene Madrid's mentoring of high school students ([Twin Cities Business](https://tcbmag.com/notable/rene-madrid/)) and Lorena Duarte's poetry, playwriting and six years on the Loft Literary Center board ([SPCO](https://content.thespco.org/people/lorena-duarte/)) reach well beyond the community.
