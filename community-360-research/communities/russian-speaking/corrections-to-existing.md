# Corrections to the existing Russian-speaking Minnesota page

Existing page: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/russian-speaking (read in full Oct. 31, 2026). Each item gives the claim as stated, what an opened source says, the link and the date checked.

## Errors or outdated claims

1. **Claim:** "Compass Ukrainian is about 22,570 (ACS 2019–2023)." (stated three times)
   **Source says:** Minnesota Compass now shows a Ukrainian population of 22,254, give or take 3,082, for 2020-2024.
   **Link:** https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/ukrainian. Update the figure and years.

2. **Claim:** "The Compass immigration table shows about 6,483 foreign-born from Russia. Date the table you open."
   **Source says:** The Compass immigrant-group page still lists 6,483 "Russian immigrants," but the page does not show survey years next to the figure. The Census Bureau's ACS 2020-2024 table B05006 shows 6,301 (give or take 665) Minnesotans born in Russia; the 2024 single-year table shows 4,923 (give or take 1,239).
   **Links:** https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/immigration ; https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05006,B04006&geo_ids=04000US27 ; https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US27. Suggest giving the dated ACS figure alongside the Compass number.

3. **Claim:** The page says there is no count of Russian speakers to use and that ACS language figures "disagree across vintages," but gives no number.
   **Source says:** ACS 2020-2024 table B16001 counts 13,851 (give or take 1,218) Minnesotans 5 and older who speak Russian at home, 5,159 of whom speak English less than "very well."
   **Link:** https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B16001&geo_ids=04000US27. Not an error, but a dated count now exists and could be added.

4. **Claim:** "Russian-speaking Minnesotans come from many countries of the former Soviet Union, including refugees of earlier decades and recent Ukrainian arrivals." and "In Minnesota the picture is made of places.. should not assume Northeast Minneapolis."
   **Source says:** Accurate in spirit. Sources add that the first post-1991 concentration was St. Louis Park, followed by spread to Minneapolis, Plymouth, Golden Valley, Hopkins, Anoka and Blaine (MinnPost, June 21, 2016), and that recent Russian asylum seekers, not only Ukrainians, have arrived since 2022 (Star Tribune via Stars and Stripes, April 12, 2023).
   **Links:** https://www.minnpost.com/new-americans/2016/06/how-russian-speaking-community-became-minnesotas-invisible-immigrants/ ; https://www.stripes.com/theaters/us/2023-04-12/antiwar-russians-flee-homeland-resettle-minnesota-9781406.html. Suggest naming post-2022 Russian asylum seekers in the overview.

5. **Claim (organization card):** Russian Camp MN / camp material implies overnight sessions; Concordia's Lesnoe Ozero "with a summer 2027 schedule posted."
   **Source says:** Russian Camp MN's own site now says it "now runs as a day camp in Minnesota." Concordia's Russian village page opened, but a 2027 schedule was not seen in the text read.
   **Links:** https://russiancampmn.com/ ; https://www.concordialanguagevillages.org/languages/russian/.

6. **Claim (East-West Connections listing used for St. Herman's):** St. Herman's is listed as a Russian-language church at "5355 38th Avenue S., Minneapolis, MN 55412," with the Resurrection Skete web address.
   **Source says:** The existing page already flags this. St. Herman's own site gives ZIP 55417, serves in English, and says most parishioners are converts. The East-West Connections list attaches the wrong web address to it.
   **Links:** https://www.sthermanmpls.org/ ; https://east-westconnections.com/russianlanguage-churches.

7. **Claim (Nasha Shkola card):** "MinnPost reported in 2016 that the Slavic Community Center established it in 2012."
   **Source says:** Correct, but MinnPost placed the school in Minnetonka in 2016; it is now at 6717 85th Ave. N., Brooklyn Park. The school says 95 percent of students speak Russian at home or have a Russian-speaking parent.
   **Links:** https://www.minnpost.com/new-americans/2016/06/how-russian-speaking-community-became-minnesotas-invisible-immigrants/ ; https://nashashkolamn.org/.

8. **Claim (St. Panteleimon card):** phone "612-517-7496."
   **Source says:** East-West Connections lists 612-517-7496, matching. No error found.

## Claims checked and supported

- St. Mary's Cathedral: founded 1887 by Carpatho-Rusyns, received into Orthodoxy 1891, present building 1905, seminary 1905, consecrated by St. Tikhon 1906, Alexis Toth canonized 1994. Supported by https://stmarysoca.org/about.html and https://www.oca.org/parishes/oca-mw-minsmk.
- St. Panteleimon marked its 70th anniversary in August 2026. Supported (Aug. 8-9, 2026) by https://spro.church/celebration-of-patronal-feast-and-70th-anniversary-of-st-panteleimon-parish/.
- Resurrection Skete at 1201 Hathaway Lane NE, Fridley; services in Church Slavonic and English, sermons in Russian and English. Supported by https://www.resurrectionskete.org/ and https://east-westconnections.com/russianlanguage-churches.
- JFCS and JFS resettled "well over 1,000 families" from the former Soviet Union, late 1980s through 1990s, by the agency's count; MNopedia confirms the programs without a number. Supported by https://jfcsmpls.org/desk-ceo-judy-halper-february-2017/ and https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/jewish-social-welfare-groups-1871-2012.
- TMORA: founded 2002 by Raymond and Susan Johnson, first exhibition in Bloomington, moved to Minneapolis 2005. Supported by https://tmora.org/about/history/.
- American Russian Center formerly Russian Soul. Supported by https://americanrussiancenter.org/ (its site says 501(c)(3) status since 2009; the existing page says "renamed in 2023," which the opened page does not state; could not confirm the year).
- Theatre Novi Most founded 1998 by Vladimir Rovinsky and Lisa Channer; raising money for legal fees for Ukrainian refugees. Supported by https://www.theatrenovimost.org/ and https://www.macalester.edu/russian-studies/about/inminnesota/.
- Russian Educational Center founded 2002 by parent volunteers; Christian, Muslim and Jewish children attend (director, 2021). Supported by https://www.studyrussian.org/ and https://eplocalnews.org/2021/08/31/states-quality-of-life-schools-draw-russians/. The existing page's address "4011 West Highway 13, Savage" was not shown on the opened pages; the 2021 article says the teaching facility is in Savage.
- Zerkalo begun by Leonid Grichener as a newsletter in 1990; North Star issues posted. Supported by https://tcbmag.com/mirror-on-the-midwest-russian-language-zerkalo-celebrates-25-years-in-minnesota/ and https://www.zerkalomn.com/ (latest North Star issues visible on the home page were January and February 2026, not "through October 2026" as the existing page says; later issues may be posted elsewhere on the site).
- Minnesota Secretary of State voter materials include Russian. Supported by Sahan Journal, Sept. 22, 2025: https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-non-english-languages-census-data/.
- Kirill Kaprizov born in Novokuznetsk; on the Wild's 2026 playoff roster. Supported by https://www.nhl.com/news/topic/playoffs/minnesota-wild-2026-stanley-cup-playoff-roster-at-a-glance.
- Mikhail Shifman holds the Ida Cohen Fine Chair. Supported by https://cse.umn.edu/physics/mikhail-shifman.
- Alfiya Battalova's Minnesota exchange year and quote. Supported by https://cdrnys.org/blog/advocacy/new-advocacy-intern-from-russia-shares-perspective-on-disability-rights-in-the-u-s/.
- Culture Care Connection says data on Russian Americans is limited and may not apply in Minnesota; 2013 finding on daily-activity limits. Supported by https://culturecareconnection.org/cultural-responsiveness/russian/.
- Gedaly Meerovich's 2016 comment on parents' shame about a child's drug use. Supported by the MinnPost article above.

## Could not verify

- "The Star Tribune (31 July 2025) reported that Russian is among the languages each representing about 2% of Minnesotans who speak a language other than English at home." The Star Tribune article was not found or opened. The ACS 2024 figures above put Russian speakers at about 15,466 of roughly 719,000 non-English speakers, about 2 percent, which is consistent, but the article itself could not be opened.
- "DHS Equal Opportunity and Access LEP resources name five primary written languages: Hmong, Russian, Somali, Spanish, Vietnamese." mn.gov pages could not be opened. An older DHS plan (DHS-4210, April 2007) lists Russian among 10 interpreter-standard languages and seven on the multilingual phone line: https://www.migrationpolicy.org/sites/default/files/language_portal/Limited%20English%20Proficiency%20Plan.pdf.
- Minnesota JCC monthly senior outings in Russian (from a state aging-resources listing, February 2026): the listing is on mn.gov and could not be opened; the JCC home page does not mention Russian programs.
- Russian-American Jews in Minnesota's current teen fellowship registration for 2026-27: rajmn.org returned a verification block.
- The Twin Cities Guide for Russian Speakers "updated in October 2026": the guide's data says "Updated on May 31, 2026," with a separate note dated 10/08/2026 about Minsk Market.
- The many national and international profiles (Brin, Spektor, Gessen, Rand, Nabokov and others) and the Hurwicz and Sorokin entries were not rechecked; they are outside Minnesota or historical, and this research focused on Minnesota facts.
