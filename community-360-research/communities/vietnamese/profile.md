# Vietnamese in Minnesota

## 1. People and place

**How many.** Minnesota Compass, using the American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024, counts 32,841 Vietnamese Minnesotans, give or take 3,477. It places 76.8 percent in the Twin Cities and 23.2 percent in Greater Minnesota ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/vietnamese)). Other careful counts differ. State Demographer Susan Brower's June 2026 presentation shows 31,155 from the 2024 survey alone, which places Vietnamese fifth on her chart of Asian Pacific groups, just behind Korean at 31,615 ([Minnesota State Demographic Center, June 27, 2026](https://mn.gov/capm/assets/SusanBrower_StateOfAsianPacificMinnesotans_June2026_tcm1051-759113.pdf)). The Demographic Center's 2026 economic report, which uses its own group definitions and 2019 to 2023 data, counts 27,200, give or take 3,900 ([The Economic Status of Minnesotans 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). The Vietnamese Community of Minnesota says more than 30,000 ([VCM history](https://vietnam-minnesota.org/our-history/)). All are estimates, and the size of the margins matters more than the last digits.

**Where, by county and city.** The Census Bureau's American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024 counts 33,577 Minnesotans who report Vietnamese ancestry alone or with another Asian group, give or take 1,746, and 25,992 who report Vietnamese alone, give or take 1,723 ([Census Reporter, ACS 2024 five-year tables B02018 and B02015](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B02015,B02018&geo_ids=04000US27,050|04000US27)). Counting people of Vietnamese ancestry alone or in combination, Hennepin County has about 10,965, Ramsey 4,559, Dakota 3,869, Anoka 3,374, Scott 2,298, Olmsted 1,990, Washington 1,674 and Carver 581. Adding the seven metro counties gives about 27,300. Outside the metro, after Olmsted, the largest counts are in Stearns (631), Blue Earth (363), Rice (330), St. Louis (323), Mower (293) and Clay (250) counties, each with a margin of error near or above half its size (same table). By city, the largest counts are Minneapolis (2,406, give or take 502), Brooklyn Park (2,253, give or take 699), St. Paul (2,181, give or take 586), Rochester (1,928, give or take 573), Bloomington (1,378), Blaine (1,206), Savage (947), Maple Grove (933), Woodbury (909), Burnsville (903), Plymouth (866) and Lakeville (863) ([Census Reporter, B02018 by place](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B02018&geo_ids=160|04000US27)). In Greater Minnesota beyond Rochester, St. Cloud (568), Mankato (359), Austin (293), Duluth (220) and Moorhead (177) have the most, though their margins are so wide that the true figures could be far lower or higher (same table). No one city holds a large share: the community is spread across the inner and outer suburbs as much as the two central cities. The ACS counts differ from Compass's 32,841 because each uses a different definition and rounding, and all of them are survey estimates.

**Where, from people's accounts.** St. Paul's University Avenue area, in and around Frogtown, became an early home because housing and storefronts were relatively cheap, according to a 2016 Minnesota Lawyer feature ([Minnesota Lawyer, Sept. 1, 2016](https://minnlawyer.com/2016/09/01/bill-of-fare-a-taste-of-vietnam-on-university-avenue/)). Heavy Table described Brooklyn Park in 2017 as having "a large and still-growing Vietnamese population" ([Heavy Table, Oct. 23, 2017](https://heavytable.com/brooklyn-parks-vietnamese-food-scene/)), and the Vietnamese Community of Minnesota now lists a Brooklyn Park address ([VCM](https://vietnam-minnesota.org/our-history/)). Oral histories recorded in 2010 follow families through Minneapolis, Richfield, Bloomington, Brooklyn Park and Eagan ([Minnesota Historical Society finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/oh125.html)). In the south, historian Tuan Hoang, who grew up there, recalls Rochester becoming the leading regional resettlement site around 1980, with an uncle's family sponsored in Winona in 1975. He writes that thousands of Vietnamese settled in Rochester between 1975 and 1995, and that many later left for warmer places or for jobs while some stayed ([Tuan Hoang, Aug. 2, 2016](https://tuannyriver.com/2016/08/02/my-memories-of-high-school/)). That is one person's memory and estimate, not a count.

**How people came.** Saigon fell on April 30, 1975. Gov. Wendell Anderson created the state's Indochinese Resettlement Office and a task force in December of that year, and MNopedia places Minnesota among the ten states receiving the most refugees ([MNopedia, Indochinese Refugee Resettlement Office](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/indochinese-refugee-resettlement-office-1975-1986)). WCCO reports that Anderson cited Minnesota as having the highest number of Southeast Asian refugees in the Midwest as of October 1975 ([WCCO, May 5, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/minnesota/news/minnesota-vietnam-war-refugee-history)). Churches and charities sponsored many families ([Minnesota Lawyer](https://minnlawyer.com/2016/09/01/bill-of-fare-a-taste-of-vietnam-on-university-avenue/); [MNHS finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/oh125.html)). Later arrivals came too: one Rochester restaurant owner has described leaving by boat at 17, spending about two years in a camp in Indonesia, and being sponsored to Rochester by Catholic Social Services in February 1981 ([Resy, February 2021](https://blog.resy.com/2021/02/the-minnesota-restaurant-that-became-a-refugees-american-story/)). The Orderly Departure Program was negotiated in May 1979 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/indochinese-refugee-resettlement-office-1975-1986)). One Compass article says the first wave arrived in the 1980s ([Minnesota Compass, May 30, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-asian-communities)), which does not match the 1975 arrivals other sources document.

**Generations and languages.** In the Compass profile, 48.5 percent are foreign-born, give or take 3.2 points, and 27.4 percent are 17 or younger, with 8.4 percent 65 or older ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/vietnamese)). The Demographic Center's narrower count finds a higher foreign-born share, about 16,600 of 27,200, and a median age of 38, give or take 2 ([Economic Status 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). Most foreign-born working-age adults have been here a long time: about 11,300 for 11 years or more, against about 1,900 for 10 years or fewer (same report). Among people 5 and older, 66.9 percent speak a language other than English at home and 35.5 percent speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/vietnamese)).

## 2. All walks of life

No single number describes this community. Figures below are from the Compass 2020 to 2024 profile unless noted, with income in 2024 dollars ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/vietnamese)).

- **Comfortable and well-off households.** Median household income is $92,644, give or take $9,598. Nearly half of households, 45.5 percent, take in $100,000 or more. Two or more earners are common: about 5,300 of 9,400 households in the state's count ([Economic Status 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). No Minnesota figure for Vietnamese household wealth could be found, and no list of the wealthiest families. Public signs of property and business wealth include Sam Nguyen, who owns both Thanh Vi and the Brooklyn Park strip mall that houses it ([CCX Media, Oct. 11, 2022](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-parks-thanh-vi-serves-up-famous-pho)).
- **Professionals and owners.** Among adults 25 and older, 36.1 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more, including 11.9 percent with a graduate or professional degree. Visible careers include chef Christina Nguyen, who won the James Beard Award for Best Chef: Midwest in 2024 ([MPR News, June 10, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/06/10/minneapols-james-beard-award-oro-nixta-hai-hai-khaluna)); University of Minnesota environmental health scholar Trân Huỳnh, whose family came from Vietnam in 1997 ([Sahan Journal, Feb. 6, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/health/non-toxic-nail-hair-salons-vietnamese-east-african-minnesota-epa-grant/)); Augsburg University's Ly Thuy Nguyen ([Augsburg University](https://www.augsburg.edu/faculty/ly-thuy-nguyen/)); chef Eric Pham, whose St. Paul restaurant Khue's, named for his mother Khue Pham of the Quang family, says the New York Times put it on its 2026 list of the 50 best restaurants in America ([Khue's](https://www.khueskitchen.com/); [MPR News, March 5, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/03/05/appetites-st-paul-chef-reopens-restaurant-after-fire-and-adds-surprises-to-the-menu)); and accountants, insurance agents and pharmacists named in the community's own listings ([VCM directory](https://vietnam-minnesota.org/business-directory/); [VCM leadership](https://vietnam-minnesota.org/leadership/)).
- **Middle-income and working families.** Owner-occupied homes are 70.1 percent of households, give or take 5.6 points. Employment among adults 18 to 64 is 80.6 percent, about the same for men and women. Median earnings of full-time workers were $53,000, give or take $6,300, in the state's 2019 to 2023 data, against $61,900 for all Minnesotans ([Demographic Center, June 2026](https://mn.gov/capm/assets/SusanBrower_StateOfAsianPacificMinnesotans_June2026_tcm1051-759113.pdf)). So the high household income leans partly on more than one paycheck, a point the numbers suggest rather than one any source argues. Restaurant, salon and auto repair work fills the community's own business directory ([VCM directory](https://vietnam-minnesota.org/business-directory/)).
- **People in poverty.** Ten and a half percent of people live below the poverty line, give or take 3.4 points, including 12.2 percent of children, give or take 4.7. Another 11 percent live between the poverty line and twice that line. Renters have it hardest: 41.3 percent spend too much of their income on housing, though the margin is wide at 13.4 points. And 23.0 percent of adults 25 and older did not finish high school, a number that sits beside the graduate degrees above. Candidate Jennifer Nguyen Moore wrote in 2018 of growing up on powdered milk and government cheese while her refugee parents helped neighbors with citizenship tests and job applications ([Coalition of Asian American Leaders, May 2, 2018](https://old.caalmn.org/?p=1811)). Vietnamese Social Services of Minnesota says it works with low-income families and new arrivals, and that about half of its 12,000-plus yearly participants are Vietnamese ([VSS](https://vssmn.org)).

**Over time.** A 2019 report from the Coalition of Asian American Leaders listed Vietnamese home ownership at 74 percent and said 54 percent spoke English less than "very well" ([CAAL, 2019](https://caalmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Factors-Impacting-Economic-Wellbeing-of-Asian-Minnesotans-2019.pdf)). Survey periods overlap and margins are wide, so comparisons show direction at most.

## 3. Work and the economy

The state's 2019 to 2023 data show about 15,700 of 19,000 Vietnamese Minnesotans ages 16 to 64 in the labor force, about 83 percent, and about 12,000 of 15,100 workers putting in 35 hours or more a week ([Economic Status 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). Detailed Vietnamese figures by occupation or industry could not be found in the pages available.

**Early business life.** A 1984 MPR report told of about 25 small, mostly family-run Southeast Asian restaurants in the Twin Cities, along with grocery stores, import shops, beauty parlors and car repair shops. Jane Kretzmann of the state refugee office estimated about 70 refugee-owned businesses statewide. Dan Tran, who ran Kwan Viet in St. Paul and arrived in 1975, said the early years were very hard and that later arrivals had help from those already here ([MPR, Nov. 16, 1984](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1984/11/16/southeast-asian-refugee-businesses-growing-in-the-twin-cities)). In Hennepin County, job projects served about 1,478 refugee residents of all backgrounds, with 898 placements by 1982 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/indochinese-refugee-resettlement-office-1975-1986)).

**Nail salons.** Salons are the Vietnamese trade best documented in the pages available, though none gives the Vietnamese share of Minnesota's salons. Ngan Hoang, who owns Cali Nails on Lake Street, a salon her mother opened about 30 years before the 2024 story, worked 22 years in salons before overhauling her products. Trân Huỳnh's mother works as a technician in Richfield. Their stories anchored a University of Minnesota outreach effort, funded by $664,000 of a federal grant, that aimed to help owners move to safer products ([Sahan Journal, Feb. 6, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/health/non-toxic-nail-hair-salons-vietnamese-east-african-minnesota-epa-grant/)). Vietnamese Social Services lists a Healthy Salons Project ([VSS](https://vssmn.org)).

**One long climb.** David Tran's path is one documented example: a hotel dishwasher who rose to sous chef and manager, earned a culinary degree and in 1997 bought Cheap Charlie's diner in Rochester ([Resy, 2021](https://blog.resy.com/2021/02/the-minnesota-restaurant-that-became-a-refugees-american-story/)).

**Home care work.** One Vietnamese-led employer works in home care. The Vietnamese Minnesotans Association in St. Paul, founded in 1993, says in its tax filings that it focuses on personal care assistance and homemaking, mostly for Vietnamese American clients because its staff speak their language; it reported about $2.2 million in revenue in fiscal 2024 ([GrantWatch summary of IRS Form 990](https://minnesota.grantwatch.com/organization-profile/573694/vietnamese-minnesotans-association/)). How many Vietnamese Minnesotans work as personal care assistants is not known.

**Unions.** No source specific to Vietnamese Minnesotans and organized labor was found.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

The full survey, with evidence for each listing, is in the accompanying business list. Places are named below only where a page shows them operating recently; others are described with the date of the source.

**Eat Street.** The Whittier Alliance named this restaurant-rich stretch of Nicollet Avenue in 1997, and Mpls.St.Paul Magazine's March 2026 tour covers a list that includes Pho 79, Caravelle, Pho Tau Bay, Quang, Pho Hoa, Lu's Sandwiches, My Huong Kitchen and Jasmine 26. The magazine calls Jasmine Deli one of the area's historic first bánh mì shops ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine, March 1, 2026](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/dining-tour-eat-street/)). Quang, at 2719 Nicollet, describes itself as family-owned since 1989 ([Quang](https://quang-restaurant.com)). TT Market, formerly Truong Thanh, sells groceries, tropical fruit, and Chinese herbs and supplements at 2520 Nicollet ([TT Market](https://ttmarket.biz)). Pho Hoa traces its company to a small shop founder Binh Nguyen opened in 1983, which it describes as a path for refugees to build businesses ([Pho Hoa](https://phohoa.com/)). Newer on the street is House of Huế at the Eat Street Crossing food hall, 2819 Nicollet, which Racket called Minnesota's first restaurant devoted to bún bò Huế. It is run by Henry and Pamela Tran, a couple from Huế who cooked in Houston and Eau Claire before it opened in late 2024 ([Racket, Dec. 8, 2025](https://racketmn.com/5-things-to-know-about-house-of-hue-mns-first-dedicated-bun-bo-hue-shop); [Eat Street Crossing](https://eatstreetcrossing.com/)).

**Little Mekong and University Avenue.** Little Mekong is not a Vietnamese district alone. Its organizers call it "Saint Paul's Asian business and cultural corridor" ([Little Mekong](https://www.littlemekong.com/)), and Visit Saint Paul traces its formation to Hmong settlement in the 1970s and places it between Mackubin and Marion streets ([Visit Saint Paul](https://www.visitsaintpaul.com/things-to-do/neighborhoods/little-mekong/)); Minnesota Lawyer gave the edges as Mackubin and Galtier in 2016 ([Minnesota Lawyer](https://minnlawyer.com/2016/09/01/bill-of-fare-a-taste-of-vietnam-on-university-avenue/)). The Asian Economic Development Association's night market began July 5, 2014, closed in 2021 during the pandemic and relaunched in 2024 ([Little Mekong](https://www.littlemekong.com/explore-the-district)). The organizers' site still showed only the 2025 market, held June 28 and 29, when, and no 2026 date from the organizers could be found ([Little Mekong Night Market](https://www.littlemekong.com/night-market)). The 2016 feature named Mai Village and Trieu Chau there. Mai Village, at 394 University, was saved from foreclosure in 2013 with last-minute help from the Hmong American Partnership, the Asian Economic Development Association, patrons, the city and its lenders, but Heavy Table found it still struggling during light-rail construction in January 2014 ([Heavy Table, Jan. 2, 2014](https://heavytable.com/vermicelli-noodle-salad-mai-village/)); whether it operates today could not be confirmed. Visit Saint Paul's undated guide to Southeast Asian restaurants still lists Trieu Chau, Hoa Bien (which it says has served Frogtown since 1978), Trung Nam French Bakery, eM Que Viet on Grand Avenue and Vina Vietnamese in Highland Park ([Visit Saint Paul](https://www.visitsaintpaul.com/blog/dine-at-these-vietnamese-restaurants-in-saint-paul/)). Hoa Bien posts hours at 1105 University ([Hoa Bien](https://hoa-bien.com)). Trung Nam, at 739 University, posts a current menu with prices and was described in a January 2026 neighborhood guide as open every day but Sunday from 8 a.m. to 1 p.m. ([Trung Nam French Bakery](https://trung-nam.squarespace.com); [Counselor Realty, Jan. 22, 2026](https://vsiskin.counselorrealty.com/blog/2026/01/22/wintertime-cafes-and-bakeries-locals-love-around-the-twin-cities)). iPho by Saigon at 704 University appears in a state tourism listing but could not be confirmed open ([Explore Minnesota](https://www.exploreminnesota.com/profile/ipho-saigon/33060)). The avenue is changing. Tay Ho closed May 28, 2026, after about two decades, its family citing "an unfortunate situation," and Sahan Journal reports the longtime landlord sold the building ([Sahan Journal, June 5, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/tay-ho-vietnamese-restaurant-closes-st-paul-university-avenue/)).

**The northern suburbs.** Brooklyn Park has Thanh Vi, named for the owner's daughter ([CCX Media](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-parks-thanh-vi-serves-up-famous-pho); [Thanh Vi](https://thanhvitogo.com)), plus Sōke BBQ & Hotpot and Stone Pho on Edinburgh Centre Drive, the latter also in Roseville ([Sōke](https://sokemn.com); [Stone Pho](https://stonepho.com)). Blaine, New Brighton, Lino Lakes and Eden Prairie also appear in the community's directory ([VCM directory](https://vietnam-minnesota.org/business-directory/)).

**A new generation of cafés.** Sahan Journal profiled four Vietnamese coffee businesses in 2025: Phê Coffee in Lowertown, Càphin on France Avenue, Giọt Cream Coffee, and the pop-up Sunday's Cà Phê ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 8, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/minneapolis-st-paul-vietnamese-coffee-phe-caphin-giot/)).

**Family lines.** Lotus has roots going back nearly 40 years before 2022 ([FOX 9, Nov. 3, 2022](https://fox9.com/news/simple-gesture-leads-to-special-moment-at-minneapolis-lotus-restaurant)), and in 2025 was reported with Minneapolis and Maple Grove locations ([Star Tribune via Sahan Journal, July 9, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/news-partners/mariah-nguyen-minnesota-aurora-gophers-soccer/)). Que Viet says it has served Vietnamese food since 1980 and calls itself home of the Minnesota State Fair egg roll ([Que Viet](https://quevietmn.com)). KROC reported in September 2025 that its fair stand, a fair vendor since 2017, took in $1 million in 2024, eighth among fair food vendors, and that chef Dat Le has owned the Minneapolis restaurant since 1996 ([KROC, Sept. 2, 2025](https://krocnews.com/minnesota-state-fair-vendor-year-round/)). CCX Media's 2021 story describes a Que Viet in Brooklyn Center, at 6100 Brooklyn Blvd., opened in 1987 by owner Minh Lam's mother, and quotes Lam saying the State Fair stand had tripled its business ([CCX Media, June 29, 2021](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-centers-que-viet-benefits-from-state-fair-presence/)). The two stories name different owners and locations, and how the restaurants are related could not be confirmed. eM Que Viet, at 1332 Grand Ave. in St. Paul, was described by Mpls.St.Paul Magazine as coming from "a veteran restaurant family" and signaling a new generation ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/deconstructed-the-savory-crepe-at-em-que-viet/)); its current status could not be confirmed. Eric Pham, of the family behind Quang, reopened Khue's Kitchen inside MidCity Kitchen in St. Paul in March 2025 after a fire destroyed his first site ([MPR News, March 5, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/03/05/appetites-st-paul-chef-reopens-restaurant-after-fire-and-adds-surprises-to-the-menu)); the restaurant, now called Khue's, lists itself as open Wednesday through Sunday in St. Anthony Park ([Khue's](https://www.khueskitchen.com/)). In Rochester, David and Janna Tran announced that Cheap Charlie's, the diner he bought in 1997, would close on July 31 after 58 years in business, with the building and land sold as they retire ([KAAL, 2026](https://www.kaaltv.com/news/cheap-charlies-in-rochester-is-closing-after-58-years-in-business/); [KAAL](https://www.kaaltv.com/news/owners-of-rochester-restaurant-reflect-on-decades-of-service-ahead-of-closing/)). The Post Bulletin had described it as still open and for sale in October 2025, so the closing date falls in 2026 ([Post Bulletin, Oct. 8, 2025](https://www.postbulletin.com/lifestyle/cheap-but-great-diner-food-at-cheap-charlies)). Vo's on Lyndale went on the market in 2023 when its owner planned to retire; its current status could not be confirmed ([Racket, April 26, 2023](https://racketmn.com/wanna-buy-vos-vietnamese-restaurant-minneapolis)).

**Professional firms and media.** VCM's directory names J&J Accounting in Eden Prairie and two Farmers Insurance agencies, and it has empty tabs for attorneys and real estate ([VCM directory](https://vietnam-minnesota.org/business-directory/); [J&J Accounting](https://jnjaccounting.com)). Both agencies post current office hours: Tri Tang's on Nicollet Avenue lists Vietnamese among five languages spoken, and Hoa (Annie) Nguyen's in Blaine lists English and Vietnamese ([Farmers, Tri Tang](https://agents.farmers.com/mn/minneapolis/tri-tang/); [Farmers, Hoa Nguyen](https://agents.farmers.com/mn/blaine/hoa-nguyen/)). On Lake Street, Cali Nails, opened by Ngan Hoang's mother and aunt soon after the family moved from California in 1994, repaired its walls and replaced supplies with Lake Street Council recovery grants after it was vandalized in the unrest following George Floyd's murder in 2020; whether it is open now could not be confirmed ([Lake Street Council](https://www.visitlakestreet.com/not-just-a-business/cali-nails)). No Vietnamese chamber of commerce in Minnesota turned up, and the nearest thing to a shared directory is VCM's own.

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

Buddhist and Catholic communities are the two with the deepest public record here. Tuan Hoang cites estimates that about 30 percent of Vietnamese refugees were Catholic, compared with about 10 percent in Vietnam, and credits Catholic sponsorship for much of Rochester's early settlement ([Tuan Hoang](https://tuannyriver.com/2016/08/02/my-memories-of-high-school/)). Those are national estimates he repeats, not Minnesota counts. No survey of Vietnamese Minnesotans' faith, or of those with no faith, could be found.

**Chùa Phật Ân, Roseville.** MNopedia calls it the state's first Vietnamese temple ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/vietnamese-community-of-minnesota)). Run by the Vietnamese Buddhist Association of Minnesota at 475 Minnesota Avenue, it posts a Sunday service at 10:30 a.m., nightly Pure Land practice, meditation and retreats, plus a Sunday Vietnamese language school. Its 2026 calendar ran from a year-end gathering on Feb. 8 through Tết on Feb. 17 to 19, the Buddha's birthday on May 24 and Vu Lan on Aug. 30 ([Chùa Phật Ân](https://phatan.org); [Candid](https://www.guidestar.org/profile/41-1290701)).

**St. Anne–St. Joseph Hiển, Minneapolis.** A University of St. Thomas dissertation traces how Vietnamese Catholics formed a community that became a parish in 1987 and merged with an old French Canadian parish ([Sharon Lee Tomlin, 2011](https://researchonline.stthomas.edu/esploro/outputs/doctoral/Breathing-New-Life-into-an-Old/991015130929603691)). The archdiocese lists Vietnamese Masses on Saturday evening, Sunday mornings and Tuesday evening at 2627 Queen Avenue North ([Archdiocese](https://www.archspm.org/location/st-anne-st-joseph-hien-catholic-church)). The parish posted a 2026 Summer Fair and raffle in June and 2026 to 2027 catechism classes in August ([parish site](https://gxannagiusehien.net)).

**St. Adalbert, St. Paul.** MNopedia dates the Vietnamese community here to two families sponsored by Father Tim Kernan around 1990, with about 200 families attending by 1996, its first Vietnamese pastor in 2001, and a youth movement in 2002 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/origins-vietnamese-community-st-adalbert-church-st-paul)). The archdiocese says St. Adalbert "primarily serves the Vietnamese Catholic community in the area" ([Archdiocese](https://www.archspm.org/location/st-adalbert-catholic-church)).

**Protestant churches.** A church directory lists a Hội Thánh Tin Lành Việt Nam, or Vietnamese Alliance Church, affiliated with the Christian and Missionary Alliance, at 3100 Grand Ave. S. in Minneapolis ([Devout church directory](https://www.devout.org/churches/mn/minneapolis/hoi-thanh-tin-lanh-viet-namvietnamese-alliance-church)). The listing gives no service times, and no page from the congregation itself could be found, so its current activity is unconfirmed. Other Vietnamese Protestant congregations, such as a Vietnamese Grace Church, appear in directory listings that could not be opened.

**Cao Đài and Hòa Hảo.** No Minnesota Cao Đài temple or Hòa Hảo group could be found in any available page. That does not show they are absent, since small groups may meet in homes without a public presence.

**Other Buddhist temples.** No second Vietnamese Buddhist temple, in Rochester, the northern suburbs or elsewhere, could be confirmed. A Carleton College exhibit on Phật Ân that might answer this could not be reached.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

**Tết.** The Vietnamese Community of Minnesota has hosted a Lunar New Year festival since 1981, MNopedia reports ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/vietnamese-community-of-minnesota)). It announced its 2026 fair, billed "50 Years of Journey," for Burnsville Center on Jan. 24 and 25, free ([VCM](https://vietnam-minnesota.org)). MNopedia reports more than 20,000 people at the January 2024 festival. Earlier years moved among Union Depot and school buildings ([VCM past events](https://vietnam-minnesota.org/past-events/)). At Chùa Phật Ân, the New Year's Eve service on Feb. 16, 2026, was announced with a Buddha-bathing ceremony and lion dance ([Chùa Phật Ân](https://phatan.org)). University of Minnesota students stage a large show; the 2025 edition at Northrop on Feb. 8 was called *Colors of the Homeland* ([Northrop](https://www.northrop.umn.edu/events/vsam-tet-show-2025-colors-homeland)). St. Adalbert's first annual Tết dates to 1995 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/origins-vietnamese-community-st-adalbert-church-st-paul)). The Lunar New Year itself fell on Feb. 17, 2026, as the temple's calendar shows.

**Tết Trung Thu.** Landmark Center and VCM listed their "20th annual" Mid-Autumn Moon Festival for Sept. 20, 2026, with lantern making, mooncake samples and a lantern parade through Rice Park ([Landmark Center](https://www.landmarkcenter.org/event/sundays-at-landmark-mid-autumn-moon-festival-8/)).

**Black April.** April 30 is a day of grief for many. VCM has held commemorations at the State Capitol, MNopedia reports, in 2015, 2018, 2023 and 2025, and its own list adds April 27, 2024. For the 50th anniversary, WCCO reported on a Saturday event in late April 2025 in which people carried a South Vietnamese flag to the Minnesota Vietnam Memorial ([WCCO, April 29, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/vietnam-war-50-years-minnesota-state-capitol)). MNopedia's text says April 30 and a photo caption says April 28, so the sources do not agree on the day. Veterans told WCCO the pain is as real as it was 50 years ago ([WCCO, May 7, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/vietnam-war-50-years-later-vietnamese-veterans-black-april)). VCM's own list shows it has often fallen on a Saturday near April 30: April 28, 2018, for the 43rd anniversary, April 27, 2019, for the 44th, and April 27, 2024 ([VCM past events](https://vietnam-minnesota.org/past-events/)). A bill introduced in the Minnesota House in February 2025 by Rep. Nash, HF 1440, proposed $500,000 from the arts and cultural heritage fund for programming on the 50th anniversary of the fall of Saigon, to be shared with Cambodian, Lao and Vietnamese Minnesotan organizations; the state's bill record shows it referred to the Legacy Finance committee with no later action listed ([Office of the Revisor, HF 1440 status](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/bills/94/2025/0/HF/1440/?body=house); [HF 1440 text](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/bills/text.php?number=HF1440&version=0&session=ls94&session_year=2025&session_number=0)). No organizer's announcement or news report of a 2026 Black April event in Minnesota could be found; a third-party listing of a 2026 Capitol gathering could not be opened or checked against the organizers' own pages.

**Summer fairs.** VCM's Summer Festival 2026 was listed for Aug. 22 and 23 at Dred Scott Park in Bloomington ([City of Bloomington](https://www.bloomingtonmn.gov/node/19135)). St. Anne–St. Joseph Hiển and St. Adalbert have held parish summer fairs too.

**Buddhist holy days.** Chùa Phật Ân's calendar listed the Buddha's birthday on May 24, 2026, and Vu Lan, a day of honoring parents, on Aug. 30 ([Chùa Phật Ân](https://phatan.org)).

**What we did not find.** No Minnesota Hùng Kings commemoration, no public giỗ tradition documented locally, and no Minnesota Our Lady of La Vang or Vietnamese Martyrs celebration appeared in available pages. That does not show families do not keep them.

## 7. Family, home and daily life

Compass counts 10,507 households with an average of 2.95 people. Married couples with children are 30.8 percent, married couples without children 19.8 percent, and people living alone 21.0 percent ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/vietnamese)). About 4,100 of 9,400 households in the state's count include children ([Economic Status 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)).

**Memory at home.** The Minnesota Historical Society's 2010 oral histories cover childhood in Vietnam, the fall of Saigon, camps, church sponsors, learning English, return trips, and family language, food and dance ([MNHS finding aid](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-finding-aids-public/library/findaids/oh125.html)). Those are five narrators, not a sample.

**Food and dress in public life.** St. Adalbert's fundraising has included sales of bánh chưng and bánh tét ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/origins-vietnamese-community-st-adalbert-church-st-paul)), and the Tết fair features áo dài and dragon dances ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/vietnamese-community-of-minnesota)). Phê Coffee's Dat Vu told Sahan Journal few people in the Midwest know Vietnamese coffee ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/minneapolis-st-paul-vietnamese-coffee-phe-caphin-giot/)).

**Growing up and growing older.** Youth come together through parish youth groups and language classes (sections 5 and 10). For elders, VCM formed a Senior Group in 2023, which moved to rented park space in Brooklyn Park after its office was sold in 2024 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/vietnamese-community-of-minnesota)), and Vietnamese Social Services runs an Elder Circle for people 55 and over, with weekly lunches and seasonal celebrations ([United Way 211, reviewed Dec. 23, 2025](https://search.211unitedway.org/search/fa2eb554-3309-5a12-aebd-0a7d434f665a); [VSS](https://vssmn.org)).

Sources specific to Minnesota Vietnamese weddings or everyday dress did not turn up.

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**The museum.** The Vietnamese Minnesotan Museum, founded in March 2025, works without a building. Sahan Journal calls it the first of its kind in the Midwest and reports more than 200 artifacts, more than 5,000 visitors, and a need for at least $200,000 to secure a space. Founders include curator Martino Nguyen and longtime community leader Oanh Pham, with Hung Nguyen as executive director ([Sahan Journal, March 26, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/vietnamese-minnesotan-museum-pop-up-exhibits/)). Its "Weaving Culture" show opened at Minneapolis Central Library on June 6, 2026; the library lists a July 20 close, and Sahan Journal reported July 31 ([Hennepin County Library](https://www.hclib.org/about/news/2026/june/weaving-culture)). The museum's site says it is still working on a physical location ([Vietnamese Minnesotan Museum](https://www.vietmnmuseum.com)).

**Exhibits and artists.** Landmark Center hosted "Making It Home: From Việt Nam to Minnesota" from Aug. 17 to Sept. 21, 2025 ([Landmark Center](https://www.landmarkcenter.org/event/50-years-of-vietnamese-americans-in-minnesota-celebration-and-exhibit-opening/)). "Re/Homing: Walk-ins Welcome" showed at Xia Gallery on University Avenue in May 2025, with work by Ly Thuy Nguyen, Quyen Nguyễn-Lê, N. Bui and Nhung Walsh, curated by Macalester's Christina Hughes ([Sahan Journal, May 2, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/vietnam-war-diaspora-art-exhibit-st-paul-minnesota/)). Trần Thị Minh Phước, whom the University of Minnesota's Kerlan blog described as the state's first Vietnamese librarian and a respected storyteller ([Kerlan Collection, July 9, 2018](https://kerlan.umn.edu/tag/diversity/)), was listed for a book signing at the 2026 moon festival ([Landmark Center](https://www.landmarkcenter.org/event/sundays-at-landmark-mid-autumn-moon-festival-8/)).

**Stage and language.** A state arts and heritage grant to the Minnesota Vietnamese Language School supported a story hour on *A Different Pond* with over 160 people, about 150 attending the play at Stages Theatre, and a Theater Mu student residency, ending at a January 2023 Lunar New Year event that drew 800 to 900 ([Minnesota Legacy Amendment, fiscal 2023](https://www.legacy.mn.gov/projects/vietnamese-heritage-and-legacy-connections)).

**Food as art.** Christina Nguyen and Birk Grudem own Hai Hai in Northeast Minneapolis; its awards list runs from James Beard finalist in 2019 and 2020 to the 2024 win ([Hai Hai](https://www.haihaimpls.com)).

**Sports.** Mariah Nguyen, a Vietnamese American whose grandparents came in the early 1980s, has played for Minnesota Aurora FC, and in 2025 moved to the Gophers, with more than a million TikTok followers ([Star Tribune via Sahan Journal, July 9, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/news-partners/mariah-nguyen-minnesota-aurora-gophers-soccer/)).

**Television.** WCCO's Pauleen Le and Art Phillips made a 2025 documentary, *Vietnam 50 Years Later: Reflections on a War that Changed Minnesota* ([WCCO](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/vietnam-war-50-years-later-vietnamese-veterans-black-april)). WCCO also reported that the new Minnesota Military and Veterans Museum near Camp Ripley, set to open in late 2026, will honor the refugee experience along with service members ([WCCO](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/vietnam/)). A Vietnamese-language outlet in Minnesota could not be confirmed currently running. Vietnam MN Radio, a Vietnamese-language program on KFAI, does not appear on the station's current weekly schedule ([KFAI schedule](https://kfai.org/schedule/)), and its program page could not be reached, so whether it still airs is unknown.

## 9. Civic and political life

**The Vietnamese Community of Minnesota.** Founded in 1981 by Phạm Văn Vy and other first-wave immigrants, it has had 11 presidents, and Tri Tang has led it since August 2023. It added advocacy to its purpose in 2004, shares a push for state funding and recognition with Cambodian and Lao communities, and received the Outstanding Service Award from the Council on Asian-Pacific Minnesotans in 2025 ([MNopedia, last modified April 10, 2026](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/vietnamese-community-of-minnesota)). It runs on volunteers. Tang and Dave Hoang went to Washington, D.C., for the 50th anniversary ([WCCO, May 6, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/video/vietnamese-community-of-minnesota-reflect-on-50-years-since-wars-end)), a trip MNopedia describes as seeking recognition and support for veterans.

**Vietnamese Social Services of Minnesota.** Led by executive director Hue T. Pham, it serves about half Vietnamese and 40 percent Karen participants ([VSS](https://vssmn.org)). That mix tells its own story of an older refugee community's institution serving newer arrivals.

**Elected and appointed office.** In a 2018 essay, Jennifer Nguyen Moore, a daughter of Vietnamese refugees, wrote that if elected Ramsey County commissioner in District 3 she would be the first Vietnamese person elected to office in Minnesota ([CAAL, May 2, 2018](https://old.caalmn.org/?p=1811); [CAAL, #MinneAsianStories](https://caalmn.org/2020/10/15/jennifer-nguyen-moore/)). She did not advance: MinnPost reported that Trista MatasCastillo and incumbent Janice Rettman took the top two spots in the August 2018 primary ([MinnPost, August 2018](https://www.minnpost.com/politics-policy/2018/08/other-races-mitra-jalali-nelson-wins-st-paul-council-seat-field-winnowed-she/)). No Vietnamese Minnesotan in the Legislature, on a county board or on a city council could be confirmed from available pages, and no source was found that names a first.

**Differences within the community.** The South Vietnamese flag carried to the Capitol in 2025 ([WCCO](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/vietnam-war-50-years-minnesota-state-capitol)) shows one strong current of memory. Augsburg's Ly Thuy Nguyen is writing a book on Vietnamese American activists and radicalism during and after the war ([Augsburg University](https://www.augsburg.edu/faculty/ly-thuy-nguyen/)), which is a reminder that politics among Vietnamese Americans have never been one thing. No Minnesota poll on Vietnamese voters' views was found.

**At the Legislature.** The 2025 bill to fund Fall of Saigon commemorations, HF 1440, would have directed money to Cambodian, Lao and Vietnamese Minnesotan groups through the Minnesota Humanities Center and the Council on Asian Pacific Minnesotans, saying past commemorations had underfunded them (section 6; [HF 1440 text](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/bills/text.php?number=HF1440&version=0&session=ls94&session_year=2025&session_number=0)).

**Immigration advocacy.** See section 13.

## 10. Education

The state's data show about 6,300 of 15,600 Vietnamese Minnesotans ages 25 to 64 with a bachelor's degree or more and about 2,900 without a high school diploma ([Economic Status 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). Both ends of that range are real.

**Language schools.** MNopedia reports that the first Vietnamese church and temple offered language classes in the late 1980s, that teachers founded the Vietnamese Language School in 2013 with VCM's support, and that it has met at Aŋpétu Téča Education Center in Roseville since 2021 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/vietnamese-community-of-minnesota)). WCCO covered that effort in May 2025 ([WCCO, May 9, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/video/minnesota-vietnamese-language-school-keeping-viet-culture-alive)). Chùa Phật Ân still runs Sunday classes ([Chùa Phật Ân](https://phatan.org)), and St. Adalbert lists Vietnamese language coursework in its Sunday school ([St. Adalbert](http://www.stadalbertchurch.org)).

**University.** The University of Minnesota's new heritage sequence, VIET 1015 in fall 2026 and VIET 1016 in spring 2027, is taught online by Ky Win Nguyen ([University of Minnesota](https://cla.umn.edu/language-center/undergraduate-students/vietnamese)). The Vietnamese Student Association of Minnesota is the student group behind the Tết Show ([Northrop](https://www.northrop.umn.edu/events/vsam-tet-show-2025-colors-homeland)).

**Students who speak Vietnamese at home.** The state's 2024–25 English learner report counts 3,071 Minnesota students whose primary home language is Vietnamese, of whom 1,033 were English learners, down from 1,239 English learners in 2020. Among the eight counties with the most English learners, Vietnamese-speaking English learners numbered 320 in Hennepin, 163 in Ramsey, 132 in Dakota, 122 in Anoka, 76 in Washington, 72 in Scott and 22 each in Olmsted and Stearns ([Minnesota Department of Education, English Learners in Minnesota 2024–25, via Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/other/261381.pdf)). So about two in three students from Vietnamese-speaking homes were not classified as English learners in 2025.

No charter school or public dual-language program in Vietnamese in Minnesota turned up. Nor did recent state test or graduation data broken out for Vietnamese students.

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Salon work.** The public concern best documented locally is chemical exposure. Ngan Hoang told Sahan Journal she read about liver and kidney damage, birth defects and cancer and changed her inventory over four years. Those are her reading and her worry, not study findings on Minnesota workers. University of Minnesota researchers planned to approach the Department of Health about a possible healthy salon program ([Sahan Journal, Feb. 6, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/health/non-toxic-nail-hair-salons-vietnamese-east-african-minnesota-epa-grant/)).

**Community-based care.** VCM has hosted a pop-up vaccination event and a workers' compensation workshop, both in October 2024 ([VCM past events](https://vietnam-minnesota.org/past-events/)). The museum names mental wellbeing among its aims ([Vietnamese Minnesotan Museum](https://www.vietmnmuseum.com)). TT Market advertises one of the state's fuller selections of Chinese herbs and supplements ([TT Market](https://ttmarket.biz)), which shows the trade exists without saying how families use it.

**What clinic data show.** MN Community Measurement's report on care delivered in 2022 breaks results out by patients' preferred language, and Vietnamese is one of the five languages large enough to report for most measures. Compared with the statewide average, Vietnamese-speaking patients had significantly higher rates of optimal diabetes care and optimal vascular care. On depression, they had significantly lower rates of follow-up at six months, of response at 12 months, and of remission at 12 months. For colorectal cancer screening and children's asthma control, the report did not single them out as different from the average ([MN Community Measurement, Minnesota Health Care Disparities, October 2023, via Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2023/mandated/232010.pdf)). These are clinic records for patients who chose Vietnamese as their language, not for every Vietnamese Minnesotan, and the report does not explain the gaps. The depression results raise questions about how well screening tools and follow-up work across language, which the report does not answer.

**Care in Vietnamese.** Named Vietnamese-focused clinics could not be found in available pages. Individual clinicians who list Vietnamese do appear: Gillette Children's lists complex-care pediatrician Uyen Truong, MD, as speaking English and Vietnamese at its Phalen, Burnsville and St. Paul campus sites ([Gillette Children's](https://gillettechildrens.org/care-team/results/complex-care-care-team/P8)). In home care, the Vietnamese Minnesotans Association at 1030 University Ave. W. in St. Paul appears on HealthPartners' list of contracted personal care assistance agencies ([HealthPartners PCA provider list, undated](https://www.healthpartners.com/content/dam/plan/providers/reference/pca-provider-list.pdf)).

## 12. Disability

**What Minnesota numbers show.** Compass reports that 8.4 percent of Vietnamese Minnesotans have a disability, give or take 1.8 points, out of 32,708 people for whom status was determined. Among those under 65 it is 6.0 percent, give or take 1.5 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/vietnamese)). The state's 2019 to 2023 report counts about 1,000 adults ages 18 to 64 with a disability, give or take 500 ([Economic Status 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). The margins are large, and no available source explains these numbers.

**Questions these numbers leave open.**
- Who decides that someone has a disability here: a family, a doctor, a school, the county? How does a language barrier affect that moment, when 35.5 percent of people 5 and older speak English less than "very well"?
- What does a label open or close for an older refugee who has lived with an injury for 50 years? For a child in a Vietnamese-speaking home?
- What produced an impairment? WCCO's 2025 coverage quotes veteran Nam Tong saying he lost his legs in the war ([WCCO, April 29, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/vietnam-war-50-years-minnesota-state-capitol)). That is one man's account, but it points to war injury, camps and flight as histories worth asking about. Salon workers' exposure concerns in section 11 raise a similar question about work. Whether either shows up in Minnesota rates is unknown.
- People living alone make up 21.0 percent of households, at all ages. How many are elders, and do they get help at home in their language?
- Does a survey question about disability fit how a family talks about a condition in Vietnamese?
- Clinic records show Vietnamese-speaking adults with depression less likely than average to get follow-up at six months or to reach remission at 12 months ([MN Community Measurement, 2023](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2023/mandated/232010.pdf)). Does that reflect screening tools, interpretation, stigma, or something else? No Minnesota source says.
- During the 2026 federal enforcement surge, the New Breath Foundation wrote that Asian community members with disabilities had been removed from their cars on the way to doctor's appointments ([New Breath Foundation, Feb. 25, 2026](https://new-breath.org/blog/diy-our-survival/)). It did not say whether any were Vietnamese, but it raises the question of how fear affects whether people seek care.

**Who is doing what.** The clearest Vietnamese-led service tied to disability is home care. The Vietnamese Minnesotans Association says in its tax filings that it focuses on personal care assistance and homemaking, helping families provide care to eligible family members, and that most clients are Vietnamese American because staff speak their language ([GrantWatch summary of IRS Form 990](https://minnesota.grantwatch.com/organization-profile/573694/vietnamese-minnesotans-association/); [HealthPartners PCA list](https://www.healthpartners.com/content/dam/plan/providers/reference/pca-provider-list.pdf)). Its description, which speaks of families caring for eligible family members, points to one place where a disability or need label opens a door: paid care at home, given in Vietnamese, sometimes by a relative. Gillette Children's lists a Vietnamese-speaking complex-care pediatrician ([Gillette Children's](https://gillettechildrens.org/care-team/results/complex-care-care-team/P8)). Vietnamese Social Services' site names elder programs but does not mention disability ([VSS](https://vssmn.org)). VCM's resource page says it is under construction ([VCM](https://vietnam-minnesota.org/resources/)). The Senior Group and Elder Circle offer connection without being disability services. No Minnesota study, thesis or state report on disability among Vietnamese Minnesotans, and no Vietnamese-led disability group in Minnesota, could be confirmed from available pages. That may reflect what could be reached rather than what exists.

## 13. What is changing now

**Deportation.** For years a 2008 agreement protected Vietnamese who came before 1995 from removal, MPR reported in December 2018, when the Trump administration was reported to be seeking to revisit it. Sia Her, then head of the Council on Asian-Pacific Minnesotans, estimated about 9,000 people nationally could be affected ([MPR News, Dec. 14, 2018](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2018/12/14/trump-administration-looks-to-deport-vietnamese-immigrants)). At a St. Paul event that month, Hoang Murphy, who described himself as the son of Vietnamese immigrants, said "I'm a son of Minnesota" ([WCCO, Dec. 20, 2018](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/deportation-concerns-sweep-minnesotas-southeast-asian-population)). State Sen. Foung Hawj's office then estimated nearly 800 Hmong, Lao, Cambodian and Vietnamese Americans in Minnesota were under orders; no Vietnamese-only state number was given ([AP via Northwest Asian Weekly, Jan. 6, 2019](https://nwasianweekly.com/2019/01/st-paul-leaders-decry-deportations-of-vietnam-era-refugees)).

Reporting has since grown sharper. A Straits Times article republished by Asia News Network on Sept. 28, 2026, citing court documents, reports 1,851 Vietnamese nationals removed by ICE from fiscal 2024 through June of fiscal 2026, with 1,094 in fiscal 2026 alone. It cites the Asian Law Caucus for more than 8,600 Vietnamese with removal orders, and the Southeast Asia Resource Action Center for half of 2025 to 2026 Vietnamese deportees having convictions at least two decades old. It reports Vietnamese authorities receiving 110 returnees on Sept. 12. It does not mention Minnesota ([Asia News Network, Sept. 28, 2026](https://asianews.network/?p=303930)). Xay Yang of Transforming Generations told MPR on Feb. 24, 2026, that 970 Vietnamese, Cambodian and Laotian people had been deported as of her latest figure, dated July 28, 2025, and that 17 percent had lived here more than 30 years. Her group is part of the Southeast Asian Defense Response Project, and the Southeast Asian Freedom Network's help line takes calls in Vietnamese ([MPR News, Feb. 24, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/02/24/southeast-asian-minnesotans-deportation-and-detention)). A 3HBC summary of an August 2026 American Prospect report dates the same 970 figure to January through mid-October 2025, so the period is reported differently ([3HBC, Aug. 18, 2026](https://hbctv.net/stories/new-national-report-puts-hmong-and-southeast-asian-minnesotans-back-at-center-of)). MN8's Montha Chum told Sahan Journal that removals can follow within about six weeks of detention, and the outlet noted repatriation agreements with Vietnam mean some support exists there ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 2, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/deportations-minnesota-mn8-hmong-laos-cambodia-vietnam/)). No available source gave a number of Vietnamese Minnesotans deported.

**How removals to Vietnam work.** Sahan Journal reported in March 2025 that Vietnam signed a repatriation agreement in 2008 covering people who arrived in or after 1995, and a 2020 memorandum to include some earlier arrivals. Citing Asian Americans Advancing Justice, it reported that four people who came before 1995 were deported to Vietnam between September 2021 and September 2023, and that in late February 2025, after tariff and visa threats, Vietnam agreed to speed travel documents for about 30 Vietnamese nationals in U.S. custody. Those are national figures. Sahan put Minnesota's Vietnamese population at 36,000 in the same story ([Sahan Journal, March 28, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/trump-deportations-southeast-asian-hmong-laos-thailand-vietnam-cambodia/)).

**Operation Metro Surge.** The New Breath Foundation, a philanthropy, wrote in February 2026 that since December 2025 more than 3,000 federal agents had made more than 3,400 arrests in the Twin Cities, that agents had gone door to door asking for the names of Hmong and Lao residents, and that Southeast Asian markets had gone quiet ([New Breath Foundation, Feb. 25, 2026](https://new-breath.org/blog/diy-our-survival/)). An op-ed by two Edina League of Women Voters members, published in July 2026, put the surge at nearly 4,000 agents and 5,000 people rounded up, arrested or deported, and said their volunteers handed out know-your-rights cards in Vietnamese among other languages ([Duluth News Tribune op-ed, July 19, 2026](https://worldwithoutgenocide.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/2026-7-19-Duluth-News-Tribune-Metro-Surge.pdf)). Those are advocates' figures, and they differ. No available source counted how many of those arrested were Vietnamese, or described Vietnamese businesses, churches or families in the surge by name. The 2026 Tết fair at Burnsville Center on Jan. 24 appears among the Vietnamese Minnesotan Museum's past events ([Vietnamese Minnesotan Museum](https://www.vietmnmuseum.com)), but no report on attendance or on fear at the fair could be opened.

**Institutions without buildings.** VCM sold its office building in 2024 and, as of 2026, has no permanent location, MNopedia reports, while the museum seeks one too. Yet Tang has pointed to younger members taking on community roles ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/vietnamese-community-of-minnesota)).

**University Avenue and the suburbs.** Tay Ho's closing and sale of its building came as Sahan Journal described the avenue as having once had many more such restaurants and markets ([Sahan Journal, June 5, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/tay-ho-vietnamese-restaurant-closes-st-paul-university-avenue/)). Meanwhile the newest cafés have opened in Lowertown and on France Avenue.

**Language and generations.** The University of Minnesota's new heritage courses and the long-running weekend schools give younger people ways in, and WCCO reports community members working to keep the language alive for future generations. Brower's chart suggests some continued new arrivals, though Vietnamese are a smaller part of recent Asian migration than people from India and China ([Demographic Center](https://mn.gov/capm/assets/SusanBrower_StateOfAsianPacificMinnesotans_June2026_tcm1051-759113.pdf)).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

Families who arrived with little built institutions that still run largely on volunteers: a 45-year-old community organization, parishes that revived aging buildings, and a temple that has kept language classes going. Vietnamese Social Services now serves Karen and other newer arrivals too ([VSS](https://vssmn.org)). Vietnamese entrepreneurs helped shape Eat Street and University Avenue, Christina Nguyen's Hai Hai brought home a James Beard Award for Best Chef: Midwest, and a new generation, from Khue's in St. Paul to House of Huế on Nicollet, is carrying family cooking forward. Vietnamese-speaking patients in Minnesota clinics show better-than-average diabetes and vascular care results ([MN Community Measurement, 2023](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2023/mandated/232010.pdf)). Home ownership near 70 percent came from a community that began with refugee sponsorship. Scholars, artists and a young museum are now writing this history on the community's own terms. Veterans of the South Vietnamese forces and their families have kept their losses in public memory at the Capitol. These are documented above; none of them erases the people still in poverty, renters under strain, or families facing removal.
