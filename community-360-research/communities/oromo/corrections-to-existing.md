# Corrections to the existing Oromo Minnesota page

Page: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/oromo

## Errors or claims that should change

1. **Claim:** MinnPost, May 19, 2016: "the Oromo Community of Minnesota" estimated up to 40,000.
   **Source says:** The estimate belongs to the Oromo Cultural Institute of Minnesota, a separate group, according to both a Minnesota Daily report and Wilder Research. Wilder says the Oromo Community of Minnesota assisted with its survey but credits the estimate to the institute. MinnPost itself could not be reopened.
   **Links:** https://mndaily.com/239129/uncategorized/oromo-people-seek-more-recognition/ ; https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/pdf-file/OromoCommunityAssessment_7-16.pdf .
2. **Claim:** Population figures use Compass's 2019 to 2023 Ethiopian profile: about 40,324 people, median income about $57,106 in 2022 dollars, poverty 13.0 percent, owner-occupied 34.7 percent, household size 3.09.
   **Source says:** Compass now gives 53,754 (plus or minus 12,826) for 2024 and 45,159 (plus or minus 5,404) for 2020 to 2024. Other current figures: 89.7 percent Twin Cities, 43.8 percent 17 or younger, 59.8 percent foreign-born, median household income $63,667 in 2024 dollars, poverty 10.9 percent, owner-occupied 35.6 percent, household size 3.30.
   **Link:** https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/ethiopian .
3. **Claim:** Oromo is the 8th most common home language in K-12 schools.
   **Source says:** Current state report for 2024-25: 3,297 students with Oromo as primary home language, 1,568 of them English learners. Oromo is sixth in the report's top-12 tables, which are built around English learners, so its overall rank among all languages is not shown. The page's rank may come from another year; the Star Tribune could not be opened.
   **Link:** https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/other/261381.pdf .
4. **Claim:** Irreechaa is held each September at Lake Nokomis (MNopedia), with Ampers noting May and September gatherings.
   **Source says:** MNopedia does say that, and it should be presented as history. For 2026, the organizer's listing and Oromo Diaspora Media place the September 19 event at Powderhorn Park, and the May 2 event was also at Powderhorn. An Australian advocacy site alone reports a Lake Nokomis celebration on the same day. Ampers says "a selected park" and does not name Nokomis.
   **Links:** https://irreechaa.org/event/ireechaa-by-the-water-sept-19-2026-at-powderhorn-park-lake-in-minneapolis/ ; https://irreechaa.org/irreechaa-tulluu-2026/ ; https://oromodiasporamedia.org/ayyaana-irreechaa-mn-fulbaana-19-2026/ ; https://advocacy4oromia.org/2026/09/20/minnesota-oromo-community-celebrates-irreecha-with-gratitude-unity-and-hope/ ; https://ampers.org/celebrating-irreechaa-in-minnesota/ .
5. **Claim:** *Oromo Witness* "released August 15, 2020"; "Hangasu arrived in St. Paul as a refugee in 2000"; most profits support the Burqaa Guddinnaa Foundation.
   **Source says:** Sahan Journal says the book came out "this week" and a photo caption mentions a release reading on Aug. 15 in St. Paul. It says he came to the U.S. as a refugee in 2000 and lives in Minnesota, without St. Paul as the arrival place. It names no foundation. Those two details could not be supported from this source.
   **Link:** https://sahanjournal.com/arts/bale-oromo-witness-ethiopia/ .
6. **Claim:** Oromo Community of Minnesota board statement on Ilfinash Qannoo, September 2026.
   **Source says:** Could not be found on the organization's home page, which listed its most recent news as Jan. 29, 2026. Oromo Diaspora Media does report a memorial in Minnesota, in a Sept. 21, 2026 post, without naming organizers.
   **Links:** https://oromocommunitymn.org/ ; https://oromodiasporamedia.org/yaadannoo-fi-sirni-gaggeessaa-weellistuu-fi-qabsooftuu-ilfinash-qannoo-minisootaatti-taasifame/ .
7. **Claim:** Compass counts Ethiopia-born people who list Somali ancestry as Somali.
   **Source says:** The current Ethiopian profile does not mention Somali or Oromo ancestry. This could not be supported from the page opened. It may appear elsewhere on Compass.
   **Link:** https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/ethiopian .
8. **Claim:** OSFNA tournament first hosted by St. Paul in 2011.
   **Source says:** MNopedia does give 2011 for St. Paul. The page should not read as the tournament's start: the federation says its first tournament was in Toronto in 1996, with a Minnesota club among six.
   **Links:** https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/oromos-minnesota-making-little-oromia ; https://osfna.org/2026/01/01/culture-and-athletic/ .
## Things the page should add

- **The community's own count has changed.** The Oromo Community of Minnesota's About page now says "Over 100,000," while its CareerForce listing says 40,000 members. https://oromocommunitymn.org/about-us/ ; https://careerforce.mn.gov/so/group/173/location/158 .
- **OSFNA 2026 dates:** August 1 to 8 in St. Paul, final August 8 at Allianz Field. https://oromoga.org/ ; https://www.allianzfield.com/events/osfna-2026 .
- **Afaan Oromo teaching:** The University of Minnesota pilot began in 2025, and Bultum Academy teaches it in Columbia Heights. https://brief.umn.edu/feature/reviving-once-endangered-language ; https://www.bultumacademy.org/theoromolanguage . These did not appear in the portions of the page read.
- **More congregations:** Oromo Evangelical Church of Minnesota in Robbinsdale (incorporated Oct. 30, 2019) and Bet'el Afaan Oromo Evangelical Church at 2120 E. 38th St. https://oecmn.org/about-us ; https://betelmn.org/contact-us/ .
- **Ethiopia's Temporary Protected Status** ended Aug. 18, 2026, according to Fragomen, and the Oromo Community of Minnesota has a Rapid Response Team. https://www.fragomen.com/insights/united-states-federal-district-court-allows-termination-of-tps-for-ethiopia-to-proceed.html ; https://oromocommunitymn.org/response-english .
