# Japanese in Minnesota
Checked: October 31, 2026

## 1. People and place

**How many.** Minnesota Compass, using the American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024, counts 13,457 Japanese Minnesotans, give or take 1,823. It places 74.1 percent in the Twin Cities and 25.9 percent in Greater Minnesota, each give or take 4.8 points ([Minnesota Compass, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/japanese)). This is a small group in survey terms, so the margins are wide, and many detailed rows (poverty by age, people 85 and older, single parents) are suppressed. Nationally, Pew Research Center counts about 1.39 million people who identify as Japanese alone or in combination in 2023, with California (410,000) and Hawaii (250,000) far ahead of any other state; Minnesota is not among the states Pew lists ([Pew Research Center, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-japanese-in-the-u-s/)).

**Age, birthplace and language.** In the Compass profile, 29.6 percent are 17 or younger and 10.7 percent are 65 or older. Women are 55.5 percent of the count. Foreign-born residents are 16.9 percent, give or take 4.7 points. Among people 5 and older, 73.1 percent speak only English at home, 26.9 percent speak another language, and 8.8 percent speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/japanese)). Compass does not say which other language; Pew's national figures show Japanese is by far the most common one, spoken at home by 26 percent of Japanese Americans 5 and older, and that 98 percent of U.S.-born Japanese Americans are English proficient against 53 percent of immigrants. That is national background, not a Minnesota measure ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-japanese-in-the-u-s/)).

**Two broad threads.** The record opened for this profile shows at least two overlapping communities. One is a long-settled Japanese American community whose Minnesota story begins in World War II. The other is a newer group of people from Japan who came for work, study or marriage, many of them tied to Japanese companies here, with children in Saturday Japanese schools (sections 3 and 10). Families of mixed heritage run through both. The Japan America Society of Minnesota describes its own network as Minnesota's sister cities with Japan, two Japanese-language Saturday schools and "over 100 Japanese corporate subsidiaries" ([Japan America Society of Minnesota, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://mn-japan.org/)).

**How the older community came.** Before the war Minnesota had almost no Japanese residents. MNopedia's entry on the St. Paul Resettlement Committee says there were eleven Japanese people living in St. Paul and about fifty in the state before 1940, and about 1,000 people of Japanese descent statewide by March 1945, 350 of them in St. Paul ([MNopedia, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/st-paul-resettlement-committee)). St. Olaf professor Ka F. Wong puts the 1940 figure at 51 and says the number had grown to "several thousand" by the end of the war ([St. Olaf College, June 25, 2025](https://wp.stolaf.edu/news/enmity-and-empathy-professors-new-book-explores-japanese-american-history-in-minnesota-following-wwii)). MNopedia's entry on the Army language school says the state's Japanese American population grew to approximately five thousand while the school was here ([MNopedia, MISLS](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/military-intelligence-service-language-school-misls)). The sources count at different moments and do not agree exactly.

Two forces brought people. First, Executive Order 9066, signed February 19, 1942, led to the forced removal of Japanese Americans from the West Coast. As the government let people leave the camps, Minnesota church, YWCA, International Institute and college networks helped them resettle. The St. Paul Resettlement Committee formed on October 5, 1942; Macalester, Hamline, St. Thomas and Bethel enrolled Japanese American students; and the committee ran a hostel at 191 West Kellogg Boulevard from December 1945 that housed 150 people over two years ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/st-paul-resettlement-committee)). Ruth and Earl Tanbara, who arrived in August 1942, are described by MNopedia as the first Japanese Americans to resettle in St. Paul because of the order ([MNopedia, Ruth Tanbara](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/tanbara-ruth-nomura-1907-2008)).

Second, the Army's Military Intelligence Service Language School moved to Camp Savage in June 1942 after, by MNopedia's account, several other states refused it, and Governor Harold Stassen agreed to take it. It moved to Fort Snelling in August 1944 and left for California after its last Minnesota class graduated in June 1946. It graduated over six thousand linguists, roughly 85 percent of them Nisei ([MNopedia, MISLS](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/military-intelligence-service-language-school-misls); [National Park Service, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://home.nps.gov/miss/learn/historyculture/langschool.htm)). St. Olaf's oral history project says the school and the colleges that accepted Nisei students were "the connection" through which many families chose Minnesota, and that more than 400 Japanese Americans had resettled in the Twin Cities by the end of 1943 ([St. Olaf, Beyond the Barbed Wire](https://pages.stolaf.edu/jam/resettlement-in-minnesota/)). Sally Sudo, now 90 and living in Edina, told the Pioneer Press in October 2026 that her brother, an MIS linguist, urged the family to move from Seattle after their release from the Minidoka camp because he found Minnesotans "very friendly" ([Pioneer Press, Oct. 9, 2026](https://www.twincities.com/2026/10/09/it-ruined-so-many-peoples-lives-book-recognizes-people-of-japanese-descent-incarcerated-during-wwii/)).

**Later arrivals.** A smaller, steady flow followed. Reiko Umetani Weston, a native of Japan, opened Fuji-Ya in downtown Minneapolis in 1959 ([Governor's proclamation, 1989, Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/sos/film/pdf/39488.pdf)). Sakura's owner Miyoko Omori came from Saitama in 1970 ([Sakura Restaurant & Bar, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.sakurastpaul.com/)). The Minnesota Japanese School has taught children of Japanese-speaking families on Saturdays since 1978 ([Minnesota Japanese School](https://www.mnjs.org/english)). No Minnesota source opened gives a count of Japanese nationals living here on work or student visas.

**Where.** Compass gives only the Twin Cities and Greater Minnesota split. Organizations and events named in this profile cluster in Minneapolis, St. Paul, Edina, Bloomington, Roseville and the north metro, with Japanese restaurants as far as Duluth (section 4). No opened source maps Japanese Minnesotans by city or neighborhood, and no source was found on why a quarter of the community lives outside the metro.

**Generations.** People in this community use generation words: Issei (immigrants from Japan), Nisei (their U.S.-born children), Sansei and Yonsei (third and fourth generations). Rep. Samantha Sencer-Mura's 2022 campaign site described her as a "fourth-generation Japanese American" ([Legislative Reference Library, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/legdb/fulldetail?id=15572)). A Yonsei writer in the October 2026 Twin Cities JACL newsletter, whose parents grew up in Hawaii, wrote of being taught the incarceration "had nothing to do with us" and of later coming to believe it had an effect anyway ([Rice Paper, Oct. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TCJACL-Rice-Paper-10-2026.pdf)). Family histories differ across Hawaii, the West Coast, Japan and Minnesota.

## 2. All walks of life

All figures here are from the Compass 2020 to 2024 profile, in 2024 dollars, unless another source is named ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/japanese)). Because the group is small, margins are large; read these as rough shapes.

- **Comfortable and well-off households.** Median household income is $82,258, give or take $19,418. Some 40.8 percent of households take in $100,000 or more. Among adults 25 and older, 48.1 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more, including 19.5 percent with a graduate or professional degree. Only 1.2 percent lack health insurance.
- **Professionals and owners.** Public careers include Edwin "Bud" Nakasone, an MIS linguist who moved from Hawaii to Minnesota, and his son, retired Gen. Paul Nakasone, former director of the National Security Agency, who joined him at a 2024 Fort Snelling ceremony ([MPR News, May 16, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/05/16/minnesotans-honor-the-states-japanese-american-veterans)); Dr. Karen Tanaka Lucas, who retired after more than 30 years as a HealthPartners family physician ([Japan America Society of Minnesota](https://mn-japan.org/)); chef Shigeyuki Furukawa of Kado no Mise and Kaiseki Furukawa ([Kado no Mise, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.kadonomise.com/)); and University of Minnesota graphic design professor Seiji Ikeda ([Rice Paper, Oct. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TCJACL-Rice-Paper-10-2026.pdf)). Reiko Weston was named Small Business Person of the Year and elevated into the Minnesota Business Hall of Fame, according to the governor's 1989 proclamation ([Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/sos/film/pdf/39488.pdf)).
- **Middle-income and working families.** Owner-occupied homes are 63.3 percent of households. Employment among adults 18 to 64 is 72.3 percent, 71.3 percent for men and 73.0 percent for women. About 38.2 percent of adults 25 and older have some college or an associate's degree, and 10.8 percent stopped at a high school diploma.
- **People with low incomes and in poverty.** Some 24.1 percent of households take in less than $35,000 (give or take 9.8 points). Of all people, 6.5 percent live below the poverty line, give or take 4.0 points, and another 13.8 percent live between the poverty line and twice that line. Housing costs weigh heavily: 35.5 percent of households are cost-burdened, including 62.4 percent of renter households (margin 16.1 points). And 37.0 percent of households are one person living alone, with an average household size of 2.19.

Taken together, the numbers show a community that is, on average, highly educated and comfortable, with a large group of single-person and renter households under real cost pressure. No opened source explains who the lower-income households are (students, elders, newcomers or others), so that remains a question.

**Wealth and hardship in history.** Sally Sudo told the Pioneer Press in 2026 that her father "lost his job," that the family could bring only what they could carry, and that her parents "lost everything" and did not live to see reparations ([Pioneer Press, Oct. 9, 2026](https://www.twincities.com/2026/10/09/it-ruined-so-many-peoples-lives-book-recognizes-people-of-japanese-descent-incarcerated-during-wwii/)). St. Olaf's project records that many families arriving in the Twin Cities had trouble finding housing, turned away with excuses such as that "the neighbors would object" ([St. Olaf](https://pages.stolaf.edu/jam/resettlement-in-minnesota/)).

## 3. Work and the economy

**Wartime and resettlement work.** The St. Paul and Minneapolis resettlement committees existed largely to find jobs and housing. St. Olaf's project credits two local women, Genevieve Steefel and Ruth Gage Colby, with helping more than 1,000 Japanese Americans find work in Minnesota ([St. Olaf](https://pages.stolaf.edu/jam/resettlement-in-minnesota/)). At Camp Savage and Fort Snelling, Japanese American instructors and soldiers did intelligence work; MNopedia says graduates served with more than 130 wartime organizations ([MNopedia, MISLS](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/military-intelligence-service-language-school-misls)). The MNHS Press managing editor, accepting the 2026 Minnesota Book Award for Wong's book, said it showed people released from the camps becoming "college students, translators, nurses, small business owners" in Minnesota ([MNHS Press newsletter, May 19, 2026](https://www.mnhs.org/mnhs-press-newsletter/may-19-2026)).

**Japanese companies and trade.** Japan is a significant trading partner. The state employment agency reports Minnesota exports to Japan of $259 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 17 percent from a year earlier, while total state exports fell 10 percent that quarter ([Minnesota DEED, published May 2026](https://mn.gov/deed/assets/2025-quarter4-exports_tcm1045-752085.docx)). In an undated release on Takeda Pharmaceuticals' Brooklyn Park expansion, Gov. Tim Walz said Japanese companies in Minnesota had grown by nearly 50 percent since he took office, to nearly 50 companies at more than 120 locations ([KSTP, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/local-news/japanese-company-completes-investment-project-in-brooklyn-park/)). In June 2022 Walz hosted a delegation from the Japan External Trade Organization at Takeda in Brooklyn Park ([CCX Media, June 23, 2022](https://ccxmedia.org/news/governor-walz-meets-with-japanese-business-leaders-in-brooklyn-park/)). The society's count of "over 100" subsidiaries and the governor's "nearly 50 companies" measure different things and were not reconciled in any source opened.

**Occupations.** Compass does not publish occupations for this group. Commuting figures show 70.3 percent of workers drive and 26.6 percent walk, bike, work at home or use other means, a relatively high non-driving share ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/japanese)).

**Labor and unions.** No Minnesota source on Japanese American union history or current union membership was found.

**Entrepreneurship.** Restaurants are the most visible owner-run businesses (section 4). The governor's 1989 proclamation credits Reiko Weston with being "the first to bring Teppanyaki and Sushi to the area" and with buying riverfront land at 420 South 1st Street in 1962, before that area was developed ([Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/sos/film/pdf/39488.pdf)).

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

There is no Japanese commercial district in Minnesota, and no Japanese grocery store in the state was confirmed open. The businesses below are restaurants, a pan-Asian market with a Japanese aisle, community media and the trade-and-culture society. The full list, with the evidence for each status, is in businesses.json. The list is short because the community is small and spread out. Some Japanese-style restaurants are owned by people of other backgrounds, and this profile does not guess ownership.

**Restaurants with documented Japanese owners or chefs.**
- **Kado no Mise, Kaiseki Furukawa and Gori Gori Peku**, 33 North First Avenue in Minneapolis's North Loop. Kado no Mise serves Edomae sushi in the style chef Shigeyuki Furukawa learned in his hometown of Tokyo; Kaiseki Furukawa serves a seasonal tasting meal; Gori Gori Peku is a Japanese whisky bar across the hall. The site lists current hours. The same group runs Sanjusan, a Japanese-Italian restaurant on the main floor ([Kado no Mise, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.kadonomise.com/); [Sanjusan](https://www.sanjusanrestaurant.com/)).
- **Cafe Yoto**, 548 North Washington Avenue, Minneapolis. A counter-service udon shop opened in early 2025 by chef Yo Hasegawa, who worked for years alongside Furukawa, Racket reported ([Racket, March 24, 2025](https://racketmn.com/inside-cafe-yoto-the-north-loops-hot-new-japanese-fast-casual-spot); [Cafe Yoto](https://cafeyoto.com/)).
- **Sakura Restaurant & Bar**, 350 St. Peter Street, St. Paul. Owner Miyoko Omori came from Saitama in 1970, waited tables at Fuji-Ya for seven years, co-founded Kikugawa in Minneapolis in 1979, and opened Sakura at Galtier Plaza in 1990; it has been at its present address since 1997 ([Sakura, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.sakurastpaul.com/)).

**History.** Fuji-Ya, founded in 1959 by Reiko Umetani Weston, was called the Twin Cities' "premiere Japanese restaurant" in a 1989 governor's proclamation. After her death in May 1988, her daughter Carol Weston carried it on, and Gov. Rudy Perpich proclaimed September 27, 1989, Fuji-Ya Restaurant Day for its 30th anniversary ([Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/sos/film/pdf/39488.pdf)). Moto-i, a Lyn-Lake sake brewpub, says its first sake was inspired by "a sip of Masumi Junmai Ginjo at Fuji Ya" ([moto-i, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://moto-i.com/)). No current page was found for Fuji-Ya. Tanpopo Noodle Shop in St. Paul is listed as closed ([Gayot](https://www.gayot.com/restaurants/tanpopo-noodle-shop-st-paul-mn-55101_37mn040902.html)).

**Other Japanese restaurants confirmed open**, ownership not established in opened sources: Ramen Kazama, 3400 Nicollet Avenue ([Ramen Kazama](https://www.ramenkazama.com/)); Kyatchi, 3758 Nicollet Avenue ([Kyatchi](https://kyatchi.com/)); moto-i in Lyn-Lake ([moto-i](https://moto-i.com/)); and Zen House, 4023 Woodland Avenue in Duluth, which posts current hours ([Zen House](https://www.zenhouseduluth.com/)).

**Groceries.** United Noodles, "Minnesota's Asian Market Since 1972," in Minneapolis and Woodbury, has a Japanese grocery section for rice, noodles, miso and curry ([United Noodles, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.unitednoodles.com/cuisine/japanese/)).

**Business associations.** The Japan America Society of Minnesota, founded in 1972 and a member of the National Association of Japan-America Societies, is the closest thing to a chamber. It offers corporate memberships, runs the Mondale Award and Scholarship Gala (its 29th, on November 14, 2026, at Oak Ridge Country Club in Hopkins), and supports the state's Japanese corporate subsidiaries ([Japan America Society of Minnesota](https://mn-japan.org/); [United Way 211 listing](https://search.211unitedway.org/search/e42cceb3-ece1-544e-b76c-667b6d51e1ef)). The Consulate-General of Japan in Chicago publishes economic overviews of Minnesota, but its pages could not be opened.

**Media.** The Twin Cities JACL publishes *The Rice Paper* several times a year, with an October 2026 issue online ([Twin Cities JACL](https://www.tcjacl.org/rice-paper/)). The Japan America Society publishes the monthly newsletter *Tsushin* ([United Way 211 listing](https://search.211unitedway.org/search/e42cceb3-ece1-544e-b76c-667b6d51e1ef)). No Minnesota Japanese-language newspaper, radio program or professional directory was found.

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

No survey opened gives the religious makeup of Japanese Minnesotans. What can be documented are the congregations people built.

**Twin Cities Buddhist Sangha.** A Jodo Shinshu (Shin) Buddhist community and member of the Buddhist Churches of America, it was founded in 1946 by families who came because of the Army language school. Its 80th-anniversary article dates its original constitution to April 14, 1946 ([Buddhist Churches of America, 2026](https://www.buddhistchurchesofamerica.org/post/twin-cities-buddhist-sangha-celebrates-its-80th-anniversary)); its own note in the JACL newsletter says 49 Nisei and Issei members signed it on July 5, 1946 ([Rice Paper, Feb. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Rice-Paper-2026-February-A8.pdf)). The sangha has never owned a building. It has met at a business college, Shir Tikva synagogue, a beauty shop, a car dealership, First Universalist Church, the Midtown YWCA and Becketwood, and now meets at Cha-Ami Japanese Cultural Center, using a portable butsudan (household altar) built by founding member Frank Tsuchiya Sr. About 70 members and friends marked the 80th anniversary on May 3, 2026, with the BCA bishop ([BCA](https://www.buddhistchurchesofamerica.org/post/twin-cities-buddhist-sangha-celebrates-its-80th-anniversary)). It meets twice a month, online and in person, under Rev. Chiemi Onikura-Bly, and holds a monthly Shotsuki Hoyo memorial service for members who died in that month of any year ([Rice Paper, Feb. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Rice-Paper-2026-February-A8.pdf)).

**Christian churches.** Christian churches were central to resettlement. The groups that sponsored the 1946 meeting founding the JACL chapter included a "United Christian Ministry to Japanese Americans" alongside the Buddhist group ([Rice Paper, Feb. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Rice-Paper-2026-February-A8.pdf)), and MNopedia says St. Paul churches raised money and offered jobs and housing ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/st-paul-resettlement-committee)). Today, an undated listing in a network of Japanese Christian ministries describes a Japanese-language congregation at Rose Hill Alliance Church, 2105 Roselawn Avenue West, Roseville, and a Japanese Fellowship Church at 4217 Bloomington Avenue in Minneapolis ([RJC Directory](https://directory.rjcnetwork.org/loc/minnesota)). Neither could be confirmed as meeting now.

**Zen.** The Minnesota Zen Meditation Center was founded in 1972 by Dainin Katagiri Roshi (1928 to 1990), a Soto Zen teacher invited from California; today's sangha, the center says, is made up mostly of "average, householding Americans" ([Minnesota Zen Meditation Center, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.mnzencenter.org/about-mzmc.html)).

**Shinto and none.** No Shinto shrine or Shinto group in Minnesota was found. Pew and Compass do not report religion for this group, and no Minnesota source was found on how many Japanese Minnesotans have no religion.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

These feed the calendar file. Dates below are what organizers announced; see calendar-entries.json for each source.

- **Obon.** The summer festival honoring ancestors, when, as the Como Park organizers describe it, families welcome ancestral spirits home and send them off again with lanterns. The **Como Park Japanese Obon Festival** at the Marjorie McNeely Conservatory is, in the Japan America Society's words, "the largest Japan-related festival in Minnesota." It ran Sunday, August 16, 2026, 3 to 9 p.m., with taiko, koto, shakuhachi, dance, martial arts, food and a lantern lighting at about 8 p.m. in the Japanese Garden and Frog Pond. Organizers have announced Sunday, August 15, 2027 ([Como Zoo and Conservatory, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://comozooconservatory.org/obon2026/)). It is produced by Como, the Japan America Society and the Saint Paul-Nagasaki Sister City Committee, and the society says Mu Performing Arts groups (the Mu-Min Chorus and Mu Daiko) and the Mikaharu-kai Dance Group have performed a "Minnesota Bon Odori" there each year ([JASM Programs](https://mn-japan.org/Programs)). The JACL chapter newsletter reported a turnout of 8,530 in 2026 ([Rice Paper, Oct. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TCJACL-Rice-Paper-10-2026.pdf)). Separately, the Twin Cities Buddhist Sangha's own Obon was listed for July 29, 2026 ([Rice Paper, Feb. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Rice-Paper-2026-February-A8.pdf)).
- **Day of Remembrance, February 19.** The date of Executive Order 9066. The Twin Cities JACL holds an event each year; in 2026, about 100 people attended a February 19 screening of *One Fighting Irishman*, about attorney Wayne M. Collins, followed by a panel ([Rice Paper, April 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Rice-Paper-2026-April-B.pdf); [JACL Weekly Digest, Feb. 17, 2026](https://jacl.org/weekly-digest/february-17-2026)). In 2025 it was held February 16 at the Minnesota History Center ([Rice Paper, Feb. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Rice-Paper-2026-February-A8.pdf)).
- **New Year (Shogatsu).** January 1 in Japan's calendar. The Japan America Society describes a Shinnenkai, its annual New Year celebration with performances, food and a raffle ([JASM Programs](https://mn-japan.org/Programs)); no 2026 or 2027 date was found. The JACL chapter's history recalls a mochi (rice cake) fundraiser that sold over 1,000 pounds ([Twin Cities JACL history](https://www.tcjacl.org/home/twin-cities-chapter-history/)).
- **Cherry trees in spring.** The Saint Paul-Nagasaki Sister City Committee's Cherry Tree Celebration was held Saturday, May 2, 2026, at the cherry grove near the Mannheimer Memorial in Como Park ([Saint Paul-Nagasaki Sister City Committee](https://www.stpaulnagasaki.org/); [Rice Paper, April 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Rice-Paper-2026-April-B.pdf)).
- **Memorial Day for Japanese American veterans.** On May 16, 2026, families and volunteers decorated the graves of 101 Minnesota Japanese American veterans at Fort Snelling National Cemetery and honored 35 more buried elsewhere ([Rice Paper, July 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Rice-Paper-2026-July-B.pdf)). In 2024 the ceremony drew Gen. Paul Nakasone and his father ([MPR News, May 16, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/05/16/minnesotans-honor-the-states-japanese-american-veterans)).
- **Hiroshima and Nagasaki commemoration, August 6 and 9.** Among its 2026 Hiroshima and Nagasaki commemoration events, the sister city committee lists a "Ceremony of the Cranes: Hiroshima Commemoration" on the 6th; its page also shows an older Obon date, so its listings may not all be current ([Saint Paul-Nagasaki Sister City Committee](https://www.stpaulnagasaki.org/)).
- **Heritage month and festivals.** May is Asian American, Native Hawaiian and Pacific Islander Heritage Month; the governor's 2025 proclamation notes it marks the arrival of the first recorded Japanese immigrant to the United States on May 7, 1843 ([Office of the Governor, April 30, 2025](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/05.01.25%20AANHPI%20Month_tcm1055-682510.pdf)). The JACL chapter co-sponsored the 4th annual Edina AAPI Festival on May 16, 2026, and staffed a booth at IFest, the revived Festival of Nations, at RiverCentre on April 10 and 11, 2026 ([Rice Paper, July 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Rice-Paper-2026-July-B.pdf); [Rice Paper, Oct. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TCJACL-Rice-Paper-10-2026.pdf)).
- **Community anniversaries in 2026.** Normandale's Japanese Garden marked 50 years with a free festival on September 19, 2026 ([City of Bloomington](https://www.bloomingtonmn.gov/node/19423)). The traveling Ireichō, a book listing more than 125,000 people of Japanese ancestry incarcerated in the war, was at Historic Fort Snelling October 9 to 11, 2026 ([Pioneer Press, Oct. 9, 2026](https://www.twincities.com/2026/10/09/it-ruined-so-many-peoples-lives-book-recognizes-people-of-japanese-descent-incarcerated-during-wwii/)). The JACL chapter's Chrysanthemum Banquet for its 80th year was October 24, 2026, with guest Tamlyn Tomita ([Rice Paper, Oct. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TCJACL-Rice-Paper-10-2026.pdf)).

No Minnesota public celebration was found for Hinamatsuri (Girls' Day, March 3), Kodomo no Hi (Children's Day, May 5), Tanabata (July 7), Setsubun, or Fred Korematsu Day (January 30). Families may mark these at home or in the Saturday schools; no opened source says so.

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Households.** Compass shows small households (2.19 people on average). Married couples without children under 18 are 28.6 percent of households, married couples with children 14.1 percent, unmarried couples 7.4 percent, and people living alone 37.0 percent ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/japanese)). No opened source explains the large share living alone.

**Families and incarceration memory.** Sally Sudo's account gives one family's daily life in camp: barracks with "only one light bulb," no running water, sand blowing in, and snakes under the floor, and older siblings whose college plans were cut off ([Pioneer Press, Oct. 9, 2026](https://www.twincities.com/2026/10/09/it-ruined-so-many-peoples-lives-book-recognizes-people-of-japanese-descent-incarcerated-during-wwii/)). Her story is one person's, not the community's.

**Gatherings.** The JACL summer picnic at Rosland Park in Edina (August 2, 2026), golf tournaments and the Chrysanthemum Banquet are long-running social events ([Rice Paper, July 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Rice-Paper-2026-July-B.pdf); [Twin Cities JACL history](https://www.tcjacl.org/home/twin-cities-chapter-history/)). Scholarship essays in the July 2026 newsletter describe the picnic as a yearly event that brings generations together ([Rice Paper, July 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Rice-Paper-2026-July-B.pdf)). A United Way 211 listing describes a volunteer group, a Nihonjinkai, that supports Japanese individuals and families in Minnesota, especially elders, with monthly meals and a Japanese-language library ([United Way 211](https://search.211unitedway.org/search/21591521-8b24-5557-8d0a-7618958ab114)).

**Heritage at home.** In July 2026, JACL board member Seiji Ikeda taught two workshops on kamon, Japanese family crests, including one on designing a personal crest ([Rice Paper, Oct. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TCJACL-Rice-Paper-10-2026.pdf)).

**Food.** United Noodles stocks rice, miso and curry for home cooking ([United Noodles](https://www.unitednoodles.com/cuisine/japanese/)); Obon and the society's events sell Japanese food. No Minnesota source on everyday family food, dress, marriage or child-rearing practice was found, and this profile does not generalize from Japan.

**Aging.** Cha-Ami Japanese Cultural Center runs aging services "for Japanese-American seniors and their caregivers": volunteer grocery delivery, chores and visits, and classes on health conditions, medical directives and housing ([Cha-Ami Aging Services, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.cha-amijcc.org/aging-services-about)). Section 11 has the state grant behind it.

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Writers.** David Mura, a Sansei poet and memoirist who settled in the Twin Cities, won Minnesota Book Awards in 1991 and again in 1997, the second for *Where the Body Meets Memory*, and has long taught at the Loft Literary Center ([Minnesota Writers on the Map, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://mnwritersmap.org/david-mura/)). Ka F. Wong's *Enmity and Empathy: Japanese Americans in Minnesota During World War II* (MNHS Press, 2025) won the 2026 Minnesota Book Award for Minnesota nonfiction ([MNHS Press newsletter, May 19, 2026](https://www.mnhs.org/mnhs-press-newsletter/may-19-2026)). St. Olaf's digital project *Beyond the Barbed Wire* holds oral histories of Japanese Minnesotans ([St. Olaf](https://pages.stolaf.edu/jam/resettlement-in-minnesota/)).

**Taiko and theater.** TaikoArts Midwest was founded as Mu Daiko in 1997 by playwright Rick Shiomi and became independent under artistic director Jennifer Weir in 2017, when it took over the taiko program Theater Mu had supported ([COMPAS, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://compas.org/artist/taikoarts-midwest/)). Its groups include Enso Daiko, Big Gravity, Edo Bayashi Ensemble and the Mighty Drumming Club; its benefit concert, Taiko Toshi, was October 29, 2026 ([Rice Paper, Oct. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TCJACL-Rice-Paper-10-2026.pdf)). Theater Mu, an Asian American theater with a 2026–27 season, co-presented *Swingposium*, about music and morale in the camps, with TaikoArts Midwest at CSPS Hall in St. Paul, October 16 to 18, 2026 ([Theater Mu](https://www.theatermu.org/); [Rice Paper, Oct. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TCJACL-Rice-Paper-10-2026.pdf)). Shiomi's play *Secret Warriors*, about the Army language school, opened at the History Theatre on March 29, 2025, with JACL members reviewing drafts ([Rice Paper, Feb. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Rice-Paper-2026-February-A8.pdf)). Harisen Daiko and a Japanese dance group listed as "Sonsei Yonsei Kai" performed at the Normandale garden's 50th ([City of Bloomington](https://www.bloomingtonmn.gov/node/19423)).

**Visual and craft arts.** Northfield ceramic artist Juliane Shibata led *Taking Shape*, a 2026 public installation of more than a thousand clay cherry-blossom pieces made by over 45 people, with the Japan America Society, shown at Normandale in September 2026 ([Rice Paper, Oct. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TCJACL-Rice-Paper-10-2026.pdf)). Ikebana International Chapter 121 is the state's flower-arranging chapter ([City of Bloomington](https://www.bloomingtonmn.gov/node/19423)). The Minneapolis Institute of Art's Japanese collection could not be opened for this profile.

**Gardens.** The Charlotte Partridge Ordway Japanese Garden at Como Park was a gift from Nagasaki, funded by the Ordway family and designed by Nagasaki landscape architect Masami Matsuda; it opened in 1979 ([The Cultural Landscape Foundation](https://www.tclf.org/landscapes/charlotte-partridge-ordway-japanese-garden)). The Normandale Japanese Garden in Bloomington was built from 1972 to 1976 with guidance from Tokyo garden architect Takao Watanabe ([City of Bloomington](https://www.bloomingtonmn.gov/node/19423)); MIS veterans paid for its Bentendo Pavilion and Taikobashi Bridge in the early 1970s ([Normandale Community College](https://www.normandale.edu/why-normandale/community/japanese-garden/jg-history.html)).

**Sports.** Minneapolis native Chuck Aoki is a four-time Paralympian in wheelchair rugby ([MPR News, July 31, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/07/31/four-time-paralympian-chuck-aoki-strives-to-make-athletes-with-disabilities-more-visible)) and is profiled in San Francisco State University's Asian Pacific Islander American biography project ([SFSU](https://apiabiography.sfsu.edu/chuck-aoki)). The JACL newsletter reported that the U.S. team won the 2026 world championship in São Paulo on August 23, with his father, Augsburg professor and chapter member Andy Aoki, there to watch ([Rice Paper, Oct. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TCJACL-Rice-Paper-10-2026.pdf)). Japanese martial arts appear at Obon ([Como](https://comozooconservatory.org/obon2026/)), but no Minnesota dojo directory was opened.

**Museums and history sites.** Historic Fort Snelling's Plank Museum and Visitor Center is a restored barracks that housed the language school ([Pioneer Press, Oct. 9, 2026](https://www.twincities.com/2026/10/09/it-ruined-so-many-peoples-lives-book-recognizes-people-of-japanese-descent-incarcerated-during-wwii/)). A historical marker stands at the Camp Savage site on the Highway 13 frontage road at Xenwood Avenue ([Scott County Historical Society](https://www.scottcountyhistory.org/blog/camp-savage-ww-ii-asset-for-victory-in-the-pacific)).

**Popular culture.** Anime and Japanese pop culture draw large Minnesota audiences beyond the Japanese community, but no Minnesota source tying them to Japanese Minnesotans was opened.

## 9. Civic and political life

**Elected officials.** Rep. Samantha Sencer-Mura (DFL, Minneapolis, House District 63A) is, per the Legislative Reference Library citing Sahan Journal, the first Japanese American legislator in Minnesota. First elected in 2022 and re-elected in 2024, she serves on the Children and Families, Education Finance, and Transportation committees; her current term runs to January 3, 2027. The library notes that her grandparents spent their teenage years in the wartime camps ([Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/legdb/fulldetail?id=15572)). In 2016 the JACL newsletter described Susan Burke, then a new chapter board member, as a Hennepin County district judge since 2015 and "the first and only Japanese American judge" in state history ([Rice Paper, Feb. 2016](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2017/04/TCJACL_Rice_Paper.2016-02.pdf)); her current status could not be confirmed.

**The Twin Cities JACL.** The chapter was organized at an open meeting at the St. Paul International Institute on September 20, 1946, with about 200 people, according to a *Northwest Nisei* article reprinted in 2026; sponsors included the resettlement committee, YWCA and YMCA groups, a Christian ministry and the Buddhist group ([Rice Paper, Feb. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Rice-Paper-2026-February-A8.pdf)). It first took the name United Citizens League and later joined the national JACL ([Twin Cities JACL history](https://www.tcjacl.org/home/twin-cities-chapter-history/); [St. Olaf](https://pages.stolaf.edu/jam/resettlement-in-minnesota/)). In 2003 its education committee pressed the state to include the wartime incarceration in Minnesota's K-12 social studies standards, which in their first draft had no such standard, and the draft was changed ([Pacific Citizen, 2017](https://www.pacificcitizen.org/a-new-beginning-for-japanese-american-activism-in-minnesota/)). In 2025 its speakers gave 27 presentations to almost 1,200 people ([Rice Paper, Feb. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Rice-Paper-2026-February-A8.pdf)). It reported assets of about $310,000 at the end of 2025 (same source).

**Solidarity and debate.** The chapter has tied its history to other groups' experiences. In 2017, Sansei activist Gordon Nakagawa drafted a statement of solidarity with CAIR-Minnesota executive director Jaylani Hussein ([Pacific Citizen, 2017](https://www.pacificcitizen.org/a-new-beginning-for-japanese-american-activism-in-minnesota/)). During the federal immigration enforcement operation in the Twin Cities in early 2026, chapter president Vini Taguchi wrote that the community had "witnessed a level of violence and abuse that rings of decades past," and the chapter partnered with Asian Media Access on a know-your-rights webinar ([Rice Paper, Feb. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Rice-Paper-2026-February-A8.pdf)). In April he wrote that "Operation Metro Surge" had officially ended but that ICE remained "a persistent threat" ([Rice Paper, April 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Rice-Paper-2026-April-B.pdf)). The chapter co-sponsored a resolution, adopted at the July 2026 national JACL convention, "in Support of Immigrant Communities Targeted by ICE" ([Rice Paper, Oct. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TCJACL-Rice-Paper-10-2026.pdf)). These are the chapter leadership's words; no survey of Japanese Minnesotans' political views was found, and not every Japanese Minnesotan belongs to the JACL.

**Friendship and diplomacy.** The Saint Paul-Nagasaki Sister City Committee calls itself the first Japanese-American city partnership, commissioned in 1955, with early programs that helped Nagasaki recover from the atomic bombing ([Saint Paul-Nagasaki Sister City Committee](https://www.stpaulnagasaki.org/)). Nagasaki's mayor led a delegation to St. Paul in August 2025 for the 70th anniversary ([Pioneer Press via Yahoo, Aug. 22, 2025](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/nagasaki-group-st-paul-visit-230300018.html)). The Japan America Society names its awards for Walter Mondale, its honorary chair and former ambassador to Japan ([JASM Programs](https://mn-japan.org/Programs)). Its 2026 award honors Ben van Lierop, a former executive director who received Japan's Order of the Rising Sun in 2022 ([Japan America Society of Minnesota](https://mn-japan.org/)). Many leaders of these friendship groups are not of Japanese descent.

**Public history.** On October 16, 2026, the National Park Service named Savage, Minnesota's 2026 American World War II Heritage City, citing Camp Savage's Japanese American language specialists ([National Park Service, Oct. 16, 2026](https://www.nps.gov/orgs/1207/nps-designates-new-wwii-heritage-cities.htm)).

## 10. Education

**Educational attainment.** Among adults 25 and older, 97.1 percent finished high school and 48.1 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/japanese)).

**Saturday schools.** Two schools teach in Japanese on Saturdays, designed in part to keep children on pace with schools in Japan:
- **Minnesota Japanese School**, since 1978, kindergarten through middle school, accredited and supported by Japan's Ministry of Education. All classes are in Japanese; it admits children of any nationality with age-appropriate fluency ([Minnesota Japanese School, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.mnjs.org/english)).
- **Minneapolis Japanese School**, 2051 Larpenteur Avenue East, St. Paul, kindergarten through grade 12, Saturdays 8:40 a.m. to 12:50 p.m., following Japan's national course of study, mainly in Japanese and math ([Minneapolis Japanese School](https://www.minneapolisjapaneseschool.org/english)). Its founder and principal, Takuzo Ishida, who died in 2019, is honored with a Japan America Society scholarship ([JASM](https://mn-japan.org/page-1075226)).

**Learning Japanese.** Concordia Language Villages runs Mori no Ike, its Japanese-language summer village, held in 2026 at a Turtle River Lake site and at Camp Potmos in Hackensack, with one- to four-week sessions including high school credit, need-based scholarships and a state stipend for low-income students ([Concordia Language Villages](https://www.concordialanguagevillages.org/languages/japanese/)). The Japan America Society runs J-Quiz, an online Japan quiz each February, and "Japan in a Suitcase" for schools ([JASM Programs](https://mn-japan.org/Programs); [United Way 211](https://search.211unitedway.org/search/e42cceb3-ece1-544e-b76c-667b6d51e1ef)). No Minnesota public school Japanese immersion program was found, and statewide enrollment in Japanese classes was not found.

**Scholarships.** The JACL chapter awarded $12,500 to five 2025 high school graduates ([Rice Paper, April 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Rice-Paper-2026-April-B.pdf)). The Japan America Society's Mondale Scholarship gives $2,000 to Minnesota college students to study in Japan ([JASM Programs](https://mn-japan.org/Programs)).

**Scholars and history in schools.** St. Olaf's Ka F. Wong and his students have recorded Japanese Minnesotans' oral histories in a project that began as a student-faculty collaboration with two students of the class of 2017 ([St. Olaf, June 25, 2025](https://wp.stolaf.edu/news/enmity-and-empathy-professors-new-book-explores-japanese-american-history-in-minnesota-following-wwii)). At the University of Minnesota, Yuichiro Onishi and Jon Matsunaga taught a 2026 course, "Community Building for Justice: Twin Cities Japanese American Activism," documenting the chapter's history ([Rice Paper, April 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Rice-Paper-2026-April-B.pdf)). The chapter offers a Military Intelligence Service curriculum guide for teachers ([Twin Cities JACL](https://www.tcjacl.org/)). Historically, Macalester, Hamline, St. Thomas and Bethel enrolled Japanese American students released from the camps ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/st-paul-resettlement-committee)).

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Coverage.** Only 1.2 percent of Japanese Minnesotans lack health insurance (margin 0.7 points) ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/japanese)).

**Culturally specific elder support.** The Minnesota Department of Human Services awarded Cha-Ami Japanese Cultural Center $52,000 in state fiscal year 2025 through its Live Well at Home program to "address the cultural, social, and health needs of Japanese immigrants and their descendants," with wellness workshops, caregiver support groups and social events ([Minnesota DHS, SFY2025 grantees](https://mn.gov/dhs/assets/LWAH%20SFY25%20Grantees_accessible_tcm1053-716193.pdf)). Cha-Ami lists volunteer grocery delivery, chore help and social visits, plus classes on medical directives and long-term housing ([Cha-Ami](https://www.cha-amijcc.org/aging-services-about)). The DHS list places Cha-Ami in St. Paul while its website gives a Champlin mailing address; its program location was not confirmed.

**Providers.** No Japanese-language clinic, Japanese-speaking provider directory, or Minnesota study of Japanese Minnesotans' health was found. Japanese American physicians are part of the community, such as Dr. Karen Tanaka Lucas ([JASM](https://mn-japan.org/)), but that does not tell us whether Japanese-speaking patients can find care in Japanese.

**Memory and wellbeing.** Sally Sudo told the Pioneer Press that some older people in the Minidoka camp "took their own life out of despair," and that stamping her family's names in the Ireichō felt like being "validated" ([Pioneer Press, Oct. 9, 2026](https://www.twincities.com/2026/10/09/it-ruined-so-many-peoples-lives-book-recognizes-people-of-japanese-descent-incarcerated-during-wwii/)). Densho's 2026 "Living History Project" invited Minnesotans to record how memories of incarceration shape their responses to current events ([Rice Paper, Oct. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TCJACL-Rice-Paper-10-2026.pdf)). No Minnesota study of intergenerational effects was found.

**Traditional practice.** No Minnesota source on kampo (Japanese herbal medicine), reiki, shiatsu or similar practice among Japanese Minnesotans was found.

## 12. Disability

**Minnesota numbers.** Compass reports that 8.3 percent of Japanese Minnesotans have a disability (give or take 3.0 points), and 7.4 percent of those under 65 (give or take 3.2) ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/japanese)). With so wide a margin, the figure cannot show whether this group's rate differs from the state's. No state agency report breaking out Japanese Minnesotans in disability services, special education or autism monitoring was found.

**A public voice.** Chuck Aoki, who grew up in Minneapolis, has said publicly that he was diagnosed at the Mayo Clinic at about age six with hereditary sensory autonomic neuropathy type II, a rare genetic condition, and became a wheelchair user at 12. He credits Courage Kenny Rehabilitation Institute in Golden Valley, where he found wheelchair basketball and rugby, and he has worked to make athletes with disabilities "more visible" ([MPR News, July 31, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/07/31/four-time-paralympian-chuck-aoki-strives-to-make-athletes-with-disabilities-more-visible)). His story is his own and does not describe a community.

**Scholarship.** Selena Moon, whose scholar page describes her as a PhD student in American Studies at the University of Minnesota, researches Japanese American disability history, including how incarceration, eugenics, immigration and marriage restrictions, and institutions affected disabled Japanese Americans, and has published on Deaf and blind Japanese Americans ([Women Also Know History, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://womenalsoknowhistory.com/individual-scholar-page/?pdb=77)).

**Services and questions.** No Japanese-specific disability organization in Minnesota was found. Cha-Ami's aging services are the closest culturally specific support (section 11). The open questions are real ones, asked here without answers:
- Who in a family names a disability, and in which language? A parent who reads Japanese may meet an English-only assessment or IEP; Compass shows 8.8 percent of people 5 and older speak English less than "very well."
- Does a label open a door (services, Saturday school accommodations, return to schools in Japan) or close one (immigration, work visas, family expectations)? No opened source answers this for Minnesota.
- How do memories of wartime incarceration shape trust in government programs, including disability programs? The JACL chapter's writing in 2026 ties that memory to present events (section 9), but no study links it to service use.
- What produced an impairment, in any given case, is a question for that person and their clinicians, not for a profile of a people.

## 13. What is changing now

**Elders and memory, 2025 to 2026.** St. Olaf's Wong said in 2025 that "time is our greatest adversary," as several people his students interviewed have since died ([St. Olaf, June 25, 2025](https://wp.stolaf.edu/news/enmity-and-empathy-professors-new-book-explores-japanese-american-history-in-minnesota-following-wwii)). In October 2026, the Ireichō visited Historic Fort Snelling and Densho scanned family photographs and recorded interviews ([Pioneer Press, Oct. 9, 2026](https://www.twincities.com/2026/10/09/it-ruined-so-many-peoples-lives-book-recognizes-people-of-japanese-descent-incarcerated-during-wwii/); [Rice Paper, Oct. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TCJACL-Rice-Paper-10-2026.pdf)). The Twin Cities Buddhist Sangha and the JACL chapter both turned 80 in 2026. A traveling exhibit on Nisei soldiers, *I Am American*, is tentatively scheduled for the Minnesota History Center from September 2029 to March 2030 ([Rice Paper, Feb. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Rice-Paper-2026-February-A8.pdf)).

**Immigration enforcement, early 2026.** The JACL chapter's newsletters describe the federal immigration operation in the Twin Cities in early 2026 as a "siege" and say over 3,000 neighbors remained missing in April; these are the chapter's own statements, not independent counts ([Rice Paper, Feb. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Rice-Paper-2026-February-A8.pdf); [Rice Paper, April 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Rice-Paper-2026-April-B.pdf)). The October 2026 newsletter says festival vendors at IFest had not "fully recovered from the Pandemic and ICE" ([Rice Paper, Oct. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TCJACL-Rice-Paper-10-2026.pdf)). No source reports enforcement actions against Japanese nationals in Minnesota.

**Generational and organizational change.** The October 2026 newsletter reports that the new national JACL executive director spoke of financial lows and the need to "revitalize as a grassroots organization," and a chapter delegate wrote that the 2026 national convention was the first time the writer had met so many Japanese Americans ([Rice Paper, Oct. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/TCJACL-Rice-Paper-10-2026.pdf)). The chapter recruited a new cohort for its "Community Kai" program in 2026 ([Rice Paper, July 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Rice-Paper-2026-July-B.pdf)).

**Business and trade.** Exports to Japan rose in late 2025 even as state exports fell ([Minnesota DEED](https://mn.gov/deed/assets/2025-quarter4-exports_tcm1045-752085.docx)). In Minneapolis, the Kado no Mise group added Cafe Yoto in 2025 ([Racket, March 24, 2025](https://racketmn.com/inside-cafe-yoto-the-north-loops-hot-new-japanese-fast-casual-spot)).

**Data change.** Compass's 2020 to 2024 profile shows a slightly larger population, a bigger Greater Minnesota share and higher median income than the 2019 to 2023 release. With margins this wide, these are not reliable signs of real change.

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **A community built twice, by choice and by force.** People released from camps built a Buddhist sangha, a JACL chapter, churches and businesses in a state where they had almost no prior roots ([BCA](https://www.buddhistchurchesofamerica.org/post/twin-cities-buddhist-sangha-celebrates-its-80th-anniversary); [MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/st-paul-resettlement-committee)).
- **Service.** More than six thousand Army linguists trained in Minnesota ([MNopedia, MISLS](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/military-intelligence-service-language-school-misls)); 101 Japanese American veterans are buried at Fort Snelling National Cemetery ([Rice Paper, July 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Rice-Paper-2026-July-B.pdf)).
- **Teaching history to everyone.** Speakers like Sally Sudo, the JACL education committee and St. Olaf's oral history work bring the incarceration into Minnesota classrooms and standards ([Pacific Citizen, 2017](https://www.pacificcitizen.org/a-new-beginning-for-japanese-american-activism-in-minnesota/); [Rice Paper, Feb. 2026](https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Rice-Paper-2026-February-A8.pdf)).
- **Solidarity.** Long work alongside Muslim and immigrant Minnesotans (section 9).
- **Public gifts.** Two Japanese gardens, the state's largest Japan-related festival, and a sister city partnership its committee calls the first between a Japanese and an American city ([TCLF](https://www.tclf.org/landscapes/charlotte-partridge-ordway-japanese-garden); [Normandale](https://www.normandale.edu/why-normandale/community/japanese-garden/jg-history.html); [JASM Programs](https://mn-japan.org/Programs); [SPNSCC](https://www.stpaulnagasaki.org/)).
- **Food and the arts.** From Reiko Weston's Fuji-Ya to Kado no Mise, from Mu Daiko to TaikoArts Midwest, and writers such as David Mura and Ka F. Wong.
- **Trade and investment.** Japanese companies and exports to Japan support Minnesota jobs ([KSTP](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/local-news/japanese-company-completes-investment-project-in-brooklyn-park/); [Minnesota DEED](https://mn.gov/deed/assets/2025-quarter4-exports_tcm1045-752085.docx)).
