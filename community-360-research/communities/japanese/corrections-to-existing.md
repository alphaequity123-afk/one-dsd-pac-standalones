# Corrections to the existing Japanese Minnesota page

Page: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/japanese

## Errors or claims that should change

1. **Claim:** "Twin Cities JACL Discrimination Helpline," described as a helpline "listed by the Twin Cities chapter" (Access routes) and "Its website also lists a discrimination helpline" (Institutions).
   **Source says:** The chapter's helpline page reposts the State of Minnesota's Discrimination Helpline, launched April 6, 2020, by the governor and lieutenant governor and run by the Minnesota Department of Human Rights (1-833-454-0148, weekdays 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.), with talking points from the Coalition of Asian American Leaders. It is not a JACL-run helpline. Name it as the state's MDHR helpline.
   **Link:** https://www.tcjacl.org/discrimination-helpline/ .
2. **Claim:** John Aiso card: "More than 6,000 graduates by 1945."
   **Source says:** The National Park Service says over 6,000 had graduated "when the facility closed in 1946"; MNopedia says the school graduated over six thousand linguists in total, with its last Minnesota class in June 1946. "By 1945" is not supported.
   **Links:** https://home.nps.gov/miss/learn/historyculture/langschool.htm ; https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/military-intelligence-service-language-school-misls .
3. **Claim:** Edwin "Bud" Nakasone "arrived at Fort Snelling on December 25, 1945."
   **Source says:** MPR News (May 16, 2024) says he moved from Hawaii to Minnesota "on Christmas Eve, 1947." The page's date comes from his own account in Discover Nikkei, which was not reopened. Sources disagree; give both or attribute the date to his account.
   **Link:** https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/05/16/minnesotans-honor-the-states-japanese-american-veterans .
4. **Claim:** Twin Cities JACL "had begun forming in 1946," with the "first business meeting" on April 30, 1948.
   **Source says:** Not wrong, but incomplete. A *Northwest Nisei* article of Sept. 28, 1946, reprinted in the February 2026 Rice Paper, says the chapter was "officially organized at an open meeting on Sept. 20 [1946] at the St. Paul International Institute," with about 200 people. Add the founding date, which is the basis for the 2026 80th anniversary.
   **Link:** https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Rice-Paper-2026-February-A8.pdf .
5. **Claim:** Saint Paul-Nagasaki Sister City Committee: "Its site lists a 2026 calendar, a Japanese Obon festival..."
   **Source says:** The committee's site lists "Sunday, August 17 Obon Festival at Como Park," which was the 2025 date. The 2026 festival was Sunday, Aug. 16, per Como Zoo and Conservatory, which has also announced Aug. 15, 2027. Use Como's page for the date.
   **Links:** https://www.stpaulnagasaki.org/ ; https://comozooconservatory.org/obon2026/ .
6. **Claim:** Cha-Ami Japanese Cultural Center is at "Saint Anthony, 2855 Anthony Lane South."
   **Source says:** Could not confirm. Cha-Ami's own website title gives a P.O. Box in Champlin; the DHS Live Well at Home grantee list places it in St. Paul. Recheck the address before publishing it.
   **Links:** https://www.cha-amijcc.org/ ; https://mn.gov/dhs/assets/LWAH%20SFY25%20Grantees_accessible_tcm1053-716193.pdf .
7. **Claim:** Twin Cities Buddhist Sangha "founded in 1946."
   **Source says:** Correct, but the founding date differs between sources: the BCA article dates the original constitution to April 14, 1946; the sangha's own note in the Rice Paper says 49 Nisei and Issei members signed it on July 5, 1946. If a day is given, give both.
   **Links:** https://www.buddhistchurchesofamerica.org/post/twin-cities-buddhist-sangha-celebrates-its-80th-anniversary ; https://www.tcjacl.org/wp/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Rice-Paper-2026-February-A8.pdf .
## Outdated

8. **Claim:** All Compass figures labeled ACS 2019 to 2023: about 13,391 people; 76.8 percent Twin Cities and 23.2 percent Greater Minnesota; 31.8 percent 17 or younger; 8.6 percent 65+; 17.3 percent foreign-born; 71.3 percent English only; 28.7 percent other language; 8.6 percent English less than "very well"; 97.0 percent high school; 51.0 percent bachelor's; median household income about $67,817 (2022 dollars); 5.1 percent poverty; 60.3 percent owner-occupied; household size 2.13; 37.9 percent living alone; 36.3 percent cost-burdened.
   **Source says:** Compass now reports 2020 to 2024: 13,457 (plus or minus 1,823); 74.1 percent Twin Cities and 25.9 percent Greater Minnesota; 29.6 percent 17 or younger; 10.7 percent 65+; 16.9 percent foreign-born; 73.1 percent English only; 26.9 percent other language; 8.8 percent English less than "very well"; 97.1 percent high school; 48.1 percent bachelor's; median household income $82,258 (2024 dollars, plus or minus $19,418); 6.5 percent poverty; 63.3 percent owner-occupied; household size 2.19; 37.0 percent living alone; 35.5 percent cost-burdened (62.4 percent of renters). Compass also has a disability row (8.3 percent, 7.4 percent under 65) and an uninsured rate (1.2 percent) the page does not use. Margins are wide; the changes should not be read as real trends. Every sentence on the page that repeats these numbers (23.2 percent, 17.3 percent, 8.6 percent LEP, 5.1 percent poverty, 36.3 percent, $67,817, 97.0 and 51.0 percent) needs updating.
   **Link:** https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/japanese .
9. **Claim:** Cha-Ami "received a $25,000 Minnesota Arts and Cultural Heritage grant for 2024 and 2025" and aging services "partly funded by the Minnesota DHS Live Well at Home program."
   **Source says:** The DHS SFY2025 grantee list shows a $52,000 Live Well at Home award to Cha-Ami. The $25,000 Legacy grant could not be checked (the Legacy site blocked access). Add the DHS amount.
   **Link:** https://mn.gov/dhs/assets/LWAH%20SFY25%20Grantees_accessible_tcm1053-716193.pdf .
10. **Claim:** Page has no mention of 2026 milestones.
    **Source says:** Consider adding the National Park Service's Oct. 16, 2026, designation of Savage as Minnesota's American World War II Heritage City; the Ireichō at Historic Fort Snelling, Oct. 9 to 11, 2026; and the 2026 Minnesota Book Award won by Ka F. Wong's *Enmity and Empathy*.
    **Links:** https://www.nps.gov/orgs/1207/nps-designates-new-wwii-heritage-cities.htm ; https://www.twincities.com/2026/10/09/it-ruined-so-many-peoples-lives-book-recognizes-people-of-japanese-descent-incarcerated-during-wwii/ ; https://www.mnhs.org/mnhs-press-newsletter/may-19-2026 .

## Checked and found consistent

- Executive Order 9066 signed February 19, 1942 (MNopedia, Ruth Tanbara).
- Language school at Camp Savage from June 1942 with 18 instructors and 200 students, moved to Fort Snelling August 1944, left in June 1946 (NPS; MNopedia).
- Ruth and Earl Tanbara arrived in St. Paul in August 1942, first to resettle there under the order (MNopedia).
- Sally Sudo: born in Seattle, held at Puyallup and Minidoka, brother served in the MIS and urged the move to Minnesota (Pioneer Press, Oct. 9, 2026).
- The state representative for House District 63A: born Feb. 2, 1989; first Japanese American legislator in Minnesota; "fourth-generation Japanese American" (Legislative Reference Library).
- Japan America Society of Minnesota founded 1972; Mondale Gala Nov. 14, 2026; more than 100 Japanese corporate subsidiaries; two Saturday schools (society website).
- Minneapolis Japanese School at 2051 Larpenteur Avenue East, St. Paul; Normandale garden 50th anniversary Sept. 19, 2026; Twin Cities Buddhist Sangha 80th anniversary with nearly 70 people; Chrysanthemum Banquet in October 2026.
