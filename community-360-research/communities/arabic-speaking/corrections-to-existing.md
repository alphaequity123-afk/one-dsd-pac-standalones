# Corrections to the existing Arabic-speaking Minnesota page

Page: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/arabic-speaking
Checked: October 31, 2026. Each item gives the claim, what the opened source says, the link and the date checked.

## Errors, outdated claims or claims that should change

1. **Claim:** Minnesota Compass (ACS 2019-2023) reports Lebanese Minnesotans at "about 6,959," 81.0 percent Twin Cities and 19.0 percent Greater Minnesota; 20.3 percent 17 or younger, 16.4 percent 65+, foreign-born 8.5 percent, language other than English 11.8 percent; median household income about $73,000 (2022 dollars); poverty 12.0 percent; 74.5 percent owner-occupied; household size 2.14; 97.9 percent high school or higher and 50.5 percent bachelor's or higher.
   **Source says:** Compass has moved to 2020-2024 data. It now shows 7,232 (give or take 1,225) in the pooled estimate and 10,066 (give or take 3,576) as the 2024 single-year total, which Compass calls the most accurate measure of size; 76.7 percent Twin Cities and 23.3 percent Greater Minnesota; 24.2 percent 17 or younger; 17.0 percent 65+; foreign-born 9.2 percent; language other than English 13.9 percent; median household income $72,873 (2024 dollars); poverty 8.8 percent; 68.6 percent owner-occupied; household size 2.12; 97.8 percent high school or higher; 50.9 percent bachelor's or higher. Every figure on the page should be updated and re-dated, and margins of error should be shown, since several are wide (income give or take $21,005; poverty give or take 5.4 points).
   **Link:** https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lebanese . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

2. **Claim:** "There is no dated total 'Arab Minnesota' population on this page," and the only people-count offered is the Lebanese Compass figure.
   **Source says:** Dated, sourced counts exist and could be given with their limits. ACS 2020-2024: 26,405 (give or take 2,573) report an Arab ancestry, plus 3,017 (give or take 809) reporting Sudanese ancestry, which the Census lists outside its Arab group; 15,208 (give or take 1,931) speak Arabic at home, 4,869 of them English less than "very well." The page's caution that ancestry, language and birthplace each catch different people remains correct.
   **Links:** https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B04006&geo_ids=04000US27 ; https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B16001&geo_ids=04000US27 . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

3. **Claim:** Star Tribune (July 31, 2025, citing ACS 2017-2021): Arabic is "about 2 percent" of Minnesotans who speak a language other than English at home.
   **Source says:** The Star Tribune article could not be opened (site blocked). A newer direct figure is available: ACS 2020-2024 counts 15,208 Arabic speakers age 5 and older. The Minnesota Department of Education also reports Arabic as the fifth most common home language of English learners in 2025 (1,664 English learners; 3,363 students in all). Suggest replacing the 2017-2021 share with these figures.
   **Links:** https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B16001&geo_ids=04000US27 ; https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/other/261381.pdf . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

4. **Claim:** "Do not assume Minneapolis" and the page's general description of where households live, without naming places.
   **Source says:** Not an error, but the page can now be specific: Anoka County has the most Arabic speakers (4,144, give or take 1,064), ahead of Hennepin (3,794), Olmsted (2,021) and Ramsey (1,605); Blaine and Rochester each have more estimated Arabic speakers than Minneapolis, though margins are wide. Anoka County schools also have the most Arabic-speaking English learners (506 in 2025).
   **Links:** https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=C16001&geo_ids=050|04000US27 ; https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/other/261381.pdf . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

5. **Claim:** "Iraqi arrival followed 1991 and 2003, and Syrian arrival followed 2011."
   **Source says:** Partly supported. State health data show Iraqi primary refugee arrivals to Minnesota were small before 2008 and peaked in 2010 (234), totaling 1,502 from 1999 to 2024, with Anoka, Olmsted and Ramsey counties receiving the most. For Syria, Minnesota numbers are small: State Department data cited by MPR News show 20 Syrian refugees arriving in Minnesota in 2016, and the state health data group Syria under "Other." Suggest giving the Iraqi timing as mainly 2008 to 2016 and noting that Syrian arrival to Minnesota after 2011 was small.
   **Links:** https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/country.csv ; https://www.mprnews.org/story/2017/02/01/who-are-the-minnesota-refugees-explainer . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

6. **Claim:** Mizna "has a film screening set for November 2, 2026, at The Main Cinema."
   **Source says:** Confirmed: Palestine Cinema Days 2026, *Return to Haifa*, Nov. 2, 2026, 7 p.m., The Main Cinema. The page could add that the 2026 (20th) Twin Cities Arab Film Festival ran Sept. 23-27, 2026.
   **Links:** https://mizna.org/event/palestine-cinema-days-2026-return-to-haifa/ ; https://mizna.org/event/2026-tcaff/ . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

7. **Claim:** The Arab American Institute's demographics page "lists Minnesota among the top 12 states and Minneapolis among the top six metro areas."
   **Source says:** Confirmed. The same page also lists Virginia twice in its list of twelve states, so the list should be quoted with care.
   **Link:** https://www.aaiusa.org/demographics . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

8. **Claim:** "We did not find a Minnesota organization focused on disability among Arabic-speaking families."
   **Source says:** Still true as of this check; none was found. The page could add the one Minnesota disability figure for any Arab group: Compass reports 13.0 percent of Lebanese Minnesotans have a disability (give or take 4.3 points), with the caution that this older, mostly U.S.-born group does not represent recent arrivals.
   **Link:** https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lebanese . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

9. **Claim:** "There is no Cedar-Riverside of Arabic Minnesota."
   **Source says:** Broadly fair, but Central Avenue in Northeast Minneapolis and Columbia Heights functions as a commercial corridor for Palestinian, Yemeni and other Arab businesses, anchored by Holy Land since the 1980s.
   **Links:** https://heavytable.substack.com/p/palestinian-bakeries-and-central ; https://tcbmag.com/hummus-for-the-masses/ . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

10. **Claim:** The page lists Maronite, Antiochian and other Orthodox, and Coptic churches as present but names none.
    **Source says:** Named, current congregations can be given: St. Maron Maronite Catholic Church (602 University Ave. NE, Minneapolis), St. Mary's Coptic Orthodox Church (501 Sixth Ave. S., South St. Paul) and St. George Antiochian Orthodox Church (West St. Paul; current service details not confirmed).
    **Links:** https://www.mynortheaster.com/event/st-maron-lebanese-festival-6/2026-09-18/ ; https://stmarymn.org/ ; https://www.oca.org/media/photos/metropolitan-tikhon-presides-and-preaches-at-pan-orthodox-lenten-vespers . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

## Claims checked and supported

- Mizna's founding in 1999 by Kathryn Haddad and Saleh Abudayyeh, Haddad as first executive director 1999-2009, and the Twin Cities Arab Film Festival beginning in 2003 and described by Mizna as the largest and longest-running SWANA-centered film festival in the Midwest: supported by https://mizna.org/about/ (checked Oct. 31, 2026).
- The Arab American Institute's estimate of about 3.7 million Arab Americans and the 2020 Census count of 2,815,469: supported by https://www.aaiusa.org/demographics (checked Oct. 31, 2026).
