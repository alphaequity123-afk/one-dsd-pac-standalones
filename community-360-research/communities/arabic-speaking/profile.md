# Arabic-speaking Minnesotans

Arabic is a language, not a single people. In Minnesota it is the home language, the heritage language or the prayer language of families whose roots lie in Iraq, Egypt, Lebanon, Palestine, Sudan, Syria, Yemen, Morocco, Jordan, Algeria and the Gulf states, and of some people whose families are Somali, Kurdish or from elsewhere. They include Sunni and Shia Muslims, Maronite and Melkite Catholics, Coptic and Antiochian Orthodox Christians, people of other faiths and people of none. This profile covers that whole range. Lebanese Minnesotans, the oldest and largest of these groups by ancestry, have their own profile, so they appear here only as part of the larger picture.

## 1. People and place

**How many: three different counts.** No single number describes this community, because the census does not have an "Arab" or "Middle Eastern" race box, and ancestry, language and birthplace each catch different people. The Arab American Institute, a national advocacy group, argues that census ancestry counts miss many Arab Americans because the ancestry question is separate from race, samples are small and later generations intermarry ([Arab American Institute](https://www.aaiusa.org/demographics)). With that caution:

- **Ancestry.** The American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024 counts 26,405 Minnesotans (give or take 2,573) who report at least one Arab ancestry. The largest reported groups are Lebanese (7,604, give or take 882), "Other Arab" (5,054), Egyptian (4,295), Iraqi (2,534), Palestinian (2,443), Moroccan (2,022, with a wide margin of 1,104), "Arab" (1,616), Syrian (1,135) and Jordanian (479). The survey lists Sudanese separately from its Arab group: 3,017 Minnesotans (give or take 809) report Sudanese ancestry. Only about 102 report Assyrian, Chaldean or Syriac ancestry, a figure too small to read closely ([Census Reporter, ACS 2020-2024 table B04006](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B04006&geo_ids=04000US27)). The single-year 2024 estimate is higher, 30,362, but its margin of error (5,360) is twice as wide ([Census Reporter, ACS 2024 one-year](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B04006&geo_ids=04000US27)).
- **Language.** In the same 2020 to 2024 survey, 15,208 Minnesotans age 5 and older (give or take 1,931) speak Arabic at home. Of those, 10,339 speak English "very well" and 4,869, or about 32 percent, speak English less than "very well" ([Census Reporter, ACS 2020-2024 table B16001](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B16001&geo_ids=04000US27)). Many people of Arab ancestry speak only English, and some Arabic speakers are not Arab, so the two counts overlap only partly.
- **Birthplace.** Among foreign-born Minnesotans, the survey estimates 2,594 born in Iraq, 2,517 in Egypt, 1,885 in Sudan, 1,370 in Saudi Arabia, 1,270 in Morocco, 721 in Lebanon, 701 in Yemen, 674 in Jordan, 572 in Syria, 411 in Algeria, 344 in the United Arab Emirates and 244 in Kuwait. Most of these margins are wide; Syria's, for example, is give or take 335 ([Census Reporter, ACS 2020-2024 table B05006](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US27)). A birthplace is not an ethnicity. The founder of Holy Land, for example, is Palestinian and came to Minnesota from Kuwait, and a St. Catherine University graduate profiled by Sahan Journal was born to a Somali family and grew up in Yemen ([WCCO](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/meet-the-family-behind-holy-land-the-hummus-mecca-of-minneapolis/); [Sahan Journal, 2021](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/muslim-travel-ban-minnesota/)).

Minnesota Compass profiles only one of these groups, Lebanese Minnesotans, as a "cultural community." Its 2020 to 2024 count is 7,232 (give or take 1,225), with a 2024 single-year estimate of 10,066 that Compass calls the most accurate measure of total size ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lebanese); [Minnesota Compass cultural communities list](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities)). The Arab American Institute lists Minnesota among the 12 states where most Arab Americans live and Minneapolis among the top six metro areas; that is an advocacy group's estimate with no margin given ([Arab American Institute](https://www.aaiusa.org/demographics)).

**Where.** The map does not look like the one many people expect. The largest number of Arabic speakers is in Anoka County (4,144, give or take 1,064), followed by Hennepin (3,794), Olmsted (2,021), Ramsey (1,605), Stearns (794), Dakota (650), Wright (519) and Clay (421) counties ([Census Reporter, ACS 2020-2024 table C16001 by county](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=C16001&geo_ids=050|04000US27)). By city, the largest estimates are Blaine (2,444, give or take 956), Rochester (1,992, give or take 949) and Minneapolis (1,675), then St. Paul, Fridley, Otsego, Eden Prairie and Brooklyn Park, each with roughly 400 to 550 and very wide margins ([Census Reporter, C16001 by place](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=C16001&geo_ids=160|04000US27)). Counting by Arab ancestry instead, Hennepin leads (7,987), then Anoka (4,501), Ramsey (2,967), Dakota (2,182), Olmsted (1,969) and Clay (943) ([Census Reporter, B04006 by county](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B04006&geo_ids=050|04000US27)). School data point the same way: in 2025, Anoka County schools had more students learning English whose home language is Arabic (506) than any other county, ahead of Ramsey (275), Hennepin (261) and Olmsted (189) ([Minnesota Department of Education, English Learners in Minnesota 2024-25](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/other/261381.pdf)).

Named places that recur in the record are Central Avenue in Northeast Minneapolis and Columbia Heights, home to Holy Land and a cluster of newer Palestinian and Yemeni businesses ([The Heavy Table, Aug. 9, 2024](https://heavytable.substack.com/p/palestinian-bakeries-and-central)); Fridley and Columbia Heights, where the Islamic Center of Minnesota has its school and mosque ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Islamic-Center-Of-Minnesota)); Northeast Minneapolis and the West Side of St. Paul, where Lebanese families settled a century ago ([MPR News, Nov. 19, 2018](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2018/11/19/theater-director-sets-out-to-gather-tell-stories-of-the-labanese-who-came-to-minnesota)); South St. Paul, home of the state's Coptic Orthodox church ([St. Mary's Coptic Orthodox Church](https://stmarymn.org/)); and Rochester. Greater Minnesota is not an afterthought: Compass places 23.3 percent of Lebanese Minnesotans outside the Twin Cities ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lebanese)), Mankato had a Lebanese community studied as early as 1958 ([Minnesota State University, Mankato archives](https://archivesspace.lib.mnsu.edu/public/repositories/4/resources/618)), and a Palestinian restaurant operated in Duluth in 2024 and 2025 ([MPR News, Oct. 8, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2024/10/08/duluth-palestinian-restauranteur-reflects-on-year-marked-by-violence-in-gaza)).

**How people came: many arrivals, no single year.**

- *Lebanese and Syrians, from about 1889.* Michael Henne, who arrived in 1889, housed and supplied newcomers above his store on Main Street in Minneapolis. By 1918 there were 104 "Syrian" families in Northeast Minneapolis; at the time, people from what is now Lebanon were usually called Syrians. The first Maronite Mass in Minneapolis was said on February 9, 1903 ([Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/lebanese-came-to-northeast-in-waves/)). Later waves came shortly before World War II and during Lebanon's civil war of 1975 to 1991 ([Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/lebanese-came-to-northeast-in-waves/)). Compass finds only 9.2 percent of today's Lebanese Minnesotans were born abroad ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lebanese)).
- *Palestinians, mostly since the 1980s.* Majdi Wadi has said his father was forced out of Palestine at 16 after the 1948 war and walked to Kuwait; his brother Wajdi came to Minnesota for college, then started Holy Land in Northeast Minneapolis ([Twin Cities Business, 2009](https://tcbmag.com/hummus-for-the-masses/)). The owner of Mim's Cafe, near the University of Minnesota's St. Paul campus, told the Minnesota Daily his family fled the West Bank when he was a month old, after the 1948 war ([Minnesota Daily, Feb. 27, 2024](https://mndaily.com/uncategorized/best-palestinian-owned-restaurants-in-the-twin-cities/02/27/2024/snoadmin/)). MPR News described Minnesota in 2024 as home to "hundreds of Palestinian families" ([MPR News, Oct. 8, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2024/10/08/duluth-palestinian-restauranteur-reflects-on-year-marked-by-violence-in-gaza)).
- *Iraqis, mostly since 2008.* State health records count 1,502 primary refugee arrivals from Iraq between 1999 and 2024, not counting small cells under five that the state suppresses. Arrivals rose from 62 in 2008 to a peak of 234 in 2010, stayed between about 120 and 220 a year through 2016, fell to single digits from 2018 to 2023 and rose to 42 in 2024. Anoka (444), Olmsted (371) and Ramsey (303) counties received the most ([Minnesota Department of Health, refugee arrivals by country and county](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/country.csv); [MDH refugee statistics page, updated Nov. 7, 2025](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/index.html)). "Iraq" here is a country of origin, so it includes Kurdish and Christian Iraqis who may not speak Arabic at home. MPR News put the number of Iraqi refugees living in Minnesota at around 2,000 in 2022 ([MPR News, Jan. 27, 2022](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2022/01/27/intimate-portraits-of-iraqi-minnesotans-confronting-the-idea-of-home)). In 2008 MPR described only "a handful" of new arrivals, including two medical professionals who had been targeted in Iraq ([MPR News, Sept. 8, 2008](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2008/09/08/from-iraq-to-minnesota-a-new-life)). Some, like aviation engineer Jamal Ali, came because they had helped U.S. forces ([WCCO, 2019](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/what-i-did-was-worth-doing-iraqi-minnesotan-jamal-ali-helping-refugees-tell-their-stories/)). Iraqi Kurds who left in the 1980s are a separate, earlier story centered on Fargo-Moorhead ([MPR News, Jan. 18, 2012](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2012/01/18/a-minnesota-iraqi-connection)).
- *Egyptians, Sudanese, Syrians, Yemenis and Moroccans.* These groups are visible in the census and in community life (sections 4, 5 and 10), but no Minnesota history of their arrival was found. Refugee numbers have been small: in 2016, State Department data cited by MPR News showed 20 refugees from Syria and 12 from Sudan resettling in Minnesota, and none from Yemen or Libya ([MPR News, Feb. 1, 2017](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2017/02/01/who-are-the-minnesota-refugees-explainer)). Other routes, such as study, work, family sponsorship or moves from other states, are not separated in any Minnesota data found. The Coptic priest at St. Mary's in South St. Paul has served there since 1999 ([St. Mary's Coptic Orthodox Church](https://stmarymn.org/about-us/fr-youannes/)). Moroccan chef Hassan Ziadi moved from Qatar to Minneapolis in 2013 ([Neighborhood Development Center](https://www.ndc-mn.org/spotlight/moroccan-flavors/)). A Sudanese student leader at the University of Minnesota said in 2020 that "the Sudanese people in Minnesota don't have a huge community" and are often disconnected from one another ([Minnesota Daily, Sept. 29, 2020](https://mndaily.com/city/sudanese-student-union-raises-awareness-about-sudanese-flooding-crisis/09/29/2020/snoadmin/)).

**Generations and languages.** The groups sit at very different points. Lebanese Minnesotans are mostly U.S.-born and 86.1 percent speak only English at home ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lebanese)). Iraqi, Sudanese and Yemeni families are more recent. Spoken Arabic varies widely from region to region, and the Minnesota Department of Health notes that Iraq alone also has Kurdish, Turkmen and Syriac or Neo-Aramaic speakers ([MDH Iraqi Refugee Health Profile](https://www.health.mn.gov/communities/rih/coe/profiles/iraqi.html)). For many Somali, Oromo and other Muslim Minnesotans, Arabic is the language of prayer and Quran study rather than of daily life. This profile does not count them as Arabic speakers unless they speak it at home.

## 2. All walks of life

The evidence is uneven. Detailed income figures exist only for Lebanese Minnesotans. For every other group, the picture has to be built from news reports about named people, and those reports tend to favor business owners and arts leaders.

**Comfortable and well-off households.** In Compass's 2020 to 2024 profile, 35.9 percent of Lebanese Minnesotan households earn $100,000 or more, 68.6 percent own their homes, and 50.9 percent of adults 25 and older hold a bachelor's degree or higher, including 16.9 percent with a graduate or professional degree ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lebanese)). Business families have built real wealth. In 2009 Twin Cities Business reported that the Wadi family's Holy Land Brand was a $10 million company and that the family owned five commercial buildings and 10 residential properties near Central Avenue ([Twin Cities Business, 2009](https://tcbmag.com/hummus-for-the-masses/)). Chef Sameh Wadi has run several well-known Minneapolis restaurants and in 2026 reopened Saffron in a building he and his brother bought ([MPR News, May 17, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/05/17/chef-sameh-wadi-saffron); [Racket, Sept. 1, 2026](https://racketmn.com/saffron-returns-bauhaus-is-back-and-toms-thai-opens-in-uptown)).

**Professionals.** Many newcomers arrived with professional careers. MPR profiled an Iraqi couple, both medical professionals, whose training did not transfer to U.S. standards ([MPR News, 2008](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2008/09/08/from-iraq-to-minnesota-a-new-life)); a civil engineer who had worked with the World Bank and his wife, the former deputy director of Baghdad's Children's Hospital, who settled in Fridley ([MPR News, Dec. 1, 2014](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2014/12/01/iraqi-refugees-dream-of-reuniting-family-in-minnesota)); and Iraqi Airways aviation engineer Jamal Ali ([WCCO, 2019](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/what-i-did-was-worth-doing-iraqi-minnesotan-jamal-ali-helping-refugees-tell-their-stories/)). The Coptic priest in South St. Paul practiced as a surgeon and urologist in Egypt for 16 years before ordination ([St. Mary's Coptic Orthodox Church](https://stmarymn.org/about-us/fr-youannes/)). Writer and organizer Kathryn Haddad taught high school for 34 years ([MPR News, April 27, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/04/27/arab-north-african-artists-minnesota-hub)).

**Middle-income and working families.** Early Lebanese immigrants worked in paper mills, on railroads and in factories, while women peddled goods door to door as far as Hibbing; one family recalled standing in Depression bread lines ([Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/lebanese-came-to-northeast-in-waves/)). Today, 82.5 percent of working-age Lebanese Minnesotans are employed ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lebanese)). Restaurant, bakery and coffee-shop work is a large part of what the news record shows for newer arrivals (sections 3 and 4).

**Hardship.** The same Lebanese profile shows that 28.1 percent of households earn under $35,000, 46.8 percent are cost-burdened by housing, 60.5 percent of renter households are cost-burdened, and 8.8 percent of people live below the poverty line, with wide margins ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lebanese)). For recent refugees the start is harder: a resettlement worker told MPR that one Iraqi family arrived with "little more than the clothes they were wearing" ([MPR News, 2008](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2008/09/08/from-iraq-to-minnesota-a-new-life)). About a third of Arabic speakers statewide report speaking English less than "very well" (section 1), which limits job choices. Families separated by war or travel bans carry costs that income tables do not show (section 13).

## 3. Work and the economy

**Peddling, mills and city jobs.** Minnesota's first Arabic-speaking economy was a supply chain. Michael Henne ran a store and acted as a wholesaler, giving goods on credit to peddlers who fanned out across the state. Men took railroad and factory jobs; one later founded Jacobs Trucking from an ice wagon, and the Saba family produced Minneapolis city employees and a police officer ([Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/lebanese-came-to-northeast-in-waves/)).

**Food manufacturing and halal trade.** Holy Land grew from an 800-square-foot storefront into a restaurant, grocery, butcher shop and factory ([Kowalski's Markets](https://www.kowalskis.com/about-us/our-local-partners/deli-partners/holy-land)). WCCO reported that it turns 2,500 pounds of dry chickpeas a day into hummus, including a line made only for Costco ([WCCO](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/meet-the-family-behind-holy-land-the-hummus-mecca-of-minneapolis/)). In 2009 Majdi Wadi was working with the then-U.S. representative for the Fifth District on a federal halal standard, and the director of the Northeast Community Development Corporation credited him with helping keep Central Avenue from further decline in the late 1990s ([Twin Cities Business, 2009](https://tcbmag.com/hummus-for-the-masses/)). Kowalski's Markets still listed Holy Land hummus among its deli partners in 2026 ([Kowalski's](https://www.kowalskis.com/about-us/our-local-partners/deli-partners/holy-land)).

**Small business with community lenders.** Hassan Ziadi opened Moroccan Flavors at the Midtown Global Market with training, design help and a loan from the Neighborhood Development Center after he said no bank would lend to a newcomer ([Neighborhood Development Center](https://www.ndc-mn.org/spotlight/moroccan-flavors/)). The Yemeni coffee shops that arrived in 2024 and 2026 are franchises of national chains run by local owners ([Sahan Journal, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/qamaria-yemeni-coffee-little-canada-minnesota/); [Hoodline, July 2026](https://hoodline.com/2026/07/yemeni-coffee-chain-haraz-takes-over-lyn-lake-s-buzziest-corner/)).

**Credentials and fit.** Doctors, engineers and other professionals from Iraq have faced the gap between foreign training and U.S. licensing ([MPR News, 2008](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2008/09/08/from-iraq-to-minnesota-a-new-life)). No Minnesota data on occupations or industries by Arab ancestry, and no source on union membership, was found.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

There is no single "Arab district" in Minnesota. The clearest commercial corridor is **Central Avenue** running north from Northeast Minneapolis into Columbia Heights. Holy Land has anchored it since the 1980s; surveys by a neighborhood development group in 2005 and 2009 found it one of the two most-visited businesses on the street ([Twin Cities Business, 2009](https://tcbmag.com/hummus-for-the-masses/)). By 2024 the northern stretch had become, in The Heavy Table's words, a "playground" for Middle Eastern food, including the Palestinian-owned Golden Nuts bakery and nut shop (opened April 2024) and a restaurant called Yemeni Cuisine ([The Heavy Table, Aug. 9, 2024](https://heavytable.substack.com/p/palestinian-bakeries-and-central)). Al Amir Bakery on Central Avenue has been known for its Iraqi-style pocket bread and grilled kebabs ([The Heavy Table, Aug. 5, 2015](https://heavytable.com/central-avenue-checklist-al-amir-bakery-to-fair-state-brewing-cooperative/)).

Other named businesses, with their status as of October 2026 (details in businesses.json):

- **Saffron**, 165 13th Ave. NE, Minneapolis: Sameh Wadi's Levantine and Palestinian restaurant, reopened in summer 2026 with posted hours ([Saffron](https://www.saffronmpls.com/); [Racket, Sept. 1, 2026](https://racketmn.com/saffron-returns-bauhaus-is-back-and-toms-thai-opens-in-uptown)).
- **Baba's Hummus House & Mana'eesh Bakery**, 2220 Lyndale Ave. S., Minneapolis: a cafe "inspired by our Palestinian heritage", with posted hours ([Baba's](https://lovebabas.com/pages/babas-cafe)). It hosted a Mizna literary reading and iftar in February 2026 ([Sahan Journal, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/ramadan-2026-events-iftar-minnesota/)).
- **Wally's Falafel, Hummus and Bakery**, Dinkytown: owner Wally Sakallah told the Minnesota Daily that each table represents a city in Palestine; the restaurant posted 2026 menus and daily hours ([Minnesota Daily, 2024](https://mndaily.com/uncategorized/best-palestinian-owned-restaurants-in-the-twin-cities/02/27/2024/snoadmin/); [Wally's](https://www.wallysfalafelandhummus.com/hours/)).
- **Mim's Cafe** (near the University of Minnesota's St. Paul campus), **Shish** (Grand Avenue, St. Paul) and **Zait & Zaatar** (near Macalester College in St. Paul), all described as Palestinian-owned in 2024 ([Minnesota Daily, 2024](https://mndaily.com/uncategorized/best-palestinian-owned-restaurants-in-the-twin-cities/02/27/2024/snoadmin/)). Current operation could not be confirmed from an opened page.
- **Pangea Market**, Coon Rapids: a halal grocery and deli with daily-baked bread and Palestinian cheeses such as Nabulsi and Akawi, open daily ([Pangea Market](https://pangeamarket.com/); [The Heavy Table, 2024](https://heavytable.substack.com/p/palestinian-bakeries-and-central)).
- **Qamaria Yemeni Coffee Co.**, Little Canada (opened May 2024), and **Haraz Coffee House**, 3005 Lyndale Ave. S., Minneapolis (soft opening July 2026, run by a local franchisee): Yemeni coffee shops that stay open late. Sahan Journal reported that such shops meet a need for evening gathering places for young Muslims that do not serve alcohol; one young customer said, "Minneapolis doesn't have a lot of places for Muslims to hang out" ([Sahan Journal, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/qamaria-yemeni-coffee-little-canada-minnesota/); [MPR News, June 12, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2024/06/12/rapidly-growing-yemeni-coffee-chains-set-sights-on-twin-cities); [Hoodline, July 2026](https://hoodline.com/2026/07/yemeni-coffee-chain-haraz-takes-over-lyn-lake-s-buzziest-corner/)).
- **Moroccan Flavors** (Hassan Ziadi) opened at the Midtown Global Market, but it no longer appears in the market's October 2026 merchant list. The market does list **Dar Medina**, a shop selling Moroccan handmade home goods ([NDC](https://www.ndc-mn.org/spotlight/moroccan-flavors/); [Midtown Global Market merchants](https://midtownglobalmarket.org/merchants)).
- **Shawarma Cart**, Edina: owned by an Egyptian American restaurateur, serving Cairo-style street food ([University of Minnesota Office for Supplier Diversity](https://osd.umn.edu/business/shawarma-cart)).
- **Almadina Restaurant**, downtown Rochester: Middle Eastern and Mediterranean food ([Downtown Rochester MN](https://www.downtownrochestermn.com/go/almadina-restaurant)). The owners' background was not stated.
- **Lebanese businesses in Northeast Minneapolis**, including Emily's Lebanese Deli (opened 1973) and Zakia's Deli (opened 2007), are covered in the Lebanese profile ([Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/lebanese-came-to-northeast-in-waves/)).
- **Falastin**, Duluth: a Palestinian deli that opened in May 2024, was hit with anti-Palestinian graffiti that summer and closed on May 3, 2025 ([MPR News, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2024/10/08/duluth-palestinian-restauranteur-reflects-on-year-marked-by-violence-in-gaza); [Northern News Now, May 6, 2025](https://www.northernnewsnow.com/2025/05/06/duluths-falastin-deli-announces-closure-after-1-year/)).

The business record leans heavily toward food. No Arab American chamber of commerce, law firm, accounting firm, clinic or insurance agency in Minnesota was identified from an opened source. That is a gap in the evidence, not proof that none exist.

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

Arabic-speaking Minnesotans belong to many faiths, and the language tells you nothing certain about a person's religion. Even within one origin country the mix is wide. The Minnesota Department of Health notes that Iraq is mostly Shia and Sunni Muslim, with Chaldo-Assyrian Christians, Sabean-Mandaeans and Yazidis as minorities, and that Christians made up 62 percent of Iraqis resettled in the United States by 2008, far above their share at home ([MDH Iraqi Refugee Health Profile](https://www.health.mn.gov/communities/rih/coe/profiles/iraqi.html)). That is a national figure; no count for Minnesota was found.

**Muslim congregations.** No Minnesota mosque was found that describes itself as primarily Arab; the mosques in the public record serve Muslims of many backgrounds. Several have Arab roots or ties in the public record:

- The **Islamic Center of Minnesota** says it began in 1969, when Muslim students who chose to stay in Minnesota started it in Dinkytown; the AbuKhadra family later donated a building in Columbia Heights, now Masjid AbuKhadra. The center bought a former school in Fridley in 1987, where the full-time Al-Amal School runs on weekdays, and it holds a weekend school, a monthly food shelf and a weekly health clinic ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Islamic-Center-Of-Minnesota)).
- The **Imam Husain Islamic Center** at 6120 Brooklyn Blvd. is listed as Shia (Jafari), with a Brooklyn Center mailing address ([Zabihah](https://www.zabihah.com/mosques/133e2ba9-776a-11ef-95ae-6045bdeb9f57/imam-husain-islamic-center-minneapolis-mn)). Its congregation's origins were not documented in any source found.
- In October 2023, people gathered at the **Brooklyn Park Islamic Center** to pray for a Maple Grove family who lost relatives in Gaza ([WCCO, Oct. 20, 2023](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/family-with-minnesota-ties-killed-in-gaza/)).

The Minneapolis Iraqi and American Reconciliation Project's sister-city delegations to Najaf have included visits to the Shrine of Imam Hussein ([IARP](https://www.reconciliationproject.org/our-story)). In 2023, Minneapolis permitted the Muslim call to prayer to be broadcast over loudspeakers, which a national civil rights group counted as a bright spot in its yearly report ([KAXE/NPR, April 2, 2024](https://www.kaxe.org/news/2024-04-02/anti-muslim-bias-reports-skyrocket-after-oct-7-hamas-attack-on-israel)).

**Christian congregations.**

- **St. Maron Maronite Catholic Church**, 602 University Ave. NE, Minneapolis, grew from the first Maronite Mass in the city in 1903. Its pastor told the Northeaster that the parish now gathers "Maronites, the Melkites," and Christians from Syria, Jordan and the Holy Land, among others ([Northeaster](https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/lebanese-came-to-northeast-in-waves/); [Northeaster festival listing, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/event/st-maron-lebanese-festival-6/2026-09-18/)). Its festival volunteers include people from Syria, Jordan, Turkey, Iraq and Palestine ([Racket, 2022](https://racketmn.com/st-marons-lebanese-festival-northeast-minneapolis)).
- **St. Mary's Coptic Orthodox Church**, 501 Sixth Ave. S., South St. Paul, holds Sunday liturgy at 8 a.m. Father Youannes Tawfik, ordained in Egypt by Pope Shenouda III, has served there since 1999 ([St. Mary's Coptic Orthodox Church](https://stmarymn.org/); [Fr. Youannes](https://stmarymn.org/about-us/fr-youannes/)). The church describes Copts as the native Christians of Egypt ([St. Mary's, Coptic overview](https://stmarymn.org/about-us/coptic-overview/)). It runs a ministry for members 55 and older ([St. Simon the Elder Ministry](https://stmarymn.org/services/senior-ministry/)) and sends youth to St. Mark's Festival (Mahragan El-Keraza), a Midwest Coptic gathering ([St. Mary's, Mahragan](https://stmarymn.org/services/sunday-school/mahragan/)).
- **St. George Antiochian Orthodox Church** in West St. Paul has hosted pan-Orthodox services for the Twin Cities' Orthodox clergy ([Orthodox Church in America, undated photo report](https://www.oca.org/media/photos/metropolitan-tikhon-presides-and-preaches-at-pan-orthodox-lenten-vespers)). No current parish page was opened, so its present services and language use were not confirmed.

**Different practice, and no practice.** Within every group, observance ranges widely. Families differ on whether they follow a local moon sighting or a calculated calendar for Ramadan and Eid (section 6), and Sunni and Shia Muslims mark Ashura differently. Some Arab Minnesotans are secular or not religious. Mizna, the state's best-known Arab arts organization, describes itself as serving "Arab and SWANA" artists without reference to faith ([Mizna](https://mizna.org/about/)). No survey of religious affiliation among Arabic-speaking Minnesotans was found.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

**Ramadan and the two Eids.** In 2026, Sahan Journal reported that Ramadan began on the evening of Tuesday, February 17, in North America and would run through March 19 or 20 ([Sahan Journal, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/ramadan-2026-events-iftar-minnesota/)). The exact day depends on moon sighting or the calculation a mosque follows ([Morocco World News, February 2026](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/02/278546/when-will-muslims-in-the-us-start-ramadan-2026/)). Iftars, the evening meals that break the fast, are public events as well as family ones. Sahan's 2026 list included Minnesota Council of Churches "Taking Heart" iftars hosted with mosques in Blaine, Bloomington, Plymouth and Coon Rapids, Muslim American Society fundraisers, and a "Baba's x Mizna Lit Series + Iftar" at Baba's on February 26 ([Sahan Journal, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/ramadan-2026-events-iftar-minnesota/)). The Lake Street Council said Ramadan 2026 "feels different" because of federal immigration enforcement in Minnesota ([Lake Street Council](https://www.visitlakestreet.com/ramadan-guide)). For Eid, large prayers have been held at the Minneapolis Convention Center; in 2023, Eid al-Adha drew "tens of thousands" there ([WCCO, June 28, 2023](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/tens-of-thousands-of-muslims-celebrate-eid-al-adha-at-minneapolis-convention-center/)). These gatherings are shared with Somali and other Muslim Minnesotans; no Eid event organized specifically by Arab Minnesotans was found.

**Twin Cities Arab Film Festival.** Mizna has produced the festival since 2003 and calls it the largest and longest-running SWANA-centered film festival in the Midwest ([Mizna](https://mizna.org/about/)). The 20th edition ran September 23 to 27, 2026, at The Main Cinema in Minneapolis, closing at the Walker Art Center ([Mizna, 2026 festival](https://mizna.org/event/2026-tcaff/)).

**Palestine Cinema Days.** On November 2, 2026, the anniversary of the Balfour Declaration, Mizna is screening *Return to Haifa* at The Main Cinema, as part of an international screening initiative with Filmlab Palestine ([Mizna](https://mizna.org/event/palestine-cinema-days-2026-return-to-haifa/)).

**St. Maron Lebanese Festival.** The festival began in 1990, partly to show Minnesotans a side of Lebanon other than war, according to the pastor ([Racket, 2022](https://racketmn.com/st-marons-lebanese-festival-northeast-minneapolis)). In 2026 it ran September 18 to 20 with food, dabke dancing and church tours ([Northeaster, 2026](https://www.mynortheaster.com/event/st-maron-lebanese-festival-6/2026-09-18/)). The Lebanese profile covers it in more depth.

**Arab-American Heritage Month.** The University of Minnesota lists April 2026 as Arab-American Heritage Month ([University of Minnesota Office for Equity and Diversity](https://diversity.umn.edu/news-events/heritage-months)). No Minnesota governor's proclamation or statewide public event for the month was found.

**Christian feasts.** Easter, Christmas and saints' days are central for Maronite, Coptic and Orthodox families; Coptic and Antiochian Orthodox Easter follow the Eastern calendar. No public Minnesota event tied to these dates was found beyond regular parish services.

**Other observances.** Families from different countries mark national days such as Lebanese Independence Day (November 22), but no Minnesota celebration of any Arab national day was found. Ashura is a major day of mourning for Shia Muslims; a Shia center exists in the Twin Cities (section 5), but no public description of how it is observed here was found. The International Festival of Minnesota, formerly the Festival of Nations, is a long-running stage for cultural booths: a 1964 photo shows a Minnesota woman making Syrian flatbread for it ([MPR News, 2018](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2018/11/19/theater-director-sets-out-to-gather-tell-stories-of-the-labanese-who-came-to-minnesota)), and the Iraqi and American Reconciliation Project ran the festival's first Iraqi cultural booth ([IARP](https://www.reconciliationproject.org/our-story); [International Festival of Minnesota](https://www.ifestmn.org/)).

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Households.** Among Lebanese Minnesotans, the average household has 2.12 people and 42.8 percent of households are a person living alone, a pattern that reflects an older, U.S.-born group ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lebanese)). No household data were found for the newer Iraqi, Sudanese, Yemeni, Egyptian or Syrian groups, so this pattern should not be assumed for them.

**Several generations at work and at home.** Family businesses often span generations. Holy Land began with recipes from "Mama Fatima" Wadi, and by the time WCCO profiled it, three generations were running the restaurant, grocery and hummus plant ([WCCO](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/meet-the-family-behind-holy-land-the-hummus-mecca-of-minneapolis/)). At St. Maron's festival, older women once made the food in the church basement; volunteers told Racket they are now passing the work to a third generation ([Racket, 2022](https://racketmn.com/st-marons-lebanese-festival-northeast-minneapolis)).

**Food.** Food is one of the most public parts of daily life: mana'eesh flatbread with za'atar, hummus, kebabs, knafeh and baklava, Iraqi pocket bread, and Yemeni Adeni chai and cardamom lattes all appear in the Minnesota record ([Racket, 2022](https://racketmn.com/st-marons-lebanese-festival-northeast-minneapolis); [The Heavy Table, 2024](https://heavytable.substack.com/p/palestinian-bakeries-and-central); [Sahan Journal, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/qamaria-yemeni-coffee-little-canada-minnesota/); [The Heavy Table, 2015](https://heavytable.com/central-avenue-checklist-al-amir-bakery-to-fair-state-brewing-cooperative/)). The Iraqi and American Reconciliation Project has used kleicha, an Iraqi date cookie, as a way to talk about home and return ([IARP](https://www.reconciliationproject.org/)).

**Families split across borders.** Many households live with relatives far away, in danger or kept apart by law. An Iraqi couple in Fridley, an engineer and a former hospital deputy director, worried in 2014 about children still in Baghdad ([MPR News, 2014](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2014/12/01/iraqi-refugees-dream-of-reuniting-family-in-minnesota)). A young woman raised in Yemen was separated from her sister for about seven years by the first travel ban ([Sahan Journal, 2021](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/muslim-travel-ban-minnesota/)). A Palestinian Minnesotan told MPR in 2025 that her family thinks of relatives in Gaza "every bite we eat, knowing that they are hungry" ([MPR News, July 31, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/07/31/palestinian-minnesotan-talks-about-family-in-gaza)). A Sudanese student leader said in 2025 that any Sudanese person in Minnesota will say their family has been affected by the war ([Minnesota Daily, Nov. 13, 2025](https://mndaily.com/campus/the-sudanese-student-union-speaks-out-against-the-war-in-sudan/11/13/2025/eicmndaily-com/)).

**Gathering places.** Mosques, churches and family homes are the main gathering places. Sahan Journal reported in 2024 that young Muslims in the Twin Cities had few places to meet in the evening that do not serve alcohol, which is one reason Yemeni coffee shops drew long lines ([Sahan Journal, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/qamaria-yemeni-coffee-little-canada-minnesota/)).

**Aging, childhood, marriage and dress.** Apart from a Coptic church ministry for members 55 and older ([St. Mary's](https://stmarymn.org/services/senior-ministry/)) and church youth programs, no Minnesota source documented customs around aging, childhood, marriage or dress for these groups. Practice varies by family, faith and country of origin.

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Mizna.** Kathryn Haddad and Saleh Abudayyeh co-founded Mizna in 1999 to give Arab and Muslim writers a place to tell their own stories. It publishes a print literary and art journal twice a year and the online platform Mizna Online, and it produces the Twin Cities Arab Film Festival. Mizna says it has supported over 400 artists and reached well over 10,000 audience members and readers, and lists a Pushcart Prize, an Utne Independent Press Award and the Ordway's Sally Award among its honors ([Mizna](https://mizna.org/about/)). It describes itself as the only literary journal of its kind ([Mizna](https://mizna.org/about/)). Mizna also states openly that it complies with the Boycott, Divestment and Sanctions movement and the Palestinian academic and cultural boycott of Israel ([Mizna](https://mizna.org/about/)). Its 2026 festival was supported by the Minnesota Humanities Center with Legacy Amendment funds ([Mizna, 2026 festival](https://mizna.org/event/2026-tcaff/)).

**New Arab American Theater Works.** Led by Kathryn Haddad as executive artistic director, this Minneapolis company produces original plays by SWANA and Arab American writers, mentors playwrights and offers workshops ([New Arab American Theater Works](https://www.newarabamericantheaterworks.org/); [MPR News, April 27, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/04/27/arab-north-african-artists-minnesota-hub)). Its production *Rosette*, by William Nour, followed a Palestinian Christian girl living as a refugee after 1948 ([NAATW news page](https://www.newarabamericantheaterworks.org/news)). It also hosts **Yalla Drum**, which it calls Minnesota's only Arabic percussion ensemble. In September 2026 the group, with about 90 students after six years, performed at Winona State University ([The Winonan, September 2026](https://winonan.org/16972/featured-stories/the-rhythm-of-resistance/)).

**Iraqi Voices.** The Iraqi and American Reconciliation Project, founded in Minneapolis in 2007, runs Iraqi Voices, an arts and storytelling lab for Iraqi refugees and immigrants ([IARP](https://www.reconciliationproject.org/our-story)). Its 2022 exhibit "Home of Memories," with portraits by artist Jaafar Alnabi and photojournalist Ahmed Alshaikhli, was shown at the Hennepin History Museum ([MPR News, Jan. 27, 2022](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2022/01/27/intimate-portraits-of-iraqi-minnesotans-confronting-the-idea-of-home)).

**Why Minnesota.** Haddad told MPR that Minnesota's distance from the coasts, its "very supportive arts funding community" and the small size of each group have pushed Arab, Muslim and SWANA artists to work across national lines ([MPR News, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/04/27/arab-north-african-artists-minnesota-hub)).

**Archives.** The University of Minnesota's Immigration History Research Center Archives holds an Arab American Collection of photographs and papers ([UMN Libraries, UMedia record](https://lib-umedia-prd-app-01.oit.umn.edu/item/p16022coll152:8.json)), and Minnesota State University, Mankato holds a 1958 study of Mankato's Lebanese community ([MSU Mankato archives](https://archivesspace.lib.mnsu.edu/public/repositories/4/resources/618)).

**Sports.** Little was found. A young Yemeni-raised Minnesotan played for a Twin Cities soccer club and was invited to represent the United States at a 2017 tournament in Norway, but she could not go because of the travel ban ([Sahan Journal, 2021](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/muslim-travel-ban-minnesota/)). No Minnesota athletes, leagues or clubs tied to these communities were identified from opened sources.

**Media.** Beyond Mizna's journal, no Arabic-language newspaper, radio program or television program based in Minnesota was identified.

## 9. Civic and political life

**Organizations.** Few Minnesota organizations serve Arabic speakers as a group. The ones with a public record are mostly cultural: Mizna, New Arab American Theater Works and the Iraqi and American Reconciliation Project (section 8). IARP helped bring about the Minneapolis City Council's unanimous 2009 vote to become a sister city of Najaf, Iraq, and says more than 50 Iraqis have visited Minneapolis through that link ([IARP](https://www.reconciliationproject.org/our-story)). The Minnesota chapter of the Council on American-Islamic Relations (CAIR-MN) has spoken for Minnesota families who lost relatives in Gaza ([WCCO, Oct. 20, 2023](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/family-with-minnesota-ties-killed-in-gaza/)). Student groups include the University of Minnesota's Arab Student Association, founded in 2020 to bring together Arab students "encompassing all religions, nationalities and ethnic backgrounds" ([Minnesota Daily, Jan. 25, 2021](https://mndaily.com/city/arab-student-association-to-connect-umn-arab-students/01/25/2021/snoadmin/)), and the Sudanese Student Union ([Minnesota Daily, 2025](https://mndaily.com/campus/the-sudanese-student-union-speaks-out-against-the-war-in-sudan/11/13/2025/eicmndaily-com/)).

**Gaza and the 2024 primary.** In Minnesota's March 2024 Democratic presidential primary, about 19 percent of voters chose "uncommitted," a protest organized by Uncommitted MN to press President Biden on a Gaza cease-fire ([NPR, March 6, 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/03/06/1236396078/in-minnesota-uncommitted-got-19-of-the-democratic-presidential-primary-votes)). NPR's report did not say who voted uncommitted, so the result cannot be read as a measure of Arab Minnesotan opinion.

**Elected office and party.** No Arab American member of the Minnesota Legislature was identified. Political views are not uniform. Dalia al-Aqidi, an Iraqi-born journalist who moved to Minneapolis in 2019, ran as the Republican nominee against the incumbent U.S. representative for the Fifth District in 2024 and lost ([WCCO, November 2024](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/ilhan-omar-dalia-al-aqidi-district-5-results/)). Majdi Wadi worked with the then-U.S. representative for the Fifth District, a Democrat, on halal food standards ([Twin Cities Business, 2009](https://tcbmag.com/hummus-for-the-masses/)).

**Speaking out, and the cost of it.** Some people describe a climate where speaking about Palestine carries risk. Haddad told MPR she knows people who "have feared losing their job" for speaking out for Arabs, Muslims or Palestinians ([MPR News, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/04/27/arab-north-african-artists-minnesota-hub)). Duluth's Falastin owner said, "It's hard to be openly Palestinian here," after her deli was vandalized ([MPR News, Oct. 8, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2024/10/08/duluth-palestinian-restauranteur-reflects-on-year-marked-by-violence-in-gaza)). Sudanese students marched near Minneapolis City Hall in November 2025 to call for an end to the war and for boycotts of businesses tied to the United Arab Emirates ([Minnesota Daily, 2025](https://mndaily.com/campus/the-sudanese-student-union-speaks-out-against-the-war-in-sudan/11/13/2025/eicmndaily-com/)).

**Debates inside the community.** The record shows differences of party (above) and of origin. Sudanese student leaders described divisions between North and South Sudanese students on campus that they hoped to bridge ([Minnesota Daily, 2020](https://mndaily.com/city/sudanese-student-union-raises-awareness-about-sudanese-flooding-crisis/09/29/2020/snoadmin/)). No survey of political views among Arabic-speaking Minnesotans was found.

## 10. Education

**Students learning English.** Arabic is the fifth most common home language among Minnesota's English learners, after Spanish, Somali, Hmong and Karen. In 2025 there were 1,664 English learners whose primary home language is Arabic, up slightly from 1,584 in 2020, and 3,363 Minnesota students in all whose primary home language is Arabic ([Minnesota Department of Education, English Learners in Minnesota 2024-25](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/other/261381.pdf)). Anoka County had the most Arabic-speaking English learners (506), followed by Ramsey (275), Hennepin (261), Olmsted (189), Dakota (140) and Washington (81) ([MDE](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/other/261381.pdf)).

**Faith-based and weekend schools.** The Islamic Center of Minnesota's Fridley building houses Al-Amal School, a full-time Islamic school, and the center runs a Saturday and Sunday school ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Islamic-Center-Of-Minnesota)). The Coptic church runs a Sunday school and youth programs ([St. Mary's, Mahragan](https://stmarymn.org/services/sunday-school/mahragan/)). Whether these schools teach Arabic as a language, and how many students they serve, was not documented in the sources opened.

**Higher education.** The University of Minnesota teaches Arabic in its Department of Asian and Middle Eastern Studies, offers a 10-week intensive summer course open to non-students, and pairs learners with native Arabic speakers through its free TandemPlus program ([University of Minnesota](https://cla.umn.edu/asian-middle-eastern-studies/languages/arabic/opportunities)). Student groups include the Arab Student Association, the University Arabic Language & Culture Club and the Sudanese Student Union (section 9). A Yemeni-raised Minnesotan who attended Wellstone International High School became St. Catherine University's first Newman Civic Fellow in 2019 ([Sahan Journal, 2021](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/muslim-travel-ban-minnesota/)). Among Lebanese Minnesotans 25 and older, 97.8 percent finished high school and 50.9 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lebanese)). No education figures were found for other Arab groups in Minnesota.

**Educational history before arrival.** As background, the Minnesota Department of Health notes that Iraq once had one of the region's strongest school systems but that by 2004 only 55 percent of Iraqis aged 6 to 24 were in school ([MDH Iraqi Refugee Health Profile](https://www.health.mn.gov/communities/rih/coe/profiles/iraqi.html)). No Minnesota study of Arabic-speaking students' school experience was found.

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**State health guidance.** The Minnesota Department of Health publishes an Iraqi Refugee Health Profile for clinicians. It lists priority conditions including diabetes and summarizes risks seen among Iraqi refugees nationally and abroad, such as anemia, chronic conditions like high blood pressure, and the effects of war and displacement ([MDH](https://www.health.mn.gov/communities/rih/coe/profiles/iraqi.html)). Its data come mostly from other states and from refugees in Jordan and Syria, so it describes risks to ask about, not Minnesotans.

**Trauma and torture.** The Center for Victims of Torture, founded in Minnesota, estimates that more than 50,000 torture survivors live in the state and runs clinics in St. Paul and St. Cloud ([CVT](https://www.cvt.org/what-we-do/communities-we-serve/americas/united-states/minnesota/)). CVT does not report how many of its Minnesota clients speak Arabic. Iraqi refugees profiled by MPR described being targeted for kidnapping and violence before they left ([MPR News, 2008](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2008/09/08/from-iraq-to-minnesota-a-new-life)).

**Community clinics.** The Islamic Center of Minnesota runs a weekly health clinic ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Islamic-Center-Of-Minnesota)). No clinic or provider network specific to Arabic-speaking Minnesotans was identified.

**Grief, stress and fear.** War in Gaza and Sudan has brought steady grief. One Maple Grove family lost 30 relatives in an October 2023 strike, according to CAIR-MN ([WCCO, 2023](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/family-with-minnesota-ties-killed-in-gaza/)). A Palestinian Minnesotan described in 2025 how her sisters there developed breathing problems from cooking over firewood ([MPR News, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/07/31/palestinian-minnesotan-talks-about-family-in-gaza)). In 2026, an imam in Willmar told Sahan Journal that fear of immigration agents was shaping whether people went to mosque programs and gatherings ([Morocco World News, quoting Sahan Journal, February 2026](https://www.moroccoworldnews.com/2026/02/278546/when-will-muslims-in-the-us-start-ramadan-2026/); [Sahan Journal, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/ramadan-2026-events-iftar-minnesota/)).

**Insurance and access.** Compass suppresses the uninsured rate for Lebanese Minnesotans because the sample is too small ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lebanese)). No Minnesota data on health insurance, chronic disease or mental health for any other Arab group were found. Traditional or religious healing practices among Arabic-speaking Minnesotans were not documented in any Minnesota source.

## 12. Disability

**What Minnesota data show.** Very little. The only Minnesota disability figure for any Arab group is Compass's Lebanese profile: 13.0 percent of Lebanese Minnesotans (give or take 4.3 points) have a disability, and 10.5 percent of those under 65 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/lebanese)). That group is older and mostly U.S.-born, so the number should not be applied to recent Iraqi, Sudanese, Yemeni or Syrian families. No Minnesota disability rate was found for Arabic speakers as a language group, for people of Arab ancestry as a whole, or for any other national group. The Department of Education reports English learners by home language (section 10), but no published count of Arabic-speaking students in special education was found.

**Organizations and services.** No Minnesota organization focused on disability among Arabic-speaking families was found, and no Minnesota disability agency page was opened that listed Arabic materials. State agency pages on mn.gov could not be opened. Families reach services the way other Minnesotans do, through counties, school districts and statewide disability resources, but no source described how well those routes work for Arabic speakers.

**Questions worth asking, with what evidence there is.**

- *Who defines disability here?* Religious communities, schools, clinics and the state may each use different words. The Arabic dialect a family speaks differs by region (section 1), so a term that works in one dialect may not land in another. No Minnesota source documented which Arabic words families here use for disability.
- *What does a label open or close?* For some families a diagnosis may open school supports or waiver services. For others, especially people with pending immigration matters, the question may be whether using a public program carries risk. In 2025 and 2026 immigrant families in Minnesota described heavy fear of federal enforcement (section 13). No source documented how that fear affects disability services for Arabic speakers specifically.
- *What produced an impairment?* Some people arrive after war, torture, kidnapping threats or long stays in countries of first refuge ([MPR News, 2008](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2008/09/08/from-iraq-to-minnesota-a-new-life); [CVT](https://www.cvt.org/what-we-do/communities-we-serve/americas/united-states/minnesota/)). MDH's Iraqi profile describes chronic disease, malnutrition and sexual violence documented among Iraqi refugees studied in other states and abroad ([MDH](https://www.health.mn.gov/communities/rih/coe/profiles/iraqi.html)). Whether any of this produces a different pattern of disability among Arabic-speaking Minnesotans is an open question; no Minnesota study has answered it.
- *Who is missing from the count?* The census has no Arab category, and ancestry counts miss many people ([Arab American Institute](https://www.aaiusa.org/demographics)). Disability data built on those categories will also miss people.

**People.** No Minnesota-based disabled Arab American leader or advocate was identified from an opened source.

## 13. What is changing now

**War abroad, felt here (2023 to 2026).** Since October 2023, Palestinian Minnesotans have lost relatives in Gaza ([WCCO, Oct. 20, 2023](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/family-with-minnesota-ties-killed-in-gaza/); [MPR News, July 31, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/07/31/palestinian-minnesotan-talks-about-family-in-gaza)). Sudan's war has killed and displaced relatives of Sudanese Minnesotans; students protested in Minneapolis on November 9, 2025, after the Rapid Support Forces seized El Fasher ([Minnesota Daily, Nov. 13, 2025](https://mndaily.com/campus/the-sudanese-student-union-speaks-out-against-the-war-in-sudan/11/13/2025/eicmndaily-com/)). In October 2023, students and community groups, including American Muslims for Palestine, the Minnesota Anti-War Committee, Jewish Voice for Peace Twin Cities and the University of Minnesota's Students for Justice in Palestine, held marches and a rally at the State Capitol on October 18 ([The Oracle, Hamline University, October 2023](https://hamlineoracle.com/11719/news/minnesota-communities-come-together-in-the-wake-of-global-tragedy/)). In September 2024, St. Maron's held its 33rd Lebanese festival while Lebanon was caught in fighting between Hezbollah and Israel; its pastor said he fled Lebanon in the 1980s and that more than half of his parishioners immigrated from Lebanon ([WCCO, September 2024](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minneapolis-church-lebanese-festival/)). After the fall of Bashar al-Assad in December 2024, a Ramsey County man who fled Syria decades earlier told WCCO he felt cautiously optimistic about Syria's future ([WCCO, December 2024](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/video/syrian-family-in-minnesota-feels-cautiously-optimistic-about-syrias-future/)). Arts groups have made Palestine a central theme, from Mizna's film programs to Yalla Drum's 2026 performance closing an exhibit on Palestinian families ([Mizna](https://mizna.org/event/palestine-cinema-days-2026-return-to-haifa/); [The Winonan, 2026](https://winonan.org/16972/featured-stories/the-rhythm-of-resistance/)).

**Voting as protest.** About 19 percent of Minnesota's 2024 Democratic primary voters chose "uncommitted" over Gaza ([NPR, March 6, 2024](https://www.npr.org/2024/03/06/1236396078/in-minnesota-uncommitted-got-19-of-the-democratic-presidential-primary-votes)).

**Hostility and closure.** Duluth's Palestinian deli was vandalized in 2024 and closed on May 3, 2025; the owners said the decision came after much consideration and reflection ([MPR News, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2024/10/08/duluth-palestinian-restauranteur-reflects-on-year-marked-by-violence-in-gaza); [Northern News Now, 2025](https://www.northernnewsnow.com/2025/05/06/duluths-falastin-deli-announces-closure-after-1-year/)). Nationally, CAIR reported a record 8,061 anti-Muslim bias complaints for 2023, nearly half after October 7 ([KAXE/NPR, 2024](https://www.kaxe.org/news/2024-04-02/anti-muslim-bias-reports-skyrocket-after-oct-7-hamas-attack-on-israel)). No Minnesota-only count of anti-Arab incidents was found.

**Immigration rules (dated and attributed).** In early June 2025, President Trump announced a full ban on travel to the United States by citizens of 12 countries and partial limits on seven more ([MPR News, June 5, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/06/05/minnesota-immigrants-face-uncertainty-amid-trump-travel-ban)). The full ban included Libya, Sudan and Yemen ([WXYZ Detroit, June 2025](https://www.wxyz.com/news/detroits-yemeni-community-faces-separation-from-families-as-new-travel-ban-takes-effect-on-12-countries)). In September 2025 the federal government moved to end Temporary Protected Status for about 6,000 Syrians nationwide ([Al Jazeera, Sept. 20, 2025](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/9/20/us-cancels-temporary-protected-status-for-syrians)); no count of affected Minnesotans was found. Iraqi refugee arrivals to Minnesota, which had fallen to single digits from 2018 to 2023, rose to 42 in 2024 ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/country.csv)). In early 2026, Sahan Journal reported a heavy federal immigration enforcement presence in Minnesota, with residents reporting harassment and racial profiling, and the Lake Street Council said enforcement was disrupting Ramadan business ([Sahan Journal, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/ramadan-2026-events-iftar-minnesota/); [Lake Street Council](https://www.visitlakestreet.com/ramadan-guide)). These are group-level reports; they say nothing about any individual's status.

**New businesses and returning ones.** Yemeni coffee shops opened in Little Canada in 2024 and Lyn-Lake in 2026, Palestinian bakeries multiplied on Central Avenue, and Saffron reopened in 2026 after nearly a decade ([Sahan Journal, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/qamaria-yemeni-coffee-little-canada-minnesota/); [Hoodline, 2026](https://hoodline.com/2026/07/yemeni-coffee-chain-haraz-takes-over-lyn-lake-s-buzziest-corner/); [The Heavy Table, 2024](https://heavytable.substack.com/p/palestinian-bakeries-and-central); [Racket, 2026](https://racketmn.com/saffron-returns-bauhaus-is-back-and-toms-thai-opens-in-uptown)).

**Generations.** Second- and third-generation families are carrying on institutions their parents built, from Holy Land to St. Maron's festival kitchen ([WCCO](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/meet-the-family-behind-holy-land-the-hummus-mecca-of-minneapolis/); [Racket, 2022](https://racketmn.com/st-marons-lebanese-festival-northeast-minneapolis)). Student groups founded since 2020 aim to connect young Arab Minnesotans across faith and nationality ([Minnesota Daily, 2021](https://mndaily.com/city/arab-student-association-to-connect-umn-arab-students/01/25/2021/snoadmin/)).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **Commercial revival.** A neighborhood development leader credited Majdi Wadi with helping keep Central Avenue from further decline in the late 1990s and said he had invested millions of dollars in the street ([Twin Cities Business, 2009](https://tcbmag.com/hummus-for-the-masses/)). After the September 11 attacks, when the store got a bomb threat, so many Minnesotans came to show support that September 13, 2001, became Holy Land's busiest day ([Twin Cities Business](https://tcbmag.com/hummus-for-the-masses/)).
- **A national arts presence from St. Paul.** Mizna has published Arab American writing since 1999 and run the Midwest's longest-running Arab film festival since 2003 ([Mizna](https://mizna.org/about/)).
- **Bridges between peoples.** IARP built a Minneapolis-Najaf sister-city relationship and community storytelling projects ([IARP](https://www.reconciliationproject.org/our-story)); St. Maron's festival has invited the public to Lebanese food and dance since 1990 ([Racket, 2022](https://racketmn.com/st-marons-lebanese-festival-northeast-minneapolis)); and churches and mosques have hosted shared iftars ([Sahan Journal, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/ramadan-2026-events-iftar-minnesota/)).
- **Skilled newcomers.** Doctors, engineers, hospital administrators and other professionals have brought their training to Minnesota, even when U.S. licensing rules kept them from using it right away ([MPR News, 2008](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2008/09/08/from-iraq-to-minnesota-a-new-life); [MPR News, 2014](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2014/12/01/iraqi-refugees-dream-of-reuniting-family-in-minnesota)).
- **Language.** More than 15,000 Minnesotans speak Arabic at home, one of the world's major languages, and 3,363 students bring it into Minnesota schools ([Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B16001&geo_ids=04000US27); [MDE](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/other/261381.pdf)).

## What could not be established

- **One reliable total.** There is no census count of "Arab Minnesotans." Ancestry (about 26,400 plus about 3,000 Sudanese), home language (about 15,200) and birthplace each tell part of the story, and margins of error are wide for every group except Lebanese.
- **Income, poverty, housing, disability and health for any group except Lebanese.** No published Minnesota tables were found for Iraqi, Egyptian, Palestinian, Sudanese, Syrian, Yemeni or Moroccan Minnesotans.
- **Arrival histories for Egyptians, Sudanese, Syrians, Yemenis and Moroccans.** No Minnesota history was found. State refugee data group Syria and Sudan under "Other."
- **Mosques by community.** No Minnesota mosque was documented as primarily Arab, Iraqi or Sudanese; the founding story of the Shia center on Brooklyn Boulevard was not found.
- **Christian groups beyond the three parishes named.** No Minnesota Chaldean, Syriac, Melkite or Arabic-language Protestant congregation was confirmed. St. George Antiochian Orthodox Church's current services could not be confirmed from a current page.
- **Businesses beyond food.** No Arab American chamber of commerce, law firm, accounting firm, insurance agency, travel agency or clinic was identified from an opened source. Several restaurants named in recent coverage (Mim's Cafe, Shish, Zait & Zaatar, Al Amir Bakery, Yemeni Cuisine, Holy Land's current hours) could not be confirmed open from their own pages.
- **Disability.** No Minnesota disability data for any Arab group but Lebanese, no Minnesota organization focused on disability among Arabic speakers, and no Minnesota source on the Arabic words families use for disability.
- **Elected officials.** No Arab American state legislator was identified.
- **Public celebrations.** No Minnesota event for any Arab national day, for Ashura, or for Coptic or Orthodox feasts was found; no Minnesota proclamation of Arab American Heritage Month was found.
- **Blocked or rate-limited sources.** Pages on mn.gov, the Star Tribune, the Minnesota Reformer, Axios, Explore Minnesota and the Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder could not be opened, and Sahan Journal limited access for long stretches. A Spokesman-Recorder report on Sudanese Minnesotans (July 2024), Sahan Journal's April 2023 report on Sudanese Minnesotans' reaction to the war, Sahan Journal's 2024 report on Lebanese Minnesotans and the Israeli strikes in Lebanon, and its December 2024 report on Syrian Minnesotans after the fall of Assad were not read.
