# Kenyans in Minnesota

## 1. People and place

**How many, and why the counts differ.** Minnesota Compass, using the American Community Survey (ACS) for 2020 to 2024, counts 20,222 Kenyan Minnesotans, give or take 3,897. It places 71.4 percent in the Twin Cities and 28.6 percent in Greater Minnesota, each give or take 7.9 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan)). Compass builds its cultural communities from a mix of self-reported race, ancestry, birthplace and parents' characteristics, so its count includes U.S.-born children of Kenyan parents ([Minnesota Compass, cultural communities](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities)). Its separate immigrant-group page lists 13,699 Kenyan immigrants ([Minnesota Compass, by immigrant group](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/immigration)).

The raw Census tables give two narrower counts. In the 2020 to 2024 ACS, 16,726 Minnesotans (give or take 1,615) were born in Kenya, and 9,342 (give or take 1,381) named Kenyan as an ancestry ([ACS B05006 via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US27); [ACS B04006 via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B04006&geo_ids=04000US27)). The single-year 2024 estimate of Kenya-born Minnesotans is 17,309, give or take 3,334 ([ACS 2024 1-year](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_1yr?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US27)). These are different questions. "Born in Kenya" includes people of every ethnicity born there, including Somali and other refugees who grew up in Kenyan camps. MinnPost noted in 2008 that many Somalis and Ethiopians lived in refugee camps in northeastern Kenya before coming to Minnesota ([MinnPost, Feb. 2008](https://www.minnpost.com/politics-policy/2008/02/minnesotas-east-african-immigrants-watch-horror-and-wait-news-kenya/)), and a 2015 St. Cloud Times story, for example, profiled two Somali friends who grew up in Kenya's Dadaab camp and settled in St. Cloud ([St. Cloud State TODAY, reprinting the St. Cloud Times, July 13, 2015](https://today.stcloudstate.edu/2015/07/13/kenyan-refugee-camp-friendship-rekindled-in-st-cloud/)). The same is true for Oromo Minnesotans: MNopedia writes that after 1992 a wave of Oromo political refugees fled Ethiopia "mainly for Kenya," many later reaching Minnesota, and that Kenya itself has about half a million Oromo people ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/oromos-minnesota-making-little-oromia)). No published source explains exactly how Compass sorts a Kenya-born Somali Minnesotan, so the overlap between this page and the Somali and Oromo pages cannot be measured. Kenya-born Somali and Oromo Minnesotans are mentioned here only where the Kenyan connection matters; their community life is described on those pages.

**A national hub.** By the ACS birthplace count, Minnesota has the second-largest Kenya-born population of any state, after Texas (22,911) and ahead of Washington (13,706) and California (12,881), 2020 to 2024 ([ACS B05006 by state](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=040|01000US)). LeadingAge Minnesota's workforce director told LeadingAge in 2024 that Minnesota's Kenyan population is the largest per capita of any state; that is her statement, not a published ranking ([LeadingAge, Oct. 23, 2024](https://leadingage.org/how-an-eye-opening-visit-to-kenya-led-to-new-connections-and-recruitment-of-nursing-staff/)).

**Where people live.** Kenyan Minnesotans are more spread across the state than Ethiopian or Liberian Minnesotans. Compass puts 28.6 percent in Greater Minnesota, against 10.3 percent of Ethiopian and 13.2 percent of Liberian Minnesotans ([Compass, Kenyan](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan); [Compass, Ethiopian](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/ethiopian); [Compass, Liberian](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/liberian)). By county, the largest Kenya-born counts in 2020 to 2024 were Hennepin (6,336), Ramsey (2,298), Dakota (1,555), Anoka (1,300) and Stearns (1,174), followed by Wright, Scott, Sherburne, Benton, Olmsted and Kandiyohi ([ACS B05006 by county](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=050|04000US27)). By city, the largest were Minneapolis (about 1,790), St. Paul (about 1,690), St. Cloud (about 1,400) and Bloomington (about 1,010), then Otsego, Brooklyn Park, New Hope, Burnsville, Lakeville and Shakopee, each a few hundred. City margins of error are large; Brooklyn Park's 556 carries a margin of 354 ([ACS B05006 by place](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=160|04000US27)). The visible institutions cluster in the northwest suburbs: the Kenyan Community Seventh-day Adventist Church in Brooklyn Center, the largest Kenyan association's registered address in Brooklyn Center, and the Kenya Embassy's October 2026 consular days in Brooklyn Park ([Kenyan Community SDA Church](https://kenyancommunitymn.adventistchurch.org/about/); [ProPublica, Mwanyagetinge](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/201575846); [Kenya Embassy, Sept. 8, 2026](https://kenyaembassydc.org/mcs-minnesota/)). Newer Kenyan associations registered in Rochester (tax exemption 2025 and 2026) and in Mankato (2026) point to communities outside the metro as well ([ProPublica, Kenya Rochester Community](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/994622702); [ProPublica, Rochester Kenyan Community Association](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/422068750); [ProPublica, Southwest Minnesota Kenyan Community Welfare Association](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/814438404)).

**Young, mostly immigrant, and still arriving.** In the Compass profile, 40.8 percent are 17 or younger and 54.7 percent are 18 to 64; the count of people 65 and older is too small to publish, and only 1.7 percent are 55 to 64. Foreign-born residents are 68.8 percent, give or take 5.7 points. In the year before the survey, 3.5 percent had lived outside the U.S. ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan)). This is a working-age community with many children and very few elders so far.

**How people came.** The available sources point to several paths rather than one founding event. Students came first in the published stories: Mshale's founder Tom Gitaa moved from Kenya to Minnesota for college, planning to return home, and graduated from Metro State in 1996 ([MPR News, June 7, 2022](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2022/06/07/minnesotas-longest-running-newspaper-serving-african-immigrants-looks-to-the-future); [Metro State, spring 2025](https://www.metrostate.edu/buzz/spring-2025/news-and-notes)). Families followed; a Kenyan-born state representative came at age nine from Nyamira County with her family, which spent 11 years in the immigration system ([CBS Minnesota, Nov. 22, 2024](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-elects-first-kenyan-born-legislator/)). Health care work is a current path: Minnesota senior-care providers are recruiting Kenyan nurses on employment-based visas ([LeadingAge](https://leadingage.org/how-an-eye-opening-visit-to-kenya-led-to-new-connections-and-recruitment-of-nursing-staff/)). Students still come: Minnesota State University, Mankato counted 49 students from Kenya in fall 2023 ([Minnesota State Mankato, FY24 report](https://mankato.mnsu.edu/globalassets/global-education/global-ed-annual-report-fy24.pdf)). The Minnesota Department of Health's refugee-arrival table for 1999 to 2024 names only nine countries of origin and does not break out Kenya, so it cannot show how many came as refugees ([MDH, refugee arrivals by country](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/country.csv)).

**Peoples and languages.** Kenyan Minnesotans come from many Kenyan peoples. The Minnesota group with the most public record is from western Kenya: the Kenyan-born state representative was born in Nyamira County ([CBS Minnesota](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-elects-first-kenyan-born-legislator/)) and was president of Mwanyagetinge, which CCX Media calls the largest organized group of Kenyans in Minnesota, in 2021 and 2022 ([CCX Media, April 15, 2021](https://ccxmedia.org/news/kenyan-immigrant-organization-demands-peaceful-justice/); [ProPublica, Mwanyagetinge](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/201575846)). Kenyans.co.ke reported in 2023 that Mwanyagetinge was founded in the mid-1980s and draws many of its members from the Kisii (Abagusii) community, who were its first official members ([Kenyans.co.ke, Feb. 20, 2023](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/85894-mwanyagetinge-inside-largest-organised-group-kenyans-minnesota)). Compass reports that 65.1 percent of Kenyan Minnesotans 5 and older speak a language other than English at home and 26.5 percent speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan)). The ACS does not publish Gikuyu, Dholuo, Ekegusii or other Kenyan languages separately for Minnesota. Its nearest line, "Swahili or other languages of Central, Eastern, and Southern Africa," counts 13,678 Minnesotans 5 and older, of whom 4,185 speak English less than "very well"; that line also includes Tanzanians, Congolese and others ([ACS B16001](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B16001&geo_ids=04000US27)).

## 2. All walks of life

**Income spreads widely.** Compass reports a median household income of $92,570 (2024 dollars, give or take $19,152) for Kenyan Minnesota households. Nearly half of households, 48.3 percent, have incomes of $100,000 or more, while 18.1 percent have less than $35,000 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan)). The margins are wide, but the picture is a community with a large comfortable middle and upper-middle group and a real share of lower-income households.

**Poverty and near-poverty.** Of Kenyan Minnesotans, 10.6 percent live below the poverty line and another 15.3 percent between 150 and 199 percent of it. Poverty among young adults 18 to 24 is 22.2 percent, though that figure's margin is 13.2 points. Among adults 18 to 64 it is 9.9 percent ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan)). The 2020 to 2024 figures differ from the 2019 to 2023 figures that are often quoted; both are estimates from small samples.

**Homes.** About 40.7 percent of households own their homes and 59.3 percent rent (margins of about 11 points). Of renter households, 37.7 percent are cost-burdened, and the median gross rent is $1,390. Average household size is 3.31. One in ten households (10.8 percent) has no vehicle ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan)).

**Education.** Among Kenyan Minnesotans 25 and older, 91.5 percent have finished high school and 39.4 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more; 13.8 percent hold a graduate or professional degree. The largest single group, 34.2 percent, has some college or an associate degree. At the other end, 8.5 percent did not finish high school ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan)).

**Professionals and public leaders.** Dr. Edwin Bogonko, a physician trained at the University of Nairobi who came to the U.S. in 1999 and practices with Allina Health in Shakopee, served as the 2024 president of the Minnesota Medical Association; Bizna Kenya reported him as the first African to hold the post ([Bizna Kenya, Oct. 7, 2024](https://biznakenya.com/dr-edwin-bogonko/); [Minnesota Medical Association, past presidents](https://www.mnmed.org/about-us/governance-and-leadership/past-presidents)). The Kenyan-born state representative, with a bachelor's degree and MBA from Bethel University, listed her occupation as business executive and management when elected ([Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/legdb/fulldetail?id=15634)). Tom Gitaa built Mshale into a newspaper business that MPR calls Minnesota's longest-running newspaper serving African immigrants ([MPR News, 2022](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2022/06/07/minnesotas-longest-running-newspaper-serving-african-immigrants-looks-to-the-future)). Augsburg University's current faculty directory lists Mzenga Wanyama, a professor who in 2018 publicly fought removal to Kenya ([Augsburg University](https://www.augsburg.edu/faculty/wanyama-shekela/); [Women's March Minnesota, 2018](https://www.womensmarchmn.com/blog/support-augsburg-professor-mzenga-wanyama/8/17/2018)).

**Working families and small owners.** Beyond the public names are wage-earning families, many tied to the health-care jobs described in section 3, and family businesses such as Tamu Grill, which opens four afternoons a week, and Karibu Grocery and Deli, opened by three siblings with loans and city grants ([Tamu Grill](https://tamugrill.com/); [Business Today Kenya](https://businesstoday.co.ke/kenyan-family-thrives-in-minessota-serving-local-delights/)).

**Hardship.** The poverty and cost-burden figures above are the main measured signs. Others come through stories: the Kenyan-born state representative's family came within 48 hours of deportation before a last-minute reversal ([CBS Minnesota](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-elects-first-kenyan-born-legislator/)). No published Minnesota figure on Kenyan wealth, net worth or homelessness was found.

## 3. Work and the economy

**Employment.** Compass reports that 90.9 percent of Kenyan Minnesotans aged 18 to 64 are employed, give or take 3.5 points, including 90.6 percent of women ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan)). The source does not break this down by occupation. Most workers drive to work (87.3 percent), and 29.7 percent travel 30 minutes or more ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan)).

**Health care and senior care.** The clearest documented industry is health care, especially long-term care. LeadingAge Minnesota, which represents senior-care providers, said in May 2024 that Kenyan diaspora professionals already make "significant contributions" to Minnesota's long-term care workforce, at an event in Burnsville with Kenya's Principal Secretary for Diaspora Affairs ([LeadingAge Minnesota, May 30, 2024](https://www.leadingagemn.org/news-insights/leadingage-minnesota-welcomes-honorable-principal-secretary-from-the-kenya-state-department-for-diaspora-affairs-at-ebenezer-ridges/)). Its workforce director told LeadingAge that "there are many Kenyans already within our workforce" and that providers want to sponsor family members of current employees ([LeadingAge, 2024](https://leadingage.org/how-an-eye-opening-visit-to-kenya-led-to-new-connections-and-recruitment-of-nursing-staff/)).

**A recruitment pipeline, 2024 to now.** In January 2024 a delegation of Minnesota providers, led by a state representative, went to Nairobi to support a new NCLEX nursing-exam center and explore recruitment ([LeadingAge Minnesota, Feb. 15, 2024](https://www.leadingagemn.org/news-insights/exploring-global-workforce-solutions-in-nairobi-kenya/)). One Fridley-based provider, Benedictine, interviewed 15 registered nurses there, made 12 offers and had six accepted; such hires typically take 18 to 24 months on EB-3 visas ([LeadingAge, Oct. 23, 2024](https://leadingage.org/how-an-eye-opening-visit-to-kenya-led-to-new-connections-and-recruitment-of-nursing-staff/)). A January 2025 report noted about 17,000 openings in Minnesota aging services, and said LeadingAge members sponsor Kenyan students and give scholarships to nurses abroad ([KAXE, Jan. 10, 2025](https://www.kaxe.org/minnesota-news/2025-01-10/staff-strapped-minnesota-nursing-homes-look-to-new-americans-for-help)). Kenya's side matters too: LeadingAge Minnesota reported that remittances from Kenyans abroad equal about 3 percent of Kenya's economy ([LeadingAge Minnesota](https://www.leadingagemn.org/news-insights/exploring-global-workforce-solutions-in-nairobi-kenya/)).

**Medicine.** Dr. Bogonko's path, from University of Nairobi graduate in 1993 to Minnesota Medical Association member from 2010 and its 2024 president, shows a physician route alongside the nursing one ([Bizna Kenya](https://biznakenya.com/dr-edwin-bogonko/)).

**Other work.** Kenyan Minnesotans also work in media (Mshale), higher education (Augsburg), food service and retail (Tamu Grill, Karibu), and business management. No Minnesota source with occupation data for Kenyan Minnesotans as a group was found, and no Kenyan-specific labor union history was found.

**Entrepreneurship and mutual money.** Some Kenyan associations pool large sums. Pamoja Kenya, a Shakopee-based mutual-benefit group the IRS classes with local benevolent life-insurance associations, took in $1,140,422 in 2025, almost all of it from members ([ProPublica, Pamoja Kenya](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/472262327)). The newly registered Southwest Minnesota Kenyan Community Welfare Association in Mankato is also a mutual-benefit group ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/814438404)). The filings do not describe benefits, so what members pay for is not established here.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

The public record of Kenyan-owned businesses in Minnesota is short. There is no Kenyan business district; Kenyan Minnesotans live across the metro and Greater Minnesota and shop in shared East African and general markets. What could be confirmed:

- **Tamu Grill and Catering**, Minneapolis, in the former Viking Bar building on Riverside Avenue. Chef George "Jojo" Ndege's family restaurant, opened under this name on July 9, 2022 after starting as Kilimanjaro Grill, specializes in tilapia, beef and coconut-curry beans, samosas, chapati and ugali; its own site lists Thursday to Sunday hours, 2 to 8 p.m. Its manager Fanaka Ndege told Kenyans.co.ke in 2022, "we're the only Kenyan restaurant in the city" ([Tamu Grill](https://tamugrill.com/); [Kenyans.co.ke, July 12, 2022](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/77156-kenyan-musician-converts-1905-american-building-posh-restaurant); [City Cast Twin Cities](https://twincities.citycast.fm/keep-it-local/tamu-grill-kenyan-food-spotlight)).
- **Karibu Grocery and Deli**, 719 Payne Ave., St. Paul (East Side). Opened in October 2018 by three Ali siblings, from a Kenyan family that came to Minnesota in the late 1990s, it sells halal meat, groceries, East African dishes and Kenyan coffee ([Business Today Kenya, 2021](https://businesstoday.co.ke/kenyan-family-thrives-in-minessota-serving-local-delights/); [Karibu](https://karibudeli.com/)).
- **Mshale**, the African immigrant newspaper founded by Tom Gitaa in the mid-1990s; Meet Minneapolis's media directory, updated October 15, 2026, lists a monthly print edition, weekly newsletter and daily website ([Meet Minneapolis](https://www.minneapolis.org/meetings-events/destination-services/planning-services/bipoc-media/)).
- **African Rhythms** on KFAI radio, Sundays noon to 2 p.m., co-hosted by George "Jojo" Ndege; its playlist page shows an October 25, 2026 show ([KFAI via Spinitron](https://spinitron.com/KFAI/show/12952/African-Rhythms)).

No Kenyan-owned professional firm, clinic, home-care agency, tax preparer, travel agent, salon or insurance agency could be confirmed open from a current page. Kenyan associations and congregations with current records are described in sections 5 and 9.

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

**Christian congregations are the most visible.** No survey of Kenyan Minnesotans' religion was found. Public life in the available sources is mainly Christian:

- **Kenyan Community Seventh-day Adventist Church**, 6625 Humboldt Ave. N., Brooklyn Center. The church says its name comes from Kenyan Adventist co-founders in 1998 and that it serves "people from every tribe and nation." Its online calendar shows youth small-group meetings through October 2026 ([About](https://kenyancommunitymn.adventistchurch.org/about/); [Calendar](https://kenyancommunitymn.adventistchurch.org/events/)). It sits next to the Brooklyn Center police station; after the police killing of Daunte Wright in April 2021, Mwanyagetinge's president called for peaceful protest ([CCX Media, April 15, 2021](https://ccxmedia.org/news/kenyan-immigrant-organization-demands-peaceful-justice/)).
- **Minnesota Swahili Christian Congregation**, at Holy Trinity Lutheran Church, 2730 E. 31st St., Minneapolis. It holds Lutheran worship in Swahili on Sundays at noon, welcoming "all Swahili speakers and people from east African countries"; its pastor is from Tanzania ([Holy Trinity Lutheran](https://htlcmpls.org/our-partners/)). MPR reported Swahili services there since 1993, then serving members from Tanzania ([MPR News, Dec. 21, 2006](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2006/12/21/newmightyfortress)).
- **Kenyan Christian Outreach Church**, St. Paul, tax-exempt since 2007 ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/841714912)). Whether it still meets was not confirmed.

No opened source names a Minnesota Catholic, Presbyterian, Anglican or Pentecostal congregation that is specifically Kenyan. Where Kenyan Minnesotans who are not Adventists worship was not measured.

**Muslims.** No Minnesota source describes Kenyan Muslim religious life, and no mosque that identifies as Kenyan was found. Kenya-born Somali Minnesotans (section 1) are part of the Muslim institutions described on the Somali page.

**Faith and civic life.** Congregations are also civic spaces. In October 2026 the Kenya Embassy held its four-day Minnesota consular service at Grace Fellowship Church in Brooklyn Park ([Kenya Embassy](https://kenyaembassydc.org/mcs-minnesota/)).

No source measures Kenyan Minnesotans of other faiths or of no faith.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

**Kenyan national days.** Kenyans.co.ke describes three national holidays created by Kenya's 2010 constitution: Madaraka Day on June 1, marking self-rule in 1963; Mashujaa (Heroes') Day on October 20; and Jamhuri (Republic) Day on December 12, marking full independence from Britain on December 12, 1963 ([Kenyans.co.ke, 2019](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/42837-kenya-gazette-official-public-holidays-kenya)). Kenya's 2026 holiday list also includes Mazingira (Environment) Day on October 10 ([CalendarLabs, 2026](https://www.calendarlabs.com/holidays/kenya/2026)). Mshale reported a Kenya Fest for Madaraka Day at Centennial Park in Brooklyn Center on June 1, 2024, organized by George "Jojo" Ndege with Kenyan associations, but that report could not be opened, and no 2025 or 2026 Minnesota Madaraka, Mashujaa or Jamhuri event could be confirmed. The Kenya Embassy in Washington hosts its own Jamhuri celebrations, with a gallery from December 14, 2024 ([Kenya Embassy](https://www.kenyaembassydc.org/)).

**Christian holidays.** CalendarLabs describes Kenya as a Christian-dominated nation where Christmas is the most celebrated holiday; Good Friday and Easter Monday are public holidays there ([CalendarLabs, 2026](https://www.calendarlabs.com/holidays/kenya/2026)). Kenyans.co.ke describes Christmas as the time most Kenyans travel to their rural homes ([Kenyans.co.ke](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/42837-kenya-gazette-official-public-holidays-kenya)). No Kenyan-specific Minnesota Christmas or Easter event was found.

**Muslim holidays.** Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha matter in Muslim Kenyan households; in Kenya, Eid al-Adha 2026 was a public holiday on May 27 ([CalendarLabs](https://www.calendarlabs.com/holidays/kenya/2026)). Minnesota mosques set their own dates. No Kenyan-specific Minnesota Eid event was found.

**Other gatherings.** Association elections, funerals and fundraisers are covered mainly in Mshale, which could not be reopened, so they are not described here.

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Households.** Compass counts 5,780 Kenyan Minnesota households, averaging 3.31 people. A third (33.1 percent) are married couples with children under 18, 16.9 percent are married couples without children at home, 7.6 percent are single mothers with children, and 19.3 percent are people living alone ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan)). The age profile, with four in ten under 18 and very few over 65, suggests that most households are raising children and that few Kenyan elders yet live in Minnesota.

**Mutual aid around life and death.** Kenyan associations in Minnesota raise money among members. Kenyans.co.ke reported in 2023 that many Mwanyagetinge members came to the U.S. young with their families and have since married into other communities ([Kenyans.co.ke](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/85894-mwanyagetinge-inside-largest-organised-group-kenyans-minnesota)). Mwanyagetinge's revenue was $135,384 in 2021 and $53,805 in 2022, all from contributions ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/201575846)), and Pamoja Kenya's members paid in over $1.1 million in 2025 ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/472262327)). Burial in Kenya is one option families face. The Kenya Embassy issues, free of charge, a letter of no objection for remains or ashes to be sent to Kenya once the family supplies proof of Kenyan citizenship, a health-authority letter that the death was not from a contagious disease and funeral-home certificates ([Kenya Embassy](https://kenyaembassydc.org/transdeceased/)). What the mutual-benefit groups above pay for is not stated in their filings.

**Papers and identity.** Practical life includes Kenyan documents. In October 2026 the Kenya Embassy held four days of consular service in Brooklyn Park for Kenyan national ID registration, e-passports, citizenship and birth certificates for Kenyan children born abroad ([Kenya Embassy, Sept. 8, 2026](https://kenyaembassydc.org/mcs-minnesota/)).

**Food.** Tamu Grill describes Kenyan cooking as "a blend of African, Indian, and Middle Eastern flavors," and its chef says ugali, a stiff cornmeal staple, is "very rare" to find in the Twin Cities and "screams 'home'" for those who know it ([Tamu Grill](https://tamugrill.com/); [City Cast Twin Cities](https://twincities.citycast.fm/keep-it-local/tamu-grill-kenyan-food-spotlight)). Karibu sells Kenyan coffee and Vimto, a drink a Kenyan customer recognized from home ([Business Today Kenya](https://businesstoday.co.ke/kenyan-family-thrives-in-minessota-serving-local-delights/)).

No Minnesota source was found on Kenyan marriage customs, dress, childhood or aging here, so those are not described.

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Media.** Mshale, founded by Tom Gitaa in the mid-1990s as a two-page digest of East African news, is Minnesota's longest-running newspaper serving African immigrants by MPR's account, and is still listed in 2026 with a monthly print edition and daily website ([MPR News, 2022](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2022/06/07/minnesotas-longest-running-newspaper-serving-african-immigrants-looks-to-the-future); [Meet Minneapolis](https://www.minneapolis.org/meetings-events/destination-services/planning-services/bipoc-media/)). Gitaa, a 1996 Metro State graduate, also serves on the board of the United Nations Association, Metro State reported in 2025 ([Metro State](https://www.metrostate.edu/buzz/spring-2025/news-and-notes)). On radio, George "Jojo" Ndege co-hosts KFAI's weekly African Rhythms ([KFAI](https://spinitron.com/KFAI/show/12952/African-Rhythms)).

**Music.** Fanaka Ndege, a Minneapolis rapper known as Fanaka Nation, was reported in 2022 to host a monthly dance night bringing Kenyans together to genge, benga and chakacha music ([Kenyans.co.ke, 2022](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/77156-kenyan-musician-converts-1905-american-building-posh-restaurant)). Visiting Kenyan artists also perform here: the Kenyan Boys Choir sang at schools, the Department of Education and the State Capitol in November 2017 ([MPR News, Nov. 10, 2017](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2017/11/10/theft-of-the-blog-missing-the-concert)).

**Running.** Kenyan elite runners are regular winners of Minnesota road races; Dominic Ondoro won his third straight Twin Cities Marathon in 2017 and Jane Kibii repeated as women's champion ([MPR News, Oct. 1, 2017](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2017/10/01/two-kenyans-win-twin-cities-marathon)). These are mostly visiting professionals, not Kenyan Minnesotans.

**Festivals.** Kenyan food and crafts appear at general festivals; Tamu Grill has run a stand at Taste of Minnesota, selling samosas, Kenyan sausage, passion juice, Kenyan coffee and beaded bracelets from Nairobi ([City Cast Twin Cities](https://twincities.citycast.fm/keep-it-local/tamu-grill-kenyan-food-spotlight)).

No Minnesota-based Kenyan novelist, visual artist or museum was found in a published source.

## 9. Civic and political life

**The first Kenyan-born state legislator.** A DFL state representative from Brooklyn Park represents House District 38A, covering Osseo and parts of Brooklyn Park. The Legislative Reference Library records her birth country as Kenya, notes she is "the first Minnesota state legislator of Kenyan heritage," and lists her committees as Housing Finance and Policy, Human Services Finance and Policy, and Legacy Finance ([Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/legdb/fulldetail?id=15634)). CBS Minnesota called her the first Kenyan-born state legislator in the country ([CBS Minnesota, Nov. 22, 2024](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-elects-first-kenyan-born-legislator/)). Her path ran through community politics: she lost a 2022 DFL Senate primary, won the 2024 House primary by 50 votes over former Brooklyn Park council member Wynfred Russell, and then won the seat with 64.78 percent ([CCX Media](https://ccxmedia.org/?s=kenyan)). She faces Republican Charlie Kistler on November 3, 2026 ([The Ballot Brief](https://theballotbrief.com/state/minnesota/hennepin-county/minnesota-house-district-38a)).

**Local candidates.** In 2020 three Kenyan-born Minnesotans ran for city councils and lost: Henry Momanyi in Brooklyn Park's West District, as an independent, and Ukasha Dakane and Janet Kitui in Edina (spelled "Edna" in the report). Momanyi campaigned on police-community relations; Kitui on affordable housing; Dakane, who ran a nonprofit on immigrant employment, on youth and minority advocacy ([Kenyans.co.ke, Nov. 5, 2020](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/58882-meet-3-kenyans-who-vied-seats-us-elections); [VOA Africa, Nov. 2, 2020](https://www.voaafrica.com/amp/kenyan-american-candidate-talks-minnesota-city-council-/5645213.html)).

**Associations.** Mwanyagetinge, tax-exempt since 2005 and registered in Brooklyn Center, is described by CCX Media as the largest organized group of Kenyans in Minnesota. Kenyans.co.ke reported in 2023 that it offers members training, job placement, advocacy and wealth-building help, holds an annual family picnic at Centennial Park in Brooklyn Center, and elects a president for two-year terms by electronic ballot after online campaigns; Roselidah Nyaberi won its February 18, 2023 election with 62 percent ([Kenyans.co.ke, Feb. 20, 2023](https://www.kenyans.co.ke/news/85894-mwanyagetinge-inside-largest-organised-group-kenyans-minnesota)). ProPublica's IRS data show its officers in 2022 and note that it is not on the IRS's most recent list of exempt organizations ([CCX Media](https://ccxmedia.org/news/kenyan-immigrant-organization-demands-peaceful-justice/); [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/201575846)). Other registered groups include the Minnesota Kenyan International Development Association (Shakopee, 2004), the Kenya Society for Academic Advancement (Brooklyn Center, 2009), and newer Kenyan community associations in Rochester and Mankato ([ProPublica, MKIDA](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/200803200); [ProPublica, KSAA](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/263290257); [ProPublica, RKCA](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/422068750)). Kenyan Women in the United States (KWITU) lists two Minnesota state representatives ([KWITU](https://www.kwitu.org/Kwitu_state_affairs)).

**Engagement in moments of crisis.** In January 2008, about 100 Kenyan Minnesotans gathered at the Capitol to sing Kenya's anthem and ask for international help to end post-election killing at home ([MPR News, Jan. 2, 2008](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2008/01/02/a-plea-for-kenya)). MinnPost that February profiled two Kenyan Minnesotans working on the crisis from here: Joshua Wanyama, a University of Minnesota architecture graduate whose Minneapolis publication African Path helped volunteers map reported violence, and Julia Opoti, a Metro State graduate and Mshale editor. Both rejected the framing of the violence as tribal; Opoti, from an ethnically mixed family in Kisumu, said, "I was raised as a Kenyan, not someone loyal to a particular tribe," and Wanyama said ethnicity mattered more to his parents' generation than to young people ([MinnPost, Feb. 2008](https://www.minnpost.com/politics-policy/2008/02/minnesotas-east-african-immigrants-watch-horror-and-wait-news-kenya/)). Those are two people's views, not a survey. In April 2021 Mwanyagetinge both called for peaceful justice after Daunte Wright's killing and organized a COVID-19 vaccine event with the Minnesota Department of Health and Hennepin County in Brooklyn Park, with a goal of 200 sign-ups ([CCX Media](https://ccxmedia.org/news/kenyan-immigrant-organization-demands-peaceful-justice/); [CCX Media](https://ccxmedia.org/news/african-immigrant-organization-holds-covid-19-vaccination-event/)).

**Ties to Kenya's government.** Kenya's ambassador Lazarus Amayo visited in August 2022, meeting political and business leaders and the diaspora at the Maple Grove Community Center while embassy staff offered consular services ([Kenya Embassy, 2022](https://kenyaembassydc.org/?p=5456)). Kenya's State Department for Diaspora Affairs has engaged Minnesota directly, through the Nairobi exam-center project and a May 2024 visit to Burnsville ([LeadingAge Minnesota](https://www.leadingagemn.org/news-insights/leadingage-minnesota-welcomes-honorable-principal-secretary-from-the-kenya-state-department-for-diaspora-affairs-at-ebenezer-ridges/)). Kenyan politics also enters Minnesota rooms; the 2008 rally shows how events at home move people here. No public source documents current disagreements among Kenyan Minnesota associations.

## 10. Education

**Attainment.** As noted in section 2, 39.4 percent of adults 25 and older hold a bachelor's degree or more, and 34.2 percent have some college or an associate degree ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan)).

**Students from Kenya.** Minnesota State University, Mankato reported 49 students from Kenya in fall 2023, out of 1,719 international students ([Minnesota State Mankato](https://mankato.mnsu.edu/globalassets/global-education/global-ed-annual-report-fy24.pdf)). Earlier arrivals studied at Metro State (Tom Gitaa, class of 1996), Bethel University (the Kenyan-born state representative's bachelor's and MBA) and the University of Minnesota (Joshua Wanyama, architecture) ([MinnPost, 2008](https://www.minnpost.com/politics-policy/2008/02/minnesotas-east-african-immigrants-watch-horror-and-wait-news-kenya/); [Metro State](https://www.metrostate.edu/buzz/spring-2025/news-and-notes); [Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/legdb/fulldetail?id=15634)).

**Scholars and teachers.** Augsburg University lists Mzenga Wanyama among its faculty ([Augsburg](https://www.augsburg.edu/faculty/wanyama-shekela/)). The Kenya Society for Academic Advancement registered in Brooklyn Center in 2009 ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/263290257)); what it does now was not found.

**Children in school.** About 8,200 Kenyan Minnesotans are under 18 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan)). No Minnesota Department of Education data for Kenyan students as a group, no Kenyan language program and no Swahili course in a Minnesota K-12 district were found in published sources.

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Coverage.** Compass reports that 7.1 percent of Kenyan Minnesotans have no health insurance, give or take 2.9 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan)).

**Health workers in the family.** Because health care is the community's clearest documented industry (section 3), Kenyan Minnesotans are providers as well as patients. Dr. Bogonko, as Minnesota Medical Association president, pledged to work on physician wellness, rural health networks, prior authorization and access to mental health care ([Bizna Kenya](https://biznakenya.com/dr-edwin-bogonko/)).

**COVID-19.** In April 2021 Mwanyagetinge's president told CCX that COVID-19 "has had a difficult impact on their culture," and the group hosted a vaccine event with the state and county that also offered food, dental checkups and testing ([CCX Media, April 15, 2021](https://ccxmedia.org/news/african-immigrant-organization-holds-covid-19-vaccination-event/)).

**Ebola news from Kenya, October 2026.** Kenya recorded its first Ebola case in early October 2026, a man who arrived in Nairobi from the Democratic Republic of Congo; by late October there were nine confirmed cases and two deaths, with all contacts traced ([The African Mirror, Oct. 28, 2026](https://theafricanmirror.africa/news/kenyas-ebola-fight-held-at-the-edges-raging-at-the-core/)). This is homeland news; no Minnesota-specific guidance for travelers to Kenya was found on the Minnesota Department of Health's Ebola page, last updated in May 2026 ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/diseases/ebola/index.html)).

No Minnesota clinic tied specifically to the Kenyan community, no Kenyan-specific Minnesota health data and no source on traditional healing practices among Kenyan Minnesotans was found.

## 12. Disability

**What the numbers show, and what they cannot.** Compass reports that 5.8 percent of Kenyan Minnesotans have a disability, give or take 2.5 points, and 5.1 percent of those under 65 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan)). For all Minnesotans in the same survey years, about 11.6 percent report a disability, and about 8.2 percent of those under 65 (computed from [ACS C18108](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=C18108&geo_ids=04000US27)). Part of the gap is age: almost no Kenyan Minnesotans are over 65, when disability rises. Other possible reasons are open questions, not findings. Do employment visas and health screening select healthier immigrants? Do the survey questions capture how Kenyan families describe a condition? Are some disabilities not named because of stigma or because the questions were asked in English? No published source answers these for Kenyan Minnesotans.

**Who has been asked.** The Minnesota Governor's Council on Developmental Disabilities' 2017 survey included 52 East African respondents, of whom 4 percent were born in Kenya, roughly two people ([Governor's Council, 2017](https://mn.gov/mnddc/extra/customer-research/MN-Racial-Ethnic-Communities-Survey2017.txt)). That survey cannot speak for Kenyan families. The state's refugee health statistics do not break out Kenya as a country of origin ([MDH refugee statistics](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/index.html)).

**Where an impairment comes from.** No Minnesota source documents what produces impairments among Kenyan Minnesotans: birth, illness, injury at work, war or violence before migration, or aging. These remain questions, and none should be assumed.

**Who defines disability, and what a label opens.** The ACS figure rests on a short set of survey questions; schools, Medical Assistance and employers each use their own definitions. Does a label open services for a Kenyan family, or does it raise worries about visas, immigration cases or stigma? No published source has asked Kenyan Minnesotans. No Kenyan-specific disability organization in Minnesota was found.

**People.** No Kenyan Minnesotan disability leader or advocate with a public record was found.

## 13. What is changing now

**A second-term election, November 3, 2026.** The state's first Kenyan-born legislator is on the ballot for a second term ([The Ballot Brief](https://theballotbrief.com/state/minnesota/hennepin-county/minnesota-house-district-38a)).

**A growing recruitment pipeline.** Since 2024, Minnesota senior-care employers have been building a pipeline for Kenyan nurses, with long visa waits of 18 to 24 months or more ([LeadingAge](https://leadingage.org/how-an-eye-opening-visit-to-kenya-led-to-new-connections-and-recruitment-of-nursing-staff/); [KAXE](https://www.kaxe.org/minnesota-news/2025-01-10/staff-strapped-minnesota-nursing-homes-look-to-new-americans-for-help)). If it grows, the community will gain newly arrived health workers and their families.

**Immigration enforcement.** In December 2025, federal immigration operations in Minnesota focused publicly on Somali residents, and The African Mirror reported that Kenyan nationals were being listed by name on a new federal deportation database, adding fear in Kenyan diaspora families ([The African Mirror, Dec. 12-13, 2025](https://theafricanmirror.africa/news/fear-uncertainty-trumps-immigration-crackdown-targets-east-african-communities/)). In 2018 Augsburg professor Mzenga Wanyama publicly fought a federal decision to remove him to Kenya after 26 years in Minnesota, saying, "I don't think that Kenya allows me to have that freedom" ([Women's March Minnesota, Aug. 17, 2018](https://www.womensmarchmn.com/blog/support-augsburg-professor-mzenga-wanyama/8/17/2018)). Kenya's The Star reported that a federal list released December 10, 2025 named 15 Kenyan nationals arrested across several states, Minnesota among them, and that ICE data showed 1,282 Kenyans on the national deportation docket as of November 24, 2024 ([The Star, Dec. 13, 2025](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/infographics/2025-12-13-kenyans-listed-for-deportation-from-the-us)). No Minnesota source quantifies how current enforcement is affecting Kenyan Minnesotans as a whole.

**Spreading out.** New Kenyan associations registered in Rochester (2025 and 2026) and Mankato (2026) ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/422068750); [ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/814438404)), and the Greater Minnesota share in Compass rose from 22.7 percent in the 2019 to 2023 data to 28.6 percent in 2020 to 2024, a change within the margin of error ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan)).

**Generational change.** With 40.8 percent under 18 and most adults foreign-born, a large Minnesota-raised generation is coming of age ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan)). How it relates to Kenyan languages, churches and associations has not been studied in any published source.

**Ties with Kenya.** The Kenya Embassy's October 2026 consular days in Brooklyn Park and Kenya's diaspora-affairs outreach show a homeland government actively courting Minnesota Kenyans ([Kenya Embassy](https://kenyaembassydc.org/mcs-minnesota/); [LeadingAge Minnesota](https://www.leadingagemn.org/news-insights/leadingage-minnesota-welcomes-honorable-principal-secretary-from-the-kenya-state-department-for-diaspora-affairs-at-ebenezer-ridges/)).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **Caring for Minnesota's elders and patients.** Kenyan nurses, nursing assistants and other health workers are a recognized part of the long-term care workforce, and Minnesota employers are traveling to Kenya to recruit more ([LeadingAge Minnesota](https://www.leadingagemn.org/news-insights/leadingage-minnesota-welcomes-honorable-principal-secretary-from-the-kenya-state-department-for-diaspora-affairs-at-ebenezer-ridges/)).
- **High work rates and education.** About nine in ten working-age adults are employed, and nearly four in ten adults have a bachelor's degree or more ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/kenyan)).
- **Leadership.** The first Kenyan-born state legislator in the U.S. by CBS's account, the first African president of the Minnesota Medical Association by Bizna Kenya's account, and the founder of the state's longest-running African immigrant newspaper ([CBS Minnesota](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-elects-first-kenyan-born-legislator/); [Bizna Kenya](https://biznakenya.com/dr-edwin-bogonko/); [MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2022/06/07/minnesotas-longest-running-newspaper-serving-african-immigrants-looks-to-the-future)).
- **Organized mutual aid.** Associations pool large sums among members, and have used their networks for public health and calls for peace ([ProPublica](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/472262327); [CCX Media](https://ccxmedia.org/news/african-immigrant-organization-holds-covid-19-vaccination-event/)).
- **Bridges to Kenya.** Minnesota-Kenya ties run through families, development groups such as the Minnesota Kenyan International Development Association (tax-exempt since 2004), the Kenya Embassy's visits and the nursing pipeline ([ProPublica, MKIDA](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/200803200); [Kenya Embassy](https://kenyaembassydc.org/mcs-minnesota/); [LeadingAge](https://leadingage.org/how-an-eye-opening-visit-to-kenya-led-to-new-connections-and-recruitment-of-nursing-staff/)).
- **Statewide presence.** Kenyan Minnesotans live in St. Cloud, Rochester, Mankato, Willmar and the outer suburbs as well as the Twin Cities ([ACS B05006 by place](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=160|04000US27)).
