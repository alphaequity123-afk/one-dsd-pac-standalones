# Corrections to the existing Chinese Minnesota page

Page: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/chinese
Checked: October 31, 2026. Each item gives the claim, what the opened source says, the link and the date checked.

## Outdated figures

1. **Claim:** Minnesota Compass (ACS 2019–2023): about 41,717 people; Twin Cities 81.8 percent; Greater Minnesota 18.2 percent; 17 or younger 25.7 percent; 65+ 8.3 percent; foreign-born 57.5 percent; language other than English 62.5 percent; English less than "very well" 29.2 percent; high school or higher 86.0 percent; bachelor's or higher 62.4 percent; less than high school 14.0 percent; median household income about $103,600 (2022 dollars); poverty 8.6 percent; owner-occupied 69.5 percent; household size 2.59.
   **Source says:** Compass now shows 2020–2024: 45,330 (±3,937); Twin Cities 81.3 percent; Greater Minnesota 18.7 percent; 17 or younger 25.9 percent; 65+ 9.5 percent; foreign-born 56.3 percent; language other than English 62.4 percent; English less than "very well" 29.9 percent; high school or higher 85.9 percent; bachelor's or higher 61.2 percent; less than high school 14.1 percent; median household income $109,309 (±$13,183, 2024 dollars); poverty 9.1 percent; owner-occupied 69.6 percent; household size 2.56. The page repeats the old figures in many sections ("Greater Minnesota is 18.2 percent," "29.2 percent LEP," "$103,600 median," "8.6 percent poverty") and all of them should be updated together, with the survey years.
   **Link:** https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/chinese . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

2. **Claim:** Compass "puts Chinese ancestry near 41,717 people" as the count.
   **Source says:** Counts depend on the definition. The State Demographer's June 2026 presentation lists 48,592 Chinese and, separately, 2,545 Taiwanese Minnesotans for 2024; the state's 2026 economic chartbook, which puts people of more than one cultural group in a separate category, counts about 30,500 for 2019–2023. The page should say that Taiwanese are counted separately and that totals vary by method.
   **Links:** https://mn.gov/capm/assets/SusanBrower_StateOfAsianPacificMinnesotans_June2026_tcm1051-759113.pdf ; https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

## Errors or claims the opened sources do not support

3. **Claim:** Liang May Seen: "On July 21, 1892, he married Liang May Seen in San Francisco"; timeline "July 21, 1892: Married Woo Yee Sing."
   **Source says:** MNopedia (the source the page cites) gives July 21, **1889** as the day she escaped to the Presbyterian Mission Home, and says only that the couple married in "that summer" of 1892. July 21, 1892, appears to conflate the two dates.
   **Link:** https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/liang-may-seen-c1871-1946 . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

4. **Claim:** "In 1904 she opened a Chinese curio shop next to Westminster Presbyterian Church."
   **Source says:** MNopedia says she opened a curio shop in 1904 and that many white women she befriended were its patrons; it does not say where the shop was. "Next to Westminster" is not supported by MNopedia.
   **Link:** https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/liang-may-seen-c1871-1946 . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

5. **Claim:** Woo Yee Sing: restaurant renamed Yuen Faung Low in 1909; his 1925 funeral "blended Chinese and American customs."
   **Source says:** The MNopedia Liang May Seen entry cited by the page does not contain either fact. Could not be confirmed from an opened source; a separate MNopedia entry on Woo Yee Sing was not opened.
   **Link:** https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/liang-may-seen-c1871-1946 . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

6. **Claim:** Walter James opened the Nankin "on October 23, 1919, at 15 South 7th Street, and the restaurant lasted until February 1999."
   **Source says:** CAAM's Walter James page confirms only that he founded the Nankin in 1919. The exact date, address and 1999 closing were not found in any opened source.
   **Link:** https://caam.org/walter-james/ . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

7. **Claim (religion):** "None and secular are common answers, while Buddhist, Protestant, Catholic, folk practice, and ancestral rite are all present too."
   **Source says:** No Minnesota source opened gives religious affiliation or nonreligion for Chinese Minnesotans. Opened sources document Chinese Protestant congregations (Twin City Chinese Christian Church in Roseville, about 500 worshippers in Mandarin, Cantonese and English; Rochester Chinese Christian Church). No Chinese Buddhist temple, Mandarin Catholic Mass or Minnesota source on ancestral or folk practice was found. The sentence should be presented as a possibility to ask about, not a finding.
   **Links:** https://tcccc.org/en/ ; https://swbts.edu/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Twin-City-Chinese-Christian-Church.pdf ; https://www.rcccmn.org/ . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

8. **Claim (language):** "Taishanese, Hokkien, and Shanghainese exist in this country and may exist in this lobby."
   **Source says:** No Minnesota source opened counts speakers of any Chinese language by name. Opened sources confirm Mandarin and Cantonese in active use (trilingual church; Chinese Community Center tutoring in both) and that one weekend school teaches traditional characters with Zhuyin. Keep as a prompt to ask, not as a Minnesota fact.
   **Links:** https://tcccc.org/en/ ; https://cccminnesota.org/about/ ; https://twincitiescls.org/ . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.
