# Somali in Minnesota
Checked: October 30, 2026

## 1. People and place

**How many.** Minnesota Compass gives two counts, and the difference matters. For 2024 alone it reports 111,722 Somali Minnesotans, give or take 20,401, and calls this the Census Bureau's most accurate estimate of size. For everything else it pools the 2020 to 2024 survey, which counts 85,533, give or take 7,711 ([Minnesota Compass, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/somali)). You will see other numbers in the news. KTTC, citing the Census Bureau's American Community Survey, reported about 107,000 in 2024 ([KTTC, Dec. 3, 2025](https://www.kttc.com/2025/12/04/by-numbers-minnesotas-somali-population-according-census-data/)), MPR News said "more than 80,000" on air ([MPR News, Sept. 30, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/09/30/somali-brief-digital-outlet-focuses-on-somali-americans)), and one Minneapolis resident estimated "close to 150,000," which is his own estimate and not an official figure ([Sahan Journal, Oct. 15, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/somali-community-minnesota-trump-omar-gang-activity/)). All of the survey numbers are estimates of people who name Somali ancestry.

**Young, and increasingly born here.** In the Compass pool, 45.7 percent are 17 or younger and 3.4 percent are 65 or older. Foreign-born residents are 52.2 percent, give or take 3.2 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/somali)). KTTC, using a different year, reports that almost 58 percent were born in the United States ([KTTC](https://www.kttc.com/2025/12/04/by-numbers-minnesotas-somali-population-according-census-data/)). Among foreign-born Somali Minnesotans, KTTC reports that 87 percent are naturalized citizens and that almost half entered the country in 2010 or later. Somali students grew from 0.7 percent of Minnesota high school graduates in 2006 to 4 percent in 2022 ([DEED, March 2026](https://mn.gov/deed/newscenter/publications/trends/march-2026/somali.jsp)). Compass reports that 43 percent of all Somali-born immigrants in the United States live in Minnesota, the largest such community in the country ([Minnesota Compass, Aug. 3, 2026](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/10-things-know-about-immigration-minnesota)).

**Where.** In the Compass pool, 77.3 percent live in the Twin Cities and 22.7 percent in Greater Minnesota. Reported local counts come from different years and methods, so they do not add up neatly:
- Minneapolis, about 33,521, with no year stated ([FOX 9, December 2025](https://www.fox9.com/news/how-many-somalis-live-minneapolis-dec-2025)). Hennepin County, close to 50,000 ([KTTC](https://www.kttc.com/2025/12/04/by-numbers-minnesotas-somali-population-according-census-data/)).
- Cedar-Riverside has the state's largest concentration ([MNopedia, last modified Sept. 25, 2026](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/somali-and-somali-american-experiences-minnesota)). Riverside Plaza there houses about 4,400 people, most of East African descent ([National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/articles/riverside-plaza-mn.htm)).
- St. Cloud, about 4,400 residents claiming Somali heritage ([MPR News, Dec. 4, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/12/04/somalis-in-st-cloud-minnesota-push-back-against-trump-comments)). Rochester, about 4,000 in 2020 by the state demographer's count ([KTTC](https://www.kttc.com/2025/12/04/by-numbers-minnesotas-somali-population-according-census-data/)).
- Willmar, 1,500 officially and over 2,000 by informal estimate in 2016 ([NBC News, Dec. 1, 2016](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/how-somali-immigrants-are-breathing-new-life-sleepy-rural-towns-n690226)).
- Student records place 76 percent of Somali students in the metro, then Central Minnesota (mostly St. Cloud and Willmar) and Southeast Minnesota (Rochester and Faribault) ([DEED](https://mn.gov/deed/newscenter/publications/trends/march-2026/somali.jsp)). Festivals and a lullaby project also show families in Owatonna, Plymouth and Faribault (section 6; [MPR News, Oct. 30, 2026](https://mprnews.org/story/2026/10/30/sing-again-project-documents-61-somali-minnesotan-grandmothers-lullabies)).

**How people came.** MNopedia traces the first Somalis in Minnesota to students and scholars. Civil war began in 1991, the United States began issuing visas to Somali refugees in 1992, and Somalis began arriving in Minnesota that year as refugees and as relatives of people already here. International Institute of Minnesota, World Relief Minnesota, Lutheran Social Services and Catholic Charities helped resettle them. In summer 1992, a group came from Sioux Falls and San Diego to Marshall for turkey-plant jobs, and others kept moving here from other states for work, housing, services and school ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/somali-and-somali-american-experiences-minnesota)). In central Minnesota, one early settler dates his arrival in St. Cloud to 1999 ([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/12/04/somalis-in-st-cloud-minnesota-push-back-against-trump-comments)).

**Languages and lineage.** Among Somali Minnesotans 5 and older, 82.7 percent speak a language other than English at home and 33.6 percent speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/somali)). Somali is one of the state's top three non-English languages, with Spanish and Hmong ([Sahan Journal, Sept. 22, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-non-english-languages-census-data/)). State education records also list Maay as a home language ([DEED](https://mn.gov/deed/newscenter/publications/trends/march-2026/somali.jsp)). Not everyone with roots in Somalia shares one background. MNopedia notes patrilineal clans and castes, and members of the Bantu ethnic group who have lived in Somalia for generations ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/somali-and-somali-american-experiences-minnesota)). Some Minnesotans identify as Somaliland American, and held their own first day at the Capitol in May 2026 (section 6).

## 2. All walks of life

No single number describes this community. The figures below come from the Compass 2020 to 2024 pool, in 2024 dollars, unless noted ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/somali)).

- **Well-off households.** One household in five, 20.6 percent, takes in $100,000 or more, give or take 4.8 points. Average household size is 4.07, so income stretches across more people. Well-known names include Abdirahman Kahin, named the 2023 National Small Business Person of the Year as owner of Afro Deli & Grill ([Sahan Journal, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/abdirahman-kahin-afro-deli-owner-national-small-business-person-of-year/)). No Minnesota wealth figure, or any public list of wealthy Somali families, could be found.
- **Professionals and owners.** Of adults 25 and older, 17.2 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more, including 3.6 percent with a graduate or professional degree. Public examples include attorney Amran Farah, a Greene Espel partner since January 2021 ([Sahan Journal, Feb. 18, 2021](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=8071)), Dr. Bashir Moallin, who runs a St. Cloud clinic ([MinnPost, July 30, 2025](https://www.minnpost.com/health/2025/07/meet-dr-bashir-he-opened-st-clouds-first-somali-run-clinic/)), nurse practitioner Munira Maalimisaq, who founded a Minneapolis clinic ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 10, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/health/minneapolis-inspire-change-clinic-ventura-village/)), and Mayor Nadia Mohamed of St. Louis Park ([Atlantic Council, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.atlanticcouncil.org/expert/nadia-mohamed/)). In 2023, 1.6 percent of Minnesota's 27,110 licensed physicians said they speak Somali ([MinnPost](https://www.minnpost.com/health/2025/07/meet-dr-bashir-he-opened-st-clouds-first-somali-run-clinic/)).
- **Middle-income and working families.** Median household income is $49,554, give or take $6,519, and 28.2 percent of households fall between $50,000 and $99,999. Employment among adults 18 to 64 is 69.6 percent: 75.7 percent for men and 64.0 percent for women. Owner-occupied homes are 15.6 percent of households, give or take 4.2 points. Married couples with children under 18 are 36.4 percent of households. Among young people who went through Minnesota high schools, 85 percent worked in Minnesota eight to eleven years later, compared with 80 percent of white classmates, though in less stable jobs ([DEED](https://mn.gov/deed/newscenter/publications/trends/march-2026/somali.jsp)).
- **People in poverty.** 30.9 percent of people live below the poverty line, give or take 5.5 points, including 35.4 percent of children. Among people 65 and older it is 58.6 percent, with a wide margin of 14.9 points. Another 32.1 percent live between the poverty line and twice that line (Compass lists 21.8 and 10.3 percent in two bands; the sum is ours). Over half of renters, 55.1 percent, spend too much of their income on housing, and 36.1 percent of adults 25 and older did not finish high school. State records show 97.2 percent of Somali high school students from 2006 to 2022 came from low-income families, measured by ever qualifying for free or reduced lunch ([DEED](https://mn.gov/deed/newscenter/publications/trends/march-2026/somali.jsp)).

**Over time.** A 2016 State Demographic Center report, as summarized in 2017, put about 57 percent in poverty ([Twin Cities Business, Aug. 15, 2017](https://tcbmag.com/the-complicated-reality-behinds-the-story-of-somali-success-in-minnesota/)). Today's figure is 30.9 percent. These use different survey periods, so they show a direction, not an exact change. That 2017 article is also a useful caution against reading success stories as the whole story: it reports mall shop owners who barely cover their rent next to celebrated firms.

## 3. Work and the economy

**The best current evidence.** A March 2026 state study by Alessia Leibert followed 26,344 Somali students who left Minnesota high schools from 2006 to 2022, defined as Black students who spoke Somali or Maay at home. Key findings ([DEED](https://mn.gov/deed/newscenter/publications/trends/march-2026/somali.jsp)):
- Payroll records for 2023 to 2025, for those 24 and older: health care 30 percent (mostly lower-wage jobs such as home health aides and nursing assistants), social assistance 21 percent, transportation 9 percent (taxi, bus and truck driving), followed by temporary help.
- Somali workers are under-represented in manufacturing, professional services, finance and construction.
- Half worked full time, against 65 percent of white peers. They held more jobs at once and changed jobs about twice as often, working nearly the same hours (1,570 a year against 1,640).
- They reached a living wage at about 27, against just under 25 for white peers.

The study covers people who attended Minnesota high schools. It does not describe adults who arrived grown, whose story differs. MNopedia notes that many could not transfer degrees or training from home, and that early jobs in farming and meat processing did not require English ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/somali-and-somali-american-experiences-minnesota)).

**Labor history.** Somali workers have been central to organizing here. The Awood Center opened in Minneapolis in 2017 to teach East African workers their rights, and the Greater Minnesota Worker Center works from St. Cloud. Sahan Journal's 2019 account names protests and walkouts at Amazon in Shakopee and worker complaints at Jennie-O in Melrose and Pilgrim's Pride in Cold Spring about line speed, restroom breaks, wage theft and religious accommodation. Pilgrim's Pride said it takes such allegations "very seriously" ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 23, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/in-fight-for-better-working-conditions-somalis-find-their-voice/)). The Confederation of Somali Community now runs an in-person help hub for Uber drivers ([Confederation of Somali Community, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.csc-mn.org/)). Current union membership figures could not be found.

**Entrepreneurs.** MNopedia notes an international reputation for small-scale business ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/somali-and-somali-american-experiences-minnesota)). The African Development Center, founded by the late Hussein Samatar, lends to small businesses and helps people with credit and buying a home, with offices in Minneapolis, Rochester, St. Cloud and Willmar ([African Development Center, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.adcminnesota.org/)). MNopedia describes its loans as interest-free, because Islamic law prohibits paying interest. MNopedia gives its founding as 2002 and 2003, and the MNopedia biography of Samatar gives 2002 and 2004 ([MNopedia biography](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/samatar-hussein-1964-2013)).

**Federal fraud cases and this workforce.** Because so many people work in publicly funded care, these cases bear on many honest workers and families. See section 13.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

**Malls.** The Somali mall is a Minnesota institution. Karmel Mall, opened in 2005 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/somali-and-somali-american-experiences-minnesota)), sits at 2910 Pleasant Ave. S. in Minneapolis. MNopedia and AP count more than 100 businesses; its owner, Basim Sabri, told Sahan Journal 650 in June 2026 ([Sahan Journal, June 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/minnesota-somali-businesses-struggle-after-immigration-crackdown/)). Sources also call it a place of "hundreds" of shops ([KALW/MPR, Jan. 3, 2026](https://www.kalw.org/2026-01-03/minnesota-somali-community-bears-brunt-of-trump-administration-policies)), so the count depends on how suites are counted. Others include Madina Mall on 13th Avenue South, where 12 of 40 shops had closed by June 2026 ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/minnesota-somali-businesses-struggle-after-immigration-crackdown/)), a second Karmel at 200 W. Lake St. ([Visit Lake Street, 2026](https://www.visitlakestreet.com/ramadan-guide)), plus Riverside Mall and 24 Mall ([Meet Minneapolis, undated](https://www.minneapolis.org/east-african-community/east-african-itinerary/)). In St. Cloud, Star City Mall holds Somali-run businesses alongside a clinic and pharmacy ([MinnPost](https://www.minnpost.com/health/2025/07/meet-dr-bashir-he-opened-st-clouds-first-somali-run-clinic/)).

**Food.** Lake Street's 2026 guide lists Hamdi Restaurant, Mama Safia's Kitchen, Quruxlow, Sanag Restaurant and others ([Visit Lake Street](https://www.visitlakestreet.com/ramadan-guide)). Afro Deli has restaurants in Stadium Village and downtown St. Paul and a State Fair stand ([Afro Deli, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.afrodeli.com/)). Baarakallah serves Cedar Avenue ([Baarakallah, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://baarakallahsomalicuisine.com/)). Hoyo makes sambusas in a $1.6 million kitchen under Midtown Global Market, and sells to 62 grocery stores and 29 school districts ([Sahan Journal, June 2, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/news-partners/hoyo-somali-midtown-global-market/)). Coffee is a growing part of the scene: Nur House Cafe opened in a former downtown Starbucks in March 2026 ([Hoodline, 2026](https://hoodline.com/2026/03/somali-coffee-shop-nur-house-lights-up-former-downtown-starbucks/)); BlueHorn Tea of Hopkins sells Somali loose-leaf tea in Somali groceries in five states ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 27, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/bluehorn-entrepreneur-yubi-hassan-somali-tea/)); and Bundle Halal delivers groceries within 60 miles ([Sahan Journal, May 12, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/bundle-halal-grocery-delivery-minnesota/)).

Safari Restaurant, one of the oldest names, has closed; its co-owner was convicted in the Feeding Our Future trial ([Sahan Journal, Feb. 12, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/public-safety/feeding-our-future-safari-restaurant-minneapolis/); [KSTP](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/guilty-on-all-counts-feeding-our-future-trial-aimee-bock-salim-said/)).

**Beyond food.** Abdinasir Fashion Furniture on 4th Avenue South, money transfer shops, electronics stalls and jewelers appear in recent reporting ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/minnesota-somali-businesses-struggle-after-immigration-crackdown/)). In St. Cloud, a tax preparer spoke publicly in 2025 ([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/12/04/somalis-in-st-cloud-minnesota-push-back-against-trump-comments)). The Somali American Bar Association counted about 20 Minnesota attorneys in 2021 ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=8071)). Isuroon runs a small business hub ([Isuroon, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://isuroon.org/)). A fuller list accompanies this profile.

**Chambers.** No current website or member count could be confirmed for a Minnesota Somali chamber of commerce. See "What could not be established."

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

**Most are Sunni Muslims, and many see Islam as central to Somali identity,** MNopedia reports ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/somali-and-somali-american-experiences-minnesota)). That is a general statement about a population and says nothing about how any one person believes or practices.

**Congregations.**
- Dar Al-Hijrah in Cedar-Riverside, opened in 1998 as the Riverside Islamic Center and reported to be the first Somali mosque in Minnesota. Community members raised $400,000 in five months to buy the building in 2006. A January 1, 2014 fire next door killed three residents above a neighboring store, damaged the mosque and closed it for more than a year ([Augsburg University, Digital Tours](https://digitours.augsburg.edu/items/show/9)).
- Abubakar As-Saddique Islamic Center at 2834 13th Ave. S., and Umatul Islam Center at 3015 2nd Ave. S., where Isuroon's food shelf is based ([Visit Lake Street](https://www.visitlakestreet.com/ramadan-guide); [Isuroon](https://isuroon.org/)). A mosque operates inside Karmel Mall.
- Dar al-Farooq in Bloomington, where a far-right group set off a homemade bomb in 2017 ([MPR News, Oct. 9, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/09/somali-community-in-minnesota-worried-about-safety-after-trump-post-to-fight-back)).
- The Islamic Center in St. Cloud, in a former school and church. A bid for a new downtown mosque was withdrawn after a contentious city meeting, and it has been vandalized several times ([New York Review, July 2, 2019](https://www.nybooks.com/online/2019/07/02/somali-and-american-portrait-of-a-minnesota-community/)).
- The Augsburg site counts more than 30 mosques in the Twin Cities, with about half founded by Somalis, as of its writing ([Augsburg](https://digitours.augsburg.edu/items/show/9)).

**Practice and daily life.** MNopedia describes the practical work of daily prayer, ablution, halal food and hijab in a state long dominated by Lutherans and Catholics, and the difficulty interest-bearing mortgages pose for some ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/somali-and-somali-american-experiences-minnesota)). A Minneapolis doctor reports not discouraging Ramadan fasting while helping patients with diabetes adjust around it, and one patient credits fasting with lowering his blood sugar ([Sahan Journal, Sept. 18, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/health/somali-minnesotans-challenges-diabetes/)).

**Differences.** Reporting from St. Cloud shows political and social differences among people of the same faith (section 9). Sources on theological schools within Somali Islam in Minnesota, Somali Christians, or people of no faith could not be opened, so nothing is claimed here about them.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

- **Ramadan and Eid.** Mosques and groups hold iftars across the state. Published 2026 listings include the 19th CAIR-MN iftar on March 1 at Coffman Memorial Union and a Deaf Iftar on March 7 in Columbia Heights ([Meet Minneapolis, 2026](https://www.minneapolis.org/calendar/holidays/ramadan/)). In 2025, Isuroon held an iftar at Norway House on March 20 ([Sahan Journal, Feb. 27, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=67078)). Sahan Journal explains the start depends on sighting the crescent moon. Boutiques and salons on Lake Street do a brisk Eid trade ([Visit Lake Street](https://www.visitlakestreet.com/ramadan-guide)). No Minnesota Somali event specifically for Eid al-Adha, Islamic New Year or Mawlid could be found, and none is claimed.
- **Somali Independence Day, July 1, and Somali Week.** The week marks the 1960 union of north and south. People in Action runs it; the Lake Street festival began in 2017, paused for two pandemic years, and on June 28, 2025 drew tens of thousands, with soccer, camel rides and food. Mayor Jacob Frey read a proclamation naming July 1 Somali Culture Day in Minneapolis ([Sahan Journal, June 30, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minneapolis-somali-day-festival-2025/); [Sahan Journal, 2025 guide](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/somali-week-independence-minnesota-events-2025/)).
- **2026 festivals**, listed by Sahan Journal: Mixed Blood Theatre at Currie Park on June 27; Owatonna on July 3; Plymouth on July 18 with Hiddo Soor; and St. Cloud on August 1, its third year. The guide also lists a Lake Street Somali Day Fest with no date. Organizers spoke of belonging in a hard year; Hiddo Soor's Mohamed Wardere said, "We came, we belong and we will stay forever" ([Sahan Journal via Wisconsin Muslim Journal, June 26, 2026](https://wisconsinmuslimjournal.org/?p=53623)).
- **Somali Night at Northrop.** The Somali Student Association's closing show of Somali Culture Week, held April 3, 2026, marking 28 years ([Northrop, 2026](https://www.northrop.umn.edu/events/somali-night-2026)).
- **Somaliland observances.** May 18 marks Somaliland's 1991 declaration of independence from Somalia ([Dawan, May 18, 2026](https://www.dawan.africa/news/35-years-on-somalilands-may-18-marked-by-recognition-talk-and-israel-contacts)). In Minnesota, Voices Through Engagement held a first Somaliland American Day at the Capitol, reported May 13, 2026 ([allAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202605130180.html)). A Minnesota May 18 celebration in 2026 could not be confirmed.
- **Maangaar Summit.** A community gathering on autism, first held September 2, 2024 in Bloomington ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/maangaar-summit-somali-autism-minnesota/)). Whether it recurs could not be confirmed.

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Households.** Average size is 4.07, and married couples with children under 18 are 36.4 percent of households. Nearly half of the community is under 18 and few are 65 or older ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/somali)). A 2014 state-funded study of 38 Somali parents raising children with autism found that families averaged over four children and that 19 percent were single-parent households. That small sample describes those families, not the whole community ([MDH, Feb. 15, 2014](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/2014/mandated/140404.pdf)).

**Grandmothers and language.** The Sing Again project, led by educator Marian Hassan with the University of Minnesota, recorded 61 grandmothers singing or reciting 67 lullabies and poems. Many live in Cedar-Riverside, others in Faribault, Rochester, St. Cloud and Willmar. Recordings and a free curriculum launched this month at the Minnesota History Center ([MPR News, Oct. 30, 2026](https://mprnews.org/story/2026/10/30/sing-again-project-documents-61-somali-minnesotan-grandmothers-lullabies)).

**Food.** Guides describe suqaar, goat and rice, sambusas, malawah, and a banana served with the meal, plus halwo for dessert and tea spiced with ginger, cardamom, cinnamon and cloves ([Meet Minneapolis](https://www.minneapolis.org/food-drink/international/somali/); [Meet Minneapolis](https://www.minneapolis.org/food-drink/international/east-african/)).

**Childhood and school.** Isuroon operates a halal food shelf begun in 2016 and mentoring for youth. MNopedia notes many schools removed pork from menus or offered alternatives and made space for prayer ([Isuroon](https://isuroon.org/); [MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/somali-and-somali-american-experiences-minnesota)).

**Two generations.** A St. Cloud counselor describes parents who carry trauma from fleeing war and young people who can feel torn between identities ([MPR News, Dec. 21, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/12/21/st-cloud-mental-health-center-aims-to-be-a-bridge-to-healing)). That is one clinician's observation.

Sources on dress, marriage customs, elder care at home, and rotating savings groups in Minnesota could not be opened, so they are left out rather than guessed.

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Arts.** Soomaal House of Art, founded in 2014 by Mohamud Mumin, Kaamil Haider and Khadija Muse, has shown work at 22nd Street and Minnehaha Avenue and started a residency in 2023 ([Sahan Journal, Feb. 6, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/somali-artists-soomaal-house-minneapolis-minnesota/)). The Somali Museum of Minnesota at 2925 Chicago Ave., with more than 1,500 artifacts by its count, runs dance and weaving programs. It gives 2011 as its founding and 2013 for its public gallery, while MNopedia dates its opening to 2013 ([Somali Museum](https://www.somalimuseum.org/mission-and-history); [MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/place/somali-museum-minnesota)). Musicians in recent programs include the band Ardaa, Nimcaan Hilaac and Nuur Caraale ([Wisconsin Muslim Journal](https://wisconsinmuslimjournal.org/?p=53623); [Northrop](https://www.northrop.umn.edu/events/somali-night-2026)).

**Media.** The Somali Brief launched in September 2026, founded by Mukhtar Ibrahim, with Ubah Ali as lead reporter and Somali TV Minnesota as first media partner ([Twin Cities Business, Sept. 28, 2026](https://tcbmag.com/sahan-journal-founder-mukhtar-ibrahim-launches-somali-american-media-company/)). KALY 101.7 FM broadcasts in Somali from south Minneapolis ([KALY, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://kalyradio.org/)). Minnesota Department of Health staff have partnered with KALY and Somali TV on health messages ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 7, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/health/measles-mmr-vaccine-minnesota-department-of-health-outreach-somali/)).

**Sports.** Somali Week 2025 included a global soccer tournament at Griffin Stadium in St. Paul ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/somali-week-independence-minnesota-events-2025/)). Hoops4Unity is a basketball league community leaders point to for youth ([Sahan Journal, July 20, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=85970)). Casual soccer is a regular sight at Currie Park ([Meet Minneapolis](https://www.minneapolis.org/east-african-community/east-african-itinerary/)).

## 9. Civic and political life

**Elected officials** (as of the records opened).
- U.S. Rep. Ilhan Omar, on the November 3, 2026 ballot in a heavily Democratic district ([MPR News, Oct. 22, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/22/rep-ilhan-omar-says-she-will-move-out-of-her-washington-dc-home-after-federal-search)). See section 13 on the federal search of her home.
- State Sen. Omar Fateh, described as the first Somali American and first Muslim in the Minnesota Senate ([Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/legdb/fulldetail?id=15559)), and State Sen. Zaynab Mohamed ([LRL](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/legdb/fulldetail.aspx?ID=15588)).
- State Rep. Mohamud Noor, Minneapolis ([LRL](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/legdb/fulldetail?id=15528)), and State Rep. Samakab Hussein, the first Somali American to represent St. Paul ([LRL](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/legdb/fulldetail?ID=15573)). These terms run to January 2027. Hodan Hassan served 2019 to 2024 ([LRL](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/legdb/fulldetail.asp?ID=15530)).
- Mayor Nadia Mohamed of St. Louis Park, believed at her 2023 election to be the first Somali American elected mayor in the country ([PBS/AP, Nov. 8, 2023](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/minnesota-city-is-believed-to-be-the-first-in-the-u-s-to-elect-a-somali-american-as-mayor)).
- Jamal Osman, Minneapolis Ward 6, the only Somali member of the council as of July 2025 ([Sahan Journal, July 31, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/democracy-politics/minneapolis-mayor-jacob-frey-omar-fateh-election/)), and Hudda Ibrahim, St. Cloud City Council ([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/12/04/somalis-in-st-cloud-minnesota-push-back-against-trump-comments)).
- Abdi Warsame, former council member, leads the Minneapolis Public Housing Authority. The Minneapolis NAACP called for his removal in June 2026 over Heritage Park conditions, and the authority said the chapter "rushed to judgment" ([FOX 9, June 25, 2026](https://www.fox9.com/news/minneapolis-naacp-demands-housing-chiefs-resignation-over-shameful-heritage-park-conditions)).

**A first.** Hussein Samatar won a Minneapolis school board seat in 2010, described by MNopedia as the first Somali American elected to public office in Minnesota and possibly the country ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/samatar-hussein-1964-2013)).

**Not one political voice.** MPR News's 2024 reporting in St. Cloud is the clearest account: traditional DFL support, a Somali organizer's own estimate that most locals are socially conservative, some Trump rally attendance, and a resident who supported Trump but felt the travel ban "went too far" ([MPR News, Oct. 1, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/10/01/st-cloud-somali-community-seeks-political-influence-but-not-all-share-same-views)). In Minneapolis in 2025, Sahan Journal's informal interviews found Somali voters split between Mayor Frey and Fateh, with many undecided; that is a small informal sample ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/democracy-politics/minneapolis-mayor-jacob-frey-omar-fateh-election/)). This month, former Republican candidate Salman Fiqy cited campaign rhetoric in leaving his party's Somali outreach ([MPR News, Oct. 16, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/16/campaign-ads-blame-fraud-violence-on-somali-minnesotans)).

**Organizations.** The Confederation of Somali Community, established in 1994, is the oldest ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/somali-and-somali-american-experiences-minnesota)). Others include CAIR-Minnesota, Isuroon, the Somali American Social Service Association in Rochester, founded in 2012 ([KTTC, Feb. 2025](https://www.kttc.com/2025/02/23/olmsted-county-immigrant-support-organization-provides-important-information/)), the festival organizers People in Action and Hiddo Soor (section 6), and the Center for African Immigrants and Refugees Organization in St. Cloud ([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/12/04/somalis-in-st-cloud-minnesota-push-back-against-trump-comments)).

## 10. Education

**The state's own measure.** Among Somali high school leavers from 2006 to 2020, 27 percent completed a postsecondary credential, against 42 percent of white classmates, and 15 percent earned a bachelor's degree, against 24 percent. Health care was the leading field at 30 percent of credentials. Three in four had limited English proficiency at some point in high school ([DEED](https://mn.gov/deed/newscenter/publications/trends/march-2026/somali.jsp)). Among all adults 25 and older, Compass reports 36.1 percent did not finish high school and 17.2 percent have a bachelor's degree or more ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/somali)).

**Schools.** MNopedia notes that Somali families created charter schools, partly to preserve the language ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/somali-and-somali-american-experiences-minnesota)). Recent reporting names Gateway STEM Academy, a St. Paul charter, and East African Elementary School, whose principal is Imam Abdisalam Adam ([MPR News, July 8, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/07/08/trumps-truth-social-post-of-st-paul-kindergarten-graduation-sparks-safety-concerns)). Surad Academy in Faribault was authorized to open in summer 2023; its current status could not be confirmed ([Idealist](https://www.idealist.org/en/nonprofit/40a0a7c9eb1a4ca0a4c8727b66690272-surad-academy-faribault)). Not every story ended well: Cedar Riverside Community School closed after what KSTP's investigation described as years of problems ([Network for Public Education reprint](https://networkforpubliceducation.org/charter_scandal/inside-a-charter-schools-collapse-records-reveal-decade-of-problems/)). This community's schools are as varied as any.

**College and language.** The University of Minnesota teaches Somali in a four-semester sequence, with an accelerated track ([University of Minnesota](https://languagetesting.umn.edu/ept/course-placement-somali)). Normandale Community College offers first- and second-year Somali and a culture course ([Normandale](https://normandale.edu/academics/degrees-certificates/world-languages/somali-2.html)). The university's Somali Student Association serves over 500 students ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/somali-and-somali-american-experiences-minnesota)). Literacy educator Marian Hassan and the Center for Advanced Research on Language Acquisition are building Sing Again into a teaching model ([MPR News](https://mprnews.org/story/2026/10/30/sing-again-project-documents-61-somali-minnesotan-grandmothers-lullabies)).

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Coverage.** 6.2 percent lack health insurance, give or take 2.0 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/somali)).

**Clinics.**
- Hayat Clinic, 3405 Third St., St. Cloud. Dr. Bashir Moallin, a former refugee, opened it in 2024. It is open seven days a week and saw about 4,500 patients in its first year ([MinnPost](https://www.minnpost.com/health/2025/07/meet-dr-bashir-he-opened-st-clouds-first-somali-run-clinic/); [MPR News, Sept. 4, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/09/04/bashir-moallin-opens-clinic-in-st-cloud-for-somali-community)).
- Inspire Change Clinic, Ventura Village, opened in October 2025 with a Somali mothers' circle. About 400 people signed up within two weeks, against the founder's expectation of 100 for the year ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/health/minneapolis-inspire-change-clinic-ventura-village/)).
- Bridge Healing Center, St. Cloud, offers counseling in Somali, English, Spanish and Arabic and added addiction treatment ([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/12/21/st-cloud-mental-health-center-aims-to-be-a-bridge-to-healing)).
- Axis Medical Clinic employs a Somali diabetes educator, and the University of Minnesota Mobile Health Initiative runs screenings at mosques ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/health/somali-minnesotans-challenges-diabetes/)).
- Isuroon, a Somali women-led group, offers doula services, therapy, a 24-hour crisis line and halal food ([Isuroon](https://isuroon.org/)).

**Diabetes.** A 2015 Mayo Clinic study found lower health literacy among Somali patients, but that literacy did not explain the diabetes gap. A researcher pointed to structural barriers not unique to Somali patients. A doctor also reports that some patients' belief that God sets the time of death can make preventive care less accepted. That is one clinician's observation ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/health/somali-minnesotans-challenges-diabetes/)).

**Measles.** CDC reports that first-dose measles vaccine coverage among Somali 2-year-olds in Hennepin County fell from above 90 percent before 2008 to 35.6 percent in 2014, after concern about autism spread. In the 2017 outbreak, U.S.-born children of Somali descent were 55 of 65 cases. Studies consistently find no link between vaccines and autism ([CDC MMWR, July 14, 2017](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/66/wr/mm6627a1.htm)). Sahan Journal reported about 35 percent in 2021 and 22 cases in the 2022 outbreak; an MDH spokesperson said, "It's not a Somali problem—it's an unvaccinated problem" ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/health/measles-mmr-vaccine-minnesota-department-of-health-outreach-somali/)). Newer coverage figures were not found.

**Faith and care.** In the 2014 state study, one parent described reciting the Quran over a child alongside medical care ([MDH](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/2014/mandated/140404.pdf)). Bridge Healing's counselors say speaking directly in Somali builds trust and that addiction is taboo in many East African families ([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/12/21/st-cloud-mental-health-center-aims-to-be-a-bridge-to-healing)).

## 12. Disability

**What a survey shows.** Compass reports 7.4 percent of Somali Minnesotans with a disability, give or take 1.5 points, and 5.5 percent of those under 65 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/somali)). This is a young population answering a census question in English or through translation. How well that question fits Somali ways of talking about disability is an open question; no opened study tested it.

**What autism records show.** Nearly all Minnesota evidence concerns autism in children, and comparison needs care.
- For 2022, in parts of Anoka, Hennepin and Ramsey counties, researchers identified 1 in 12 Somali 8-year-olds (8.26 percent), compared with 1 in 28 of all 8-year-olds there. Among Somali children with IQ tests on file, 65 percent had intellectual disability, but 46 percent had no IQ test on file. The project "does not examine what causes autism" ([University of Minnesota MN-ADDM, Sept. 26, 2025](https://addm.umn.edu/detailed-findings/press-briefing-2025-09-26)).
- A peer-reviewed paper using 2014 to 2016 data reports higher prevalence among Somali children than among Hispanic, Hmong and other Asian children, and suggests differences may point to barriers to service use ([Esler and colleagues, Feb. 9, 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41661527/)).
- The earlier Minneapolis study, from 2010 records, found 1 in 32 Somali children and 1 in 36 white children, a difference not considered significant. All the Somali children identified also had an intellectual disability, and the authors said the study was not designed to explain why ([MPR News, Dec. 16, 2013](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2013/12/16/study-mpls-somali-white-children-equally-susceptible-to-autism)).
- Earlier figures include 1 in 29 for 2018 ([Sahan Journal, May 31, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/health/autism-diagnosis-signs-symptoms-help-minnesota-somali-parents/)) and 1 in 16 preschoolers for 2020 ([Sahan Journal, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/maangaar-summit-somali-autism-minnesota/)).
- The project cautions that its counts cover only part of the metro and that earlier Minnesota studies used different methods and should not be compared ([MN-ADDM FAQ](https://addm.umn.edu/qa)).

**Open questions, kept open.** No source opened establishes why the Somali number is higher. These are questions, not findings: Does it reflect outreach, schools, or who gets a label? How do language and testing shape who is counted, given how many children lack IQ records? The project counts a child through a clinical diagnosis, a special education classification, or a billing code ([MN-ADDM FAQ](https://addm.umn.edu/qa)), and federal charges now allege false autism diagnoses in some billing ([Sahan Journal, May 21, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/public-safety/fraud-charges-minnesota-social-services-federal-officials/)). Whether that touched these counts was not examined by any source opened. None of this should be read as saying children's autism is not real.

**Who names it.** The 2014 state report says no word for autism existed in Somali ([MDH](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/2014/mandated/140404.pdf)). A working group of parents, autistic people, medical workers and linguists settled on *maangaar* ("unique mind") and *maangooni* ("separate mind") to replace words meaning "slow-minded," "sick person" or "crazy person," hoping to speak of strengths ([Sahan Journal, updated April 7, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/health/autism-somali-new-terminology-community-acceptance-minnesota/)). In a 2024 summit, speakers named the phrase *Cidna ha u sheegin*, "Don't tell anyone," and autistic speaker Mahad Dahir, not speaking until age 4 and later a student body president in Washington state, urged people to tell their stories ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/maangaar-summit-somali-autism-minnesota/)).

**What parents reported.** The 2014 study, by the University of Minnesota, the Confederation of Somali Community and SoLaHmo, interviewed 33 people working in the field and 70 parents, 38 of them Somali. Somali parents were especially likely to discuss a belief that vaccines caused autism, and reported hard searches for safe housing. A quarter of those field interviewees said it was difficult or impossible to generalize ([MDH](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/2014/mandated/140404.pdf)). Parents at the 2024 summit described long waits and building their own networks over WhatsApp ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/maangaar-summit-somali-autism-minnesota/)).

**What a label opens, and closes.** Advocate Idil Abdull, a self-described Somali autism mom, told a Senate committee in January 2025 that early therapy matters for Minnesota-born Somali children, that the state does not report the race or ethnicity of children served, and that she saw unequal treatment in oversight of Somali-owned agencies compared with white-owned ones. These are her statements ([testimony, Jan. 29, 2025](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2025/human/20250129/Human_20250129_Testimony-EIDBI-Abdull.pdf)). The state autism benefit grew from a predicted $38 million a year in 2020 to nearly $450 million in 2025 ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/public-safety/fraud-charges-minnesota-social-services-federal-officials/)). Search warrants on April 28, 2026 included five autism centers ([AP via Anchorage Daily News](https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2026/04/28/federal-officers-raid-22-businesses-many-linked-to-somali-immigrants-in-minneapolis/)). A dated, independent count of how many Somali families have lost services could not be found.

**People and groups.** Somali Parents Autism Network, founded in 2014 by parents ([SPAN, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.somaliautism.org/about-us/)). Isuroon lists 245D home care and parent capacity services ([Isuroon](https://isuroon.org/)). Anisa Hagi-Mohamed of St. Cloud, a parent and teacher, organized the Maangaar Summit with SPAN's Abdulkadir Hassan. A Deaf Iftar and Minnesota Deaf Muslim Community iftar appear in Ramadan listings, though Somali involvement is not stated ([Meet Minneapolis](https://www.minneapolis.org/calendar/holidays/ramadan/); [Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=67078)).

**What remains missing.** No Minnesota study was found on physical, sensory or psychiatric disability among Somali adults, or on how war and displacement relate to impairment here. Compass poverty among those 65 and older (58.6 percent, wide margin) raises a question about elders with disabilities that no source opened answers.

## 13. What is changing now

This has been an extraordinarily hard year. Below is what is dated and attributed. Being accused is not being convicted, and what some people did is not what a community did.

**Fraud cases: what is proven and what is alleged.**
- *Proven:* Federal prosecutors built the Feeding Our Future case around pandemic child-meal money. A jury convicted founder Aimee Bock, who is white, on all seven counts; she was sentenced on May 21, 2026 to 500 months and $243 million in restitution ([MPR News, May 21, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/05/21/aimee-bock-feeding-our-future-fraud-sentencing)). Of 78 people charged by Sahan Journal's count (79 by MPR's), 66 had pleaded guilty or been convicted by May 21, two were acquitted in 2024, and nine cases were pending. Most defendants are Somali, and MPR reports nearly all are U.S. citizens ([Sahan Journal, May 21, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/public-safety/aimee-bock-feeding-our-future-sentencing-fraud-2/)). Sahan Journal's tracker listed 28 sentences as of October 2 ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/public-safety/who-has-been-sentenced-feeding-our-future/)).
- *Alleged:* On May 21, 2026, officials announced charges against 15 Minnesotans over about $90 million in seven programs ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/public-safety/fraud-charges-minnesota-social-services-federal-officials/)). On October 30, officials in St. Cloud announced four more people charged and 17 search warrants; three pleaded not guilty at first appearances. None of those named is from St. Cloud. MPR reports prosecutors have charged more than 100 people across programs since 2022 and that no one arrested in last winter's immigration surge was charged with fraud. Gov. Walz's office called the event "a political spectacle" before an election ([MPR News, Oct. 30, 2026](https://mprnews.org/story/2026/10/30/fraud-announcement-in-st-cloud-to-have-scott-bessent-kash-patel)). As simple arithmetic, 100 people would be about 1 in 1,000 of the 2024 Compass estimate even if every one were Somali, and they are not.
- *Not proven:* A December 2025 video accused Somali-run child care centers of fraud. State inspectors at the nine named centers reported they were operating as expected, with children present at all but one that had not yet opened, while investigations continued at four. Some providers reported harassment and one was broken into ([MPR News, Jan. 4, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/01/04/somali-owned-child-care-centers-viral-video-operating-as-expected-investigators-say)). On April 28, 2026, federal officers executed 22 warrants, many at businesses with Somali ties; the opened report does not say anyone was charged ([AP via Anchorage Daily News](https://www.adn.com/nation-world/2026/04/28/federal-officers-raid-22-businesses-many-linked-to-somali-immigrants-in-minneapolis/)).

**Immigration enforcement.** In December 2025, President Trump called Somalis "garbage" ([MPR News, Dec. 4, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/12/04/walz-criticizes-trump-remarks-somali-immigrants-immigration-crackdown)), and St. Paul's mayor said the vast majority of Somali Minnesotans are citizens ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 2, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/ice-enforcement-somali-immigrants-minnesota-minneapolis-stpaul/)). Operation Metro Surge followed. Border czar Tom Homan announced its end on February 12, 2026, claiming more than 4,000 arrests, a number not independently verified. Federal agents fatally shot Renee Good and Alex Pretti in Minneapolis ([Sahan Journal, Feb. 12, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/border-czar-tom-homan-minnesota-ice-operation-metro-surge-ending/)). In its statewide figures the city reports Ecuador and Mexico as the top countries of arrest ([City of Minneapolis, June 10, 2026](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/news/2026/june/updated-assessment/)).

**Temporary Protected Status.** Few hold it: a 2023 report found about 430 of Minnesota's Somalis did ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 24, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/temporary-protected-status-somali-minnesota/)). In August 2026 a federal judge allowed the administration to end it, citing a Supreme Court ruling that summer. An appeal has been filed, and CAIR-MN says the challenge continues ([MPR News, Aug. 17, 2026](https://mprnews.org/story/2026/08/17/judges-order-allows-end-tps-protections-people-somalia)).

**Business.** A Minneapolis assessment puts Metro Surge damage at nearly $700 million, including $445 million in business revenue, from December 2025 through April 2026 ([City of Minneapolis](https://www.minneapolismn.gov/news/2026/june/updated-assessment/)). Somali owners told Sahan Journal in June of months of unpaid rent; Madina Mall lost 12 of 40 shops, some Karmel tenants owed a combined $1.5 million, and a $100 million state relief bill failed. Others, such as two electronics shops with online customers, held steady ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/minnesota-somali-businesses-struggle-after-immigration-crackdown/)).

**Safety and rhetoric.**
- On October 8, 2026, President Trump urged Minnesotans to "fight back" against Somali immigrants. CAIR-MN met imams on safety plans, and neighbors planned patrols outside mosques ([MPR News, Oct. 9, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/09/somali-community-in-minnesota-worried-about-safety-after-trump-post-to-fight-back)).
- In July, the president reposted a clip of a St. Paul charter kindergarten graduation, and families worried for the school's safety ([MPR News, July 8, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/07/08/trumps-truth-social-post-of-st-paul-kindergarten-graduation-sparks-safety-concerns)).
- Campaign ads this fall tied Somali Minnesotans to fraud and gangs. State Sen. Zaynab Mohamed called it "unfathomable to paint an entire community with the actions of just a few" ([MPR News, Oct. 16, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/16/campaign-ads-blame-fraud-violence-on-somali-minnesotans)).
- A proposed federal bill carries the acronym SOMALIA; CAIR-MN called the name discriminatory. Its passage was not reported ([Sahan Journal, March 13, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/democracy-politics/pete-stauber-fraud-bill-somali-leaders-criticism/)).

**The search of Rep. Omar's home.** MPR News reported on October 21 and 22, 2026 that federal agents had searched her Washington home the week before. The Justice Department has said only that it is a criminal investigation, and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin has referred to possible fraud in her family's immigration paperwork. Neither report says she has been charged. She says she did nothing wrong, and her attorneys say there is no legitimate basis for suggesting she broke a law about her citizenship status ([MPR News, Oct. 22, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/22/rep-ilhan-omar-says-she-will-move-out-of-her-washington-dc-home-after-federal-search); [MPR News, Oct. 21, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/21/ilhan-omar-says-she-wont-be-intimidated-federal-investigation)).

**Youth violence: a debate inside the community.** Ramsey County Sheriff Bob Fletcher and a gang investigator described 12 Somali youth gangs, about 300 youths, and 14 homicides and more than 100 shootings in two years. Sahan Journal notes it did not independently confirm these figures. Somali leaders objected to how publicly it was aired, without denying a problem; activist Abdirizak Bihi said parents want more facts ([Sahan Journal, July 20, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/?p=85970)). Law enforcement leaders called an October 2, 2026 Eden Prairie shooting, which wounded two Somali American teens, gang-related ([MPR News, Oct. 9, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/09/somali-community-in-minnesota-worried-about-safety-after-trump-post-to-fight-back)). Residents told Sahan Journal the actions of a small group should not shame everyone; as one put it, "Five fingers are not the same" ([Sahan Journal, Oct. 15, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/somali-community-minnesota-trump-omar-gang-activity/)).

**A terrorism charge.** On October 7, 2026, an 18-year-old Somali American from Richfield was arrested, accused of plotting an attack at Mall of America ([MPR News, Oct. 9, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/10/09/somali-community-in-minnesota-worried-about-safety-after-trump-post-to-fight-back)). It is an allegation: a judge ordered him held on October 19, and the defense argues an FBI informant began contact when he was 16 and that the FBI supplied the inert rifle. No indictment had been reported on the pages opened ([FOX 9, Oct. 19, 2026](https://www.fox9.com/news/teen-accused-mall-america-terror-plot-ordered-remain-detained-during-case)).

**Generational change.** People born here now outnumber immigrants in some counts (section 1), and a March 2026 state study says Minnesota's economic competitiveness relies in part on Somali workers ([DEED](https://mn.gov/deed/newscenter/publications/trends/march-2026/somali.jsp)). In 2017, University of Minnesota's Ryan Allen expected second-generation gains ([Twin Cities Business](https://tcbmag.com/the-complicated-reality-behinds-the-story-of-somali-success-in-minnesota/)). Grandmothers singing for grandchildren who may not speak Somali is its own answer to that change ([MPR News](https://mprnews.org/story/2026/10/30/sing-again-project-documents-61-somali-minnesotan-grandmothers-lullabies)).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **Work.** Young Somali workers are employed at higher rates than white peers in the state's own records, and over half work in health care and social assistance, where the state says employers struggle to fill jobs ([DEED](https://mn.gov/deed/newscenter/publications/trends/march-2026/somali.jsp)).
- **Business and places.** Reporters credit Somali shops with reviving Willmar's Main Street area and abandoned St. Cloud shopping centers ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/how-somali-immigrants-are-breathing-new-life-sleepy-rural-towns-n690226); [New York Review](https://www.nybooks.com/online/2019/07/02/somali-and-american-portrait-of-a-minnesota-community/)).
- **Filling gaps.** Clinics, food shelves, doulas and parent groups appeared where services fell short (sections 11 and 12).
- **Leadership and language.** Somali Minnesotans have reached Congress, the Legislature and city hall (section 9). They coined new words for autism and recorded their grandmothers' songs, both of which others can learn from.
- **Solidarity in a hard year.** More than 100 people gathered at a St. Cloud library in December 2025, including the mayor and a school board member, and Twin Cities faith leaders of many traditions stood with neighbors ([MPR News, Dec. 4, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/12/04/somalis-in-st-cloud-minnesota-push-back-against-trump-comments); [MPR News, Dec. 4, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/12/04/walz-criticizes-trump-remarks-somali-immigrants-immigration-crackdown)).

- A full tally of Somali-owned businesses, a current count of trucking or home-care firms, or Somali business listings for Rochester, Faribault, Mankato and Owatonna. Coverage outside the metro and St. Cloud is thin.

- Minnesota sources on Sufi or Salafi differences, Somali Christians, or people of no faith. Carleton College's Religions in Minnesota pages refused the connection.
- Minnesota sources on dress, marriage customs, elder care at home, rotating savings, Somali Bantu organizations, or LGBTQ Somali Minnesotans. No source on these could be opened.
- Named scholars at Minnesota universities, Minneapolis Public Schools' Somali programs, statewide Somali graduation rates, and recent federal refugee and naturalization reviews affecting Somalis.
- The 2025 Minneapolis mayoral result, the current roster of Somali American officials beyond the records listed, and how many Somali candidates are on the 2026 ballot.
