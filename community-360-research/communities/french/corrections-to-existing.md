# Corrections to the existing French-speaking Minnesota page

Page: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/french
Checked: October 31, 2026. Each item gives the claim as stated, what the opened source says, the link and the date checked.

## Errors, omissions or claims that should change

1. **Claim:** The page is "a language door rather than a people," and France, Belgium, Switzerland and Quebec streams "are smaller in Minnesota human services, but real."
   **Source says:** The largest group connected to this page is Minnesotans of French and French Canadian descent, which the page does not describe. The 2025 American Community Survey counts 127,624 Minnesotans naming French ancestry and 34,940 naming French Canadian (2020-2024: 150,045 and 41,490). Almost all were born in the U.S., their median age is 52.7, and only 2.6 percent speak a language other than English at home. The page's history of the French Canadian founding of St. Paul, St. Anthony and Little Canada, the Red River Valley farm towns, French parishes and the French-language press appears only in individual biographies.
   **Links:** https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT1Y2025.B04006?g=040XX00US27 ; https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B04006?g=040XX00US27 ; https://data.census.gov/table/ACSSPP1Y2025.S0201?g=040XX00US27&t=533 . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

2. **Claim:** "There is no Minnesota Compass cultural-community row for 'French' or 'Francophone,' so do not invent a census to fill the gap," and "Do not invent a Minnesota Francophone attainment rate."
   **Source says:** Correct that Minnesota Compass has no such profile. But the Census Bureau publishes Minnesota counts that can be used with their margins: French ancestry (with a full income, education, poverty and disability profile), French Canadian, Belgian, Swiss and Haitian ancestry; birthplace by country (France 1,626; Haiti 1,653; Cameroon 4,077; Togo 1,731, among others, 2020-2024); and home language (16,918 French speakers and 1,914 Haitian Creole speakers age 5+, 2020-2024). The page should cite these rather than saying no count exists.
   **Links:** https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities ; https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B05006?g=040XX00US27 ; https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B16001?g=040XX00US27 . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

3. **Claim:** "USCIS states that Temporary Protected Status for Haiti ended effective July 27, 2026, after the Supreme Court's June 25, 2026 decision in Mullin v. Doe."
   **Source says:** Partly supported. Sahan Journal (July 2, 2026) confirms a 6-3 Supreme Court ruling on June 25, 2026, allowing TPS for Haitians and Syrians to end, and says it would end "by the end of July, but no final date has been set." The USCIS page could not be opened, so the July 27 effective date and the case name were not confirmed. Sahan Journal estimates about 4,000 Haitians live in Minnesota.
   **Link:** https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/supreme-court-ends-tps-haiti-minnesota/ . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

4. **Claim:** Cameroon TPS "benefits ended August 5, 2025."
   **Source says:** Not confirmed; the USCIS page could not be opened. Sahan Journal (June 2024) said Cameroonians were then eligible for TPS through June 2025. Should be rechecked.
   **Link:** https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-cameroonian-community-more-than-doubles/ . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

5. **Claim:** Cameroon-born figures "show about 1,400 Minnesotans born in Cameroon in 2016 and about 3,600 in 2022" and "Cameroon has English-speaking and French-speaking regions."
   **Source says:** Confirmed. Sahan Journal also ties the growth partly to the conflict in Cameroon's English-speaking regions, so many Cameroonian Minnesotans may be English speakers; the page should say so. The newer 2020-2024 estimate is 4,077 (give or take 949).
   **Links:** https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-cameroonian-community-more-than-doubles/ ; https://data.census.gov/table/ACSDT5Y2024.B05006?g=040XX00US27 . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

6. **Claim:** French is a "CLAS fifteen line."
   **Source says:** Supported in substance: DHS's Language Block policy lists 15 languages, including French. Worth adding that Haitian Creole is not among the 15, which matters for the page's advice to book Kreyòl.
   **Link:** https://mn.gov/dhs/assets/language-block-required-documents-available-general-public_tcm1053-646949.pdf . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

7. **Claim:** Star Tribune (July 31, 2025): French is about 2 percent of Minnesotans who speak a language other than English at home.
   **Source says:** The Star Tribune article could not be opened. A January 2026 Department of Public Safety report using Census data lists 16,313 French speakers among 658,004 people speaking a language other than English, about 2.5 percent, which is consistent.
   **Link:** https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/mandated/260600/Report.pdf . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

8. **Claim:** Our Lady of Lourdes is listed as a faith organization for this page, with the note "We could not confirm whether it is a current center for African or Haitian French-speaking worship."
   **Source says:** The archdiocese lists its Masses in English only. The same is true of St. Louis, King of France, founded in 1868 for French-speaking Catholics, which the page does not mention. Both are historic French Canadian parishes rather than current French-language worship sites.
   **Links:** https://www.archspm.org/location/our-lady-of-lourdes-catholic-church/ ; https://www.archspm.org/location/st-louis-king-of-france-catholic-church/ . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

9. **Claim:** "We did not find a Minnesota organization focused on them" (French-speaking African or Haitian Minnesotans), and the organization list omits French-heritage and newcomer groups.
   **Source says:** Several exist and are current: the Senegalese Association of Minnesota (since 2009; Wolof classes, immigration help), Diaspora Forces (Togolese and African community, Minneapolis), Vanse (supports Haitian immigrants), Minnesota Accueil (volunteer network for French speakers, active in 2026), the French-American Heritage Foundation and the Little Canada Historical Society.
   **Links:** https://senegalesemn.com/ ; https://diasporaforces.org/ ; https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/tps-ends-haiti-minnesota-deportation/ ; https://www.minnesotaaccueil.com/actualites.html ; https://www.canadiandays.org/244/Historical-Society . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

10. **Claim:** Dates of significance list only Haitian Independence Day, the 2010 earthquake and a general note on West African independence days, and say "Do not put Bastille Day (July 14) on a Haitian or Senegalese case note."
   **Source says:** Bastille Day is celebrated publicly each July 14 by the Alliance Française, and Little Canada's Canadian Days (held every year since 1977) is the state's main French Canadian heritage festival. Haitian Flag Day (May 18) was celebrated in Minneapolis in 2025. These are missing.
   **Links:** https://www.afmsp.org/bastille-day-2026-afmsp ; https://www.canadiandays.org/433/About-Us ; https://dancemn.org/submissions/afoutayi-ayiti-haiti-flag-day-celebration/ . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

## Claims checked and supported

- Alliance Française: begun in the 1920s under Prof. Jacques Fermand; about 100 members by the 1950s; incorporated Oct. 29, 1975; more than 1,200 members and 900 students a year; "6th largest" in the U.S.; Bastille Day and March Mois de la Francophonie; address 227 Colfax Ave. N. All confirmed (https://www.afmsp.org/mission ; https://www.afmsp.org/afmsp50). Note: the Alliance's own 2020-2021 directory called it the 5th largest at that time (https://www.afmsp.org/s/frdtopost.pdf).
- Afoutayi moving its activities to Toronto: confirmed, "by Summer 2026" (https://www.afoutayidmaco.com/).
- Minnesota Cameroon Community: founded 2008, August soccer tournament, Attorney General petition in 2022, community center closed: confirmed by Sahan Journal, which describes the group as in "a transitional period" (https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-cameroonian-community-more-than-doubles/).
- Our Lady of Lourdes bought in 1877 by the French Canadian community; oldest continuously used church in Minneapolis: confirmed (https://www.archspm.org/location/our-lady-of-lourdes-catholic-church/).
- Jean-Baptiste Faribault: born in Berthier, Quebec (MNHS gives 1774); North West Company 1798; 1839-40 stone house for $5,000; 1833 purchase of an enslaved woman; hosted Father Galtier; died Aug. 20, 1860: confirmed (https://www.mnhs.org/sibley/learn/jean-baptiste-faribault). MNHS names the Quebec firm as McNides & Co.
- Pierre Bottineau: born 1816 near the Turtle River; languages; St. Anthony; land for St. Anthony of Padua and Osseo's first Catholic church: confirmed by the Northeaster (https://www.mynortheaster.com/news/respected-guide-hunter-pierre-bottineaus-legacy-in-northeast/).
- Lucien Galtier's 1841 log chapel named for St. Paul: consistent with Ramsey County History, which adds that Benjamin Gervais and Vital Guerin gave the land (https://rchs.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/11/RCHS_Fall1964_Wall.pdf).

## Not checked

Biographies of people outside Minnesota (Toussaint, Danticat, Love, Mutombo, Kidjo, Beauvais, Gay, Raoul, Diarra), Joseph Rolette and Joseph Nicollet details, the Congolese Community of Minnesota address (its website did not load), Haitian Communities of Minnesota, Minnesota African Women Coalition, national organizations and international media entries.
