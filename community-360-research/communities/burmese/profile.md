# Burmese and Myanmar-Origin Minnesotans
Checked: October 31, 2026

This page covers people in Minnesota whose families come from Burma, also called Myanmar. That includes Bamar people (the country's largest group, sometimes called Burman), along with Karenni, Mon, Kachin, Chin, Rohingya and others. Karen Minnesotans, by far the largest group from Burma in this state, have their own page, and this one does not repeat it. Where the two overlap, as they often do in data, agencies and churches, this page says so and points across.

A note on names. "Burmese" can mean three different things: a nationality (anyone from Burma), a language (the Myanmar language spoken by Bamar people and used as a common tongue by many others), and, loosely, the Bamar people themselves. Census and state data use the first meaning. Many people from Burma's ethnic states do not call themselves Burmese at all. The Karen Organization of Minnesota (KOM) says Burma has more than 135 ethnic groups, that the Bamar make up about two-thirds of the population and control the military and government, and that the seven largest minority nationalities are the Chin, Kachin, Karen, Karenni, Mon, Rakhine and Shan ([KOM, Ethnic Groups, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/ethnic-groups/)).

## 1. People and place

**How many, and what the count covers.** Minnesota Compass, using the American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024, counts 19,372 Burmese Minnesotans, give or take 3,438 ([Minnesota Compass, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)). That number is a country-of-origin umbrella, not a count of Bamar people. Compass explains that people who write "Karen" on the census are recoded as Burmese, with nothing left to show their original answer, and that most Burmese Minnesotans probably first identified as Karen ([Minnesota Compass, May 30, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-asian-communities)). A researcher at APM Research Lab told Sahan Journal in 2019 that these numbers are likely conservative, because immigrant and refugee communities tend to be undercounted ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 13, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/)).

Other estimates sit in a similar range:
- Pew Research Center, counting only people who name Burmese alone, puts Minnesota at about 17,000 for 2021 to 2023, fourth among states after Indiana (25,000), California (22,000) and New York (21,000). Pew lists the Minneapolis metro, at about 14,000, ahead of Indianapolis (12,000) and Fort Wayne (9,000) among metros with the largest Burmese populations ([Pew Research Center, May 1, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-burmese-in-the-u-s/)).
- Sahan Journal reported in August 2026 that about 22,000 Minnesotans are from Myanmar, most of them Karen ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 18, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/tps-cut-myanmar-minnesota-karen-community-trump-immigration/)).
- Rep. Betty McCollum's office said in 2024 that her Fourth District alone is home to about 17,000 Burmese and Karen refugees ([Office of Rep. McCollum, Feb. 1, 2024](https://mccollum.house.gov/media/press-releases/mccollum-huizenga-establish-first-ever-congressional-burma-caucus)).

**Groups inside the umbrella, as estimated.** No public source counts Bamar Minnesotans on their own. The estimates that exist come from KOM and from community members, and their authors call them estimates.
- **Karenni.** KOM's current estimate is 2,000 to 5,000 ([KOM press kit, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/about/press-kit/)). A Karenni community volunteer estimated 4,000 to 5,000 in 2025, mostly in the Twin Cities and Austin ([Sahan Journal, March 6, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/maplewood-minnesota-first-karenni-grocery-store/)). A 2017 Catholic newspaper attributed a much higher figure, about 12,000 Karenni, to KOM and described St. Paul as home to the largest and fastest growing Karenni population in the country ([The Catholic Spirit, April 6, 2017](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/st-bernard-welcome-karenni-converts-easter/)). These figures do not agree, and the gap is not explained by any source opened.
- **Mon.** KOM estimates about 1,000 Mon Minnesotans, most living in St. Paul, and notes that the largest U.S. Mon communities are in Fort Wayne, Indiana, and Akron, Ohio ([KOM, Ethnic Groups](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/ethnic-groups/)).
- **Chin.** KOM lists Chin people among the communities it serves and among those Minnesota has received ([KOM press kit](https://mnkaren.org/about/press-kit/); [KOM statement, Nov. 26, 2025](https://mnkaren.org/news/kom-statement-on-announcement-of-tps-termination-for-burma/)). No Minnesota estimate of Chin residents was found.
- **Kachin.** A family of four Kachin sisters in St. Paul, all St. Paul College students, told WCCO in 2025 that they chose Minnesota after the 2021 coup ([CBS Minnesota, April 4, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-sisters-raise-money-myanmar-earthquake-relief/)). No count was found.
- **Others.** KOM put the combined total for groups other than Karen at more than 3,000 in late 2025 ([KSTP, Nov. 2025](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/minnesotas-karen-community-tracking-termination-of-tps-status-for-thousands-from-southeast-asia/)), and Literacy Minnesota cited more than 2,000 ([Literacy Minnesota, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.literacymn.org/discover-karen-culture-with-karen-organization-of-minnesota)). No Minnesota source was found that describes Rohingya, Shan or Rakhine residents as a settled group (see "What could not be established").

**Young, and mostly born abroad.** In the Compass profile, 41.1 percent are 17 or younger and only 3.4 percent are 65 or older. Two in three, 66.9 percent, were born outside the U.S. ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)). Nationally, Pew puts the median age at 29.1 and the median age of U.S.-born Burmese Americans at just 7.3, a sign of how new the second generation is ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-burmese-in-the-u-s/)).

**Where.** Compass places 83.0 percent in the Twin Cities and 17.0 percent in Greater Minnesota, give or take 4.9 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)). In 2019 APM Research Lab put nearly 95 percent in Ramsey County ([Sahan Journal, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/)). The two figures come from different periods and methods.
- **St. Paul and its east and north suburbs.** The Burmese Buddhist temple describes its community as "largely Burmese" and "centered around St. Paul" ([Sitagu Dhamma Vihara, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/newbies/newbies.html)). St. Bernard's parish in St. Paul's North End has served Karenni Catholics since about 2009, and in 2017 the parish's outreach visited Karenni neighbors at the nearby Arkwright Apartments ([The Catholic Spirit, 2017](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/st-bernard-welcome-karenni-converts-easter/)). The first Karenni grocery in the Twin Cities opened in a Maplewood strip mall in 2025 ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/maplewood-minnesota-first-karenni-grocery-store/)).
- **Chisago City.** The Burmese temple moved from a house in Maplewood to 23.3 acres at 32500 Lofton Ave. in Chisago City in early 2014, about 35 miles northeast of the Twin Cities ([Sitagu Dhamma Vihara, About, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/About/about.html)). Families drive there; the temple does not describe a local Burmese neighborhood.
- **Austin.** KOM says Karenni people live mostly in St. Paul and Austin ([KOM, Ethnic Groups](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/ethnic-groups/)), and a Karenni store called Loikaw21 opened in Austin during the pandemic ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/maplewood-minnesota-first-karenni-grocery-store/)).
- **Rochester and elsewhere.** State refugee data show small numbers of first arrivals from Burma in Olmsted County in 2011 to 2013, and a few in Hennepin and Anoka counties ([Minnesota Department of Health, refugee arrival data, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/index.html)).

**How people came.** The paths differ by group and by decade.
- **Early Bamar arrivals.** Voice of America's Burmese service reported in 2004 that when one Burmese man, U Than Aung, arrived in St. Paul about 11 years earlier, he was apparently the only Burmese person in the city, and that about a thousand Burmese immigrants had since settled in the area, most of them Karen ([VOA Burmese, Aug. 4, 2004](https://burmese.voanews.com/a/a-27-a-2004-08-04-2-1-93480114/1227733.html)). In 1995 the Christian Science Monitor already listed Burmese among the ethnic groups served by Twin Cities nonprofits ([Christian Science Monitor, Dec. 21, 1995](https://www.csmonitor.com/1995/1221/21013.html)). The temple's own history credits lay founders U Aung Koe and Dr. Aung Than, with Buddhists "of many different ethnic groups living in Minnesota," for starting the monastery society in 2004 ([Sitagu Dhamma Vihara, About](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/About/about.html)). How most early Bamar Minnesotans came, as students, professionals, asylum seekers or refugees, was not documented in any Minnesota source found.
- **Refugee arrivals.** Minnesota Department of Health data list 9,632 first-arrival refugees from Burma who were eligible for health screening from 1999 through 2024. Of those counted in the top counties, 9,105 first settled in Ramsey County. Arrivals climbed from a few dozen a year around 2000 to a peak of 1,027 in 2011, stayed above 650 a year through 2016, then fell to 59 in 2021 and 54 in 2022 before rising to 205 in 2023 and 242 in 2024 ([MDH, refugee arrival data](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/index.html)). These are first arrivals only; people who moved here from other states are counted separately, and some small cells are suppressed, so the true total is higher. In 2025, 38 of 545 new arrivals came from Burma ([MDH, Refugee Health Report 2025](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/25rhrpt.pdf)). In 2024 the state also recorded 8 secondary arrivals from Burma, refugees who first settled in another state ([MDH, Refugee Health Report 2024](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/24rhrpt.pdf)). The data do not show ethnicity, so they cannot say how many were Bamar, Karen, Karenni or others.
- **Karenni.** The Catholic Spirit reported in 2017 that the Karenni families it profiled had spent about ten years in Thai refugee camps, with dwindling food, before being offered resettlement around 2010 ([The Catholic Spirit, 2017](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/st-bernard-welcome-karenni-converts-easter/)).
- **After the 2021 coup.** Some people came on other visas and later received Temporary Protected Status, which the U.S. created for Burma after the coup (section 13). The Kachin sisters mentioned above are one public example of families who came after 2021 ([CBS Minnesota, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-sisters-raise-money-myanmar-earthquake-relief/)).

**Languages.** Among people 5 and older in the Compass profile, 89.1 percent speak a language other than English at home and 68.1 percent speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)). Nationally, Pew finds 35 percent of Burmese immigrants and 75 percent of the U.S.-born are proficient in English ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-burmese-in-the-u-s/)). KOM staff work in Burmese and Karenni, as well as Sgaw and Pwo Karen and other languages ([KOM press kit](https://mnkaren.org/about/press-kit/)). KOM describes Karenni as one language, Kayah Li, with small dialect differences across a dozen subgroups ([KOM, Ethnic Groups](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/ethnic-groups/)). KOM also says many Mon people no longer speak Mon and that some leaders teach it at monastery schools ([KOM, Ethnic Groups](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/ethnic-groups/)). The Burmese temple in Chisago City offers classes in Burmese language and culture for children and adults ([Sitagu Dhamma Vihara, About](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/About/about.html)).

## 2. All walks of life

All Compass figures here are for the whole Burmese umbrella, 2020 to 2024, in 2024 dollars, and carry wide margins ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)). They mostly describe Karen households, so they say little about Bamar, Karenni or Mon households on their own.

- **Comfortable households.** Median household income is $85,747, give or take $15,049, and 37.2 percent of households take in $100,000 or more. Households average 5.05 people, so these totals often combine several earners. Nationally, Pew puts the median Burmese household at $67,600 in 2023 and median personal earnings at $33,800, well below Asian Americans overall ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-burmese-in-the-u-s/)). No public reporting was found on wealthy Burmese families in Minnesota, so none are named.
- **Professionals, owners and people in public life.** Bachelor's degrees or higher are held by 11.8 percent of adults 25 and older. Visible careers include restaurant owner Chris Tunbaw of Mandalay Kitchen ([Racket, Jan. 12, 2024](https://racketmn.com/mandalay-kitchen-frogtown-st-paul-menu-burmese-thai-cuisine)), the Karenni couple who self-funded a 20,000-square-foot grocery ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/maplewood-minnesota-first-karenni-grocery-store/)), the St. Paul artist and art teacher Saw Kennedy, who curated a large Myanmar painting show in 2022 ([MPLSART, Dec. 2022](https://www.mplsart.com/events/window-to-the-soul-a-myanmar-group-exhibit)), and a Karenni priest of the Diocese of Loikaw who served a St. Paul parish ([The Catholic Spirit, 2017](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/st-bernard-welcome-karenni-converts-easter/)). The temple names a physician, Dr. Aung Than, among its founders ([Sitagu Dhamma Vihara, About](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/About/about.html)). Nationally, 23 percent of Burmese immigrants 25 and older hold a bachelor's degree or more, higher than the Minnesota umbrella figure ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-burmese-in-the-u-s/)).
- **Middle-income and working families.** Owner-occupied homes are 67.9 percent of households, give or take 9.5 points. Employment among adults 18 to 64 is 69.3 percent, 75.2 percent for men and 63.7 percent for women. Another 23.1 percent of people live between the poverty line and twice that line. Most commuters drive, and about a third of households have three or more vehicles.
- **People in poverty and under strain.** Poverty is 22.2 percent, give or take 7.6 points, and 25.8 percent among children. Two in three adults 25 and older, 67.0 percent, did not finish high school. Among renters, 41.7 percent spend too much of their income on housing. Compass wrote in 2024 that Burmese Minnesotans had the highest poverty rate and the lowest median household income, home ownership and employment of any Asian cultural community it tracks, and tied this mainly to the conditions under which many, "but not all," arrived ([Minnesota Compass, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-asian-communities)).

**Change over time.** The Compass profile for 2019 to 2023, as quoted on the existing page, showed poverty at 29.6 percent and owner-occupancy at 59.0 percent. The 2020 to 2024 profile shows 22.2 and 67.9 percent. Survey periods overlap and margins are wide, so this suggests a direction rather than a measured change.

## 3. Work and the economy

**Who works, and where.** Compass counts about 7,434 workers 16 and older in the umbrella group, nearly four in five of whom drive to work ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)). No Minnesota source broke out industries or occupations for Bamar, Karenni or Mon workers. Reporting on the larger Karen community describes meat processing and factory jobs in Albert Lea, Austin, Marshall, Willmar and Worthington, and Karen families moving from Ramsey County for those jobs and for cheaper housing ([Sahan Journal, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/); [Mpls.St.Paul Magazine, Dec. 19, 2021](https://mspmag.com/arts-and-culture/looking-back-at-minnesotas-refugee-history/)). The Karen page covers this in depth.

**Small businesses and side work.** The Karenni grocery owners told Sahan Journal they ran a cleaning service at night, on top of day jobs, to save for the store without loans ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/maplewood-minnesota-first-karenni-grocery-store/)).

**Work permits and TPS.** Temporary Protected Status gave some people from Burma permission to work. Sahan Journal reported that about 1,200 Minnesotans from Myanmar lost that status on Aug. 7, 2026 (section 13) ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 18, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/tps-cut-myanmar-minnesota-karen-community-trump-immigration/)). How many lost work authorization as a result, and in which jobs, was not reported.

**Labor history and unions.** No Minnesota source was found on union membership or labor organizing among people from Burma.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

Commercial life for Myanmar-origin Minnesotans is small and mostly shared with the Karen community. KOM says Karen entrepreneurs have opened more than two dozen businesses, which it shares only by email ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/maplewood-minnesota-first-karenni-grocery-store/)); those are on the Karen page. The places below are the ones tied to Burmese food or to other Myanmar groups.

- **Mandalay Kitchen**, 383 University Ave. W., Frogtown, St. Paul. Its own site calls it a Knyaw, Burmese and Thai restaurant and lists tea leaf salad, nan gyi thoke and mohinga; a notice says it is open with a limited menu ([Mandalay Kitchen, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.mandalaykitchenstp.com/)). Chef Chris Tunbaw, who is Karen, named it for the city of Mandalay and opened it in November 2023 ([Racket, 2024](https://racketmn.com/mandalay-kitchen-frogtown-st-paul-menu-burmese-thai-cuisine)). One food writer called it the Twin Cities' first formal restaurant to center Burmese food of any kind, while noting that he did not mean to fold Karen food into a general Burmese label ([My Annoying Opinions, Dec. 12, 2023](https://myannoyingopinions.com/2023/12/12/mandalay-kitchen-st-paul-mn/)). Racket reports a chalkboard explaining that mohinga changes by region, with soybeans in Shan State and more fish in Arakan ([Racket, 2024](https://racketmn.com/mandalay-kitchen-frogtown-st-paul-menu-burmese-thai-cuisine)).
- **Karenni Grocery Store**, Maplewood strip mall. Opened with a grand opening in early March 2025, with fruit, Southeast Asian ingredients, wild boar, betel nuts and leaves, and Karen and Karenni clothing ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/maplewood-minnesota-first-karenni-grocery-store/)). No current sign of operation was found.
- **Loikaw21**, Austin. A Karenni store that opened during the pandemic, named by the Maplewood owners as the only other Karenni market they knew of ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/maplewood-minnesota-first-karenni-grocery-store/)). Its current status could not be confirmed.
- **XIA Gallery & Cafe**, St. Paul, a gallery that is not Myanmar-owned, hosted "Window to the Soul" in December 2022, billed as the largest collection of paintings from Myanmar ever shown in the U.S. ([MPLSART, 2022](https://www.mplsart.com/events/window-to-the-soul-a-myanmar-group-exhibit)).

No Bamar-owned restaurant, grocery, law or accounting firm, insurance or tax office, travel agency, salon, media outlet or chamber of commerce in Minnesota was found. A Burmese-language media outlet based in Minnesota was not found either. This is a short list, and it should be read as the limit of what could be documented, not as proof that no such businesses exist.

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

**Many faiths under one country name.** Faith often follows ethnicity among people from Burma, though not neatly. KOM says Karenni people were traditionally animist and Buddhist, and that many have become Christian, mostly Catholic ([KOM, Ethnic Groups](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/ethnic-groups/)). It credits the Mon with bringing Theravada Buddhism and writing to Burma ([KOM, Ethnic Groups](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/ethnic-groups/)). No count of Minnesotans from Burma by faith was found.

**Theravada Buddhism: Sitagu Dhamma Vihara, Chisago City.** This is the one Burmese Buddhist temple found in Minnesota.
- *History.* Lay devotees U Aung Koe and Dr. Aung Than founded its society on May 2, 2004, the full moon of Kasone, the Buddha's day. Early guidance came from a Burmese abbot in Fort Wayne, and the influential Myanmar monk Ashin Nyanissara, known as Sitagu Sayadaw, gave a sermon in St. Paul on Sept. 18, 2005. In 2006 it was named Sitagu Dhamma Vihara, part of a network that includes temples in Austin, Texas, and West Palm Beach, Florida. It began in a house at 1519 County Road C East in Maplewood, renamed its society the Theravada Dhamma Society of Minnesota in 2011, bought its Chisago City land for $265,000 in 2014, and welcomed Ashin Dr. Osadhasara as abbot in 2015 ([Sitagu Dhamma Vihara, About](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/About/about.html)). The temple's own history mentions "unfortunate disharmonies" during one abbot's tenure, without detail.
- *Today.* Two to four monks live there. The temple says it is "primarily supported by an ethnic Burmese community" and serves as a community center on weekends, while welcoming non-Burmese visitors and people of any faith or none. An American-born monk, Bhikkhu Cintita, ordained in Myanmar, leads English classes and a book club ([Sitagu Dhamma Vihara, newcomers page](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/newbies/newbies.html); [schedule](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/schedule/schedule.html)). A 27-foot replica of Yangon's Shwedagon Pagoda, completed in 2019, stands on the grounds and serves as a consecrated hall where monks have been ordained ([Attractions](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/Attractions/Attractions.html)). Each summer the temple hosts Burmese boys who live there as temporary novice monks, a rite of passage in Myanmar ([Sangha page](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/programs/Sangha/Sangha.html)).
- *Practice and etiquette.* The temple asks visitors to remove shoes, not to touch monks, not to point feet toward monks or Buddha images, to hand offerings with both hands, and not to offer monks food after noon. Women who wish to speak with a monk are asked to do so in an open space or with others present ([Etiquette](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/Community/Etiquette/Etiquette.html)).
- The temple's pages are undated. They mention 2022 and 2023 as recent, and no 2025 or 2026 dated item was found on them.

**Catholic life: Karenni at St. Bernard's, St. Paul.** About 2009, the first Karenni refugees came to St. Bernard's in the North End, by the pastor's account because they heard the church bells. The parish rented a bus to bring families to Mass and hired Karenni staff. In 2017 a Karenni priest from the Diocese of Loikaw celebrated two Karenni-language Masses a month, Karenni families prayed the rosary in their language most Saturdays, and more than two dozen Karenni adults, who had practiced Buddhism or animism, were to be received into the church at Easter ([The Catholic Spirit, 2017](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/st-bernard-welcome-karenni-converts-easter/)). Whether these Masses continue in 2026 was not confirmed.

**Kachin and other Christian ties.** Minneapolis has an old tie to Kachin Christianity. In a 2013 sermon at Bethlehem Baptist Church, a Kachin pastor from Myanmar called it the Kachins' "mother church," because as the First Swedish Baptist Church it sent missionaries Ola and Minnie Hanson to the Kachin in 1890; he credited Hanson with creating a Kachin written language and translating the Bible ([Bethlehem Baptist Church, Oct. 12-13, 2013](https://bethlehem.church/sermon/lets-ask-god-for-more/); [Bethlehem Baptist, church history, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://bethlehem.church/churchplantinghistory/)). That is a historical link, not a sign of a Kachin congregation here, and no Kachin church in Minnesota was found. No Chin congregation in Minnesota was found either.

**Islam.** No Rohingya or other Burmese Muslim congregation in Minnesota was found. KOM mentions Karen Muslims in Burma (see the Karen page).

**Differences inside the community.** Religion and politics are tied together in Burma's history. KOM describes the Bamar as controlling the military and government, and ethnic minorities as facing forced labor and displacement ([KOM, Ethnic Groups](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/ethnic-groups/)). That history means a Bamar Buddhist temple and a Karenni Catholic parish may feel very different to the people in them, even when both are "Burmese" on a form. No Minnesota reporting was found on relations between these groups here, apart from the shared protests after the 2021 coup (section 9).

## 6. Celebrations and observances

- **Thingyan, the Water Festival and Burmese New Year.** The Chisago City temple calls its Water Festival "generally the biggest Burmese festival of the year," held around the Burmese New Year in April or May, with live folk music and dancing rehearsed for weeks, costumes, food, getting wet, and a chance to pay respects to the monks. It says the festival is not religious and is sometimes also a showcase of Karen folk culture ([Sitagu Dhamma Vihara, Events](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/Community/Events/Events.html)). As background, a New York resolution describes Thingyan as usually April 13 to 16, set each year by Myanmar's calendar board ([New York State Assembly, 2025](https://legislation.nysenate.gov/pdf/bills/2025/K1163)). No dated Minnesota Thingyan event for 2026 was found.
- **Kasone full moon (the Buddha's birth, awakening and death).** The temple's society was founded on this day in 2004 ([Sitagu Dhamma Vihara, About](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/About/about.html)). No dated Minnesota event was found.
- **Waso: start of the rains retreat.** The temple holds a robe offering "generally shortly after the full moon of July," at the start of the three-month retreat for monks ([Events](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/Community/Events/Events.html)).
- **Kathina (Kathein) robe offering.** "Generally after the full moon of October," after monks complete the retreat ([Events](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/Community/Events/Events.html)).
- **Sticky rice festival (Htamane).** Generally in February, community members spend the day and night making and competing over sticky rice ([Events](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/Community/Events/Events.html)).
- **Karenni DeeKu Festival.** Named for sticky rice boiled in bamboo leaves and tied in bundles of three for unity, it commemorates a Karenni victory over invaders. The Karenni Community of Minnesota held it Sept. 19 and 20, 2026 in St. Paul ([KOM listing, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/karenni-deeku-festival-2026/)). In 2024 it ran Sept. 21 and 22, with a youth dance evening and DeeKu at Community School of Excellence and volleyball and youth soccer at West Minnehaha Recreation Center ([KOM listing, 2024](https://mnkaren.org/event/karenni-deeku-festival-2/)).
- **Karenni Kay Htoe Boe.** KOM calls this the most important Karenni celebration, telling how the earth came to be, with dancing around a pole for good health. In 2025 it was held April 19 at Como Park Senior High, with a main ceremony and entertainment, and a volleyball tournament April 20 at North Dale Recreation Center ([KOM listing, 2025](https://mnkaren.org/event/karenni-kay-htoe-boe-festival/2025-04-19/)). No 2026 listing was found.
- **Mon National Day.** KOM says Mon people mark it every year, honoring the founding of Hanthawaddy, the last Mon kingdom ([KOM, Ethnic Groups](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/ethnic-groups/)). No Minnesota event or date was found.
- **Union Day, Feb. 12.** Marks the Panglong Agreement signed Feb. 12, 1947 by Aung San and ethnic leaders ([Burma News International, Feb. 12, 2018](https://www.bnionline.net/en/news/71st-union-day-celebrated-throughout-myanmar)). No Minnesota observance was found.
- **Anniversary of the 2021 coup, Feb. 1.** Rep. McCollum and a Michigan colleague launched the Congressional Burma Caucus on Feb. 1, 2024, the third anniversary ([Office of Rep. McCollum, 2024](https://mccollum.house.gov/media/press-releases/mccollum-huizenga-establish-first-ever-congressional-burma-caucus)). No yearly Minnesota gathering on the date was found.
- **Karen New Year, Karen Martyrs' Day and wrist-tying** belong to the Karen community and are on that page.
- **Christian holidays** matter to Karenni Catholics and other Christians. The Easter Vigil at St. Bernard's was the occasion for Karenni baptisms in 2017 ([The Catholic Spirit, 2017](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/st-bernard-welcome-karenni-converts-easter/)).

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Large households with children.** Compass shows an average household of 5.05 people, with married couples raising children under 18 making up 48.7 percent of households and single mothers with children 11.3 percent ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)). Nationally, Pew finds 63 percent of Burmese immigrant adults are married ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-burmese-in-the-u-s/)).

**Names.** The Catholic Spirit explained in 2017 that Karenni people do not use last names; "Reh" marks a man's name and "Meh" a woman's ([The Catholic Spirit, 2017](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/st-bernard-welcome-karenni-converts-easter/)). The temple's pages and VOA's Burmese service use the honorific "U" before men's names, as in U Aung Koe and U Than Aung ([Sitagu Dhamma Vihara, About](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/About/about.html); [VOA Burmese, 2004](https://burmese.voanews.com/a/a-27-a-2004-08-04-2-1-93480114/1227733.html)). Forms that demand a family surname may not fit either pattern.

**Food.** Dishes that appear in Minnesota include mohinga (catfish noodle soup often called the national dish), tea leaf salad, noodle salads, chickpea fritters and stuffed tofu ([Racket, 2024](https://racketmn.com/mandalay-kitchen-frogtown-st-paul-menu-burmese-thai-cuisine); [My Annoying Opinions, 2023](https://myannoyingopinions.com/2023/12/12/mandalay-kitchen-st-paul-mn/)). The Karenni grocery owners said Karen and Karenni shoppers buy different versions of the same seasonings because their recipes differ, and that betel nuts and leaves sell well ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/maplewood-minnesota-first-karenni-grocery-store/)). DeeKu, sticky rice in bamboo leaves, is central to the Karenni festival of the same name ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/karenni-deeku-festival-2026/)).

**Dress.** KOM says Mon people commonly wear red longyis to signify bravery and purity ([KOM, Ethnic Groups](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/ethnic-groups/)). The Maplewood grocery sells Karen and Karenni clothing ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/maplewood-minnesota-first-karenni-grocery-store/)).

**Childhood and coming of age.** Summer novice ordination at the Chisago City temple is one documented rite of passage for Buddhist boys ([Sangha page](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/programs/Sangha/Sangha.html)). Sahan Journal reported in 2025 that Karenni and K'nyaw parents are less likely to speak English and navigate U.S. systems, which can leave teenagers between two worlds ([Sahan Journal, Jan. 16, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/health/knyaw-karenni-karen-opioid-use-teens-minnesota/)).

**Aging.** Only 3.4 percent of the umbrella group is 65 or older, and several older age bands are too small for Compass to show ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)). No Minnesota source was found on elder care among Bamar, Karenni or Mon families.

**Distance from home.** Families watch events in Myanmar closely. After the 2008 cyclone, MPR reported worry in Minnesota's Karen community ([MPR News, May 6, 2008](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2008/05/06/myanmar)). After the March 2025 earthquake, St. Paul College students from Myanmar told KSTP they did not know whether relatives were alive, and raised money for relief ([KSTP, 2025](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/minnesota-students-from-myanmar-fundraising-for-recovery-efforts-following-earthquake/)).

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

- **Visual art.** "Window to the Soul," which opened Dec. 9, 2022 at XIA Gallery & Cafe, gathered the work of 31 artists in realist, modern and landscape styles, including scenes of monks, markets and horse carts. Its curator, Saw Kennedy (Eh Moo), born in Myanmar in 1973, is a St. Paul artist and teacher working in charcoal and watercolor ([MPLSART, 2022](https://www.mplsart.com/events/window-to-the-soul-a-myanmar-group-exhibit)).
- **Music and dance.** The temple's Water Festival features Burmese folk music and dancing rehearsed for weeks ([Events](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/Community/Events/Events.html)). Young Karenni dancers and singers perform at DeeKu ([KOM, 2024](https://mnkaren.org/event/karenni-deeku-festival-2/)).
- **Sports.** Volleyball and youth soccer tournaments are part of Karenni festivals ([KOM, 2024](https://mnkaren.org/event/karenni-deeku-festival-2/); [KOM, 2025](https://mnkaren.org/event/karenni-kay-htoe-boe-festival/2025-04-19/)).
- **Youth spaces.** The Urban Village in St. Paul's Frogtown describes itself as a community space for young people from Myanmar, offering mentoring and cultural programs for Karenni and K'nyaw youth ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/health/knyaw-karenni-karen-opioid-use-teens-minnesota/)).
- **Media.** The temple points people to Shwe Videos, a Burmese-language streaming source in Texas, for recordings of its events ([Events](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/Community/Events/Events.html)). No Minnesota-based Burmese-language newspaper, radio show or channel was found.

## 9. Civic and political life

**Protest after the coup.** Within weeks of the Feb. 1, 2021 coup, Minnesotans from Burma rallied at the State Capitol. On Feb. 28, 2021, a crowd demanded release of detained leaders, and one protester said "People of Burma are fighting for democracy, freedom and justice" ([FOX 9, Feb. 28, 2021](https://www.fox9.com/news/minnesotans-protest-military-coup-in-myanmar); [Patch, March 1, 2021](https://patch.com/minnesota/saintpaul/dozens-rally-minnesota-capitol-support-myanmar-protesters)). On March 13, 2021, teens and young adults marched to the Capitol at a rally that FOX 9 said was organized by people of multiple ethnic groups from Myanmar ([FOX 9, March 13, 2021](https://www.fox9.com/news/teens-march-in-st-paul-against-military-coup-in-myanmar-call-for-u-s-intervention)). That shared protest across groups that have long been divided at home is one of the clearest public signs of how Myanmar's peoples relate in Minnesota.

**Representation.** Rep. Betty McCollum, whose district includes St. Paul and the East Metro, co-chairs the Congressional Burma Caucus, launched in 2024. KOM's co-executive director welcomed it and asked for resettlement opportunities for refugees and displaced people ([Office of Rep. McCollum, 2024](https://mccollum.house.gov/media/press-releases/mccollum-huizenga-establish-first-ever-congressional-burma-caucus)). In 2026 McCollum criticized the end of TPS and Operation PARRIS (section 13) ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 18, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/tps-cut-myanmar-minnesota-karen-community-trump-immigration/); [Sahan Journal, April 28, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/operation-parris-minnesota-refugees-interviews-canceled/)). No Minnesota elected official of Bamar, Karenni, Mon, Kachin or Chin origin was found.

**Organizations.** KOM, founded in 2008 by refugees from Burma, says it serves all ethnic groups from Burma, including Karen, Karenni, Mon and Chin, and over 2,000 people a year ([KOM press kit](https://mnkaren.org/about/press-kit/)). The Karenni Community of Minnesota organizes cultural, youth and sports events ([KOM, 2024](https://mnkaren.org/event/karenni-deeku-festival-2/)). The Theravada Dhamma Society of Minnesota runs the Chisago City temple ([Sitagu Dhamma Vihara, Contact](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/contact/contact.html)). No Minnesota organization for Bamar, Chin, Kachin, Mon or Rohingya people as such was found.

**The Rohingya.** The United States determined on March 21, 2022 that the Burmese military committed genocide and crimes against humanity against the Rohingya ([USCIRF, March 21, 2022](https://www.uscirf.gov/news-room/releases-statements/uscirf-welcomes-state-departments-determination-genocide-committed)). No Minnesota report on Rohingya residents, or on how other Myanmar groups here view that history, could be opened.

**Debates inside the community.** The names people use are themselves a debate: younger K'nyaw people reject "Karen" as a colonial term, and Karenni grocers find themselves explaining that they are not Karen ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/health/knyaw-karenni-karen-opioid-use-teens-minnesota/); [Sahan Journal, March 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/maplewood-minnesota-first-karenni-grocery-store/)). A Karenni volunteer called her community "the least represented here" ([Sahan Journal, March 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/maplewood-minnesota-first-karenni-grocery-store/)).

## 10. Education

**Adults.** In the Compass profile, 67.0 percent of adults 25 and older did not finish high school, 13.0 percent have a diploma or GED, 8.3 percent have some college, and 11.8 percent have a bachelor's degree or more ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)).

**Children and languages in school.** The Minnesota Department of Education's 2024-25 report lists Karen as the fourth most common home language among English learners (3,419) and among all students (5,320). Burmese does not appear among the top 12 languages, so no statewide count of Burmese-speaking students was found ([MDE, English Learners in Minnesota 2024-25](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/other/261381.pdf)). No count of Karenni-, Mon-, Chin- or Kachin-speaking students was found.

**Community schooling.** The Chisago City temple teaches Burmese language and culture to children and adults and holds summer novice training ([Sitagu Dhamma Vihara, About](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/About/about.html); [Sangha page](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/programs/Sangha/Sangha.html)). KOM says some Mon leaders teach the Mon language at monastery schools, without naming one in Minnesota ([KOM, Ethnic Groups](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/ethnic-groups/)). The Urban Village offers homework help ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/health/knyaw-karenni-karen-opioid-use-teens-minnesota/)). Community School of Excellence, a St. Paul charter school, hosted the 2024 DeeKu evening ([KOM, 2024](https://mnkaren.org/event/karenni-deeku-festival-2/)).

**College.** St. Paul College enrolls students from Myanmar, including the Kachin sisters who led earthquake relief in 2025 ([KSTP, 2025](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/minnesota-students-from-myanmar-fundraising-for-recovery-efforts-following-earthquake/); [CBS Minnesota, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-sisters-raise-money-myanmar-earthquake-relief/)). No Burmese or Myanmar student association at a Minnesota college was found, and no Minnesota scholar of Burma was identified.

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Coverage.** In the Compass profile, 6.4 percent have no health insurance ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)).

**Language access.** The Minnesota Department of Health's translated-materials page has a full Karen (S'gaw) section but only one Burmese-language item, the WE-Check well-being and emotions screen ([MDH, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/translation/index.html)). With 68.1 percent of the umbrella group speaking English less than "very well," that gap matters, and a Burmese-language document will not reach a Karenni, Mon or Chin speaker who does not read Burmese.

**New arrivals.** MDH tracks health screening of eligible new refugee arrivals and publishes most results by world region, not by country, so no Burma-specific screening figures are public ([MDH, refugee arrival data](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/index.html)).

**Mind and spirit.** The Chisago City temple describes its mission as supporting spiritual well-being through meditation, study, community and personal guidance, and offers multi-day silent retreats ([Sitagu Dhamma Vihara, About](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/About/about.html); [Events](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/Community/Events/Events.html)). Sahan Journal reported in 2025 that Karenni and K'nyaw teenagers appear to be at high risk of substance use, that they often struggle in standard treatment programs, and that community groups have answered with mentoring and cultural programs ([Sahan Journal, Jan. 16, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/health/knyaw-karenni-karen-opioid-use-teens-minnesota/)). The Karen page covers mental health services in more depth.

**Traditional healing.** No Minnesota source was found on traditional medicine among Bamar, Karenni or Mon families.

## 12. Disability

**What Minnesota numbers show.** Compass reports that 13.8 percent of the Burmese umbrella group has a disability, give or take 3.3 points, and 11.0 percent of those under 65 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)). The existing page's figure was 12.9 percent for 2019 to 2023. They cover a group that is mostly Karen.

**What could not be found.** No Minnesota study, agency report or community organization was found that looks at disability among Bamar, Karenni, Mon, Chin, Kachin or Rohingya families.

**Questions the evidence raises.**
- *Who reaches whom?* State health materials exist in Karen but barely in Burmese, and not in Karenni, Mon or Chin ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/translation/index.html)). How a parent who speaks only Karenni learns about early intervention, special education or waiver services is not documented.
- *What does a label open or close?* With 67.0 percent of adults lacking a high school diploma and many years spent in camps, written notices and English-only plans may not reach families ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)). Whether that affects who is identified as disabled, and when, is an open question.
- *What produced an impairment?* Refugees from Burma lived through war, displacement and long camp stays (sections 1 and 13). No source opened measures how much disability among Minnesotans from Burma comes from those experiences, and none should be read as saying so.
- *Where do people turn?* Families name monks, priests and community groups as trusted sources of help (section 5). Whether those places are linked to disability services in Minnesota was not found.

## 13. What is changing now

**TPS ends.** On Nov. 24, 2025, the federal government announced the end of Temporary Protected Status for Burma, effective Jan. 26, 2026. The City of St. Paul said about 4,000 Burmese nationals nationwide held that status ([City of Saint Paul, Nov. 26, 2025](https://www.stpaul.gov/news/federal-changes-temporary-protected-status)). Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem said conditions in Burma had improved enough for people to return ([KSTP, Nov. 2025](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/minnesotas-karen-community-tracking-termination-of-tps-status-for-thousands-from-southeast-asia/)). KOM said Burma remained unsafe and that it could not tell how many Minnesotans were affected because the data are not broken out ([KOM statement, Nov. 26, 2025](https://mnkaren.org/news/kom-statement-on-announcement-of-tps-termination-for-burma/)). A lawsuit delayed the end until Aug. 7, 2026, when a federal court declined to extend relief; the International Refugee Assistance Project said the ruling left only a discrimination claim to be argued ([IRAP, Aug. 7, 2026](https://refugeerights.org/news-resources/federal-court-allows-termination-of-tps-to-proceed-for-nearly-4000-people-from-burma)). Sahan Journal reported that about 1,200 Minnesotans from Myanmar lost the status that day ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 18, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/tps-cut-myanmar-minnesota-karen-community-trump-immigration/)). The U.S. began offering TPS to people from Myanmar after the 2021 coup ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 18, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/tps-cut-myanmar-minnesota-karen-community-trump-immigration/)), so it covered people already in the country rather than refugees, who have their own status. No source broke the Minnesota figure down by ethnicity.

**Operation PARRIS.** Beginning in early January 2026, federal immigration officials called about 5,600 refugees in Minnesota on the path to permanent residency for new interviews. Sahan Journal reported in April 2026 that many were Karen, that interviews ran up to five hours with little notice, and that some notices were later canceled, leaving families unsure what to do ([Sahan Journal, April 28, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/operation-parris-minnesota-refugees-interviews-canceled/)). KOM's director said the cancellations caused "a lot of distress" ([same](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/operation-parris-minnesota-refugees-interviews-canceled/)). How many Karenni, Mon or Bamar refugees were called was not reported.

**Arrivals have nearly stopped.** First arrivals from Burma fell from over 1,000 in 2011 to 38 in 2025 ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/index.html); [MDH, 2025 report](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/25rhrpt.pdf)). KOM has asked the U.S. to reopen resettlement for people from Burma ([KOM statement, 2025](https://mnkaren.org/news/kom-statement-on-announcement-of-tps-termination-for-burma/)).

**Homeland in crisis.** The 2021 coup, the civil war and the March 2025 earthquake keep families focused on relatives there. Students raised money after the quake, and a protester in 2021 said the young are "the future of Burma" ([KSTP, 2025](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/minnesota-students-from-myanmar-fundraising-for-recovery-efforts-following-earthquake/); [FOX 9, March 13, 2021](https://www.fox9.com/news/teens-march-in-st-paul-against-military-coup-in-myanmar-call-for-u-s-intervention)).

**A new generation.** Four in ten in the umbrella group are children ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)). The rise of Karenni businesses and festivals, and the use of K'nyaw over Karen by younger people, show communities defining themselves more precisely than the census does ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/health/knyaw-karenni-karen-opioid-use-teens-minnesota/)).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **A landmark of faith built by a small community.** A Burmese temple that began in a Maplewood house now has a Shwedagon replica on 23 acres in Chisago City, supported entirely by donations and open to anyone ([Sitagu Dhamma Vihara, About](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/About/about.html); [newcomers page](https://www.sitagu.org/mn/newbies/newbies.html)).
- **Self-made businesses.** Karenni entrepreneurs opened groceries in Austin and Maplewood, the second from savings earned cleaning at night ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/maplewood-minnesota-first-karenni-grocery-store/)). Mandalay Kitchen brought mohinga and tea leaf salad to University Avenue ([Racket, 2024](https://racketmn.com/mandalay-kitchen-frogtown-st-paul-menu-burmese-thai-cuisine)).
- **Civic voice.** People from several Myanmar groups rallied together at the Capitol in 2021, and Minnesota's congressional delegation now co-leads the Burma Caucus ([FOX 9, 2021](https://www.fox9.com/news/teens-march-in-st-paul-against-military-coup-in-myanmar-call-for-u-s-intervention); [Office of Rep. McCollum, 2024](https://mccollum.house.gov/media/press-releases/mccollum-huizenga-establish-first-ever-congressional-burma-caucus)).
- **Care for home.** Students and families raise money for relief after disasters in Myanmar ([CBS Minnesota, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-sisters-raise-money-myanmar-earthquake-relief/)).
- **Art and culture.** A St. Paul curator brought what was billed as the largest show of Myanmar paintings ever exhibited in the U.S. to University Avenue ([MPLSART, 2022](https://www.mplsart.com/events/window-to-the-soul-a-myanmar-group-exhibit)).
- **Ownership.** Two in three households in the umbrella group own their homes ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)).

## What could not be established

- **A count of Bamar Minnesotans**, or any income, schooling or housing figure for Bamar, Karenni, Mon, Chin or Kachin people separately. Every number on this page is either the census umbrella, which is mostly Karen, or an estimate.
- **Chin, Kachin, Shan, Rakhine and Rohingya communities in Minnesota.** No Minnesota congregation, organization or count was found for any of them. The existing page's mention of Chin households in Worthington and Rohingya households from Cox's Bazar could not be supported. A 2017 Twin Cities march against the treatment of Rohingya, announced by CAIR-Minnesota, could not be opened.
- **How early Bamar Minnesotans came** (students, professionals, asylum seekers or refugees), and how many came after 2021.
- **Current status of the Chisago City temple's events.** Its pages are undated, and no 2025 or 2026 dated event, including a Water Festival, was found. The Carleton College "Religions in Minnesota" pages on the temple could not be reached.
- **Whether Karenni-language Masses continue at St. Bernard's**, and whether the Karenni grocery in Maplewood and Loikaw21 in Austin are open today.
- **2026 Kay Htoe Boe date**, any Minnesota Mon National Day, Union Day or Thingyan event with a date, and any 2027 or 2028 announcements.
- **Bamar-owned businesses** of any kind, professional firms, media outlets or a chamber of commerce.
- **Disability research** on any Myanmar group in Minnesota, and Burmese-, Karenni- or Mon-language disability materials.
- **Statewide counts of Burmese-, Karenni- or Mon-speaking students.**
- **Blocked or failed:** KARE 11, CAIR, Carleton College's religion exhibits, Minnesota Reformer, Minnesota Lawyer, AsAm News, Minnesota Digital Library, Ucanews, Frontier Myanmar, Lost Footsteps, Shan Herald, Desiring God and the Census Bureau data service. Sahan Journal's search pages were rate-limited, as was one Sahan article on Myanmar art.
