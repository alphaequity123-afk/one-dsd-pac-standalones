## Claims checked and supported

- "The Census recodes some people who write Karen into Burmese, with nothing left to show the original response." Supported. Compass says respondents indicating Karen are recoded into Burmese with nothing to indicate their original response, and that the majority of Burmese Minnesotans probably first identified as Karen. https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-asian-communities (May 30, 2024).
- "The 2017 Rohingya atrocities .. named as genocide by the United States in 2022." Supported. USCIRF, March 21, 2022, welcomed the State Department's determination that atrocities by the Burmese military against the Rohingya constitute genocide and crimes against humanity. https://www.uscirf.gov/news-room/releases-statements/uscirf-welcomes-state-departments-determination-genocide-committed.
- "MSP Magazine's state refugee timeline has refugees from Burma arriving in Minnesota in 1993, most of them named as Karen." Accurately describes the magazine. https://mspmag.com/arts-and-culture/looking-back-at-minnesotas-refugee-history/ (Dec. 19, 2021). But see "Needs context" below.

## Errors or unsupported claims

1. **Claim:** "Worthington, Albert Lea, Austin, Marshall, and Willmar are a Karen/Burmese pattern in public telling (MSP Magazine on Minnesota's refugee history names those towns for Karen)."
   **What the source says:** The MSP Magazine timeline names Albert Lea, Austin, Marshall and Willmar. It does not name Worthington. Worthington is documented for Karen people elsewhere (see the Karen page), but not in the cited source.
   **Link:** https://mspmag.com/arts-and-culture/looking-back-at-minnesotas-refugee-history/.

2. **Claim:** "MSP Magazine's state refugee timeline has refugees from Burma arriving in Minnesota in 1993, most of them named as Karen in that telling, while later waves include Chin, Bamar, and others." Also, the page's "Doors and Sources" cites "MSP Magazine on Minnesota's refugee history (1993 Burma-origin arrivals ...)".
   **Needs context:** The magazine does say 1993. Other sources date substantial Karen refugee arrivals later. KOM's press kit says KOM itself was founded by refugees from Burma in 2008; the state health department's refugee arrival data begin in 1999 and show a few dozen first arrivals from Burma a year through 2003, about 150 to 190 a year in 2004 to 2006, and 600 or more a year from 2007 on; VOA's Burmese service reported in 2004 that a Burmese man who came to St. Paul about 1993 was apparently then the only Burmese person in the city. No source found describes "later waves" of Chin or Bamar refugees to Minnesota.
   **Links:** https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/index.html ; https://burmese.voanews.com/a/a-27-a-2004-08-04-2-1-93480114/1227733.html ; https://archive.mpr.org/stories/2004/08/09/karen-refugees-from-burma-are-finding (all).

3. **Claim:** "The partners also include Burmese, Chin, and Rohingya groups" and "the Burmese, Chin, and Rohingya groups they name."
   **What sources show:** No Minnesota Chin or Rohingya organization, congregation or mosque was found. The Bamar-led institution found is a temple society (Theravada Dhamma Society of Minnesota, Chisago City). KOM says it serves Chin people among others. The claim cannot be supported as written.
   **Links:** https://www.sitagu.org/mn/About/about.html ; https://mnkaren.org/about/press-kit/.

4. **Claim:** "Camps in Thailand (Mae La and others) and in Bangladesh (Cox's Bazar and others) are the origin-of-arrival for many Minnesota families"; "a household that formed in Mae La or Cox's Bazar"; "a Hakha Chin Christian plant worker in Worthington."
   **What sources show:** Thai camps are well documented for Karen and Karenni Minnesotans. No Minnesota source was found showing Rohingya families from Cox's Bazar, or Chin workers in Worthington. These read as facts but are illustrations without Minnesota evidence.
   **Links:** https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/st-bernard-welcome-karenni-converts-easter/ (Karenni from Thai camps, 2017).

5. **Claim:** Thingyan "is a water festival in the homeland and quieter in Minnesota."
   **What the source says:** Minnesota's Burmese temple calls its Water Festival "generally the biggest Burmese festival of the year," with folk music and dance rehearsed for weeks. "Quieter" is not supported. No dated 2026 Minnesota event was found, though.
   **Link:** https://www.sitagu.org/mn/Community/Events/Events.html.

6. **Claim:** "Roseville as a Karen Organization of Minnesota address (a Karen door)" and "The Karen Organization of Minnesota is a Karen door, not automatically this page."
   **Needs context:** KOM describes itself as serving all ethnic groups from Burma, including Karen, Karenni, Mon and Chin, with staff who speak Burmese and Karenni, and it lists Karenni events. It is a door for more than Karen households.
   **Link:** https://mnkaren.org/about/press-kit/.

7. **Missing current facts.** The page does not mention the end of Temporary Protected Status for Burma (announced Nov. 24, 2025; in effect after an Aug. 7, 2026 court ruling, with about 1,200 Minnesotans affected by Sahan Journal's count) or Operation PARRIS (re-interviews of about 5,600 Minnesota refugees from January 2026). Both are dated in the new profile.
   **Links:** https://www.stpaul.gov/news/federal-changes-temporary-protected-status ; https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/tps-cut-myanmar-minnesota-karen-community-trump-immigration/ ; https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/operation-parris-minnesota-refugees-interviews-canceled/.

## Not checked

- "The CLAS fifteen interpreter cards say Burmese."
- Naming claims about Bamar honorifics U and Daw beyond the use of "U" in the temple's history and VOA's Burmese service. The Karenni use of "Reh" and "Meh" instead of last names is documented by The Catholic Spirit (2017).
- Claims about Rohingya language (Rohingya or Bengali), Hakha and Falam Chin interpreters, chinlone, thanaka, saung music, and the suggested books.
