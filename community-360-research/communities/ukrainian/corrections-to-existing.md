# Corrections to the existing Ukrainian page

Page checked: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/ukrainian (read in full on Oct. 31, 2026). All dates checked below are Oct. 31, 2026.

## Outdated figures

1. **Population and profile figures use the 2019–2023 vintage.**
   - Claim as stated: Compass "counts about 22,570 (ACS 2019–2023)"; 72.8% Twin Cities, 27.2% Greater Minnesota; 31.1% age 17 or younger; 13.5% age 65+; 30.3% foreign-born; 30.8% speak a language other than English; 13.2% speak English less than "very well"; 69.2% English only; 93.9% high school or higher; 53.7% bachelor's or higher; median household income about $95,000 (2022 dollars); poverty 8.2%; owner-occupied 66.4%; uninsured 4.5%.
   - What the opened source says now: the Compass profile is now 2020–2024: 22,254 (±3,082); 71.9% Twin Cities, 28.1% Greater Minnesota; 28.8% age 17 or younger; 15.2% age 65+; 31.3% foreign-born; 30.0% language other than English; 13.7% English less than "very well"; 70.0% English only; 94.5% high school or higher; 53.2% bachelor's or higher; median household income $94,322 (±$13,367, 2024 dollars); 7.7% below poverty; 68.0% owner-occupied; 5.3% uninsured; average household size 2.46 (unchanged). It also now reports disability: 10.9% (±3.1).
   - Link: https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/ukrainian
   - Note: the page's warning that a five-year estimate does not capture all post-2022 arrivals still holds.

2. **"Compass ... about 22,570" versus other counts.** Not an error, but the page could add that the 2024 one-year ACS counts 19,849 (±3,043) Minnesotans of Ukrainian ancestry and 6,647 (±2,021) born in Ukraine, and that the Ukrainian American Community Center told the Legislature in June 2025 the community was "approximately 25,000."
   - Links: https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B04006&geo_ids=04000US27 ; https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US27 ; https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/DhB1hP9jLk2PakdiFQu2CA.pdf

## Errors

3. **Kramarczuk founders' dates.**
   - Claim as stated: "Their birth and death years are not publicly documented."
   - What the opened source says: the James Beard Foundation says Wasyl Kramarczuk died in 1991 and Anna Kramarczuk in 2008, and that the business dates to 1954, when they bought Central Provisions; Orest Kramarczuk told KSTP his parents came to the United States in 1949.
   - Links: https://www.jamesbeard.org/stories/americas-classic-kramarczuks ; https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/standing-with-ukraine/

4. **St. Michael's service language.**
   - Claim as stated: "Its site does not say which language services use."
   - What the opened source says: the site still does not state a liturgical language, but its notices are posted in both Ukrainian and English and it says longtime members worship alongside recent immigrants. The page could also name the priest, Fr. Myron Korostil.
   - Links: https://www.stmichaelsandstgeorgesuoc.org/services ; https://www.stmichaelsandstgeorgesuoc.org/events

5. **Calendar wording.** The page says "some Ukrainian churches have moved Nativity to 25 December." St. Constantine Ukrainian Catholic Church in Minneapolis has used the Gregorian calendar since 1943, by its own timeline, so in Minneapolis this is not new.
   - Link: https://stconstantine.org/history.html

6. **Holodomor date.** The page says Holodomor Remembrance "is the fourth Saturday of November in many Ukrainian rooms." In Minnesota, the community center reports a 2023 House resolution naming the last week of November Holodomor Memorial Week, with a memorial day given as Nov. 25 on one page and Nov. 28 on another. The page could mention the Minnesota designation.
   - Links: https://old.uaccmn.org/holodomor/ ; https://old.uaccmn.org/advocate-for-ukraine/

## Claims that could not be supported

7. **"The Minnesota branch of the Ukrainian Congress Committee of America, active since 1949, was by 1980 the governing body for Ukrainian organizations in the state."** MNopedia lists the branch's records as 1949–1986 but does not say it was the governing body by 1980. The source for that wording (possibly the 1981 "They Chose Minnesota" chapter) was not available online. Link: https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/ukrainian-american-community-northeast-minneapolis

8. **"CBS Minnesota reported more than 15,000 Ukrainian refugees in Minnesota in March 2023, without giving its source."** Confirmed as stated; the page is right to flag it. No other opened source supports a number that high. MDH screening data show about 2,800 arrivals from Ukraine in 2022–2024. Links: https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/ukrainian-community-thanks-minnesotans-for-a-year-of-support/ ; https://www.health.mn.gov/communities/rih/stats/country.csv

9. **"No Minnesota organization that specifically serves Ukrainian disabled people or families."** Still true as far as could be found. The page could add the Protez Foundation, a Minnesota-founded nonprofit providing prosthetics to Ukrainians with war-related limb loss, which the page mentions only through national groups. Link: https://www.protezfoundation.org/

10. **Bronko Nagurski, Granovsky biography details, Disability Hub "6 weeks to a year," Alight's "hubs in Minneapolis and Nairobi," International Institute's address and Zerkalo** were not re-checked.

## Checked and found accurate

- Ukrainian Festival postponed in 2026; planned for Sept. 17–18, 2027 (https://ukrainianfestivalmn.com/).
- Festival at the community center since 2006 (https://uaccmn.org/arts-and-culture/ukrainian-festival-of-minnesota/).
- St. Constantine founded 1913; parochial school ground broken 1956 (https://stconstantine.org/history.html).
- St. Michael's formed 1925, moved to 505 Fourth St. in 1926; Divine Liturgy Sundays at 10 a.m. (MNopedia; https://www.stmichaelsandstgeorgesuoc.org/services).
- Community center organized 1964 by Dmytro Mandybur as the Ukrainian-American Home (MNopedia).
- Ukrainian Gift Shop founded November 1947; no walk-in showroom; Marie Procai retired 1987 at 90 and died 1993 per the family's page; Luba Perchyshyn died Sept. 7, 2020 (https://ukrainiangiftshop.com/pages/our-story).
- MPR's February 2023 figures (more than 2,700 parole applications; more than 900 arrivals using federally funded services) (https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/02/23/ukrainians-in-minnesota-pay-it-forward-one-year-after-fleeing-war).
- CBS's "healing zone" for more than 50 wounded soldiers (March 2023) and FOX 9's report that the center raised $750,000 (August 2022) (https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/ukrainian-community-thanks-minnesotans-for-a-year-of-support/ ; https://fox9.com/news/local-ukrainian-man-urges-minnesotans-to-not-forget-refugees-still-in-need).
- KSTP's July 2022 report that the International Institute was serving close to 100 Ukrainians and using its Refugee Family Fund (https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/minnesota-has-welcomed-as-many-as-300-ukrainian-refugees/).
- Cheremosh: dancers about 4 to 24, part of the Ukrainian American Youth Association, 2026–27 registration open (https://www.cheremoshmn.com/).
- MDH Ukrainian clinical guidance exists and lists Uniting for Ukraine, humanitarian parole, TPS and Lautenberg pathways (https://www.health.mn.gov/communities/rih/coe/clinical/ukrainian.html).
