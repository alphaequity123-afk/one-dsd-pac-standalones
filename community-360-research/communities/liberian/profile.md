# Liberians in Minnesota

## 1. People and place

**How many.** No single count settles the size of this community, and the honest answer is a range. Minnesota Compass, using the American Community Survey (ACS) for 2020 to 2024, counts 22,571 people in its Liberian cultural community, give or take 4,423. Compass also gives a 2024-only figure of 23,608, give or take 10,872, and says the Census Bureau's 2024 changes to how it records ancestry for Black and white respondents make that single-year figure its best estimate of the community's true size ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/liberian)). Two narrower Census measures for the same 2020 to 2024 period give smaller numbers. People born in Liberia number 15,297 in Minnesota, give or take 1,615. People who wrote "Liberian" as an ancestry number 13,579, give or take 2,154 ([Census Reporter, ACS 2020-2024 table B05006](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US27,16000US2707966,16000US2707948,16000US2743000,16000US2758000,05000US27053,05000US27123,05000US27003,01000US,16000US2713456,16000US2739878,16000US2747680,16000US2754880); [Census Reporter, table B04006](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B04006&geo_ids=04000US27,16000US2707966,16000US2707948,16000US2743000,16000US2758000,05000US27053,05000US27123,05000US27003,01000US,16000US2713456,16000US2739878,16000US2747680,16000US2754880)). The gap matters: many U.S.-born children of Liberian parents, and many adults, may record themselves as Black or African American rather than Liberian.

Community and public estimates run higher. Sahan Journal reported about 30,000 in 2020 ([Sahan Journal, Jan. 5, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cried-with-joy-minnesotas-liberians-find-elation-relief-in-new-residency-law/)), as did MPR News in 2019 and 2024 ([MPR News, March 11, 2019](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2019/03/11/liberians-minnesota-deportation-deadline); [MPR News, July 26, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/07/26/ones-to-watch-these-minnesota-men-are-running-for-liberia-at-the-paris-olympics)). CCX Media cited an estimated 35,000 in July 2026 ([CCX Media, July 2, 2026](https://ccxmedia.org/news/olm-receives-400k-to-expand-brooklyn-park-facility/)). Gov. Tim Walz used about 40,000 in a 2019 meeting with Liberia's ambassador, as reported by the Liberian Embassy ([Embassy of Liberia, 2019](https://liberianembassyus.org/node/6198)), and the Organization of Liberians in Minnesota (OLM) also uses about 40,000 ([OLM, Seniors Enrichment Program](https://www.olmbp.org/sep)). Lower official-sounding counts have appeared too: Attorney General Keith Ellison's office said "nearly 16,000" people born in Liberia or claiming Liberian ancestry in 2019 ([Minnesota Attorney General, March 26, 2019](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Office/Communications/2019/03/26_LiberianDEDStatus.asp)). In 2007, MPR said "as many as 25,000," adding that demographers were not sure ([MPR News, Sept. 13, 2007](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2007/09/13/liberianstatus)). None of these is a census of the community.

**Largest in the nation.** By the Liberia-born measure, Minnesota leads the states compared. It has 15,297 people born in Liberia, followed by Pennsylvania at 13,214, New Jersey at 8,684 and Maryland at 6,043. Minnesota holds about 15 percent of the 99,221 Liberia-born people in the United States ([Census Reporter, ACS 2020-2024, states compared](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US06,04000US10,04000US11,04000US13,04000US24,04000US25,04000US26,04000US27,04000US34,04000US36,04000US37,04000US39,04000US42,04000US44,04000US48,04000US51,04000US53,04000US55,04000US17,04000US12)). State Demographer Susan Brower put Minnesota's share at about 17 percent in 2014, the largest in the country ([CBS Minnesota/AP, July 29, 2014](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/liberians-in-minnesota-concerned-over-ebola-outbreak/)). Then-Gov. Mark Dayton wrote in 2018 that Minnesota had "the largest community of Liberian Americans of any state" ([Gov. Dayton letter, March 29, 2018](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2018/other/181224/governor/assets/2018_03_29_ded_letter.pdf)).

**Where.** Compass places 86.8 percent in the Twin Cities and 13.2 percent in Greater Minnesota, each give or take 6.1 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/liberian)). Hennepin County holds most people born in Liberia (9,672), followed by Anoka (2,303) and Ramsey (815). Brooklyn Park has 4,544 Liberia-born residents, give or take 1,153, and Brooklyn Center has 1,147, give or take 320. Smaller numbers live in Minneapolis (620), St. Paul (359) and Rochester (360, with a margin nearly as large) ([Census Reporter, B05006](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US27,16000US2707966,16000US2707948,16000US2743000,16000US2758000,05000US27053,05000US27123,05000US27003,01000US,16000US2713456,16000US2739878,16000US2747680,16000US2754880)). City officials put Brooklyn Park's Liberian population above 7,000 ([CCX Media, June 18, 2026](https://ccxmedia.org/news/minnesota-united-to-host-june-22-youth-soccer-clinic-in-brooklyn-park/)), and Sahan Journal used the same 7,000 in 2020 ([Sahan Journal, Jan. 5, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cried-with-joy-minnesotas-liberians-find-elation-relief-in-new-residency-law/)). The City of Brooklyn Park calls itself "home of the largest population of Liberians outside of the Republic of Liberia" ([City of Brooklyn Park, July 23, 2021](https://www.brooklynpark.org/community-story/united-community-liberian-independence-day/)). Pastor Francis Tabla told Luther Seminary in 2015 that "Brooklyn Park is seen as the capital city of Liberia in Minnesota" ([Luther Seminary, Nov. 16, 2015](https://www.luthersem.edu/story/2015/11/16/d-min-alums-minister-to-immigrant-populations-in-the-twin-cities/)). Daily life also reaches Crystal, New Hope, Maple Grove, Robbinsdale, Blaine, Coon Rapids, Mounds View and St. Paul, where the businesses, churches and people named below are found. No Minnesota source was found describing a distinct Liberian community in a Greater Minnesota town.

**How people came.** The Minnesota community grew mostly from Liberia's civil wars, which began in 1989 and ended in 2003 ([Gov. Dayton letter, 2018](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2018/other/181224/governor/assets/2018_03_29_ded_letter.pdf); [MPR News/AP, 2007](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2007/09/13/liberianstatus)). In 1991 President George H.W. Bush granted Liberians in the U.S. Temporary Protected Status (TPS), and Liberians have been covered by TPS or Deferred Enforced Departure (DED) since then ([Minnesota Attorney General, 2019](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Office/Communications/2019/03/26_LiberianDEDStatus.asp); [Sahan Journal, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cried-with-joy-minnesotas-liberians-find-elation-relief-in-new-residency-law/)). MPR News wrote that in 1991 "the U.S., and specifically Minnesota, began resettling refugees from Liberia" ([MPR News, July 26, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/07/26/ones-to-watch-these-minnesota-men-are-running-for-liberia-at-the-paris-olympics)). Many came by other routes: a student visa, a cultural trip that could not end because the war worsened, or a medical visa for a sick child ([Sahan Journal, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cried-with-joy-minnesotas-liberians-find-elation-relief-in-new-residency-law/)). Some moved here from other states to be near family ([CCX Media, Dec. 18, 2019](https://ccxmedia.org/news/maple-grove-liberian-immigrant-celebrates-u-s-senate-vote/)). A smaller earlier presence existed: OLM says it was founded in 1973 ([OLM, About](https://www.olmbp.org/about)). Refugee arrivals are now few; Minnesota recorded six primary refugee arrivals from Liberia in 2020 ([Minnesota Department of Education, English Learners report, 2024](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2024/other/240747.pdf)).

**Generations.** This is a young community. Compass counts 46.0 percent at age 17 or younger and 4.1 percent at 65 or older, and 59.9 percent born outside the U.S., give or take 6.1 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/liberian)). That means many households combine adults who lived through the wars with children born and raised in Minnesota. OLM counts "at least 5,000 seniors" in the community, which is its own estimate and larger than the Census figures suggest ([OLM, Seniors Enrichment Program](https://www.olmbp.org/sep)).

**Languages.** English is Liberia's official language, and most Liberian Minnesotans speak it. Among people 5 and older, Compass finds 83.6 percent speak only English at home, 16.4 percent speak another language, and 7.1 percent speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/liberian)). The state demographer's 2019 analysis put the share of Liberian Minnesotans who do not speak English well or at all at 2 percent, the same as the state overall ([Minnesota State Demographic Center presentation, Feb. 6, 2019](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/7c13a07c-694f-44ce-a330-86bb54727e62.pdf)). Those figures do not capture Liberian English, the everyday English many people speak, or the many Liberian languages spoken at home. OLM's senior program offers health information "in Liberian dialects," a sign that some elders prefer them ([OLM, Seniors Enrichment Program](https://www.olmbp.org/sep)). No Minnesota count of speakers of Kpelle, Bassa, Kru, Grebo, Vai, Mandingo or other Liberian languages was found.

## 2. All walks of life

All figures in this list come from the Compass profile for 2020 to 2024, in 2024 dollars, unless noted ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/liberian)). The sample is small, so margins of error are wide.

- **Comfortable and well-off households.** About 22.4 percent of households earn $100,000 or more, and another 23.2 percent earn $75,000 to $99,999. Median household income is $67,197, give or take $14,040. Households are large, averaging 3.54 people, so income per person is lower than the household figure suggests.
- **Professionals and owners.** Among adults 25 and older, 28.8 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more, including 6.5 percent with a graduate or professional degree. Public careers include a former Brooklyn Center mayor, a former Brooklyn Park council member with a public health background, a travel agency owner who spent 13 years at a commercial bank, and church founders who built a $2.8 million sanctuary (sections 4, 5 and 9). In health care, many Liberian Minnesotans work as nurses, medication aides and nursing assistants (section 3).
- **Middle-income and working families.** Owner-occupied homes are 36.4 percent of households, give or take 8.4 points. Employment among adults 18 to 64 is 83.8 percent, 87.8 percent for men and 80.0 percent for women. Another 23.0 percent of people live between the poverty line and twice that line. Most commute by car (93.6 percent).
- **People in poverty.** About 20.4 percent of people live below the poverty line, give or take 8.3 points. Among renters, 50.1 percent spend too much of their income on housing; across all households, 43.9 percent do. About 11.5 percent of households have no vehicle, 8.1 percent of people have no health insurance, and 8.0 percent of adults did not finish high school. Single mothers head 15.7 percent of households. A Maple Grove mother of five described to CCX Media a time when she and her children were homeless, before she organized a pandemic relief drive for others ([CCX Media, March 30, 2020](https://ccxmedia.org/news/liberians-organize-to-help-community-during-covid-19-outbreak/)).

**Hidden costs.** Many families support relatives in Liberia. Marie Zar, a nurse at North Memorial Medical Center, told FOX 9 in 2018, "I work here and send money to my people there to survive" ([FOX 9, March 26, 2018](https://www.fox9.com/news/liberians-rally-at-minnesota-capitol-as-ded-program-deadline-approaches)). Years of temporary status also carried costs: a St. Paul nursing assistant and her son told Sahan Journal that renewals cost the family about $1,200 a year, and that her employer took her off the schedule while her status was in limbo ([Sahan Journal, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cried-with-joy-minnesotas-liberians-find-elation-relief-in-new-residency-law/)).

## 3. Work and the economy

**Health care.** Health care is the best-documented field. The St. Paul Pioneer Press reported in 2018 that "36 percent of Liberian workers are employed in health-related fields," citing Census surveys ([Pioneer Press via Inforum, April 7, 2018](https://www.inforum.com/news/liberian-immigrants-face-uncertain-fate-as-temporary-status-ends)). FOX 9 reported an estimate of at least 3,000 to 4,000 Liberians in Minnesota working in health care ([FOX 9, March 31, 2018](https://www.fox9.com/news/end-of-ded-program-could-impact-minnesota-health-care-industry)). Nicole Mattson, an administrator at a Good Samaritan Society care community in Robbinsdale, told MPR that 60 percent of her employees were immigrants and "over half of those are Liberians" ([MPR News, March 11, 2019](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2019/03/11/liberians-minnesota-deportation-deadline); [Pioneer Press via Inforum, 2018](https://www.inforum.com/news/liberian-immigrants-face-uncertain-fate-as-temporary-status-ends)). Abdullah Kiatamba of African Immigrant Services told FOX 9 that health care "provided an easy way for them to integrate into society" ([FOX 9, 2018](https://www.fox9.com/news/end-of-ded-program-could-impact-minnesota-health-care-industry)). Governor Dayton wrote that losing these workers would cause "a workforce crisis in Minnesota's health care industry" ([Gov. Dayton letter, 2018](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2018/other/181224/governor/assets/2018_03_29_ded_letter.pdf)). Not everyone works in care. One woman told MPR she had worked for Boston Scientific and Medtronic ([MPR News, 2019](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2019/03/11/liberians-minnesota-deportation-deadline)).

**Training pipelines.** In 2021 the Brooklyn Park Economic Development Authority approved $117,770 for OLM to train 50 residents as nursing assistants and 10 as medication aides, open to residents of any background, through three local schools ([CCX Media, April 26, 2021](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-liberian-organization-partner-for-healthcare-workers-training-program/)). OLM says this work later reached more than 90 people who lost jobs during the pandemic ([OLM, Community Enrichment Program](https://www.olmbp.org/cep)). African Career, Education & Resource, Inc. (ACER) in Brooklyn Center advertises health care training scholarships ([ACER](https://acerinc.org/)).

**Labor.** Liberian workers have taken part in labor organizing. The Pioneer Press quoted Alfreida Daniels, a Liberian-born organizer with the Minneapolis Regional Labor Federation, and reported that SEIU Healthcare Minnesota was discussing joint advocacy with nursing home companies on DED ([Pioneer Press via Inforum, 2018](https://www.inforum.com/news/liberian-immigrants-face-uncertain-fate-as-temporary-status-ends)).

**Business support.** The Liberian Business Association (LIBA), founded in 2016, told CCX Media in 2020 it had helped about 250 minority-owned businesses ([CCX Media, Oct. 8, 2020](https://ccxmedia.org/news/liberian-business-association-connects-dots-for-minority-owned-businesses/)). In a 2023 Senate handout, LIBA-Diaspora said it had trained 120 small businesses in QuickBooks with city funding, run a Brooklyn Center micro-loan program of $5,000 loans to 20 businesses, and held Hennepin County contracts ([LIBA handout, Minnesota Senate, March 20, 2023](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2023/jobs/20230320/Jobs_20230320_Handout-LIBA-Work-in-the-Community.pdf)). Women business owners, "mostly Liberia, Nigeria, Somalia and other African countries," formed the Ignite Business Women Investment Group cooperative. Its 27 members pooled nearly $300,000 and, with other funding, bought the Shingle Creek Center strip mall in Brooklyn Center in October 2023 ([Next City, Feb. 20, 2024](https://nextcity.org/features/black-immigrant-women-bought-minneapolis-strip-mall-shingle-creek-cent)).

**Employment under strain.** Temporary immigration status shaped working life for decades. Employers told reporters they would struggle without Liberian staff, and workers described being pulled off schedules when permits lapsed (sections 2 and 13).

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

Liberian commercial life centers on the northwest suburbs, especially Brooklyn Boulevard, Shingle Creek Parkway and the Zane Avenue corridor. CCX Media reported in 2018 that "a lot of Liberians live and work in Brooklyn Center and Brooklyn Park, including at businesses along the Zane Avenue corridor" ([CCX Media, March 12, 2018](https://ccxmedia.org/news/hundreds-local-liberians-worry-deportation/)). The full list, with status for each, is in businesses.json; highlights follow. The list is short because few Liberian-owned businesses have current, open web pages or recent news coverage. Most professional firms, salons and home care agencies could not be found under a Liberian identity.

- **Carol's African Restaurant**, 6940 Brooklyn Blvd. Opened May 9, 2026, by Daniel and Jamecar Killen and named for Jamecar's mother, Carol Gbanjah, after about a decade of family catering. It serves mostly Liberian food, including dried rice, jollof rice and house-made ginger juice. More than 200 people came to its opening ([Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder, June 27, 2026](https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/06/27/carols-african-restaurant-brooklyn-park/); [CCX Media, July 7, 2026](https://ccxmedia.org/news/new-liberian-restaurant-offers-west-african-flavors-in-brooklyn-center/)). Sources disagree on the city: the Spokesman-Recorder says Brooklyn Park and CCX says Brooklyn Center.
- **Milah's Royal Bistro**, 7648 N. Humboldt Ave., Brooklyn Park. Founded by Milah Kai, a native of Liberia ([menu listing](https://www.roostcafeandbistro.com/milahs-royal-bistro-55444/)); named in 2025 and 2026 coverage of the local food scene ([CCX Media, 2025](https://ccxmedia.org/news/liberian-independence-day-celebrated-through-local-business-impact/); [Hoodline, June 2026](https://hoodline.com/2026/06/liberian-hot-spot-carol-s-packs-brooklyn-park-corner-on-opening-night/)).
- **Christina's Child Care Center** in Crystal, started by Ophelia Zeogar in her basement 19 years before 2025 and now run with her U.S.-born daughter, Christina Fraser, who said, "As Liberians, it's a big part of what we do here" ([CCX Media, July 2025](https://ccxmedia.org/news/liberian-independence-day-celebrated-through-local-business-impact/)).
- **Cavalla Travel & Tours**, Shingle Creek Center, 6044 Shingle Creek Parkway, Brooklyn Center. Owner Jannie Seibure, a native of Liberia, opened a storefront with luggage and travel goods in 2024 after 15 years in the area ([CCX Media, June 27, 2024](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-center-travel-business-expands-in-shingle-creek/)).
- **Jambo Africa**, 1601 Freeway Blvd., Brooklyn Center, serves Nigerian, Kenyan and Liberian food and hosts Liberian Independence Day events. The city approved its move to a larger site in July 2026 ([CCX Media, Aug. 11, 2026](https://ccxmedia.org/news/jambo-africa-restaurant-approved-for-new-brooklyn-center-location/)).
- **African groceries and delis.** Value Foods African Market in Brooklyn Park and Waterside Market on Brooklyn Boulevard sell West African foods ([Value Foods Corp.](https://valuefoodsmn.com/about.php); [Atly listing](https://www.atly.com/location/WatersideMarket1.md)). African Foods & Gifts in the Crystal Shopping Center, owned by Marlene Nah, served pepper soup and cassava leaf in 2022 ([CCX Media, Feb. 15, 2022](https://ccxmedia.org/news/crystals-african-foods-gifts-goes-beyond-the-cassava-leaf/)). Neither Liberian ownership nor current operation could be confirmed for all of these.
- **Media.** The AfricaPaper is a Minnesota community newspaper whose associate editor, James Kokulo Fasuekoi, is a native of Liberia ([The AfricaPaper, About](https://theafricapaper.com/about-us/)). CCX Media, the northwest suburbs' community station, covers Liberian life regularly ([CCX Media, Liberian coverage](https://ccxmedia.org/?s=Liberian)). No Liberian-specific radio or television program based in Minnesota was found.

**Gone or uncertain.** Mama Ti's, a Brooklyn Park restaurant serving Liberian home cooking, lost business in 2014 when customers asked staff "do you have Ebola?" ([Food Republic, Oct. 16, 2014](https://www.foodrepublic.com/2014/10/16/its-hard-out-there-for-a-liberian-restaurant/)); no current sign of it was found.

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

**A mostly Christian public life, with a real Muslim community.** Churches are the most visible institutions. The Liberian Ministers Association, whose executive director in a Luther Seminary biography was Rev. Dr. Josef A. Howard of Bethel World Outreach Church in Robbinsdale, brings Liberian pastors together ([Working Preacher, Luther Seminary](https://www.workingpreacher.org/authors/rev-dr-josef-a-howard)). OLM names the association as a partner ([OLM, About](https://www.olmbp.org/about)).

**Named congregations.**
- **Ebenezer Community Church**, 9200 West Broadway Ave., Brooklyn Park. Pastors Francis and Christine Tabla founded it 25 years ago with eight members. The church describes itself as non-denominational, representing "the rich cultures and traditions of West Africa and beyond," with more than 800 members and services Sundays at 9 and 11 a.m. ([Ebenezer Community Church](https://www.ebenezercommunitychurch.org/)). It began as a Bible study at Pilgrim Baptist Church in St. Paul and had 432 members in 2015 ([Luther Seminary, 2015](https://www.luthersem.edu/story/2015/11/16/d-min-alums-minister-to-immigrant-populations-in-the-twin-cities/)). It bought 4.3 acres for $745,000 in 2006, broke ground in 2015 and dedicated a $2.8 million building ([FrontPage Africa](https://frontpageafricaonline.com/liberian-immigrants-promoting-gospel-in-minnesota/)).
- **United Christian Fellowship Church** in north Minneapolis, described by CCX Media in 2018 as one of the oldest Liberian churches in Minnesota, founded and led for more than 20 years by Rev. Charles Goah, who died that year ([CCX Media, May 11, 2018](https://ccxmedia.org/news/beloved-liberian-pastor-charles-goah-dies/)).
- **Redeemed Life Church**, Mounds View, led by Pastor Alexander B. Collins, who received a 2018 Governor's Community Leadership Award ([The AfricaPaper](https://theafricapaper.com/us-liberian-pastor-receives-minnesota-governors-award/)). He shipped donated police uniforms to Liberia in 2019 and traveled to Kakata for a park-naming ceremony in 2022 ([CCX Media, Dec. 14, 2019](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-police-to-donate-old-uniforms-to-liberian-officers/); [CCX Media, May 26, 2022](https://ccxmedia.org/news/liberian-city-names-parkland-after-brooklyn-park/)).
- **Holy Trinity Episcopal Church** on Van Buren Avenue in St. Paul, where Rev. James Nyebe Wilson II, a Liberian, was priest in charge in 2018 ([Pioneer Press via Inforum, 2018](https://www.inforum.com/news/liberian-immigrants-face-uncertain-fate-as-temporary-status-ends)). Its current leadership was not confirmed.
- **Masjid Al-Ansar Islamic Community Center**, 4900 France Ave. N., Brooklyn Center. It opened in October 2022 after members spent years renting space and praying "in schools or outside in the park." CCX Media reported that the congregation is mainly West African immigrants, "a majority from Liberia," and that buying the building took years because most members live paycheck to paycheck ([CCX Media, Oct. 14, 2022](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-center-mosque-officially-opens-after-years-of-planning/); [CCX Media, March 24, 2020](https://ccxmedia.org/news/despite-parking-concerns-mosque-approved-for-brooklyn-center-site/)). OLM lists the masjid as a partner ([OLM, About](https://www.olmbp.org/about)).

**Faith in hard times.** Pastors carried their congregations through war news, the 2014 Ebola epidemic and deportation fears. Tabla said that during Ebola, "I dreaded my phone ringing" because so many members were losing relatives ([Luther Seminary, 2015](https://www.luthersem.edu/story/2015/11/16/d-min-alums-minister-to-immigrant-populations-in-the-twin-cities/)). Prayer runs through public life; a Brooklyn Park woman facing deportation in 2019 told MPR she was "depending on the Almighty God" ([MPR News, 2019](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2019/03/11/liberians-minnesota-deportation-deadline)).

**What is not documented.** No Minnesota source was found on Catholic parishes with Liberian ministries, on traditional Liberian religious practice here, or on Liberian Minnesotans who hold no religion. Their absence from sources does not mean they are absent from the community.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

**Liberian Independence Day, July 26.** This is the community's largest public celebration. Liberia declared independence on July 26, 1847 ([CCX Media, July 25, 2019](https://ccxmedia.org/news/residents-celebrate-liberian-independence-day/)). People greet each other with "My 26th is on you" during the season ([Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder, July 25, 2019](https://spokesman-recorder.com/2019/07/25/liberian-independence-celebrations-kick-off/)). In Minnesota the day has grown into a multi-day weekend:

- **We Are Liberian Parade and Day in the Park.** The first Liberian Independence Day parade in Minnesota stepped off on Shingle Creek Parkway in Brooklyn Center on Saturday, July 26, 2025, ending at Centennial Park, with about 70 vendors planned ([CCX Media, July 2025](https://ccxmedia.org/news/liberian-independence-day-celebrated-through-local-business-impact/)). The second was held at North Hennepin Community College in Brooklyn Park on Sunday, July 26, 2026, from 11 a.m. to 7 p.m. ([Sahan Journal, July 24, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minnesota-liberian-independence-day-festival-2026/)). Co-founder Marie Dweh said the mission is "to bridge the gap between the older generation and the new generation."
- **OLM events.** OLM held an Independence Twin Ball at the Marriott Northwest in Brooklyn Park on Friday, July 24, 2026, and a Liberia Youth Music Festival with a mock match between Liberia's rival clubs Mighty Barrolle and Invincible Eleven on Saturday, July 25 ([OLM, Twin Ball](https://www.olmbp.org/event-details/independence-twin-ball); [OLM, Youth Festival + Sports](https://www.olmbp.org/event-details/youth-festival-sports)). OLM's page places the youth festival at Northwoods Park Field, while Sahan Journal listed the soccer match at Robbinsdale Armstrong High School at 5 p.m.
- **Concerts.** Sahan Journal listed the Tribeville Music Festival at the Armory in Minneapolis on July 24 and the Mama Liberia Concert at the Varsity Theater on July 25, 2026 ([Sahan Journal, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minnesota-liberian-independence-day-festival-2026/)).
- **A canceled match.** A friendly between Minnesota United and Liberia's national team, the Lone Stars, was planned for Allianz Field on July 26, 2026, and canceled after some team members were denied U.S. visas ([CCX Media, July 16, 2026](https://ccxmedia.org/news/newsmakers-liberia-national-soccer-team-to-play-minnesota-united-july-26/)).
- **Earlier years.** In 2019 OLM held an art show, banquet, soccer and kickball tournaments and a prayer service, and both Gov. Walz and Brooklyn Park proclaimed Liberian Independence Week ([CCX Media, 2019](https://ccxmedia.org/news/residents-celebrate-liberian-independence-day/)). Brooklyn Park proclaimed July 26, 2021, Liberian Independence Day ([CCX Media, July 15, 2021](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-to-celebrate-liberian-independence-day-july-26/)). In 2015 the community gathered at Park Center High School ([CBS Minnesota, July 25, 2015](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/after-tough-year-liberian-community-celebrates-independence-day/)).

**Christian holy days.** Christmas is a national holiday in Liberia ([Office Holidays, Liberia 2026](https://www.officeholidays.com/countries/liberia/2026)), and churches here are central to community life. No Minnesota source describing a distinctly Liberian way of keeping them here was found.

**Muslim holy days.** With a majority-Liberian congregation at Masjid Al-Ansar, Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha matter to many families. When the city weighed the mosque's new site in 2020, members urged it to act quickly because Ramadan was approaching ([CCX Media, 2020](https://ccxmedia.org/news/despite-parking-concerns-mosque-approved-for-brooklyn-center-site/)). No Minnesota report on how its Eid prayers are held was found.

**Other Liberian national days.** Liberia's calendar includes Armed Forces Day (Feb. 11), Decoration Day (second Wednesday in March), National Fast and Prayer Day (second Friday in April), National Unification Day (May 14), Flag Day (Aug. 24) and National Thanksgiving Day (first Thursday in November) ([Office Holidays, Liberia 2026](https://www.officeholidays.com/countries/liberia/2026)). Fast and Prayer Day dates to an act of 1883 ([Liberia Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://www.mofa.gov.lr/node/5184)). No Minnesota observance of these days was found.

**Other gatherings.** The Liberian National Cultural Ambassadors, a U.S.-based troupe of former national performers, held their annual festival in Minnesota for the first time at Concordia Academy in August 2015; its Minnesota chapter was led by Isabella Wreh-Fofana ([The AfricaPaper, 2015](https://theafricapaper.com/us-based-west-africa-ballet-dance-troupe-comes-to-twin-cities/)).

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Households.** Compass counts 6,122 Liberian households with an average of 3.54 people. Married couples with children under 18 head 32.5 percent of households, single mothers 15.7 percent, and 18.5 percent are people living alone ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/liberian)).

**Families split across an ocean.** Temporary status kept many parents from traveling for decades. Women told Sahan Journal in 2020 that they had not seen sons in Liberia since the boys were small, and one could not go home to bury her mother in 2017 ([Sahan Journal, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cried-with-joy-minnesotas-liberians-find-elation-relief-in-new-residency-law/)). A Maple Grove mother told CCX Media that she and her U.S.-citizen daughter cried at the thought of separation before the 2019 law passed ([CCX Media, Dec. 18, 2019](https://ccxmedia.org/news/maple-grove-liberian-immigrant-celebrates-u-s-senate-vote/)).

**Food.** Carol's serves dried rice and jollof rice with fish or chicken; customer listings for Milah's Royal Bistro mention palava sauce, check rice, cassava toborgee and fufu ([CCX Media, 2026](https://ccxmedia.org/news/new-liberian-restaurant-offers-west-african-flavors-in-brooklyn-center/); [menu listing](https://www.roostcafeandbistro.com/milahs-royal-bistro-55444/)). The Spokesman-Recorder named sour milk (a homemade yogurt), pepper kala (a fried snack eaten with pepper sauce) and kanyan (peanuts with garri) as Independence Day favorites ([Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder, 2019](https://spokesman-recorder.com/2019/07/25/liberian-independence-celebrations-kick-off/)). Hospitality runs through the stories: the Killens told the Spokesman-Recorder that 40 or 50 people would come to their house on a Saturday just to eat ([Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder, 2026](https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/06/27/carols-african-restaurant-brooklyn-park/)).

**Childhood and youth.** OLM runs a youth program for ages 12 to 18 on the last Friday of each month, with career speakers; OLM says the program grew from concern about school dropout and drug use among some youth ([OLM, Youth Enrichment Program](https://www.olmbp.org/yep)). Soccer, kickball and basketball tournaments bring children into Liberian culture each July ([Sahan Journal, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minnesota-liberian-independence-day-festival-2026/)).

**Aging.** OLM's senior program meets weekly on Wednesdays with health talks, exercise, computer and reading classes, and quilting and gardening. OLM says 98 percent of participating seniors need rides ([OLM, Seniors Enrichment Program](https://www.olmbp.org/sep)).

**Dress and marriage.** No Minnesota source was found that documents dress or marriage customs in detail; Independence Day coverage mentions traditional clothes as part of the celebration ([Sahan Journal, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minnesota-liberian-independence-day-festival-2026/)).

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Sports.** Soccer is the strongest shared pastime. Cletus Constance, founder of the Liberian Sporting Association of Minnesota, told Sahan Journal soccer is "a big deal in the Liberian community" and that on the field "everyone puts aside their differences." He said his Independence Day events grew from about 1,000 people in 2021 to 3,000 the next year ([Sahan Journal, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minnesota-liberian-independence-day-festival-2026/)). Minnesota has produced several of Liberia's Olympic sprinters. Emmanuel Matadi, a state champion at St. Paul's Johnson Senior High who won NCAA Division II titles at Minnesota State Mankato, carried Liberia's flag at the 2016 Rio Olympics ([MPR News, Aug. 12, 2016](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2016/08/12/st-paul-sprinter-pushed-through-adversity-to-race-in-rio)). Liberia's 2024 Olympic relay team also included Akeem Sirleaf of North St. Paul High, Joseph Fahnbulleh of Hopkins High and Jabez Reeves of Minnesota State Mankato ([MPR News, July 26, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/07/26/ones-to-watch-these-minnesota-men-are-running-for-liberia-at-the-paris-olympics)). Daniel Killen, co-owner of Carol's, was a Minnesota Mr. Soccer ([Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder, 2026](https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/06/27/carols-african-restaurant-brooklyn-park/)).

**Music.** Concerts were part of the 2026 Independence weekend (section 6). Twin Cities hip-hop artist EL.i.BE, originally from Liberia, layered his rhymes over traditional instruments on his 2017 album *Liberation* ([Essentially Pop, May 2017](https://essentiallypop.com/epop/2017/05/el-i-be-liberation/)). Kokou Kah, who calls his sound "True Fusion" Liberian-American music, performed in First Avenue's Best New Bands of 2021 showcase and played a free Rochester concert in 2022 ([The Current, 2022](https://www.thecurrent.org/events/kokou-kah-08312022)).

**Dance and visual art.** The Nimely Pan African Dance Company, a Liberian folkloric company, was founded in Minnesota in 1994 by Nimely Napla before moving to Oakland ([Dancers' Group, 2011](https://dancersgroup.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/10/RotundaPR_Nimely.pdf)). Installation artist Catherine Kennedy, a Liberian native, exhibited work on women in the civil war at an African food market in Crystal in 2010 ([MPR News, July 22, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/07/22/art-hounds-ladysmith-liberia-and-dance)).

**Media.** Liberian news reaches Minnesota through The AfricaPaper, CCX Media, Sahan Journal and Liberian outlets such as FrontPage Africa, which covers Minnesota events ([FrontPage Africa, Jan. 9, 2026](https://frontpageafricaonline.com/liberian-born-u-s-citizen-hospitalized-after-alleged-rough-encounter-with-ice-agents-in-minnesota/)). Mshale, Minnesota's African immigrant newspaper, covers Liberian politics closely, but its pages could not be opened for this profile.

## 9. Civic and political life

**Elected officials.** Mike Elliott, who came from Liberia with his family at 11, was elected Brooklyn Center's first Black mayor and first Liberian American mayor in 2018 with 55 percent of the vote, after losing in 2014 ([Sahan Journal, 2021](https://sahanjournal.com/public-safety/mike-elliott-mayor-brooklyn-center/)). He led the city through the April 2021 police killing of Daunte Wright and lost re-election in 2022 to April Graves ([CCX Media, Dec. 2022](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-center-mayor-at-final-meeting-lets-celebrate-our-differences/)). Wynfred Russell, who grew up in a refugee camp and came to Minnesota when the University of Minnesota hired him, was sworn in as Brooklyn Park's first Liberian American council member in January 2019 ([FOX 9, 2019](https://www.fox9.com/news/history-made-brooklyn-park-swears-in-first-liberian-american-city-council-member); [CCX Media, Jan. 8, 2019](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-swears-in-first-liberian-american-council-member/)). He served one term, from 2019 to 2022 ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/democracy-politics/minnesota-primary-huldah-hiltsley-wynfred-russell/)). He twice sought the DFL nomination for Minnesota House District 38A. He lost in 2024 by 50 votes, 955 to 1,005 ([CCX Media, Aug. 14, 2024](https://ccxmedia.org/news/hiltsley-edges-russell-in-house-38a-primary-to-face-olson/)). He lost again on Aug. 11, 2026, with 41.8 percent to Rep. Huldah Momanyi-Hiltsley's 48.8 percent ([CCX Media, Aug. 11, 2026](https://ccxmedia.org/news/state-primaries-janigo-pha-win-senate-challenges-momanyi-hiltsley-advances-in-house-race/)). Russell told Sahan Journal there are "very few West Africans" in the Minnesota House ([Sahan Journal, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/democracy-politics/minnesota-primary-huldah-hiltsley-wynfred-russell/)).

**Organizations.**
- **Organization of Liberians in Minnesota (OLM)**, 7001 78th Ave. N., Brooklyn Park, founded 1973 and incorporated 1979. Its members elect a nine-member board every two years, and it calls itself "the single largest representative and voice of all Liberians" in Minnesota ([OLM, About](https://www.olmbp.org/about)). Its programs include senior and youth programs, monthly food distribution to about 250 families, voter outreach, and membership in the Brooklyn Center Multicultural Advisory Committee on policing ([OLM, Community Enrichment Program](https://www.olmbp.org/cep)). The 2026 Legislature put $400,000 for its community center in the bonding bill. Executive Director Kamaty Diahn said, "This community has always craved for a community center" ([CCX Media, July 2, 2026](https://ccxmedia.org/news/olm-receives-400k-to-expand-brooklyn-park-facility/); [SF 1249, 2025](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/bills/94/2025/0/SF/1249/versions/latest/)).
- **ACER**, 6096 Shingle Creek Parkway, Brooklyn Center, founded by Russell, works on civic engagement, housing, health and small business ([ACER](https://acerinc.org/)); it was established in 2008, according to Next City ([Next City, 2024](https://nextcity.org/features/black-immigrant-women-bought-minneapolis-strip-mall-shingle-creek-cent)).
- **African Immigrant Services**, Brooklyn Park, led by Abdullah Kiatamba, a Liberian immigrant, during the DED fights; it lined up 10 lawyers for green card applications in 2020 ([Sahan Journal, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cried-with-joy-minnesotas-liberians-find-elation-relief-in-new-residency-law/); [MPR News, March 28, 2018](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2018/03/28/for-liberians-in-minnesota-uncertain-year-ahead)).
- **We Are Liberian**, co-founded by Marie Dweh, organizes the Independence Day parade ([Sahan Journal, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minnesota-liberian-independence-day-festival-2026/)).
- **SARX (Strengthening African Resilience Towards Excellence)**, a Brooklyn Park health-equity nonprofit led by Russell, organized the planned Lone Stars match ([CCX Media, July 16, 2026](https://ccxmedia.org/news/newsmakers-liberia-national-soccer-team-to-play-minnesota-united-july-26/)).

**Ties between cities.** Brooklyn Park has had a sister-city relationship with Kakata, Liberia, since 2012 ([CCX Media, 2022](https://ccxmedia.org/news/liberian-city-names-parkland-after-brooklyn-park/); [City of Brooklyn Park, Sister Cities](https://www.brooklynpark.org/sister-cities/)). Kakata named a six-acre park after Brooklyn Park in 2022. The city has sent used police uniforms to Margibi County and playground equipment to Paynesville ([CCX Media, Dec. 14, 2019](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-police-to-donate-old-uniforms-to-liberian-officers/); [City of Brooklyn Park, April 30, 2020](https://www.brooklynpark.org/community-story/from-brooklyn-park-minnesota-to-paynesville-liberia/)).

**Thirty years of advocacy.** The long campaign for permanent status is the community's defining civic story. Several hundred people rallied at the State Capitol in March 2018 ([CCX Media, March 26, 2018](https://ccxmedia.org/news/liberians-rally/); [FOX 9, March 26, 2018](https://www.fox9.com/news/liberians-rally-at-minnesota-capitol-as-ded-program-deadline-approaches)). In 2019 Attorney General Ellison co-led nine states and the District of Columbia in backing Liberians' lawsuit against the end of DED ([Minnesota Attorney General, 2019](https://www.ag.state.mn.us/Office/Communications/2019/03/26_LiberianDEDStatus.asp)). Abdullah Kiatamba called the 2019 law "the biggest win we've ever achieved collectively" ([Sahan Journal, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cried-with-joy-minnesotas-liberians-find-elation-relief-in-new-residency-law/)).

**Truth-telling.** In June 2008, Liberia's Truth and Reconciliation Commission held public hearings at Hamline University in St. Paul, described by The Advocates for Human Rights as the first public hearings of a national truth commission held in a diaspora. More than 1,600 statements were taken in the diaspora project ([The Advocates for Human Rights](https://www.theadvocatesforhumanrights.org/Liberia_TRC_Diaspora_Project); [Mitchell Hamline Open Access, 2009](https://open.mitchellhamline.edu/dri_press/7/)).

**Debates inside the community.** In 2003, MPR reported that some Liberians in Minnesota accused others of raising money for rebel groups, and that suspicions of rebel ties divided the community ([MPR News archive, Aug. 7, 2003](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/2003/08/07/liberians-minnesota-divided-country-s)). In 2020 the federal government deported Alexander Mentol Zinnah, who had lived in Minnesota, saying he had been a member of Charles Taylor's rebel group and a police commander under Taylor ([CBS Minnesota/AP, April 29, 2020](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesotan-who-was-fmr-liberian-security-forces-commander-deported/)). These cases show that people who survived the wars and people accused of wartime roles have sometimes lived in the same state.

## 10. Education

**Attainment.** Among Liberian Minnesotans 25 and older, 92.0 percent finished high school, 34.4 percent have some college or an associate degree, 22.3 percent hold a bachelor's degree and 6.5 percent hold a graduate degree ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/liberian)). Short-term health care credentials, such as nursing assistant and medication aide training, are a common path into work (section 3).

**Schools.** Public schools in the northwest suburbs are part of community life: Mike Elliott graduated from Brooklyn Center High School, and Park Center High School in Brooklyn Park has hosted Independence Day gatherings ([Sahan Journal, 2021](https://sahanjournal.com/public-safety/mike-elliott-mayor-brooklyn-center/); [CBS Minnesota, 2015](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/after-tough-year-liberian-community-celebrates-independence-day/)). No Minnesota count of Liberian students was found. The state's English learner report groups students by language, and with 83.6 percent of Liberian Minnesotans speaking only English at home, those counts are a poor guide to how many Liberian children are in school ([Minnesota Department of Education, 2024](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2024/other/240747.pdf); [Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/liberian)).

**Higher education and scholars.** Mike Elliott graduated from Hamline University ([Sahan Journal, 2021](https://sahanjournal.com/public-safety/mike-elliott-mayor-brooklyn-center/)). ## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Survey evidence.** A study of 996 African immigrants in the Twin Cities, 14 percent of them Liberian, found low self-reported rates of diabetes, hypertension and smoking overall. Compared with Somali respondents, Liberians were more likely to report diabetes or hypertension, but Liberians reported more health-protective behaviors on every measure ([BMC Public Health, 2015, via PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25895917/)). This is self-report from one survey. Compass finds 8.1 percent without health insurance ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/liberian)).

**Ebola, 2014.** The epidemic in Liberia hit Minnesota families hard; Pastor Francis Tabla recalled members calling him daily, sobbing, as relatives died ([Luther Seminary, 2015](https://www.luthersem.edu/story/2015/11/16/d-min-alums-minister-to-immigrant-populations-in-the-twin-cities/)). Patrick Sawyer, a former Minnesota resident whose wife and children lived in the Twin Cities suburbs, died in Nigeria in July 2014; Nigerian health officials attributed his death to Ebola. The Minnesota Department of Health met with West African community members on recognizing the disease and traveling safely ([CBS Minnesota/AP, July 29, 2014](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/liberians-in-minnesota-concerned-over-ebola-outbreak/)). Abdullah Kiatamba headed the Minnesota African Task Force Against Ebola and warned that the community could be stigmatized ([MPR News, Oct. 5, 2014](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2014/10/05/ebola-travel)). Pastor Harding Smith said in 2015, "We saw Liberians in record numbers who were discriminated against," at work and at school ([CBS Minnesota, 2015](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/after-tough-year-liberian-community-celebrates-independence-day/)).

**Community health work.** OLM partnered with Hennepin County and the state health department on COVID-19 outreach, testing and vaccination, and belongs to a West African HIV/AIDS task force ([OLM, Community Enrichment Program](https://www.olmbp.org/cep)). Arthur Biah of Brooklyn Park and his wife started the Liberian Health Initiative, holding health fairs to help Liberians here live with diabetes and hypertension ([CCX Media, Dec. 20, 2018](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-man-creates-liberian-health-initiative/)). OLM's senior program includes blood pressure checks and diabetes discussions ([OLM, Seniors Enrichment Program](https://www.olmbp.org/sep)).

## 12. Disability

**What the numbers show, and do not.** Compass counts 3.4 percent of Liberian Minnesotans with a disability, give or take 1.5 points, and 2.9 percent among those under 65 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/liberian)). That is low, but the community is very young, the sample is small, and the Census disability questions may not match how families describe a condition. Whether the low number reflects youth, a healthy-worker pattern, under-reporting, or differences in how questions are understood is an open question. No source settled it.

**Minnesota-specific evidence is thin.** No Minnesota study, Department of Human Services report, or University of Minnesota research on disability among Liberian Minnesotans was found. No culturally specific disability service for Liberian families in Minnesota was found either. Questions that remain open include: Who in a family decides whether to seek a diagnosis? What does a label open, such as waiver services or special education, and what might families fear it closes, such as a child's standing or a parent's immigration case? What part do war injury, untreated childhood illness, workplace injury in care jobs, or aging play in impairments here? Sources do not answer these for Minnesota.

**People and organizations.** Prince and Agnes Cole of Blaine, both disabled, started the Disability Motivational Network in 2016 with $110 of their own money. They have built an accessible orphanage and clinic in Liberia ([CBS Minnesota, July 16, 2024](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/prince-cole-agnes-cole-disability-motivational-network-liberia-acessible-orphanage/)). Prince Cole told WCCO that in Liberia, "society don't count you amongst the population," which is his own experience. The network also runs a disability support group on the last Saturday of each month ([Disability Motivational Network](https://www.disabilitymotivationalnetwork.org/)). Its GiveMN page says it has served more than 5,000 people with disabilities in Liberia and attributes poor care there to "Liberian cultural norms of not valuing people with disabilities"; that is the organization's own view about Liberia, not a finding about families in Minnesota ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Disability-Motivational-Network)).

**Liberians as disability workers.** Many Liberian Minnesotans work in direct care for elderly and disabled Minnesotans (section 3). The Pioneer Press described a Liberian medical assistant who had cared for a disabled resident in Robbinsdale for years; he called her his "Liberian princess" ([Pioneer Press via Inforum, 2018](https://www.inforum.com/news/liberian-immigrants-face-uncertain-fate-as-temporary-status-ends)). Their own use of disability services is a separate question, and no source measured it.

**Status and services.** Families with temporary or uncertain status may weigh any contact with public programs carefully. No Minnesota source was found that measures this for disability services among Liberian families.

## 13. What is changing now

**DED ended June 30, 2026.** President Biden had extended DED for eligible Liberians through June 30, 2026 ([White House memorandum, June 28, 2024](https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/briefing-room/statements-releases/2024/06/28/memorandum-on-extending-eligibility-for-deferred-enforced-departure-for-liberians/)). The Immigration Policy Tracking Project reports that President Trump let it expire on that date, and cites a Liberian Post estimate that about 4,000 Liberians in the U.S. were left vulnerable to deportation ([Immigration Policy Tracking Project](https://immpolicytracking.org/policies/deferred-enforced-departure-for-liberians-allowed-to-expire-on-june-30-2026/)). Most longtime residents had a chance at green cards under the Liberian Refugee Immigration Fairness Act of December 2019, which covered Liberians in the U.S. continuously since Nov. 20, 2014 ([Sahan Journal, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cried-with-joy-minnesotas-liberians-find-elation-relief-in-new-residency-law/)); Congress extended the application deadline into December 2021 ([CCX Media, Dec. 31, 2020](https://ccxmedia.org/news/relief-for-liberians-congress-extends-green-card-citizenship-application-deadline/)). No Minnesota count of people affected by DED's end was found.

**Operation Metro Surge.** Liberian Minnesotans were among those caught up in the weekslong federal immigration crackdown in the Twin Cities that the Department of Homeland Security called Operation Metro Surge, which DHS said had produced more than 2,500 arrests by mid-January 2026. Garrison Gibson, a Liberian man who had lived in Minnesota for about two decades under an order of supervision, was arrested in his north Minneapolis home in January 2026 by agents using a battering ram. U.S. District Judge Jeffrey Bryan ruled the arrest unlawful, and Gibson was detained again at a check-in and released again ([AP via Bangor Daily News, Jan. 16, 2026](https://www.bangordailynews.com/2026/01/16/nation/a-liberian-man-released-after-his-battering-ram-arrest-in-minneapolis-is-back-in-custody-again/); [KSTP, Jan. 2026](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/minnesota-man-detained-and-released-by-ice-2-times-in-a-week-speaks-out/)). The Department of Homeland Security described him as having a criminal record and a final removal order; KSTP reported that Minnesota court records it found showed only a dismissed paraphernalia charge. FrontPage Africa, citing WCCO, reported that Emmanuel Sackie, a Liberian-born U.S. citizen, said agents handcuffed and bruised him in a Brooklyn Park parking lot, in an incident reported in January 2026 ([FrontPage Africa, Jan. 9, 2026](https://frontpageafricaonline.com/liberian-born-u-s-citizen-hospitalized-after-alleged-rough-encounter-with-ice-agents-in-minnesota/)). At the July 2026 festival, Patrick Weah told Sahan Journal that fear and uncertainty from the surge had affected people's ability to go to work and earn money ([Sahan Journal, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minnesota-liberian-independence-day-festival-2026/)).

**Visas and travel.** Some Lone Stars players and staff were denied U.S. visas in July 2026, canceling the planned Independence Day match in St. Paul ([CCX Media, July 16, 2026](https://ccxmedia.org/news/newsmakers-liberia-national-soccer-team-to-play-minnesota-united-july-26/)).

**New institutions.** The community is building permanent places: OLM's $400,000 center expansion (2026), the Masjid Al-Ansar building (2022), Ebenezer's sanctuary, Carol's restaurant (2026), and the women-owned Shingle Creek Center (2023). The nonprofit CAPI reopened its expanded immigrant center in Brooklyn Center in October 2026, in a city CCX Media describes as having a large concentration of Liberians ([CCX Media, Oct. 15, 2026](https://ccxmedia.org/news/new-brooklyn-center-capi-building-expands-opportunities-for-immigrants/)).

**Generations.** Organizers speak openly about bridging generations. Marie Dweh said the parade's goal is to "bridge the gap"; OLM's Kamaty Diahn said the celebrations build "that sense of attachment" to roots ([Sahan Journal, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minnesota-liberian-independence-day-festival-2026/)). Christina Fraser, a first-generation American, told CCX Media she wants to make sure "our culture lives on through me" ([CCX Media, 2025](https://ccxmedia.org/news/liberian-independence-day-celebrated-through-local-business-impact/)).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **Caring for Minnesota.** Thousands of Liberian Minnesotans staff nursing homes, assisted living and home care, work employers and governors have called essential ([Gov. Dayton letter, 2018](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2018/other/181224/governor/assets/2018_03_29_ded_letter.pdf); [MPR News, 2019](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2019/03/11/liberians-minnesota-deportation-deadline)).
- **Persistence in advocacy.** Nearly three decades of rallies, lobbying and lawsuits helped win the 2019 residency law ([Sahan Journal, 2020](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cried-with-joy-minnesotas-liberians-find-elation-relief-in-new-residency-law/)).
- **Institution building with modest means.** Congregations bought land and buildings through member giving; the masjid's members saved for years while living paycheck to paycheck; women business owners pooled savings to buy a mall (sections 3, 4 and 5).
- **Mutual aid.** Volunteers ran a COVID-19 relief drive with a Brooklyn Center church, and OLM distributes food to about 250 families a month ([CCX Media, 2020](https://ccxmedia.org/news/liberians-organize-to-help-community-during-covid-19-outbreak/); [OLM, Community Enrichment Program](https://www.olmbp.org/cep)).
- **Public leadership.** The first Liberian American mayor and council member in their cities, a state-level Governor's award, and a sister-city bond that links Brooklyn Park to Kakata (section 9).
- **Culture and sport.** Olympic sprinters raised in Minnesota, a growing Independence Day festival, and a long soccer tradition that, in Cletus Constance's words, brings everyone together (section 8).
- **Global ties.** Minnesota Liberians send money, equipment and expertise home, from disability services in Monrovia to fire trucks and police uniforms for Liberian towns ([OLM, About](https://www.olmbp.org/about); [CCX Media, 2019](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-police-to-donate-old-uniforms-to-liberian-officers/)).

## What could not be established

- **An exact population.** Estimates range from about 13,600 (ancestry) to 40,000 (community estimate). No source reconciles them, and none counts U.S.-born children who identify as Liberian but record another race or ancestry.
- **Greater Minnesota.** Compass says 13.2 percent live outside the Twin Cities, but no source described a Liberian community in a specific Greater Minnesota town.
- **Ethnic and language groups within the community.** No Minnesota count by Liberian ethnic group (Kpelle, Bassa, Kru, Grebo, Gio, Mano, Krahn, Vai, Mandingo and others) or by home language was found. No Minnesota chapter of a Liberian county association was confirmed, although OLM names county and tribal associations as partners.
- **Professional firms and many business types.** No Liberian-owned law, accounting, insurance, tax or real estate firm in Minnesota was confirmed. Home care agencies, salons and markets were hard to tie to Liberian ownership with an opened, current page.
- **Religion beyond a few congregations.** The figure of 30 or more Liberian churches could not be confirmed. Catholic and other Liberian ministries, traditional practice and people of no faith were not documented.
- **Family customs.** No Minnesota source describing marriage, naming, dress or funeral customs among Liberian families here in general terms was found.
- **Disability, mental health and special education.** No Minnesota-specific study or data on Liberian residents was found.
- **The effect of DED's 2026 end in Minnesota.** No Minnesota count of affected people or report on what happened to them after June 30, 2026, was found.
- **Blocked sources.** Star Tribune, Mshale, the State of Minnesota's Help Me Connect and MinnesotaHelp directories, the Legislative Coordinating Commission's Legacy project pages, Wilder Research's Speaking for Ourselves reports, the Minnesota Secretary of State's election results, USCIS and the full text of A House with Two Rooms could not be opened. Facts available only there are not used here.
