# Corrections to the existing Liberian Minnesota page

Page: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/liberian

## Errors or claims that should change

1. **Claim:** Minnesota Compass (ACS 2019–2023) counts about 20,720 Liberian Minnesotans, 90.5 percent in the Twin Cities and 9.5 percent in Greater Minnesota; 43.7 percent 17 or younger, 4.4 percent 65 or older, 63.2 percent foreign-born; 80.3 percent English only, 19.7 percent another language, 8.7 percent English less than "very well"; 90.2 percent high school or higher, 29.5 percent bachelor's or higher; median household income about $59,903; poverty 20.9 percent; 31.2 percent owner-occupied and 68.8 percent renter; household size 3.49; disability 3.9 percent.
   **Source says:** Compass now shows 2020–2024 data. Population used in analysis 22,571 (give or take 4,423), with a 2024 single-year total of 23,608 (give or take 10,872) that Compass calls its most accurate estimate of size. Twin Cities 86.8 percent, Greater Minnesota 13.2 percent; 46.0 percent 17 or younger; 4.1 percent 65 or older; 59.9 percent foreign-born; 83.6 percent English only, 16.4 percent another language, 7.1 percent English less than "very well"; 92.0 percent high school or higher, 28.8 percent bachelor's or higher; median household income $67,197 (2024 dollars); poverty 20.4 percent; 36.4 percent owner-occupied, 63.6 percent renter; household size 3.54; disability 3.4 percent. Every figure on the page is one vintage out of date, and several have wide margins of error that the page does not give.
   **Link:** https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/liberian .
2. **Claim:** Wynfred Russell's "campaign site listed him on the August 11, 2026 primary ballot for Minnesota House District 38A."
   **Source says:** The primary has happened. The incumbent state representative won the DFL primary with 48.8 percent (1,822 votes); Russell received 41.8 percent (1,561); Richard Klatte 9.4 percent. Russell had also lost the 2024 primary by 50 votes.
   **Links:** https://ccxmedia.org/news/state-primaries-janigo-pha-win-senate-challenges-momanyi-hiltsley-advances-in-house-race/ ; https://ccxmedia.org/news/hiltsley-edges-russell-in-house-38a-primary-to-face-olson/ .
3. **Claim:** The disability and immigration sections describe DED as a current, ongoing protection ("Many Liberians in Minnesota have lived for years under temporary legal protections"; "DED, TPS, and the decades of temporary status are current structure for some").
   **Source says:** DED for Liberians was extended only through June 30, 2026, and the Immigration Policy Tracking Project reports it was allowed to expire on that date. The page should say DED has ended and that LRIF (Dec. 20, 2019) was the route to green cards for most long-term residents.
   **Links:** https://bidenwhitehouse.archives.gov/briefing-room/statements-releases/2024/06/28/memorandum-on-extending-eligibility-for-deferred-enforced-departure-for-liberians/ ; https://immpolicytracking.org/policies/deferred-enforced-departure-for-liberians-allowed-to-expire-on-june-30-2026/ .
4. **Claim:** Ebenezer Community Church "began as a Bible study of four people in September 2000" and "dedicated a 34,000-square-foot building in 2021."
   **Source says:** The church's own site says Pastors Francis and Christine Tabla founded it "with just 8 members," and Tabla told Luther Seminary in 2015 they "started with about eight people." FrontPage Africa reports a $2.8 million building dedicated after a June 7, 2015, groundbreaking, and Tabla dated the land purchase to January 2006, "about twelve years ago," which points to a dedication around 2018, not 2021. The 34,000-square-foot figure and 2021 date were not found in any opened source.
   **Links:** https://www.ebenezercommunitychurch.org/ ; https://www.luthersem.edu/story/2015/11/16/d-min-alums-minister-to-immigrant-populations-in-the-twin-cities/ ; https://frontpageafricaonline.com/liberian-immigrants-promoting-gospel-in-minnesota/ .
5. **Claim:** Brooklyn Park and Brooklyn Center are a concentration only "in local knowledge," to be confirmed on each family.
   **Source says:** Census data confirm it. ACS 2020–2024 counts 4,544 Liberia-born residents of Brooklyn Park (give or take 1,153) and 1,147 in Brooklyn Center, out of 15,297 statewide; Hennepin County holds 9,672. Brooklyn Park city officials put the city's Liberian population above 7,000.
   **Links:** https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US27,16000US2707966,16000US2707948,16000US2743000,16000US2758000,05000US27053,05000US27123,05000US27003,01000US,16000US2713456,16000US2739878,16000US2747680,16000US2754880 ; https://ccxmedia.org/news/minnesota-united-to-host-june-22-youth-soccer-clinic-in-brooklyn-park/ .
6. **Claim:** Partners include "the Liberian Community of Minnesota / Liberian Community Center historically."
   **Source says:** No organization named "Liberian Community of Minnesota" was found. The organization is the Organization of Liberians in Minnesota (OLM), founded 1973 and incorporated 1979, which operates the "Liberian Community/OLM Center" in Brooklyn Park now, not only historically. It received $400,000 in the 2026 bonding bill to expand it.
   **Links:** https://www.olmbp.org/about ; https://ccxmedia.org/news/olm-receives-400k-to-expand-brooklyn-park-facility/ .
7. **Claim:** The 2026 Liberian Independence Day parade was "again on July 26" (We Are Liberian section).
   **Source says:** Correct, but the Sahan Journal article itself is inconsistent: its text mentions "a Saturday morning parade," while its listing gives Sunday, July 26 (July 26, 2026, was a Sunday). The listing date fits the calendar. Separately, the planned Minnesota United vs. Liberia match at Allianz Field on July 26, 2026, which OLM advertised, was canceled over visa denials; the page does not mention it.
   **Links:** https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minnesota-liberian-independence-day-festival-2026/ ; https://ccxmedia.org/news/newsmakers-liberia-national-soccer-team-to-play-minnesota-united-july-26/ .
8. **Claim:** Liberian Sporting Association "Partners with OLM on an annual soccer match; the 2026 match was on July 25."
   **Source says:** The date is right. Venue sources differ: Sahan Journal lists Robbinsdale Armstrong High School at 5 p.m.; OLM's event page lists Northwoods Park Field, 9 a.m. to 8 p.m.
   **Links:** https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minnesota-liberian-independence-day-festival-2026/ ; https://www.olmbp.org/event-details/youth-festival-sports .
9. **Claim:** Watch Night is listed among "ordinary" Liberian church calendar dates.
   **Source says:** No opened source documents Liberian Minnesotans keeping Watch Night; the shared calendar's Watch Night entry is defined by the 1862 Emancipation vigil, which is African American history. Suggest dropping it for this community unless a source is found.
   **Link:** none found.
## Claims that could not be supported

- "Compass African American includes some Liberian ancestry that other counts exclude." The Compass Liberian profile notes the 2024 change in how ancestry is collected for Black respondents, but no opened source documents how the Compass African American profile treats Liberian ancestry. https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/liberian .
- "The Star Tribune reported more than 30 Liberian churches in the Twin Cities in 2019." The Star Tribune could not be opened, and no other source repeated the figure.
- Independence Day brings "closed shops in some pockets." No source found.
- Patricia Jabbeh Wesley as an expert witness at the 2008 St. Paul hearings: The Advocates' TRC Diaspora Project page, opened, confirms the June 2008 Hamline hearings, more than 20 witnesses and more than 1,600 statements, but does not name her. https://www.theadvocatesforhumanrights.org/Liberia_TRC_Diaspora_Project .
- CECLC, hepatitis B screening guidance, Kolokwa usage, Mshale's founding date, and Matadi's 2024 Paris time were not checked.

## Claims checked and supported

- Mike Elliott: came from Liberia at 11, first Black and first Liberian American mayor of Brooklyn Center, 55 percent in 2018 after losing in 2014, Brooklyn Center High School and Hamline (Sahan Journal, https://sahanjournal.com/public-safety/mike-elliott-mayor-brooklyn-center/).
- Wynfred Russell: refugee camp, scholarship, University of Minnesota job, sworn in January 2019 as Brooklyn Park's first Liberian American council member (FOX 9; CCX Media).
- Abdullah Kiatamba: Minnesota African Task Force Against Ebola (MPR, Oct. 5, 2014); 200 to 500 Minnesotans losing Ebola TPS, "heartbreaking and troubling" (MPR, May 15, 2017); African Immigrant Services executive director in 2018 (MPR, March 28, 2018).
- OLM: founded 1973, incorporated 1979, nine-member board elected every two years, partners including the Liberia Ministers Association and Masjid Al-Ansar; 2021 training of 60 residents; $400,000 in 2026 (OLM About page; CCX Media 2021 and 2026).
- We Are Liberian: first parade July 26, 2025, Shingle Creek Parkway to Centennial Park, about 70 vendors; Marie Dweh co-founder; 2026 at North Hennepin Community College (CCX Media 2025; Sahan Journal 2026).
- Disability Motivational Network: founded 2016 by Prince and Agnes Cole of Blaine; support group on the last Saturday of each month (WCCO 2024; own site; GiveMN).
- Emmanuel Matadi: born 1991, fled 1999, California then St. Paul, Johnson High state champion, Rio flag bearer (MPR, Aug. 12, 2016).
- Liberian Refugee Immigration Fairness Act signed Dec. 20, 2019, for those continuously present since Nov. 20, 2014, plus spouses and children under 21 (Sahan Journal, Jan. 5, 2020).
- Population counts cited by others (Sahan Journal more than 20,000 via Compass in 2026; CCX Media 35,000 in 2026; Sahan Journal about 30,000 statewide and 7,000 in Brooklyn Park in 2020) are quoted accurately.
