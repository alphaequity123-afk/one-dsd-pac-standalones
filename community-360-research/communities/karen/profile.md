# Karen in Minnesota
Checked: October 30, 2026

## 1. People and place

**How many.** No official count of Karen Minnesotans exists. Census data folds Karen people into "Burmese," and a researcher at APM Research Lab told Sahan Journal that those numbers are likely conservative ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 13, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/changing-minnesota/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/)). The closest public number is Minnesota Compass's Burmese profile: 19,372 people, give or take 3,438, for 2020 to 2024. That profile covers anyone counted as Burmese, so it does not describe Karen people alone, and its wide range matters ([Minnesota Compass, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)).

Community and agency estimates sit in the same range. The Karen Organization of Minnesota, often called KOM, estimates over 20,000 Karen people, plus 2,000 to 5,000 Karenni and about 1,000 Mon, and says plainly that these are estimates ([KOM press kit, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/about/press-kit/); [KOM, Ethnic Groups, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/ethnic-groups/)). The Minnesota Department of Health's toolkit also says over 20,000 ([MDH, Aug. 20, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)). Sahan Journal reports about 22,000 Minnesotans from Myanmar, most of them Karen ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 18, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/tps-cut-myanmar-minnesota-karen-community-trump-immigration/)). Older figures show the growth, though each was an estimate: nearly 3,000 in 2010 ([MPR News, Jan. 11, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/01/11/karen-myanmar-burma-new-year)), about 6,500 in 2016 ([WellShare International, April 5, 2016](https://wellshareinternational.org/who-are-the-karen/)), and more than 17,000 in 2021 ([MPR News via InForum, Feb. 4, 2021](https://www.inforum.com/news/after-myanmar-coup-karen-refugees-in-minnesota-fear-for-homeland)).

**Young, and many born abroad.** In the Burmese profile, 41.1 percent are 17 or younger and 3.4 percent are 65 or older. About two in three, 66.9 percent, were born abroad ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)).

**Where.** Compass places 83.0 percent of its Burmese group in the Twin Cities and 17.0 percent in Greater Minnesota, give or take 4.9 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)). The state health toolkit says Karen people are concentrated in Ramsey County, with smaller communities in Freeborn, Lyon, Nobles, Kandiyohi and Mower counties ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

- **St. Paul's North End and East Side, and the suburbs next door.** KOM set up its first office on Jackson Street in the North End in 2009 ([KOM history, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/about/history-of-kom/)). Karen shops line Rice Street and Arcade Street (section 4). The weaving circle meets at the East Side Freedom Library, and The Urban Village began in a storefront on Arcade Street ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 24, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/karen-traditional-weaving-circles-minnesota-youth/); [MindSite News, Oct. 22, 2026](https://mindsitenews.org/2026/10/22/minnesotas-karen-teens-navigate-two-cultures-and-opioid-crisis/)). Maplewood holds several Karen Baptist churches (section 5).
- **Roseville.** KOM moved to its office off Rice Street in Roseville in March 2015, and Calvary Karen Baptist Church is on Larpenteur Avenue there ([KOM history](https://mnkaren.org/about/history-of-kom/); [KOM flu clinic listing, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/free-flu-shot-clinics-2/2026-12-03/)). An early New Year drew more than a thousand people to Roseville Lutheran Church in January 2010 ([MPR News, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/01/11/karen-myanmar-burma-new-year)). I could not find a count of Karen residents or students for Roseville itself.
- **Worthington.** An estimated 300 to 400 Karen people lived in the area in 2010, most working at JBS ([The Globe, Nov. 3, 2010](https://www.dglobe.com/news/people-of-god-karen-find-spiritual-home-at-indian-lake-baptist)). In 2024, AP reported about 60 refugee families at the Karen Sunday service at Indian Lake Baptist Church, and Karen as the third most spoken of 44 languages at the intermediate school ([AP via KSAT, Oct. 31, 2024](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2024/10/31/takeaways-from-aps-report-on-how-immigration-transformed-a-minnesota-farm-town/)).
- **Austin.** The city's Welcome Center counted 1,224 Karen and Karenni residents in 2014, up from 463 in 2012. That is the most recent city count I could find ([MPR News via Grand Forks Herald, Sept. 15, 2014](https://www.grandforksherald.com/newsmd/growing-karen-population-brings-new-changes-to-austin)).
- **Albert Lea.** The Karen community grew from about four families in 2012 to about 40 by 2019. Community leaders then put the Karen population of Albert Lea, Austin and the surrounding area at about 1,500 ([Sahan Journal, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/changing-minnesota/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/)).
- **Also named.** Marshall and Willmar appear in the same 2019 report. KOM ran a satellite office in Marshall from 2014 to 2019 ([KOM history](https://mnkaren.org/about/history-of-kom/)), though a state workforce page still lists a Marshall address ([CareerForce, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://careerforce.mn.gov/so/group/191/location/203)). WellShare also named Faribault in 2016 ([WellShare](https://wellshareinternational.org/who-are-the-karen/)).

**How people came.** The Karen are a people of Burma, also called Myanmar, and of Thailand, with no country of their own. The ancestral land is called Kawthoolei ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)). As background, a civil war dating to the 1940s drove many into camps along the Thai border. A Minnesota House document says those camps held 93,269 people in 2015, 84 percent of them Karen ([Minnesota House document, 2020 to 2021](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/nLMA_6GxeEezIM-kPX0ExQ.pdf)). The state toolkit says families averaged 8 to 12 years in camps, that first arrivals came in the early 1990s, and that larger numbers came in the early 2000s ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)). KOM's own page dates the first refugees to 2000 ([KOM, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/resources/karen-owned-businesses/)). About 500 Karen people a year were reaching Minnesota around 2010, and half the state's new refugees that fall were from Myanmar ([MPR News, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/01/11/karen-myanmar-burma-new-year)). Karen people made up about half of Minnesota's first-arrival refugees in 2008 and more than 30 percent in 2014 ([WellShare](https://wellshareinternational.org/who-are-the-karen/)).

Many then moved again. Twin Cities employers were reluctant to hire people with limited English and no U.S. work history around the Great Recession, and plants in Greater Minnesota were more willing. Families also cited cheaper rent and a quieter life ([Sahan Journal, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/changing-minnesota/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/)). Others arrive from other states for family and work ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

**Generations and languages.** S'gaw Karen is the writing system most people understand. West Pwo, East Pwo, Bwe and other forms also exist, and the toolkit advises asking for S'gaw when translating. It estimates that Karen literacy among Minnesota's Karen population could range from 30 to 40 percent, adds that this is hard to measure, and says most children raised here likely cannot read or write Karen ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)). A Minnesota House document says 70 percent of Karen refugees speak S'gaw and 7 percent speak Pwo, citing an out-of-state profile ([Minnesota House document](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/nLMA_6GxeEezIM-kPX0ExQ.pdf)). In the Burmese profile, 89.1 percent of people 5 and older speak a language other than English at home, and 68.1 percent speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)). KOM staff work in English, Sgaw Karen, Pwo Karen, Karenni, Burmese, Thai and several other languages ([KOM press kit](https://mnkaren.org/about/press-kit/)).

**Names used.** Some community groups write Knyaw or K'nyaw for themselves, and KOM says about 20,000 people here are referred to as "Karen" or "Knyaw" ([KOM statement, Nov. 26, 2025](https://mnkaren.org/news/kom-statement-on-announcement-of-tps-termination-for-burma/)). Karenni and Mon people are separate communities who often share these agencies. Karenni leaders estimate 4,000 to 5,000 people, mostly in the Twin Cities and Austin ([Sahan Journal, March 6, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/maplewood-minnesota-first-karenni-grocery-store/)).

## 2. All walks of life

No single picture fits. The numbers below are from Compass's 2020 to 2024 Burmese profile, in 2024 dollars, so they cover more than Karen households and carry wide ranges ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)).

- **Comfortable households.** Median household income is $85,747, give or take $15,049, and 37.2 percent of households take in $100,000 or more, give or take 10.7 points. Households are large, averaging 5.05 people, so these totals often rest on several earners. I found no Minnesota figure for Karen wealth, and no public reporting on wealthy Karen families, so I name none.
- **Professionals and owners.** Sgt. Ler Htoo of the St. Paul Police Department is the first known Karen police officer in the country to reach that rank, and he counted five other Karen officers in the department in 2024 ([MPR News, Feb. 14, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/02/14/first-known-us-karen-police-officer-promoted-to-sergeant)). Chef Chris Tunbaw owns Mandalay Kitchen in Frogtown ([CBS Minnesota, Nov. 26, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-karen-population-tps-ending-myanmar)). Ehtalow Zar, a St. Paul teacher, is one of the first two people licensed to teach Karen in Minnesota ([Sahan Journal, Jan. 6, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/education/minnesota-teachers-heritage-language-licenses-hmong-karen-somali/)). Therapists June Nin Way at Wilder and Eh Ta Lar at Humboldt High work in the community ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 23, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-karen-community-burma-tps-ending-immigration-ice/)). KOM says over two dozen Karen-owned businesses have been established, among them groceries, restaurants and auto shops ([KOM](https://mnkaren.org/resources/karen-owned-businesses/)). Among adults 25 and older, 7.9 percent hold a bachelor's degree, give or take 3.6 points; the graduate figure is too small for Compass to show.
- **Working and middle-income families.** Owner-occupied homes are 67.9 percent of households, give or take 9.5 points. In Greater Minnesota, families moved from shared apartments to renting and to buying mobile homes ([Sahan Journal, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/changing-minnesota/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/)), and some bought houses in Worthington by 2010 ([The Globe, 2010](https://www.dglobe.com/news/people-of-god-karen-find-spiritual-home-at-indian-lake-baptist)). Employment among adults 18 to 64 is 69.3 percent, 75.2 percent for men and 63.7 percent for women. Daily work includes plant jobs and trades such as tree trimming ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 18, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/tps-cut-myanmar-minnesota-karen-community-trump-immigration/)).
- **People in poverty.** Compass puts 22.2 percent below the poverty line, give or take 7.6 points, including 25.8 percent of children, give or take 9.8 points. Another 23 percent or so live between the poverty line and twice that line. Among renters, 41.7 percent spend too much on housing, with a wide range of 16.4 points. And 67.0 percent of adults 25 and older did not finish high school, give or take 7.6 points. Fathers often work jobs without parental leave, and the toolkit names this as a cause of strain on new mothers ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

## 3. Work and the economy

**Before Minnesota.** KOM describes most Karen people in Burma as subsistence farmers, growing rice and vegetables and raising animals, with life set by planting and harvest ([KOM, Economy, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/economy/)). The state toolkit adds hunting and fishing ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

**Plants and factories.** Named employers in reporting include JBS in Worthington, Select Foods in Albert Lea, Quality Pork Processors and Hormel in Austin, and Viracon, an architectural glass maker in Owatonna popular with workers from Austin and Albert Lea. KOM employment counselors helped connect workers to these jobs from about 2010 ([Sahan Journal, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/changing-minnesota/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/)). Worthington's Karen pastor moved there around 2010 to join a sister working at JBS ([The Globe, 2010](https://www.dglobe.com/news/people-of-god-karen-find-spiritual-home-at-indian-lake-baptist)). JBS Worthington sponsored the 2022 Karen New Year in St. Paul ([KOM, 2022](https://mnkaren.org/news/celebrating-2761-karen-new-year-in-st-paul/)).

**Service work and careers.** KOM trains people for interpreting and translating, construction and manufacturing, along with commercial driving, nursing assistant work and financial coaching ([KOM Employment Services, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/programs/employment-services/)). Karen-language home care is also a business, with at least one agency in St. Paul serving Ramsey, Washington, Mower, Freeborn and Lyon counties ([Karen Home Health Care, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.karenhomehealthkare.com/home-health-care-about-us)). State government has also hired for the community: the Department of Natural Resources posted its first full-time Karen outreach job in 2024 ([Sahan Journal, March 8, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/climate-environment/minnesota-dnr-job-postings-karen-hmong-community-outreach-coordinator/)).

**Unions.** Albert Lea's labor history runs through the United Food and Commercial Workers ([The American Prospect, March 30, 2026](https://prospect.org/2026/03/30/apr-2026-magazine-when-ice-blows-through-rural-america-minnesota/)). I could not find reporting on Karen members or stewards.

**Enforcement and work.** In 2026 Albert Lea residents described fear and arrests touching the Select Foods workforce, though that report does not separate Karen workers ([The American Prospect, 2026](https://prospect.org/2026/03/30/apr-2026-magazine-when-ice-blows-through-rural-america-minnesota/)). See section 13.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

Rice Street in St. Paul and Roseville is the clearest Karen commercial corridor, with Arcade Street on the East Side and University Avenue in Frogtown close behind. Heavy Table's 2023 food guide walks through several places, and describes Thai dishes alongside Burmese curries, mohinga and tea leaf salad ([Heavy Table, March 6, 2023](https://heavytable.com/from-myanmar-to-rice-street-karen-food-in-saint-paul/)). KOM keeps its list of Karen-owned businesses by email request rather than posting it ([KOM](https://mnkaren.org/resources/karen-owned-businesses/)).

**Confirmed operating as of this check:**
- **Mandalay Kitchen**, 383 University Ave. W., Frogtown, serving Knyaw, Burmese and Thai food. Its own site shows current hours and a notice about a limited menu ([Mandalay Kitchen, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mandalaykitchenstp.com/)). Chef Tunbaw opened it in November 2023 ([Racket, Jan. 12, 2024](https://racketmn.com/mandalay-kitchen-frogtown-st-paul-menu-burmese-thai-cuisine)).
- **Knyaw and Thai Kitchen**, 1048 Rice St., St. Paul, with online ordering and site images dated fall 2026 ([Knyaw and Thai Kitchen, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://knyawandthai.com/)).
- **Karen Home Health Care**, 1299 Arcade St., St. Paul, with posted office hours and a Karen-speaking registered nurse ([Karen Home Health Care](https://www.karenhomehealthkare.com/home-health-care-about-us)).
- **Karenni Grocery**, Maplewood, a neighboring community's store that opened in 2025, the first of its kind in the Twin Cities by its owners' account ([Sahan Journal, March 6, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/maplewood-minnesota-first-karenni-grocery-store/)).

**Named in recent years, but I could not confirm they are open today:** Karen Thai on Larpenteur Avenue, Kyaw Karen Market at 945 Rice St., Nam Mu Asian Market at 1377 Arcade St. (formerly Original Karen Market), Friends Cafe at 1711 Rice St., Asian Fusion at 1700 Rice St., Karen Village in Maplewood, Karen Grocery on Pennsylvania Avenue, Karen and Nepali Foods on Larpenteur Avenue, K'Nyaw Grocery Store and Karenni Thai Food in Austin, and Loikaw21 in Austin. Details and links are in the companion business list. In Austin, a legal assistant and her husband were opening a grocery in 2019, and Willmar's boba shop had a Karen owner in 2026, though neither report names the shop ([Sahan Journal, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/changing-minnesota/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/); [The American Prospect, 2026](https://prospect.org/2026/03/30/apr-2026-magazine-when-ice-blows-through-rural-america-minnesota/)).

**Makers.** KOM's weaving circle sells bags, shirts, sarongs, table runners and scarves at the Swede Hollow Art Fair, the Saint Paul Art Crawl and the Little Mekong Night Market ([KOM Weaving Circle, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/programs/social-services/weaving-circle/)).

I did not find a Karen chamber of commerce, or named Karen law, accounting or insurance firms. In Worthington and Albert Lea, I found no named Karen shop on a page I could open.

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

**More than one faith.** The state toolkit, written by a Karen American, says Karen Minnesotans practice Christianity, Buddhism, and animism or traditional spiritual practice, and that people often confide in pastors and monks ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)). KOM names five beliefs among Karen people generally: animism, Buddhism, Christianity, Lehkai and Telahkon. It adds that there is much exchange between Buddhist and animist practice, and that villages at home have often been mainly one faith ([KOM, Religion, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/religion/)). A House document also lists Islam ([Minnesota House document](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/nLMA_6GxeEezIM-kPX0ExQ.pdf)).

**How the shares are told.** No Minnesota count by faith exists. MPR said in 2010 that about half of those who fled Myanmar are Baptist ([MPR News, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/01/11/karen-myanmar-burma-new-year)). Culture Care Connection, a health project, says almost all Karen in the U.S. are Christian ([Culture Care Connection, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://culturecareconnection.org/cultural-responsiveness/karen/)). KOM says only that many refugees in the U.S. are Christian. These do not agree, and Buddhist events and groups are active here.

**History, as background.** Baptist missionaries reached Burma in 1813, and a pastor told MPR that an old Karen story of a lost golden book helped people receive the Bible ([MPR News, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/01/11/karen-myanmar-burma-new-year)). KOM also records armed conflict between Christian and Buddhist Karen groups in Burma, after a Buddhist faction broke away and aligned with the military ([KOM, Religion](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/religion/)). I found no Minnesota reporting on how that history plays out here.

**Christian congregations, named.**
- Ebenezer Karen Baptist Church, 1056 Gervais, Maplewood, host of the 2026 Martyrs' Day and a 2026 flu clinic ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/76th-commemoration-of-karen-martyrs-day/)).
- First Karen Baptist Church, 3000 Hadley Ave. N., Oakdale, and Calvary Karen Baptist Church, 965 Larpenteur Ave. W., Roseville, both 2026 flu clinic hosts ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/free-flu-shot-clinics-2/2026-12-03/)).
- Hosanna Karen Baptist Church, 1818 E. Gervais Ct., Maplewood ([KOM venue listing, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/venue/hosanna-karen-baptist-church/)).
- Trinity Karen Baptist Church, 2220 Edgerton St., Maplewood ([Converge North Central, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://northcentral.converge.org/church/trinity-karen-baptist-church/)).
- First Baptist Church in downtown St. Paul, whose pastor said in 2010 that about half the congregation was Karen ([MPR News, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/01/11/karen-myanmar-burma-new-year)). I could not confirm today's share.
- Zion Karen Baptist Church, Albert Lea, formerly First Baptist, bought by the community in 2018 after worshiping in a mobile building ([Sahan Journal, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/changing-minnesota/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/)).
- Indian Lake Baptist Church, Worthington, founded by Swedish immigrants, where Karen families worship in their own language. Others in 2010 attended First Baptist in Worthington or a Lao church in Bigelow ([The Globe, 2010](https://www.dglobe.com/news/people-of-god-karen-find-spiritual-home-at-indian-lake-baptist); [AP via KSAT, 2024](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2024/10/31/takeaways-from-aps-report-on-how-immigration-transformed-a-minnesota-farm-town/)).
- The Karen Theological Study Center and St. Paul's Karen Baptist Church, both named in reporting on Karen language teaching ([Sahan Journal, Feb. 3, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/education/karen-language-classes-st-paul-public-schools-refugees-minnesota/)).

**Buddhist life.** The Karen Buddhist Association of Minnesota is a co-organizer of the 2027 New Year ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/karen-new-year-celebration-2766/)). A rural site at 43695 Peaceful Valley Road in Harris hosted a Novitiate Ceremony in July 2025 and the Wrist Tying Ceremony in August 2026 ([Karen Culture Organization of Minnesota, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://karenculture.org/events); [KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/karen-traditional-wrist-tying-ceremony-2026/2026-08-22/)). I could not open a page describing who runs that site or whether monks live there. KOM says monks and elders both take part in wrist-tying ([KOM, Celebrations, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/celebrations/)).

**Animist practice and spirit belief.** The toolkit says spirit belief came before Christianity and Buddhism, that gifts to nature spirits continue in rural villages at home, and that wrist-tying in Minnesota is directly tied to those beliefs ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)). KOM names guardian spirits called Ther Myng Khae ([KOM, Religion](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/religion/)). I found no named Minnesota group that identifies itself as animist.

**Differences and silence.** KOM mentions Karen Muslims, and says Karenni people are often Catholic ([KOM, Religion](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/religion/); [KOM, Ethnic Groups](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/ethnic-groups/)). I found no Minnesota congregation of Karen Muslims, Adventists, Catholics or Anglicans, and no data on people of no faith. Flu clinics and other gatherings are often held at churches, and a church list leaves out Buddhist, animist and nonreligious households.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

- **Karen New Year.** Falls on the first day of the Karen month KOM spells Pyathoe, generally late December or early January. It marks the end of one rice harvest and the start of the next, and it is the largest celebration ([KOM, Celebrations](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/celebrations/)). St. Paul's gatherings fall on nearby dates, so the day moves. A two-day gathering ended Dec. 31, 2023 at Washington Technology Magnet School ([CBS Minnesota, Dec. 31, 2023](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/karen-community-celebrates-new-year-in-st-paul/)). Year 2765 was held Jan. 1, 2026 at the same school, with a free noon meal, games and prizes ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/karen-new-year-2765/)). **Year 2766 is announced for Jan. 2 to 3, 2027 in St. Paul, venue still to come**, organized by the Karen Culture Organization of Minnesota, Karen Community of Minnesota, KOM, The Urban Village, the Karen Buddhist Association of Minnesota and KNYAW Visual Arts ([KOM, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/karen-new-year-celebration-2766/)). The 2022 program opened with salutes to the Karen and U.S. flags, then speeches, a singing contest and a culture show ([KOM, 2022](https://mnkaren.org/news/celebrating-2761-karen-new-year-in-st-paul/)). In 2010 there were don and courtship dances, and more than a thousand people came ([MPR News, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/01/11/karen-myanmar-burma-new-year)). I found no New Year listing for Worthington, Austin or Albert Lea, so I cannot say whether they hold their own.
- **Karen Martyrs' Day.** August 12, the anniversary of the 1950 death of Saw Ba U Gyi, an early Karen National Union leader; others who resisted are honored too ([KOM, Celebrations](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/celebrations/)). Minnesota usually gathers on a nearby weekend. In 2025 the Karen Community of Minnesota hosted it Aug. 9 and 10 at Todd Park in Austin with volleyball, soccer and caneball ([KOM, 2025](https://mnkaren.org/event/75th-commemoration-of-karen-martyrs-day/)). In 2026 it was Aug. 15 at Ebenezer Karen Baptist Church in Maplewood, with a ceremony, a shared lunch and songs ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/76th-commemoration-of-karen-martyrs-day/)). August was chosen partly because outdoor fields were open, and the 2020 gathering moved online because of the pandemic ([KOM, about 2020](https://mnkaren.org/news/looking-back-on-karen-celebrations-in-minnesota/)).
- **Wrist-Tying Ceremony.** Held in August and sometimes at weddings. Elders and monks place items in a person's hand and tie a white or yellow string so the spirit stays and the person can live free from fear ([KOM, Celebrations](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/celebrations/)). A contributor to Drum Publications ties it to the August full moon and describes seven kinds of offerings ([Drum Publications, undated, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://drumpublications.org/wrist.php)). In 2026 the Karen Culture Organization of Minnesota held it Aug. 22 and 23 at Harris, with the main ceremony Sunday morning, soccer, volleyball and caneball, a pageant and food ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/karen-traditional-wrist-tying-ceremony-2026/2026-08-22/)). Sources disagree on its nature: Drum and a House document say it is not religious; the state toolkit ties it to spirit belief. The name "Karen National Wrist-tying Day" did not appear on any page I opened, in Minnesota or abroad, where it is simply called a ceremony or celebration ([Karen Information Center via BNI, Aug. 2013](https://www.bnionline.net/en/kic/item/16048-hope-hpa-an-wrist-tying-ceremony-unites-karen.html)).
- **Karen Revolution Day (Jan. 31) and Karen National Day (Feb. 11).** KOM lists these for the 1949 uprising and a 1948 demonstration for a homeland ([KOM, Celebrations](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/celebrations/)). I did not find a Minnesota event for either.
- **Christmas, Easter and "Sweet December."** The toolkit says people mark Christmas, Easter or Buddhist New Year according to their faith ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)). KOM describes Sweet December, Nov. 30, as the start of Christmas for Christians, with church games, singing and a midnight rice soup called may klaw ([KOM, Celebrations](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/celebrations/)). Worthington's Karen members asked to go caroling in 2009 ([The Globe, 2010](https://www.dglobe.com/news/people-of-god-karen-find-spiritual-home-at-indian-lake-baptist)). I did not find a dated Minnesota event for these this year.
- **Buddhist New Year or Water Festival.** Mid-April, with water sprinkled on elders and offerings to spirits, according to KOM ([KOM, Celebrations](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/celebrations/)). I found no Minnesota date.
- **Novitiate Ceremony.** Held July 26 and 27, 2025, in Harris ([Karen Culture Organization of Minnesota](https://karenculture.org/events)). I could not find a 2026 date.
- **A neighbor's festival.** The Karenni DeeKu Festival, held Sept. 19 and 20, 2026 in St. Paul, belongs to the Karenni community and is noted here because families and agencies overlap ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/karenni-deeku-festival-2026/)).

## 7. Family, home and daily life

All of what follows comes from the sources named, and the toolkit itself warns it describes general patterns, not any one family.

**Households.** The toolkit says Karen families often have five to seven children; KOM says four or five ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf); [KOM, Family, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/family/)). Several generations often share a roof, and grandparents help raise children. Older siblings, especially the oldest sisters, help with little ones. A large share of mothers stay home, and fathers often work full time, according to the toolkit.

**Names and marriage.** Karen tradition has no family surname system, so spouses and full siblings may have different last names ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)). Introductions often use Saw for men and Naw for women. KOM describes a matriarchal tradition in which a husband joins his wife's clan and household ([KOM, Family](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/family/)). A Minnesota family's wedding clothes show married status through the Chay Moe Thu and sarong ([TPT Originals, May 25, 2021](https://www.tptoriginals.org/worn-within-why-is-weaving-essential-to-a-karen-wedding/)). Dress also signals life stage, as Smithsonian curators describe: white for unmarried women and girls, black and red for married women, red sarongs for men ([Smithsonian Folklife Festival, Nov. 14, 2025](https://festival.si.edu/blog/karen-weaving-circle)). One Albert Lea farmer told a reporter that a Karen family sometimes buys a goat from him for a wedding ([The American Prospect, 2026](https://prospect.org/2026/03/30/apr-2026-magazine-when-ice-blows-through-rural-america-minnesota/)).

**Food.** Meals center on rice, with soup, vegetables boiled or pickled, chili paste, fish or shrimp paste, and meat. A favorite noodle dish is Kaw Nor, in spicy fish broth. A first food for babies is May Klor, a plain rice porridge ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

**Birth and childhood.** After birth, resting and staying warm, bone broth and warm drinks are common. The toolkit says more Karen mothers here are struggling with postpartum depression because the village help they knew is thinner and families are stretched by work. It also says first-generation parents often show love through acts of service, not words, and can struggle across language and culture with U.S.-born children ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

**Elders, death and respect.** Elders are respected from a young age. Touching the head of an older person is very rude. Talk about a death yet to come can be deeply upsetting. Weddings and funerals are open community gatherings, and people give to a grieving family even if they do not know it ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

**Outdoors.** A KOM leader said in 2024 that hunting and fishing are what many Karen people like to do, and the state has translated its regulations into Karen since 2021 ([Sahan Journal, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/climate-environment/minnesota-dnr-job-postings-karen-hmong-community-outreach-coordinator/)).

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Weaving.** Women formed the weaving circle in 2015, according to the Smithsonian's account, to revive backstrap weaving here, first using donated yarn. Motifs carry meaning: coix seed beads stand for rice, and a diamond some call a spider stands for persistence ([Smithsonian, 2025](https://festival.si.edu/blog/karen-weaving-circle)). Sahan Journal says the circle has met on Wednesdays for 12 years at the East Side Freedom Library, a count that differs slightly from the Smithsonian's ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 24, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/karen-traditional-weaving-circles-minnesota-youth/)). Coordinator Hta Thi Yu Moo took three youth and one adult weaver to the 2025 Smithsonian Folklife Festival in Washington ([KOM, Nov. 17, 2025](https://mnkaren.org/news/the-weaving-circle/)). The youth camp ran five weeks in July 2026 with eight teens ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 24, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/karen-traditional-weaving-circles-minnesota-youth/)).

**Theater and visual art.** Writer and actor Ehkhudah Zar adapted the Karen legend *Muyehpen* for Exposed Brick Theater, opening May 19, 2023, at the Historic Mounds Theatre in St. Paul, possibly the first English stage version ([MPR News, May 19, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/05/19/karen-story-muyehpen-finds-new-life)). *Karen Youth Circle*, a January 2026 show at Xia Gallery, came from a workshop by The Urban Village and the Center for Asian Minnesota Youth, with mentors Ku Gay Nahpay and Eh Soe Dwe ([Sahan Journal, Jan. 9, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/things-to-do-twin-cities-karen-pakistani-indigenous-art/)). Theater Mu has worked with KOM's afterschool program since 2023 ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/news/youth-voices-take-the-stage-through-theater-mu-partnership/)). KNYAW Visual Arts is named as a New Year co-organizer; I could not open a page describing it further.

**Dance and culture groups.** The Karen Culture Organization of Minnesota, 1380 Jessie St., St. Paul, teaches traditional dance to children and youth and runs New Year and wrist-tying gatherings ([Karen Culture Organization of Minnesota, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://karenculture.org/)).

**Sports.** The Karen Football Association, a Minnesota nonprofit, runs men's and women's semi-professional teams and a youth academy from age 4 ([Karen Football Association, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://karenfa.com/)). In 2020 its youth teams were the first all-Karen sides invited to the National Futsal Championships ([FOX 9, Feb. 10, 2020](https://www.fox9.com/news/minnesota-soccer-teams-represent-karen-community-on-national-stage)). Its women's team began in 2025 as the first in its league focused on Karen women, with players ranging from teens to single mothers ([Sahan Journal, June 20, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/karen-football-association-womens-team-first-year/)). Soccer, volleyball and caneball fill Martyrs' Day and wrist-tying weekends (section 6).

**Media.** Sahan Journal and MPR News carry the most sustained coverage I found. I did not find a Minnesota-based Karen newspaper or broadcaster. Karen News, a Karen-focused site, has covered Minnesota at times ([Karen News, June 18, 2018](https://karennews.org/?p=8422)), and KOM runs its own news and online culture presentations every other month ([KOM, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/karen-culture-presentation-online-4/2026-08-21/)).

## 9. Civic and political life

**Organizations.** The Karen Community of Minnesota, a volunteer body, began in 2003. KOM grew out of it with training from Vietnamese Social Services and gained nonprofit status in December 2008. Its first grants took effect Oct. 1, 2009 ([KOM history](https://mnkaren.org/about/history-of-kom/)). KOM now reports over 3,000 clients a year and 30 to 35 staff ([KOM history](https://mnkaren.org/about/history-of-kom/); [KOM press kit](https://mnkaren.org/about/press-kit/)) and calls itself the first Karen-led social service agency in the country. Its leaders in this period have included Eh Tah Khu, Alexis Walstad in 2025, and Alice Buckner since at least November 2025 ([Sahan Journal, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/climate-environment/minnesota-dnr-job-postings-karen-hmong-community-outreach-coordinator/); [Sahan Journal, June 9, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-impact-trump-revised-travel-ban/); [CBS Minnesota, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-karen-population-tps-ending-myanmar)). Other named groups include The Urban Village, the Karen Culture Organization of Minnesota, the Karen Buddhist Association of Minnesota and the Karen Football Association.

**Elected office.** I found no Karen person holding elected office in Minnesota on any page I could open. That is not proof that none exists.

**Advocacy.** In June 2018 the Karen Community of Minnesota planned an Advocacy Day at the Capitol, asking for more classroom help, teaching of Karen language and culture, and more college enrollment ([Karen News, 2018](https://karennews.org/?p=8422)). After the 2021 coup, Minnesotans wrote to Congress and some planned to demonstrate in Washington ([MPR News via InForum, 2021](https://www.inforum.com/news/after-myanmar-coup-karen-refugees-in-minnesota-fear-for-homeland)). A 2024 House bill proposed a KOM grant; the state's page shows only its first reading ([Minnesota Revisor, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/bills/93/2024/0/HF/5296)). Rep. Betty McCollum co-chairs the Congressional Burma Caucus and has spoken for Karen Minnesotans, and Sen. Tina Smith has backed permanent residency ([Sahan Journal, April 30, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/operation-parris-minnesota-refugees-interviews-canceled/); [KSTP, undated, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/minnesotas-karen-community-tracking-termination-of-tps-status-for-thousands-from-southeast-asia/)).

**Public service.** Karen people serve in St. Paul police, state government and schools (sections 2, 3 and 10). The St. Paul police also refer some Karen and Karenni youth to The Urban Village's mentoring ([MindSite News, 2026](https://mindsitenews.org/2026/10/22/minnesotas-karen-teens-navigate-two-cultures-and-opioid-crisis/)).

**Debates inside the community.** I could not find Minnesota reporting that sets out disagreements over the Karen National Union or the war at home, so I will not guess at them. What is visible in print is more modest: differing names (Karen or Knyaw), and differing accounts of religion and of wrist-tying, noted above.

## 10. Education

**In St. Paul schools.** About 4,700 Minnesota public school students came from Karen-speaking homes in 2023, about 2,500 of them in St. Paul Public Schools, making Karen the fifth most common home language statewide ([Sahan Journal, Feb. 3, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/education/karen-language-classes-st-paul-public-schools-refugees-minnesota/)). That fall the district began Karen classes at Como Park, Harding, Humboldt and Washington Technology, plus online, with a course for beginners and one for students who speak but cannot read or write Karen. The article says Burma's military regime banned Karen from public schools after 1962, so many children learned it in churches, temples or camps ([Sahan Journal, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/education/karen-language-classes-st-paul-public-schools-refugees-minnesota/)). The same report says a handful of charter schools also teach Karen. I could not confirm which ones today.

**Teachers.** A 2023 law opened a portfolio route to heritage language licenses. Ehtalow Zar of Johnson Senior High became one of the first two licensed in Karen, in Minnesota "and maybe the nation" ([MPR News, Jan. 15, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2025/01/15/mn-now-minnesota-licenses-first-ever-karen-language-teacher); [Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/education/minnesota-teachers-heritage-language-licenses-hmong-karen-somali/)). Hsajune Dyan was an assistant principal at Washington Technology in 2021 ([MPR News via InForum, 2021](https://www.inforum.com/news/after-myanmar-coup-karen-refugees-in-minnesota-fear-for-homeland)), and TahSoGhay Collah taught third grade in Worthington in 2024 ([AP via KSAT, 2024](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2024/10/31/takeaways-from-aps-report-on-how-immigration-transformed-a-minnesota-farm-town/)).

**Greater Minnesota.** Karen students are among growing groups in six Nobles County districts ([Sahan Journal, Sept. 8, 2021](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/us-2020-census-minnesota-diversity-immigrants-worthington-austin/)). Austin schools saw nearly 100 new Karen or Karenni students in one year around 2014 and planned a liaison ([MPR News via Grand Forks Herald, 2014](https://www.grandforksherald.com/newsmd/growing-karen-population-brings-new-changes-to-austin)).

**College.** In 2018 the Karen Community of Minnesota named money, qualifications and needing to work as reasons for low college enrollment ([Karen News, 2018](https://karennews.org/?p=8422)). Karen students have attended Bethel and Augsburg ([MPR News via InForum, 2021](https://www.inforum.com/news/after-myanmar-coup-karen-refugees-in-minnesota-fear-for-homeland)). I found no Karen enrollment count for any Minnesota college, and no campus association page I could open.

**After school.** KOM runs youth programs, including an afterschool program, and The Urban Village offers tutoring and mentoring ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/news/youth-voices-take-the-stage-through-theater-mu-partnership/); [MindSite News, 2026](https://mindsitenews.org/2026/10/22/minnesotas-karen-teens-navigate-two-cultures-and-opioid-crisis/)).

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Clinics and partners.** M Health Fairview's Roselawn, Rice Street and Bethesda clinics were among the first in St. Paul to hire Karen staff. With KOM, Fairview created culture broker roles from 2017, a postpartum home visit program, and what KOM calls the first Karen-specific addiction programs in the country ([KOM, Dec. 27, 2023](https://mnkaren.org/news/a-partnership-from-the-beginning-kom-and-m-health-fairview/)). KOM's 2026 flu clinics are held at churches and its own office ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/free-flu-shot-clinics-2/2026-12-03/)).

**Mental health.** A trial in two St. Paul clinics from 2013 to 2018 tested therapy and case management from the Center for Victims of Torture for Karen adults with major depression. People who received it showed less pain and fewer symptoms and functioned better socially ([Center for Victims of Torture, Feb. 5, 2020](https://www.cvt.org/articles/clinicians-comment-on-landmark-study-of-refugee-benefits-from-mental-health-interventions/)). Wilder's ThayGeMoo, Karen for "living root," began in 2021 to address fewer mental health referrals of Karen students than of others ([Wilder Research, Oct. 2023](https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/SBMH_ThayGeMoo_Report_10-23.pdf)). In nine interviews, which Wilder says may not represent the wider community, students and parents reported better moods and communication. KOM also offers trainings on stress, depression, suicide prevention and parenting ([KOM, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/programs/community-health/mental-health-and-family-relationships/)).

**Substance use.** In 2026, reporters described St. Paul partners' work on youth opioid use: a low-barrier buprenorphine program at Fairview Roselawn, KOM case managers who drive people to care, and mentors at Humboldt. Staff report fewer overdoses, but the state told reporters it does not keep data that fine, and its student survey cannot separate Karen youth ([MindSite News, 2026](https://mindsitenews.org/2026/10/22/minnesotas-karen-teens-navigate-two-cultures-and-opioid-crisis/)).

**Traditional and modern.** KOM says illness may be linked to imbalance in wind, water and fire, that healers may use herbs and plants, and that many also find modern medicine helpful ([KOM, Health, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/healthcare/)). The toolkit lists turmeric, lime, lemon and honey for discomforts, along with elders and traditional midwives during pregnancy ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

**Gaps in care.** Culture Care Connection reports that patients preferring Karen had the lowest rate of optimal diabetes care in a 2017 analysis, 32 percent against 45 percent statewide, and that a 2014 screening found 86 percent reporting war trauma. Neither claim names its sample on the page ([Culture Care Connection](https://culturecareconnection.org/cultural-responsiveness/karen/)). The toolkit notes gestational diabetes and high blood pressure as hard to manage on a diet high in carbohydrates and salt ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

## 12. Disability

**What Minnesota evidence shows.** I found no Minnesota count of Karen people with disabilities, and no Minnesota study of how Karen families understand disability, on any page I could open. The state toolkit, KOM's health page and KOM's family pages do not take up disability directly ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf); [KOM, Health](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/healthcare/); [KOM, Family Resource Connections, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/programs/social-services/family-resource-connections/)). That silence is worth noting, and it should not be read as an absence of disabled Karen people.

**Services with a Karen door.**
- KOM's Caregivers Program supports unpaid in-home caregivers of adults over 60 or adults with neurological dysfunction, with counseling, training, support groups and substitute care, funded through the Metropolitan Area Agency on Aging ([KOM, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/programs/social-services/caregivers-program/)).
- The same page points to a KOM Home and Community Based Services program, in partnership with the Department of Human Services, for people who need support to live independently.
- Karen Home Health Care offers Karen-language home care for elderly clients ([Karen Home Health Care](https://www.karenhomehealthkare.com/home-health-care-about-us)).
- The state's Aging and Disability Resources directory appears to list Karen-focused adult day programs in Maplewood and St. Paul, but it could not be opened, so I cannot describe them.

**Questions the evidence raises.**
- *Who defines disability here?* A Wilder therapist said there is "no language in Karen" for what some children experience, and Wilder Research says symptoms can be hard to translate ([Wilder, undated, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.wilder.org/living-roots-how-thaygemoo-is-supporting-the-mental-health-of-karen-students-in-saint-paul-public-schools/); [Wilder Research, 2023](https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/SBMH_ThayGeMoo_Report_10-23.pdf)). KOM, however, says terms for depression and anxiety translate fairly similarly, while clear definitions for diagnoses remain hard ([KOM, Health](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/healthcare/)). Those accounts should be asked about, not settled. These sources concern mental health. I found nothing comparable on intellectual, developmental, physical or sensory disability.
- *What does a label open or close?* St. Paul's lower mental health referral rate for Karen students suggests a door that was not being opened, and the reason was not settled by these sources ([Wilder Research, 2023](https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/SBMH_ThayGeMoo_Report_10-23.pdf); [MindSite News, 2026](https://mindsitenews.org/2026/10/22/minnesotas-karen-teens-navigate-two-cultures-and-opioid-crisis/)). The toolkit says addiction, suicide and mental health carry stigma, and that some see them as a character flaw ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)). Whether the same is true for a child with a developmental disability, or a person with a war injury, is a question no opened source answered.
- *What produced an impairment?* Sources describe war, displacement, malnutrition and malaria at home, and high infant deaths ([KOM, Economy](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/economy/); [MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)), and the CVT trial tracked pain alongside depression ([Center for Victims of Torture, 2020](https://www.cvt.org/articles/clinicians-comment-on-landmark-study-of-refugee-benefits-from-mental-health-interventions/)). None of these sources measures how much disability among Karen Minnesotans comes from any of it, and none should be read as saying so.
- *Who carries the care?* The toolkit describes multigenerational homes and a weakening of village-style help under the pressure of work. How that falls on families caring for a disabled member here is not documented in anything I could open.
- *How does information travel?* With oral and visual communication preferred and literacy in Karen estimated at 30 to 40 percent, written notices may miss people, by the toolkit's account ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

**Labeled background from Karen State.** A Japanese project led by Makoto Kono, from 2018 to 2023, visited 1,895 households in 10 villages in Karen State and interviewed 10 people with disabilities and their families ([KAKEN, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/grant/KAKENHI-PROJECT-18K11784)). It is not about Minnesota, and I read only its project summary.

## 13. What is changing now

**Federal enforcement and legal status, 2025 and 2026.** All of this is group-level reporting, dated.
- June 9, 2025: a travel ban covering Myanmar took effect, and KOM described families who had spent years trying to reunite ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-impact-trump-revised-travel-ban/)).
- Nov. 26, 2025: the government announced an end to Temporary Protected Status for Myanmar. KOM said the numbers were hard to track and called for resettlement to reopen ([KOM, Nov. 26, 2025](https://mnkaren.org/news/kom-statement-on-announcement-of-tps-termination-for-burma/)).
- December 2025: Wilder therapists described clients' fear after surprise visits by immigration officials, and USCIS said such visits are routine ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 23, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-karen-community-burma-tps-ending-immigration-ice/)).
- Jan. 9, 2026: Operation PARRIS began re-examining Minnesota refugees without green cards, an initial 5,600 people ([Sahan Journal, Jan. 21, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-refugees-immigration-operation-parris/)). By spring, many of those called in were Karen, a judge's order halting arrests had been vacated when the case was dismissed, and interviews and their cancellations were causing distress ([Sahan Journal, April 30, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/operation-parris-minnesota-refugees-interviews-canceled/)). A Burma news site reported in January that a Karen refugee was held in Texas and released, citing community members ([Burma Insight, Jan. 27, 2026](https://burmainsight.com/karen-refugee-wrongly-detained-by-ice-in-minnesota-released/)).
- Aug. 7, 2026: about 1,200 Minnesotans from Myanmar lost that protection after a court ruling. A lawyer stressed that this does not by itself make someone deportable. KOM's director said interviews bring back interrogations in Thai camps ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 18, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/tps-cut-myanmar-minnesota-karen-community-trump-immigration/)).

**Greater Minnesota.** In March 2026, a reporter found Albert Lea residents seeing little letup after federal drawdown announcements ([The American Prospect, 2026](https://prospect.org/2026/03/30/apr-2026-magazine-when-ice-blows-through-rural-america-minnesota/)).

**Young people.** MinnPost and MindSite reported in October 2026 on Karen and Karenni teens navigating opioids, school pressure and two cultures, along with fears that funding cuts and enforcement stress could reverse gains ([MindSite News, 2026](https://mindsitenews.org/2026/10/22/minnesotas-karen-teens-navigate-two-cultures-and-opioid-crisis/)). Language classes and the summer weaving camp point the other way, toward culture being taken up anew ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 24, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/karen-traditional-weaving-circles-minnesota-youth/)).

**From arrival to rootedness.** I could not open current state tables on new refugee arrivals. Firsts keep coming: the first Karen officer in St. Paul in 2014, the first sergeant in 2024, the first licensed Karen language teachers in 2025. KOM now serves people from many countries and updated its mission in 2023 to refugee and immigrant communities broadly ([KOM press kit](https://mnkaren.org/about/press-kit/)).

**Disagreements in print.** The clearest are over how to count people, what to call them, what share are Christian, and whether wrist-tying is religious. I could not find a published account of political divisions inside the Minnesota community.

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **Institution building in one generation.** A volunteer group formed in 2003 became an agency of over 30 staff, serving refugees of many backgrounds ([KOM history](https://mnkaren.org/about/history-of-kom/)).
- **Reviving small towns and congregations.** In Worthington, a tiny congregation once prayed for five new families, and about 60 now fill the Karen service ([AP via KSAT, 2024](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2024/10/31/takeaways-from-aps-report-on-how-immigration-transformed-a-minnesota-farm-town/)). In Albert Lea, the community bought and kept a church building in use ([Sahan Journal, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/changing-minnesota/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/)).
- **Work in the state's food plants and factories.** Karen workers have joined major Minnesota employers named in section 3.
- **Keeping language alive.** Language passed down through churches, temples and camps has now reached licensed public school classrooms ([Sahan Journal, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/education/karen-language-classes-st-paul-public-schools-refugees-minnesota/)).
- **Craft and story.** Weavers brought a backstrap tradition to a national stage, and Karen artists brought an old legend to Minnesota theaters ([Smithsonian, 2025](https://festival.si.edu/blog/karen-weaving-circle); [MPR News, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/05/19/karen-story-muyehpen-finds-new-life)).
- **New models of care.** Culture brokers, the postpartum home visit program and Karen-focused addiction work grew out of partnership with community leaders ([KOM, 2023](https://mnkaren.org/news/a-partnership-from-the-beginning-kom-and-m-health-fairview/)).
- **Showing up for one another.** Funeral giving, shared meals and the practical help of people such as Saw Poe Aye, who helps new arrivals settle in, are reported as everyday life ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf); [Sahan Journal, Aug. 18, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/tps-cut-myanmar-minnesota-karen-community-trump-immigration/)).

## What could not be established

- **Whether New Year celebrations are held in Worthington, Austin or Albert Lea.** The St. Paul event is the only one I could document. The 2027 venue had not been posted when checked.
- **The Gregorian date of the lunar first day of the Karen New Year for 2027 and 2028, and the lunar date of wrist-tying.** I could not confirm them, and the organizers' weekends are what can be stated.

- **A 2027 or 2028 date for Martyrs' Day or wrist-tying gatherings.** None was posted.
- **Wealthy families, named law, accounting or insurance firms, or a chamber of commerce.** None found.
- **Karen elected officials, and Karen college enrollment.** None found on pages I could open.
- **Disability counts, or any Minnesota study on Karen families and intellectual, developmental, physical or sensory disability.** Not found. PACER's Karen-language materials and the state's work on lead screening were not checked.
