# Karen in Minnesota
Checked: October 31, 2026

## 1. People and place

**How many.** No official count of Karen Minnesotans exists. Census data folds Karen people into "Burmese," and a researcher at APM Research Lab told Sahan Journal that those numbers are likely conservative ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 13, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/changing-minnesota/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/)). The closest public number is Minnesota Compass's Burmese profile: 19,372 people, give or take 3,438, for 2020 to 2024. That profile covers anyone counted as Burmese, so it does not describe Karen people alone, and its wide range matters ([Minnesota Compass, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)). Compass explains why: the census recodes people who answer "Karen" as Burmese, leaving no trace of the original answer, and Compass judges it "probably safe to assume" that most Burmese Minnesotans first identified as Karen ([Minnesota Compass, May 30, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-asian-communities)).

Community and agency estimates sit in the same range. The Karen Organization of Minnesota, often called KOM, estimates over 20,000 Karen people, plus 2,000 to 5,000 Karenni and about 1,000 Mon, and says plainly that these are estimates ([KOM press kit, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/about/press-kit/); [KOM, Ethnic Groups, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/ethnic-groups/)). The Minnesota Department of Health's toolkit also says over 20,000 ([MDH, Aug. 20, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)). Sahan Journal reports about 22,000 Minnesotans from Myanmar, most of them Karen ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 18, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/tps-cut-myanmar-minnesota-karen-community-trump-immigration/)). Older figures show the growth, though each was an estimate: nearly 3,000 in 2010 ([MPR News, Jan. 11, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/01/11/karen-myanmar-burma-new-year)), about 6,500 in 2016 ([WellShare International, April 5, 2016](https://wellshareinternational.org/who-are-the-karen/)), and more than 17,000 in 2021 ([MPR News via InForum, Feb. 4, 2021](https://www.inforum.com/news/after-myanmar-coup-karen-refugees-in-minnesota-fear-for-homeland)).

**Young, and many born abroad.** In the Burmese profile, 41.1 percent are 17 or younger and 3.4 percent are 65 or older. About two in three, 66.9 percent, were born abroad ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)).

**Where.** Compass places 83.0 percent of its Burmese group in the Twin Cities and 17.0 percent in Greater Minnesota, give or take 4.9 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)). The state health toolkit says Karen people are concentrated in Ramsey County, with smaller communities in Freeborn, Lyon, Nobles, Kandiyohi and Mower counties ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

- **St. Paul's North End and East Side, and the suburbs next door.** KOM set up its first office on Jackson Street in the North End in 2009 ([KOM history, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/about/history-of-kom/)). Karen shops line Rice Street and Arcade Street (section 4). The weaving circle meets at the East Side Freedom Library, and The Urban Village began in a storefront on Arcade Street ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 24, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/karen-traditional-weaving-circles-minnesota-youth/); [MindSite News, Oct. 22, 2026](https://mindsitenews.org/2026/10/22/minnesotas-karen-teens-navigate-two-cultures-and-opioid-crisis/)). Maplewood holds several Karen Baptist churches (section 5).
- **Roseville.** KOM moved to its office off Rice Street in Roseville in March 2015, and Calvary Karen Baptist Church is on Larpenteur Avenue there ([KOM history](https://mnkaren.org/about/history-of-kom/); [KOM flu clinic listing, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/free-flu-shot-clinics-2/2026-12-03/)). An early New Year drew more than a thousand people to Roseville Lutheran Church in January 2010 ([MPR News, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/01/11/karen-myanmar-burma-new-year)). A Karen student who started school in the Roseville Area School District after moving from Indiana in 2009 wrote in 2017 that her family came to Minnesota because more Karen people had settled here ([ThreeSixty Journalism, Nov. 2017](https://threesixty.stthomas.edu/what-does-it-mean-to-be-karen-student-shares-her-experience-as-member-of-ethnic-group/)). No count of Karen residents or students for Roseville itself was found.
- **Worthington.** The first Karen families arrived in 2008, according to a local volunteer and one of those families; by 2014 a conservative local estimate put the number of Karen families in Worthington at over 35 ([The Globe, July 11, 2014](https://www.dglobe.com/community/karen-family-finds-peace-happiness-in-southwest-minnesota)). Another 2010 report put the area's Karen population at 300 to 400, most working at JBS ([The Globe, Nov. 3, 2010](https://www.dglobe.com/news/people-of-god-karen-find-spiritual-home-at-indian-lake-baptist)). The two counts use different units and years and cannot be reconciled from these reports. In 2024, AP reported about 60 refugee families at the Karen Sunday service at Indian Lake Baptist Church, and Karen as the third most spoken of 44 languages at the intermediate school ([AP via KSAT, Oct. 31, 2024](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2024/10/31/takeaways-from-aps-report-on-how-immigration-transformed-a-minnesota-farm-town/)). A Karen Community of Worthington now has its own officers, and its 2024 New Year drew people from Brewster, Round Lake and Fulda as well; its vice president said some elders had moved away in earlier years, and that one group had moved to Austin for work but came back for the holiday ([The Globe, Jan. 2, 2024](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/karen-people-of-southwest-minnesota-celebrate-the-new-year)). The Karen community was featured in Worthington's King Turkey Day Grand Parade on Sept. 19, 2026 ([The Globe, Sept. 20, 2026](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/king-turkey-day-brings-fun-and-a-win-to-worthington)).
- **Austin.** The city's Welcome Center counted 1,224 Karen and Karenni residents in 2014, up from 463 in 2012, and said more than 110 families had settled in Austin the year before. Its director said people from Burma had been moving to the area for meatpacking jobs since 2011, and that many had earlier commuted from St. Paul ([Austin Daily Herald, Sept. 14, 2014](https://www.austindailyherald.com/news/welcome-center-staff-more-people-needing-services-as-asian-population-is-booming-622101); [MPR News via Grand Forks Herald, Sept. 15, 2014](https://www.grandforksherald.com/newsmd/growing-karen-population-brings-new-changes-to-austin)). No more recent count for Austin was found.
- **Albert Lea.** Freeborn County's public health director said in 2023 that Karen residents had been moving into Albert Lea and the area since 2008 and that the population continued to grow ([Albert Lea Tribune, Feb. 3, 2023](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/public-health-hires-community-health-worker-to-assist-karen-residents-786977/)). Zion Karen Baptist Church began with four families worshiping together in 2012; its assistant pastor said in 2019 that many newcomers came from the Twin Cities to join family or to work, many at Select Foods ([Albert Lea Tribune, March 10, 2019](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/karen-church-gives-building-same-purpose-but-different-congregation-713625/)). A Select Foods occupational health nurse estimated in 2018 that about 300 Karen people worked at the plant ([Albert Lea Tribune, Oct. 19, 2018](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/our-hearts-are-broken-704665/)). In 2019, community leaders put the Karen population of Albert Lea, Austin and the surrounding area at about 1,500 ([Sahan Journal, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/changing-minnesota/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/)), and in 2026 a church youth leader said Zion's congregation counted 250 households ([Albert Lea Tribune, March 2, 2026](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/i-want-to-be-someone-who-people-can-count-on-woman-aims-to-serve-the-karen-community-in-any-way-she-can-823002de)). No official count for the city was found.
- **Also named.** Marshall and Willmar appear in the same 2019 report. In 2017 a Karen woman and her husband opened an Asian grocery in Marshall as they moved their family there from Worthington, and she said she knew people in Marshall's Karen community ([Marshall Independent, March 2017](https://www.marshallindependent.com/news/business/2017/03/an-asian-array/)). KOM ran a satellite office in Marshall from 2014 to 2019 ([KOM history](https://mnkaren.org/about/history-of-kom/)), though a state workforce page still lists a Marshall address ([CareerForce, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://careerforce.mn.gov/so/group/191/location/203)). WellShare also named Faribault in 2016 ([WellShare](https://wellshareinternational.org/who-are-the-karen/)).

**How people came.** The Karen are a people of Burma, also called Myanmar, and of Thailand, with no country of their own. The ancestral land is called Kawthoolei ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

*Background from Burma, not about Minnesota.* KOM's history page says Karen people hoped British rule, which began over all of Burma in 1886, would free them from Burmese rule; that Karen sided with the Allies in World War II while Burmese forces fought with Japan; and that at independence in 1948 the Karen were not granted their own land ([KOM, Karen History, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-history/)). Political scientist Nehginpao Kipgen wrote that the Panglong Agreement of Feb. 12, 1947 was signed between Burmans led by Gen. Aung San and the Shan, Kachin and Chin, that one of its principles was full autonomy in internal administration for the frontier areas, and that the Karen sent observers to the signing ([Bangkok Post, Oct. 21, 2015](https://www.bangkokpost.com/opinion/opinion/737432/peace-process-needs-to-be-inclusive)). A Burma News International summary of scholar Matthew Walton's work lists the Karen National Union among groups that went underground in January 1949, soon after independence ([Burma News International, July 14, 2016](https://www.bnionline.net/en/opinion/analysis/item/1978-panglong-signatories-took-longer-time-to-go-to-war-scholar.html)). This is one scholar's account of the Karen role at Panglong, and no Minnesota source was found on how Karen Minnesotans view that agreement today.

As background, a civil war dating to the 1940s drove many into camps along the Thai border. A Minnesota House document says those camps held 93,269 people in 2015, 84 percent of them Karen ([Minnesota House document, 2020 to 2021](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/nLMA_6GxeEezIM-kPX0ExQ.pdf)). The state toolkit says families averaged 8 to 12 years in camps, that first arrivals came in the early 1990s, and that larger numbers came in the early 2000s ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)). KOM's own page dates the first refugees to 2000 ([KOM, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/resources/karen-owned-businesses/)). In August 2004, MPR reported nearly 200 Karen refugees from Burma arriving in Minnesota as part of a wave of 4,000 coming to the United States ([MPR News archive, Aug. 9, 2004](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/2004/08/09/karen-refugees-from-burma-are-finding)). About 500 Karen people a year were reaching Minnesota around 2010, and half the state's new refugees that fall were from Myanmar ([MPR News, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/01/11/karen-myanmar-burma-new-year)).

**State arrival numbers.** The Minnesota Department of Health publishes primary refugee arrivals, meaning people whose first U.S. home was Minnesota, by country of origin and county. These count everyone from Burma, not Karen people alone, and they leave out the many who resettled elsewhere first and then moved here. Adding the yearly rows for Burma in MDH's table gives about 9,630 primary arrivals from 1999 through 2024, not counting a few cells MDH suppresses as under five. Arrivals were small until 2004, ran from about 440 to 1,030 a year from 2007 through 2016, and peaked at 1,027 in 2011. They dropped to 167 in 2017, recovered to 392 in 2019, fell to 59 in 2021 and 54 in 2022, and rose again to 205 in 2023 and 242 in 2024. About 9,100 of these arrivals, roughly 95 percent, were first resettled in Ramsey County ([MDH, primary arrivals by country and county, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/country.csv); [MDH, Refugee Health Statistics](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/index.html)). In 2025, 38 refugees arrived from Burma, 7 percent of the state's 545 primary arrivals ([MDH, Minnesota Refugee Health Report 2025](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/25rhrpt.pdf)). MDH also says there is no systematic way to identify all secondary refugees moving to Minnesota ([MDH, Refugee Health Statistics](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/index.html)), which matters for a community that grew so much through second moves. Karen people made up about half of Minnesota's first-arrival refugees in 2008 and more than 30 percent in 2014 ([WellShare](https://wellshareinternational.org/who-are-the-karen/)).

Many then moved again. Twin Cities employers were reluctant to hire people with limited English and no U.S. work history around the Great Recession, and plants in Greater Minnesota were more willing. Families also cited cheaper rent and a quieter life ([Sahan Journal, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/changing-minnesota/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/)). Others arrive from other states for family and work ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

**Generations and languages.** S'gaw Karen is the writing system most people understand. West Pwo, East Pwo, Bwe and other forms also exist, and the toolkit advises asking for S'gaw when translating. It estimates that Karen literacy among Minnesota's Karen population could range from 30 to 40 percent, adds that this is hard to measure, and says most children raised here likely cannot read or write Karen ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)). A Minnesota House document says 70 percent of Karen refugees speak S'gaw and 7 percent speak Pwo, citing an out-of-state profile ([Minnesota House document](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/nLMA_6GxeEezIM-kPX0ExQ.pdf)). In the Burmese profile, 89.1 percent of people 5 and older speak a language other than English at home, and 68.1 percent speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)). KOM staff work in English, Sgaw Karen, Pwo Karen, Karenni, Burmese, Thai and several other languages ([KOM press kit](https://mnkaren.org/about/press-kit/)). KOM says S'gaw is the common language because many Pwo speakers also speak S'gaw, and that an American Baptist missionary created a written script around 1830 from the Burmese alphabet ([KOM, Communication, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/communication/)). In schools, the Minnesota Department of Education counted 5,320 students statewide whose primary home language was Karen in 2024-25, the fourth most common language other than English. Of these, 3,419 were English learners, up from 3,268 in 2020, and 2,634 of the English learners were in Ramsey County schools ([MDE, English Learners in Minnesota 2024-25, via Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/other/261381.pdf)).

**Names used.** Some community groups write Knyaw or K'nyaw for themselves, and KOM says about 20,000 people here are referred to as "Karen" or "Knyaw" ([KOM statement, Nov. 26, 2025](https://mnkaren.org/news/kom-statement-on-announcement-of-tps-termination-for-burma/)). Karenni and Mon people are separate communities who often share these agencies. Karenni leaders estimate 4,000 to 5,000 people, mostly in the Twin Cities and Austin ([Sahan Journal, March 6, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/maplewood-minnesota-first-karenni-grocery-store/)).

## 2. All walks of life

No single picture fits. The numbers below are from Compass's 2020 to 2024 Burmese profile, in 2024 dollars, so they cover more than Karen households and carry wide ranges ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)).

- **Comfortable households.** Median household income is $85,747, give or take $15,049, and 37.2 percent of households take in $100,000 or more, give or take 10.7 points. Households are large, averaging 5.05 people, so these totals often rest on several earners. No Minnesota figure for Karen wealth, and no public reporting on wealthy Karen families, was found, so none are named.
- **Professionals and owners.** Sgt. Ler Htoo of the St. Paul Police Department is the first known Karen police officer in the country to reach that rank, and he counted five other Karen officers in the department in 2024 ([MPR News, Feb. 14, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2024/02/14/first-known-us-karen-police-officer-promoted-to-sergeant)). Chef Chris Tunbaw owns Mandalay Kitchen in Frogtown ([CBS Minnesota, Nov. 26, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-karen-population-tps-ending-myanmar)). Ehtalow Zar, a St. Paul teacher, is one of the first two people licensed to teach Karen in Minnesota ([Sahan Journal, Jan. 6, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/education/minnesota-teachers-heritage-language-licenses-hmong-karen-somali/)). Therapists June Nin Way at Wilder and Eh Ta Lar at Humboldt High work in the community ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 23, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-karen-community-burma-tps-ending-immigration-ice/)). KOM says over two dozen Karen-owned businesses have been established, among them groceries, restaurants and auto shops ([KOM](https://mnkaren.org/resources/karen-owned-businesses/)). Among adults 25 and older, 7.9 percent hold a bachelor's degree, give or take 3.6 points; the graduate figure is too small for Compass to show.
- **Working and middle-income families.** Owner-occupied homes are 67.9 percent of households, give or take 9.5 points. In Greater Minnesota, families moved from shared apartments to renting and to buying mobile homes ([Sahan Journal, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/changing-minnesota/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/)), and some bought houses in Worthington by 2010 ([The Globe, 2010](https://www.dglobe.com/news/people-of-god-karen-find-spiritual-home-at-indian-lake-baptist)). Employment among adults 18 to 64 is 69.3 percent, 75.2 percent for men and 63.7 percent for women. Daily work includes plant jobs and trades such as tree trimming ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 18, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/tps-cut-myanmar-minnesota-karen-community-trump-immigration/)).
- **People in poverty.** Compass puts 22.2 percent below the poverty line, give or take 7.6 points, including 25.8 percent of children, give or take 9.8 points. Another 23 percent or so live between the poverty line and twice that line. Among renters, 41.7 percent spend too much on housing, with a wide range of 16.4 points. And 67.0 percent of adults 25 and older did not finish high school, give or take 7.6 points. Fathers often work jobs without parental leave, and the toolkit names this as a cause of strain on new mothers ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

## 3. Work and the economy

**Before Minnesota.** KOM describes most Karen people in Burma as subsistence farmers, growing rice and vegetables and raising animals, with life set by planting and harvest ([KOM, Economy, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/economy/)). The state toolkit adds hunting and fishing ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

**Plants and factories.** Named employers in reporting include JBS in Worthington, Select Foods in Albert Lea, Quality Pork Processors and Hormel in Austin, and Viracon, an architectural glass maker in Owatonna popular with workers from Austin and Albert Lea. KOM employment counselors helped connect workers to these jobs from about 2010 ([Sahan Journal, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/changing-minnesota/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/)). A Select Foods occupational health nurse estimated in 2018 that about 300 Karen people worked at its Albert Lea plant ([Albert Lea Tribune, 2018](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/our-hearts-are-broken-704665/)). Worthington's Karen pastor moved there around 2010 to join a sister working at JBS ([The Globe, 2010](https://www.dglobe.com/news/people-of-god-karen-find-spiritual-home-at-indian-lake-baptist)). JBS Worthington sponsored the 2022 Karen New Year in St. Paul ([KOM, 2022](https://mnkaren.org/news/celebrating-2761-karen-new-year-in-st-paul/)).

**Service work and careers.** KOM trains people for interpreting and translating, construction and manufacturing, along with commercial driving, nursing assistant work and financial coaching ([KOM Employment Services, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/programs/employment-services/)). Karen-language home care is also a business, with at least one agency in St. Paul serving Ramsey, Washington, Mower, Freeborn and Lyon counties ([Karen Home Health Care, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.karenhomehealthkare.com/home-health-care-about-us)). Trades training reaches beyond KOM: in 2018 Dakota County Technical College said more than 20 Karen students were completing its automotive technician program ([DCTC News, Dec. 19, 2018](https://blogs.dctc.edu/dctc-news/2018/12/19/the-karen-people/)). Local government in Greater Minnesota has hired Karen staff too: Freeborn County Public Health created a Karen-speaking community health worker job in 2023, filled by a former nursing assistant at Select Foods and a senior home ([Albert Lea Tribune, Feb. 3, 2023](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/public-health-hires-community-health-worker-to-assist-karen-residents-786977/)), and Albert Lea schools employ Karen success coaches and interpreters ([Albert Lea Tribune, Feb. 28, 2023](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/man-breaking-down-barriers-for-albert-leas-karen-youth-788309/)). State government has also hired for the community: the Department of Natural Resources posted its first full-time Karen outreach job in 2024 ([Sahan Journal, March 8, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/climate-environment/minnesota-dnr-job-postings-karen-hmong-community-outreach-coordinator/)).

**Unions.** Albert Lea's labor history runs through the United Food and Commercial Workers ([The American Prospect, March 30, 2026](https://prospect.org/2026/03/30/apr-2026-magazine-when-ice-blows-through-rural-america-minnesota/)). No reporting on Karen members or stewards was found.

**Enforcement and work.** In 2026 Albert Lea residents described fear and arrests touching the Select Foods workforce, though that report does not separate Karen workers ([The American Prospect, 2026](https://prospect.org/2026/03/30/apr-2026-magazine-when-ice-blows-through-rural-america-minnesota/)). See section 13.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

Rice Street in St. Paul and Roseville is the clearest Karen commercial corridor, with Arcade Street on the East Side and University Avenue in Frogtown close behind. Heavy Table's 2023 food guide walks through several places, and describes Thai dishes alongside Burmese curries, mohinga and tea leaf salad ([Heavy Table, March 6, 2023](https://heavytable.com/from-myanmar-to-rice-street-karen-food-in-saint-paul/)). KOM keeps its list of Karen-owned businesses by email request rather than posting it ([KOM](https://mnkaren.org/resources/karen-owned-businesses/)).

**Confirmed operating as of this check:**
- **Mandalay Kitchen**, 383 University Ave. W., Frogtown, serving Knyaw, Burmese and Thai food. Its own site shows current hours and a notice about a limited menu ([Mandalay Kitchen, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mandalaykitchenstp.com/)). Chef Tunbaw opened it in November 2023 ([Racket, Jan. 12, 2024](https://racketmn.com/mandalay-kitchen-frogtown-st-paul-menu-burmese-thai-cuisine)).
- **Knyaw and Thai Kitchen**, 1048 Rice St., St. Paul, with online ordering and site images dated fall 2026 ([Knyaw and Thai Kitchen, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://knyawandthai.com/)).
- **Karen Home Health Care**, 1299 Arcade St., St. Paul, with posted office hours and a Karen-speaking registered nurse ([Karen Home Health Care](https://www.karenhomehealthkare.com/home-health-care-about-us)).
- **Karenni Grocery**, Maplewood, a neighboring community's store that opened in 2025, the first of its kind in the Twin Cities by its owners' account ([Sahan Journal, March 6, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/maplewood-minnesota-first-karenni-grocery-store/)).

**Named in recent years, but not confirmed open today:** Karen Thai on Larpenteur Avenue, Kyaw Karen Market at 945 Rice St., Nam Mu Asian Market at 1377 Arcade St. (formerly Original Karen Market), Friends Cafe at 1711 Rice St., Asian Fusion at 1700 Rice St., Karen Village in Maplewood, Karen Grocery on Pennsylvania Avenue, Karen and Nepali Foods on Larpenteur Avenue, K'Nyaw Grocery Store and Karenni Thai Food in Austin, and Loikaw21 in Austin. Details and links are in the companion business list. In Austin, a legal assistant and her husband were opening a grocery in 2019, and Willmar's boba shop had a Karen owner in 2026, though neither report names the shop ([Sahan Journal, 2019](https://sahanjournal.com/changing-minnesota/lured-by-jobs-and-housing-karen-refugees-spread-across-minnesota/); [The American Prospect, 2026](https://prospect.org/2026/03/30/apr-2026-magazine-when-ice-blows-through-rural-america-minnesota/)).

**Makers.** KOM's weaving circle sells bags, shirts, sarongs, table runners and scarves at the Swede Hollow Art Fair, the Saint Paul Art Crawl and the Little Mekong Night Market ([KOM Weaving Circle, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/programs/social-services/weaving-circle/)).

**Greater Minnesota shops.** Ton Kao Asian Grocery Store opened on College Drive in Marshall on March 13, 2017, co-owned by a Karen woman; it sold Thai and Southeast Asian groceries, frozen fish and T-shirts with a Karen logo ([Marshall Independent, March 2017](https://www.marshallindependent.com/news/business/2017/03/an-asian-array/)). A Worthington family said in 2014 that they shopped at Top Asian Foods and at "the Karen store on Oxford Street," which the report did not name ([The Globe, 2014](https://www.dglobe.com/community/karen-family-finds-peace-happiness-in-southwest-minnesota)). Neither could be confirmed open now.

No Karen chamber of commerce, and no named Karen law, accounting or insurance firm, was found. No named Karen-owned shop in Albert Lea was found on a page that could be opened.

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

**More than one faith.** The state toolkit, written by a Karen American, says Karen Minnesotans practice Christianity, Buddhism, and animism or traditional spiritual practice, and that people often confide in pastors and monks ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)). KOM names five beliefs among Karen people generally: animism, Buddhism, Christianity, Lehkai and Telahkon. It adds that there is much exchange between Buddhist and animist practice, and that villages at home have often been mainly one faith ([KOM, Religion, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/religion/)). A House document also lists Islam ([Minnesota House document](https://www.house.mn.gov/comm/docs/nLMA_6GxeEezIM-kPX0ExQ.pdf)).

**How the shares are told.** No Minnesota count by faith exists. MPR said in 2010 that about half of those who fled Myanmar are Baptist ([MPR News, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/01/11/karen-myanmar-burma-new-year)). Culture Care Connection, a health project, says almost all Karen in the U.S. are Christian ([Culture Care Connection, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://culturecareconnection.org/cultural-responsiveness/karen/)). KOM says only that many refugees in the U.S. are Christian. These do not agree, and Buddhist events and groups are active here.

**History, as background.** Baptist missionaries reached Burma in 1813, and a pastor told MPR that an old Karen story of a lost golden book helped people receive the Bible ([MPR News, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/01/11/karen-myanmar-burma-new-year)). KOM also records armed conflict between Christian and Buddhist Karen groups in Burma, after a Buddhist faction broke away and aligned with the military ([KOM, Religion](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/religion/)). No Minnesota reporting was found on how that history plays out here.

KOM names guardian spirits called Ther Myng Khae ([KOM, Religion](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/religion/)). No named Minnesota group that identifies itself as animist was found.

**Differences and silence.** KOM mentions Karen Muslims, and says Karenni people are often Catholic ([KOM, Religion](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/religion/); [KOM, Ethnic Groups](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/ethnic-groups/)). No Minnesota congregation of Karen Muslims, Adventists, Catholics or Anglicans was found, and no data on people of no faith. Flu clinics and other gatherings are often held at churches, and a church list leaves out Buddhist, animist and nonreligious households.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

- **Karen New Year.** Falls on the first day of the Karen month KOM spells Pyathoe, generally late December or early January. It marks the end of one rice harvest and the start of the next, and it is the largest celebration ([KOM, Celebrations](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/celebrations/)). St. Paul's gatherings fall on nearby dates, so the day moves. A two-day gathering ended Dec. 31, 2023 at Washington Technology Magnet School ([CBS Minnesota, Dec. 31, 2023](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/karen-community-celebrates-new-year-in-st-paul/)). Year 2765 was held Jan. 1, 2026 at the same school, with a free noon meal, games and prizes ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/karen-new-year-2765/)). **Year 2766 is announced for Jan. 2 to 3, 2027 in St. Paul, venue still to come**, organized by the Karen Culture Organization of Minnesota, Karen Community of Minnesota, KOM, The Urban Village, the Karen Buddhist Association of Minnesota and KNYAW Visual Arts ([KOM, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/karen-new-year-celebration-2766/)). The 2022 program opened with salutes to the Karen and U.S. flags, then speeches, a singing contest and a culture show ([KOM, 2022](https://mnkaren.org/news/celebrating-2761-karen-new-year-in-st-paul/)). In 2010 there were don and courtship dances, and more than a thousand people came ([MPR News, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/01/11/karen-myanmar-burma-new-year)). The January 2025 gathering, year 2764, was Jan. 4 and 5 at Washington Technology Magnet School, with a volleyball tournament and concert night Saturday and the main ceremony, resource fair and free boxed lunch Sunday ([KOM, 2025](https://mnkaren.org/event/karen-new-year-2764/2025-01-04/)).

Greater Minnesota holds its own New Year too. **Albert Lea** celebrated at Southwest Middle School on a Saturday in January 2018 ([Albert Lea Tribune, Jan. 15, 2018](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/celebrating-a-new-year-686123/)), and at Albert Lea High School on a Saturday in December 2025, with several hundred people, dance by the Karen Culture Organization of Minnesota and local groups, songs from churches and an elders center, a youth show on clothing and instruments, sports and an evening concert. Mayor Rich Murray and the school superintendent spoke, and a speaker from the Karen Organization of America urged parents to carry the tradition on ([Albert Lea Tribune, Dec. 22, 2025](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/karen-new-year-celebration-brings-music-culture-fun-639a96a9); [Albert Lea Tribune editorial, Dec. 26, 2025](https://www.albertleatribune.com/opinion/editorial-tribune-thumbs-6c17cfbb)). **Worthington's** Karen Community held its New Year at St. Mary's School on Monday, Jan. 1, 2024, with music, dance, storytelling, food and a Karen harp; the mayor received a plaque of appreciation. Its chairman said the holiday belongs on the first moon of January, but that the community chose Jan. 1 because everyone has to work and that day is a holiday for all; its vice president said the 2023 gathering was the first in several years ([The Globe, Jan. 2, 2024](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/karen-people-of-southwest-minnesota-celebrate-the-new-year)). No Karen New Year listing for **Austin** was found.
- **Karen Martyrs' Day.** August 12, the anniversary of the 1950 death of Saw Ba U Gyi, an early Karen National Union leader; others who resisted are honored too ([KOM, Celebrations](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/celebrations/)). Minnesota usually gathers on a nearby weekend. In 2025 the Karen Community of Minnesota hosted it Aug. 9 and 10 at Todd Park in Austin with volleyball, soccer and caneball ([KOM, 2025](https://mnkaren.org/event/75th-commemoration-of-karen-martyrs-day/)). In 2026 it was Aug. 15 at Ebenezer Karen Baptist Church in Maplewood, with a ceremony, a shared lunch and songs ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/76th-commemoration-of-karen-martyrs-day/)). August was chosen partly because outdoor fields were open, and the 2020 gathering moved online because of the pandemic ([KOM, about 2020](https://mnkaren.org/news/looking-back-on-karen-celebrations-in-minnesota/)).
- **Wrist-Tying Ceremony.** Held in August and sometimes at weddings. Elders and monks place items in a person's hand and tie a white or yellow string so the spirit stays and the person can live free from fear ([KOM, Celebrations](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/celebrations/)). A contributor to Drum Publications ties it to the August full moon and describes seven kinds of offerings ([Drum Publications, undated, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://drumpublications.org/wrist.php)). In 2026 the Karen Culture Organization of Minnesota held it Aug. 22 and 23 at Harris, with the main ceremony Sunday morning, soccer, volleyball and caneball, a pageant and food ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/karen-traditional-wrist-tying-ceremony-2026/2026-08-22/)). Sources disagree on its nature: Drum and a House document say it is not religious; the state toolkit ties it to spirit belief. The name "Karen National Wrist-tying Day" did not appear on any page opened, in Minnesota or abroad, where it is simply called a ceremony or celebration ([Karen Information Center via BNI, Aug. 2013](https://www.bnionline.net/en/kic/item/16048-hope-hpa-an-wrist-tying-ceremony-unites-karen.html)).
- **Karen Revolution Day (Jan. 31) and Karen National Day (Feb. 11).** KOM lists these for the 1949 uprising and a 1948 demonstration for a homeland ([KOM, Celebrations](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/celebrations/)). No Minnesota event was found for either.
- **Christmas, Easter and "Sweet December."** The toolkit says people mark Christmas, Easter or Buddhist New Year according to their faith ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)). KOM describes Sweet December, Nov. 30, as the start of Christmas for Christians, with church games, singing and a midnight rice soup called may klaw ([KOM, Celebrations](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/celebrations/)). Worthington's Karen members asked to go caroling in 2009 ([The Globe, 2010](https://www.dglobe.com/news/people-of-god-karen-find-spiritual-home-at-indian-lake-baptist)). No dated Minnesota event for these was found this year.
- **Buddhist New Year or Water Festival.** Mid-April, with water sprinkled on elders and offerings to spirits, according to KOM ([KOM, Celebrations](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/celebrations/)). No Minnesota date was found.
- **Novitiate Ceremony.** Held July 26 and 27, 2025, in Harris ([Karen Culture Organization of Minnesota](https://karenculture.org/events)). No 2026 date was found.
- **A neighbor's festival.** The Karenni DeeKu Festival, held Sept. 19 and 20, 2026 in St. Paul, belongs to the Karenni community and is noted here because families and agencies overlap ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/karenni-deeku-festival-2026/)).

## 7. Family, home and daily life

All of what follows comes from the sources named, and the toolkit itself warns it describes general patterns, not any one family.

**Households.** The toolkit says Karen families often have five to seven children; KOM says four or five ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf); [KOM, Family, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/family/)). Several generations often share a roof, and grandparents help raise children. Older siblings, especially the oldest sisters, help with little ones. A large share of mothers stay home, and fathers often work full time, according to the toolkit.

**Names and marriage.** Karen tradition has no family surname system, so spouses and full siblings may have different last names ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)). Introductions often use Saw for men and Naw for women. KOM describes a matriarchal tradition in which a husband joins his wife's clan and household ([KOM, Family](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/family/)). A Minnesota family's wedding clothes show married status through the Chay Moe Thu and sarong ([TPT Originals, May 25, 2021](https://www.tptoriginals.org/worn-within-why-is-weaving-essential-to-a-karen-wedding/)). Dress also signals life stage, as Smithsonian curators describe: white for unmarried women and girls, black and red for married women, red sarongs for men ([Smithsonian Folklife Festival, Nov. 14, 2025](https://festival.si.edu/blog/karen-weaving-circle)). One Albert Lea farmer told a reporter that a Karen family sometimes buys a goat from him for a wedding ([The American Prospect, 2026](https://prospect.org/2026/03/30/apr-2026-magazine-when-ice-blows-through-rural-america-minnesota/)). A young Karen writer from the Roseville area described traditional roles in 2017, women cooking and keeping house and men earning money, and wrote that in Minnesota these roles are blending, with Karen women becoming interpreters, teachers and lawyers and men sharing child care ([ThreeSixty Journalism, Nov. 2017](https://threesixty.stthomas.edu/what-does-it-mean-to-be-karen-student-shares-her-experience-as-member-of-ethnic-group/)).

**Food.** Meals center on rice, with soup, vegetables boiled or pickled, chili paste, fish or shrimp paste, and meat. A favorite noodle dish is Kaw Nor, in spicy fish broth. A first food for babies is May Klor, a plain rice porridge ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)). KOM adds that meals are shared in family groups, and names a pounded fermented fish paste, nya u, as a well-known dish, with turmeric, ginger, cardamom, tamarind, lime and garlic among common seasonings ([KOM, Food, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/food/)). Gardening carries over too: in Albert Lea, a neighboring church has offered free garden plots to Karen families, many of whom come from farming backgrounds ([Albert Lea Tribune, 2019](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/karen-church-gives-building-same-purpose-but-different-congregation-713625/)).

**Birth and childhood.** After birth, resting and staying warm, bone broth and warm drinks are common. The toolkit says more Karen mothers here are struggling with postpartum depression because the village help they knew is thinner and families are stretched by work. It also says first-generation parents often show love through acts of service, not words, and can struggle across language and culture with U.S.-born children ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

**Manners and speech.** KOM's guide for outsiders says that in Karen culture being direct is considered rude, decisions are usually made by consensus, displays of anger are not respected, and a Karen person may not say outright that a need is unmet. It also advises not to call Karen people Burmese unless they say so themselves ([KOM, Communication](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/communication/)). These are general patterns described by one organization, not rules for any person.

**Elders, death and respect.** Elders are respected from a young age. Touching the head of an older person is very rude. Talk about a death yet to come can be deeply upsetting. Weddings and funerals are open community gatherings, and people give to a grieving family even if they do not know it ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

**Outdoors.** A KOM leader said in 2024 that hunting and fishing are what many Karen people like to do, and the state has translated its regulations into Karen since 2021 ([Sahan Journal, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/climate-environment/minnesota-dnr-job-postings-karen-hmong-community-outreach-coordinator/)).

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Weaving.** Women formed the weaving circle in 2015, according to the Smithsonian's account, to revive backstrap weaving here, first using donated yarn. Motifs carry meaning: coix seed beads stand for rice, and a diamond some call a spider stands for persistence ([Smithsonian, 2025](https://festival.si.edu/blog/karen-weaving-circle)). Sahan Journal says the circle has met on Wednesdays for 12 years at the East Side Freedom Library, a count that differs slightly from the Smithsonian's ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 24, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/karen-traditional-weaving-circles-minnesota-youth/)). Coordinator Hta Thi Yu Moo took three youth and one adult weaver to the 2025 Smithsonian Folklife Festival in Washington ([KOM, Nov. 17, 2025](https://mnkaren.org/news/the-weaving-circle/)). The youth camp ran five weeks in July 2026 with eight teens ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 24, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/karen-traditional-weaving-circles-minnesota-youth/)).

**Theater and visual art.** Writer and actor Ehkhudah Zar adapted the Karen legend *Muyehpen* for Exposed Brick Theater, opening May 19, 2023, at the Historic Mounds Theatre in St. Paul, possibly the first English stage version ([MPR News, May 19, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/05/19/karen-story-muyehpen-finds-new-life)). The play has traveled: scenes and an artist talk at Southwest Minnesota State University in Marshall on Oct. 30, 2023 ([SMSU, Oct. 16, 2023](https://www.smsu.edu/today/articles/2023/10-16-2023_muyehpen-presentation.html)); a free one-night performance at Worthington's Memorial Auditorium on May 4, 2025, with the Nobles County Integration Collaborative ([The Globe, April 21, 2025](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/karen-story-muyehpen-coming-to-memorial-auditorium)); and a sold-out free performance at the Minnesota History Center in St. Paul on April 17, 2026 ([Minnesota Historical Society, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.mnhs.org/events/46004010808)). Zar told The Globe she performs it in English for Karen youth born here who may not know the story. No Austin staging was found. *Karen Youth Circle*, a January 2026 show at Xia Gallery, came from a workshop by The Urban Village and the Center for Asian Minnesota Youth, with mentors Ku Gay Nahpay and Eh Soe Dwe ([Sahan Journal, Jan. 9, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/things-to-do-twin-cities-karen-pakistani-indigenous-art/)). Theater Mu has worked with KOM's afterschool program since 2023 ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/news/youth-voices-take-the-stage-through-theater-mu-partnership/)). KNYAW Visual Arts is named as a New Year co-organizer; no page describing it further could be opened.

**Dance and culture groups.** KOM describes the don dance, which began among Pwo Karen and is now an expression of Karen national pride, and the bamboo dance, performed at Christmas, New Year, church functions and pagoda festivals ([KOM, Art](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/art/)). Groups named at Albert Lea's 2025 New Year included Sae Moe Wah Mu and the Mu Naw Sa Team ([Albert Lea Tribune, Dec. 22, 2025](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/karen-new-year-celebration-brings-music-culture-fun-639a96a9)), and Karen Community Performers appeared at Worthington's International Festival on July 12, 2025 ([The Globe, July 10, 2025](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/31st-annual-international-festival-schedule-of-events)). The Karen Culture Organization of Minnesota, 1380 Jessie St., St. Paul, teaches traditional dance to children and youth and runs New Year and wrist-tying gatherings ([Karen Culture Organization of Minnesota, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://karenculture.org/)).

**Sports.** The Karen Football Association, a Minnesota nonprofit, runs men's and women's semi-professional teams and a youth academy from age 4 ([Karen Football Association, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://karenfa.com/)). In 2020 its youth teams were the first all-Karen sides invited to the National Futsal Championships ([FOX 9, Feb. 10, 2020](https://www.fox9.com/news/minnesota-soccer-teams-represent-karen-community-on-national-stage)). Its women's team began in 2025 as the first in its league focused on Karen women, with players ranging from teens to single mothers ([Sahan Journal, June 20, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/karen-football-association-womens-team-first-year/)). Soccer, volleyball and caneball fill Martyrs' Day and wrist-tying weekends (section 6).

**Media.** Sahan Journal and MPR News carry the most sustained coverage. No Minnesota-based Karen newspaper or broadcaster was found. Karen News, a Karen-focused site, has covered Minnesota at times ([Karen News, June 18, 2018](https://karennews.org/?p=8422)), and KOM runs its own news and online culture presentations every other month ([KOM, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/karen-culture-presentation-online-4/2026-08-21/)).

## 9. Civic and political life

**Organizations.** The Karen Community of Minnesota, a volunteer body, began in 2003. KOM grew out of it with training from Vietnamese Social Services and gained nonprofit status in December 2008. Its first grants took effect Oct. 1, 2009 ([KOM history](https://mnkaren.org/about/history-of-kom/)). KOM now reports over 3,000 clients a year and 30 to 35 staff ([KOM history](https://mnkaren.org/about/history-of-kom/); [KOM press kit](https://mnkaren.org/about/press-kit/)) and calls itself the first Karen-led social service agency in the country. Its leaders in this period have included Eh Tah Khu, Alexis Walstad in 2025, and Alice Buckner since at least November 2025 ([Sahan Journal, 2024](https://sahanjournal.com/climate-environment/minnesota-dnr-job-postings-karen-hmong-community-outreach-coordinator/); [Sahan Journal, June 9, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-impact-trump-revised-travel-ban/); [CBS Minnesota, 2025](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-karen-population-tps-ending-myanmar)). Other named groups include The Urban Village, the Karen Culture Organization of Minnesota, the Karen Buddhist Association of Minnesota and the Karen Football Association.

**Elected office.** No Karen person holding elected office in Minnesota was found on any page that could be opened. That is not proof that none exists. Karen residents do hold civic roles below elected office: Albert Lea's assistant Karen pastor also serves on the city's Police Reserves and is called on to interpret on police calls ([Albert Lea Tribune, Feb. 28, 2023](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/man-breaking-down-barriers-for-albert-leas-karen-youth-788309/)), and the Karen Community of Worthington has an elected chairman, vice president and treasurer ([The Globe, Jan. 2, 2024](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/karen-people-of-southwest-minnesota-celebrate-the-new-year)). Hsajune Dyan, then St. Paul Public Schools' Karen cultural specialist, chaired KOM's board in 2017 and was named a Bush Fellow that year ([Bethel University, Oct. 11, 2017](https://www.bethel.edu/news/articles/2017/october/hsajune-dyan)). Local leaders recognize the community in public: Albert Lea's mayor thanked Karen residents at the 2025 New Year ([Albert Lea Tribune, Dec. 22, 2025](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/karen-new-year-celebration-brings-music-culture-fun-639a96a9)).

**Advocacy.** In June 2018 the Karen Community of Minnesota planned an Advocacy Day at the Capitol, asking for more classroom help, teaching of Karen language and culture, and more college enrollment ([Karen News, 2018](https://karennews.org/?p=8422)). After the 2021 coup, Minnesotans wrote to Congress and some planned to demonstrate in Washington ([MPR News via InForum, 2021](https://www.inforum.com/news/after-myanmar-coup-karen-refugees-in-minnesota-fear-for-homeland)). In December 2021, two leaders of The Urban Village traveled to the Myanmar border region with a Karen diaspora group to help displaced people; one, Ku Hser, said he had once had to hide in the jungles himself ([MPR News, Dec. 8, 2021](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2021/12/08/minnesotans-travel-to-myanmar-region-to-support-persecuted-karen)). A 2024 House bill proposed a KOM grant; the state's page shows only its first reading ([Minnesota Revisor, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/bills/93/2024/0/HF/5296)). Rep. Betty McCollum co-chairs the Congressional Burma Caucus and has spoken for Karen Minnesotans, and Sen. Tina Smith has backed permanent residency ([Sahan Journal, April 30, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/operation-parris-minnesota-refugees-interviews-canceled/); [KSTP, undated, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/minnesotas-karen-community-tracking-termination-of-tps-status-for-thousands-from-southeast-asia/)).

**Public service.** Karen people serve in St. Paul police, state government and schools (sections 2, 3 and 10). The St. Paul police also refer some Karen and Karenni youth to The Urban Village's mentoring ([MindSite News, 2026](https://mindsitenews.org/2026/10/22/minnesotas-karen-teens-navigate-two-cultures-and-opioid-crisis/)).

**Debates inside the community.** No Minnesota reporting was found that sets out disagreements over the Karen National Union or the war at home, so none are suggested here. What is visible in print is more modest: differing names (Karen or Knyaw), and differing accounts of religion and of wrist-tying, noted above.

## 10. Education

**In St. Paul schools.** About 4,700 Minnesota public school students came from Karen-speaking homes in 2023, about 2,500 of them in St. Paul Public Schools, making Karen the fifth most common home language statewide ([Sahan Journal, Feb. 3, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/education/karen-language-classes-st-paul-public-schools-refugees-minnesota/)). That fall the district began Karen classes at Como Park, Harding, Humboldt and Washington Technology, plus online, with a course for beginners and one for students who speak but cannot read or write Karen. The article says Burma's military regime banned Karen from public schools after 1962, so many children learned it in churches, temples or camps ([Sahan Journal, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/education/karen-language-classes-st-paul-public-schools-refugees-minnesota/)). The same report says a handful of charter schools also teach Karen. Which ones do so today was not confirmed.

**Teachers.** A 2023 law opened a portfolio route to heritage language licenses. Ehtalow Zar of Johnson Senior High became one of the first two licensed in Karen, in Minnesota "and maybe the nation" ([MPR News, Jan. 15, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2025/01/15/mn-now-minnesota-licenses-first-ever-karen-language-teacher); [Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/education/minnesota-teachers-heritage-language-licenses-hmong-karen-somali/)). Hsajune Dyan was an assistant principal at Washington Technology in 2021 ([MPR News via InForum, 2021](https://www.inforum.com/news/after-myanmar-coup-karen-refugees-in-minnesota-fear-for-homeland)), and TahSoGhay Collah taught third grade in Worthington in 2024 ([AP via KSAT, 2024](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2024/10/31/takeaways-from-aps-report-on-how-immigration-transformed-a-minnesota-farm-town/)).

**Statewide counts.** In 2024-25 the Minnesota Department of Education counted 5,320 students whose primary home language was Karen, 3,419 of them English learners ([MDE, English Learners in Minnesota 2024-25](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/other/261381.pdf)). This is a home language count, so it leaves out Karen children whose families report English, and it does not count Karen students by ethnicity.

**Background on schooling at home.** KOM says that after Gen. Ne Win took power in 1962 he nationalized missionary and private schools, that Karen schools disappeared in Burma, and that refugees on the Thai border have taught their own children since 1978 ([KOM, Education, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/education/)).

**Greater Minnesota.** Albert Lea schools use Karen success coaches who call home for teachers, translate school messages and attend parent-teacher conferences, because many parents do not speak English ([Albert Lea Tribune, Feb. 28, 2023](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/man-breaking-down-barriers-for-albert-leas-karen-youth-788309/)). One success coach and interpreter, who graduated from Albert Lea High School in 2017, now works in Adult Basic Education ([Albert Lea Tribune, March 2, 2026](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/i-want-to-be-someone-who-people-can-count-on-woman-aims-to-serve-the-karen-community-in-any-way-she-can-823002de)). Karen students are among growing groups in six Nobles County districts ([Sahan Journal, Sept. 8, 2021](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/us-2020-census-minnesota-diversity-immigrants-worthington-austin/)). Austin schools saw nearly 100 new Karen or Karenni students in one year around 2014 and planned a liaison ([MPR News via Grand Forks Herald, 2014](https://www.grandforksherald.com/newsmd/growing-karen-population-brings-new-changes-to-austin)).

**College.** In 2018 the Karen Community of Minnesota named money, qualifications and needing to work as reasons for low college enrollment ([Karen News, 2018](https://karennews.org/?p=8422)). Karen students have attended Bethel and Augsburg ([MPR News via InForum, 2021](https://www.inforum.com/news/after-myanmar-coup-karen-refugees-in-minnesota-fear-for-homeland)). Dakota County Technical College reported more than 20 Karen students in its automotive program in 2018, supported by its TRIO program; an instructor said many were still building English reading and writing skills ([DCTC News, 2018](https://blogs.dctc.edu/dctc-news/2018/12/19/the-karen-people/)). A Karen student at the University of Minnesota Rochester used his 2021 capstone to tutor Karen children in St. Paul schools, and said that in middle and high school he had not wanted to be Karen ([Med City Beat, 2021](https://www.medcitybeat.com/partnered/2021/capstone-project-has-paw-ler-shwe-reflecting-on-his-roots)). In 2017 Bethel University reported that Hsajune Dyan, a doctoral student, was expected to become the state's first Karen licensed school administrator, according to a St. Paul assistant superintendent; whether that happened was not confirmed ([Bethel University, 2017](https://www.bethel.edu/news/articles/2017/october/hsajune-dyan)). A St. Paul ESL teacher from Myanmar said in 2023 that about half his high school students were Karen, many born in Thai camps, some with schooling there and some without ([Rootstalk, Spring 2023](https://rootstalk.grinnell.edu/issues/volume-ix-issue-1/sang-sang/)). No Karen enrollment count for any Minnesota college, and no Karen student association page, was found.

**After school.** KOM runs youth programs, including an afterschool program, and The Urban Village offers tutoring and mentoring ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/news/youth-voices-take-the-stage-through-theater-mu-partnership/); [MindSite News, 2026](https://mindsitenews.org/2026/10/22/minnesotas-karen-teens-navigate-two-cultures-and-opioid-crisis/)).

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Clinics and partners.** M Health Fairview's Roselawn, Rice Street and Bethesda clinics were among the first in St. Paul to hire Karen staff. With KOM, Fairview created culture broker roles from 2017, a postpartum home visit program, and what KOM calls the first Karen-specific addiction programs in the country ([KOM, Dec. 27, 2023](https://mnkaren.org/news/a-partnership-from-the-beginning-kom-and-m-health-fairview/)). KOM's 2026 flu clinics are held at churches and its own office ([KOM, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/event/free-flu-shot-clinics-2/2026-12-03/)).

**How people rate their own health.** In Wilder Research's Speaking for Ourselves study, 101 Karen adults in Hennepin and Ramsey counties were interviewed. Karen respondents rated their health lower than the other four communities: 3 percent excellent, 6 percent very good, 18 percent good, 59 percent fair and 14 percent poor. On emotional health, 63 percent said fair. Yet 85 percent said they would definitely seek professional help for a serious emotional problem, the highest share of any group, and 78 percent said they would not be at all embarrassed to. Wilder warns these are perceptions, not incidence data, and the study is from 2015 ([Wilder Research, Sept. 2015](https://nationalepinet.org/portal/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/Wilder-Perceptions-of-Health-Mental-Health-and-Health-Care-Access-among-Immigrants-and-Refugees-in-the-Twin-Cities.pdf)).

**Language access.** MDH's S'gaw Karen page, updated Oct. 2, 2026, offers translated materials on lead, tuberculosis, flu, immunizations, hepatitis B, safe infant sleep, depression and anxiety around pregnancy, the WE-Check well-being screen, WIC, diabetes, tick-borne diseases and more ([MDH, Karen translated materials, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/translation/karen.html)). In 2019 a Karen-language animated video on healthy relationships and domestic violence was produced by the Karen Chemical Dependency Collaborative, Karen community members, HealthEast Roselawn Clinic, the Minnesota Coalition for Battered Women and TPT ([MDH Refugee and International Health Quarterly, April 18, 2019](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/MNMDH/bulletins/23d26bd)).

**Greater Minnesota.** Freeborn County Public Health's Karen-speaking community health worker, hired in 2023, helps families apply for food support and medical assistance, schedule appointments and get rides ([Albert Lea Tribune, Feb. 3, 2023](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/public-health-hires-community-health-worker-to-assist-karen-residents-786977/)).

**Mental health.** A trial in two St. Paul clinics from 2013 to 2018 tested therapy and case management from the Center for Victims of Torture for Karen adults with major depression. People who received it showed less pain and fewer symptoms and functioned better socially ([Center for Victims of Torture, Feb. 5, 2020](https://www.cvt.org/articles/clinicians-comment-on-landmark-study-of-refugee-benefits-from-mental-health-interventions/)). Wilder's ThayGeMoo, Karen for "living root," began in 2021 to address fewer mental health referrals of Karen students than of others ([Wilder Research, Oct. 2023](https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/SBMH_ThayGeMoo_Report_10-23.pdf)). In nine interviews, which Wilder says may not represent the wider community, students and parents reported better moods and communication. KOM also offers trainings on stress, depression, suicide prevention and parenting ([KOM, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/programs/community-health/mental-health-and-family-relationships/)).

**Substance use.** In 2026, reporters described St. Paul partners' work on youth opioid use: a low-barrier buprenorphine program at Fairview Roselawn, KOM case managers who drive people to care, and mentors at Humboldt. Staff report fewer overdoses, but the state told reporters it does not keep data that fine, and its student survey cannot separate Karen youth ([MindSite News, 2026](https://mindsitenews.org/2026/10/22/minnesotas-karen-teens-navigate-two-cultures-and-opioid-crisis/)).

**Traditional and modern.** KOM says illness may be linked to imbalance in wind, water and fire, that healers may use herbs and plants, and that many also find modern medicine helpful ([KOM, Health, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/healthcare/)). The toolkit lists turmeric, lime, lemon and honey for discomforts, along with elders and traditional midwives during pregnancy ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

**Gaps in care.** Culture Care Connection reports that patients preferring Karen had the lowest rate of optimal diabetes care in a 2017 analysis, 32 percent against 45 percent statewide, and that a 2014 screening found 86 percent reporting war trauma. Neither claim names its sample on the page ([Culture Care Connection](https://culturecareconnection.org/cultural-responsiveness/karen/)). The toolkit notes gestational diabetes and high blood pressure as hard to manage on a diet high in carbohydrates and salt ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

## 12. Disability

**What Minnesota evidence shows.** No Minnesota count of Karen people with disabilities, and no Minnesota study of how Karen families understand disability, was found on any page that could be opened. The closest number is Minnesota Compass's broader Burmese profile: 13.8 percent have a disability, give or take 3.3 points, and 11.0 percent of those under 65, for 2020 to 2024. It covers everyone counted as Burmese, which takes in Karen people, and does not separate them ([Minnesota Compass, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/burmese)). Compass explains that census answers of "Karen" are recoded as Burmese ([Minnesota Compass, May 30, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-asian-communities)). The state toolkit, KOM's health page and KOM's family pages do not take up disability directly ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf); [KOM, Health](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/healthcare/); [KOM, Family Resource Connections, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/programs/social-services/family-resource-connections/)). That silence is worth noting, and it should not be read as an absence of disabled Karen people.

**Lead, a documented risk for children.** MDH says there is no safe lead level and that high levels have been linked to neurological deficits and developmental challenges. In an analysis of refugee children arriving from 2016 through October 2018, MDH and its Lead Program found that refugees from Afghanistan, Somalia and Burma had higher rates of elevated blood lead and lower rates of follow-up. Across all refugee children matched, 197 of 2,209 (9 percent) had elevated levels at first test, and 64 of those did not receive follow-up. MDH named imported foods, spices, medicines, herbs and cosmetics, and older housing, as risks after arrival, and changed its reporting so counties would learn of children over six with elevated levels ([MDH Refugee and International Health Quarterly, April 18, 2019](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/MNMDH/bulletins/23d26bd)). In 2025, 35 of 193 newly arrived refugee children screened statewide, from all countries, had elevated blood lead ([MDH, Minnesota Refugee Health Report 2025](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/25rhrpt.pdf)). MDH's public lead screening data are grouped by year, not by country, so no Burma-specific or Karen-specific lead rate is public ([MDH, Refugee Health Statistics](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/index.html)). MDH offers eight lead fact sheets in S'gaw Karen, including ones on lead in imported products, lowering a child's blood lead, and lead in pregnancy ([MDH, Karen translated materials](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/translation/karen.html)). None of these sources measures whether lead has caused lasting impairment in Karen children here, and none should be read as saying so.

**Services with a Karen door.**
- KOM's Caregivers Program supports unpaid in-home caregivers of adults over 60 or adults with neurological dysfunction, with counseling, training, support groups and substitute care, funded through the Metropolitan Area Agency on Aging ([KOM, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://mnkaren.org/programs/social-services/caregivers-program/)).
- The same page points to a KOM Home and Community Based Services program, in partnership with the Department of Human Services, for people who need support to live independently.
- Karen Home Health Care offers Karen-language home care for elderly clients ([Karen Home Health Care](https://www.karenhomehealthkare.com/home-health-care-about-us)).
- The state's Aging and Disability Resources directory appears to list Karen-focused adult day programs in Maplewood and St. Paul, but it could not be opened, so they are not described here. Albert Lea's 2025 New Year program included songs from a group called the Htaw Meh Pa Elders Center, which the report did not describe further ([Albert Lea Tribune, Dec. 22, 2025](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/karen-new-year-celebration-brings-music-culture-fun-639a96a9)).
- In schools, Karen interpreters and success coaches, such as Albert Lea's, attend parent-teacher conferences and carry school messages home, one route by which families hear about services; no opened source described their part in special education specifically ([Albert Lea Tribune, Feb. 28, 2023](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/man-breaking-down-barriers-for-albert-leas-karen-youth-788309/)). The Department of Education reports how many English learners also receive special education only for all languages together, so no figure for Karen-speaking students was found ([MDE, English Learners in Minnesota 2024-25](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/other/261381.pdf)).
- Written materials on disability rights in Karen are hard to find. MDH's Karen page has no disability-specific item ([MDH, Karen translated materials](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/translation/karen.html)). PACER Center, Minnesota's parent center for families of children with disabilities, announced Karen-language parent guides in January 2024 by other accounts, but that announcement could not be opened: PACER's current website returned "page not found" for its older translation and press pages, and its site search showed no Karen results, so whether the guides are still offered could not be confirmed ([PACER Center search, checked Oct. 31, 2026](https://www.pacer.org/?s=karen)).

**Questions the evidence raises.**
- *Who defines disability here?* A Wilder therapist said there is "no language in Karen" for what some children experience, and Wilder Research says symptoms can be hard to translate ([Wilder, undated, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://www.wilder.org/living-roots-how-thaygemoo-is-supporting-the-mental-health-of-karen-students-in-saint-paul-public-schools/); [Wilder Research, 2023](https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/SBMH_ThayGeMoo_Report_10-23.pdf)). KOM, however, says terms for depression and anxiety translate fairly similarly, while clear definitions for diagnoses remain hard ([KOM, Health](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/healthcare/)). Those accounts should be asked about, not settled. These sources concern mental health. Nothing comparable was found on intellectual, developmental, physical or sensory disability.
- *What does a label open or close?* St. Paul's lower mental health referral rate for Karen students suggests a door that was not being opened, and the reason was not settled by these sources ([Wilder Research, 2023](https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/SBMH_ThayGeMoo_Report_10-23.pdf); [MindSite News, 2026](https://mindsitenews.org/2026/10/22/minnesotas-karen-teens-navigate-two-cultures-and-opioid-crisis/)). The toolkit says addiction, suicide and mental health carry stigma, and that some see them as a character flaw ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)). Whether the same is true for a child with a developmental disability, or a person with a war injury, is a question no opened source answered.
- *What produced an impairment?* Sources describe war, displacement, malnutrition and malaria at home, and high infant deaths ([KOM, Economy](https://mnkaren.org/history-culture/karen-culture/economy/); [MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)), and the CVT trial tracked pain alongside depression ([Center for Victims of Torture, 2020](https://www.cvt.org/articles/clinicians-comment-on-landmark-study-of-refugee-benefits-from-mental-health-interventions/)). None of these sources measures how much disability among Karen Minnesotans comes from any of it, and none should be read as saying so.
- *Who carries the care?* The toolkit describes multigenerational homes and a weakening of village-style help under the pressure of work. How that falls on families caring for a disabled member here is not documented in any source that could be opened.
- *How does information travel?* With oral and visual communication preferred and literacy in Karen estimated at 30 to 40 percent, written notices may miss people, by the toolkit's account ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf)).

**Labeled background from Karen State.** A Japanese project led by Makoto Kono, from 2018 to 2023, visited 1,895 households in 10 villages in Karen State and interviewed 10 people with disabilities and their families ([KAKEN, checked Oct. 30, 2026](https://kaken.nii.ac.jp/grant/KAKENHI-PROJECT-18K11784)). It is not about Minnesota, and only its project summary was read.

## 13. What is changing now

**Federal enforcement and legal status, 2025 and 2026.** All of this is group-level reporting, dated.
- June 9, 2025: a travel ban covering Myanmar took effect, and KOM described families who had spent years trying to reunite ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-impact-trump-revised-travel-ban/)).
- Nov. 26, 2025: the government announced an end to Temporary Protected Status for Myanmar. KOM said the numbers were hard to track and called for resettlement to reopen ([KOM, Nov. 26, 2025](https://mnkaren.org/news/kom-statement-on-announcement-of-tps-termination-for-burma/)).
- December 2025: Wilder therapists described clients' fear after surprise visits by immigration officials, and USCIS said such visits are routine ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 23, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-karen-community-burma-tps-ending-immigration-ice/)).
- Jan. 9, 2026: Operation PARRIS began re-examining Minnesota refugees without green cards, an initial 5,600 people ([Sahan Journal, Jan. 21, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/minnesota-refugees-immigration-operation-parris/)). By spring, many of those called in were Karen, a judge's order halting arrests had been vacated when the case was dismissed, and interviews and their cancellations were causing distress ([Sahan Journal, April 30, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/operation-parris-minnesota-refugees-interviews-canceled/)). A Burma news site reported in January that a Karen refugee was held in Texas and released, citing community members ([Burma Insight, Jan. 27, 2026](https://burmainsight.com/karen-refugee-wrongly-detained-by-ice-in-minnesota-released/)).
- Aug. 7, 2026: about 1,200 Minnesotans from Myanmar lost that protection after a court ruling. A lawyer stressed that this does not by itself make someone deportable. KOM's director said interviews bring back interrogations in Thai camps ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 18, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/tps-cut-myanmar-minnesota-karen-community-trump-immigration/)).

**Greater Minnesota.** In March 2026, a reporter found Albert Lea residents seeing little letup after federal drawdown announcements ([The American Prospect, 2026](https://prospect.org/2026/03/30/apr-2026-magazine-when-ice-blows-through-rural-america-minnesota/)).

**Young people.** MinnPost and MindSite reported in October 2026 on Karen and Karenni teens navigating opioids, school pressure and two cultures, along with fears that funding cuts and enforcement stress could reverse gains ([MindSite News, 2026](https://mindsitenews.org/2026/10/22/minnesotas-karen-teens-navigate-two-cultures-and-opioid-crisis/)). Language classes and the summer weaving camp point the other way, toward culture being taken up anew ([Sahan Journal, Aug. 24, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/karen-traditional-weaving-circles-minnesota-youth/)).

**From arrival to rootedness.** New primary arrivals from Burma have fallen from over 1,000 in 2011 to 38 in 2025, after a short rise to 242 in 2024 (section 1) ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/country.csv); [MDH, 2025 report](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/25rhrpt.pdf)). In Greater Minnesota, the community is visibly settled: a church bought in Rushmore in 2023, a New Year at Albert Lea High School in 2025 with the mayor and superintendent, and a place in Worthington's King Turkey Day parade in 2026 (sections 5, 6 and 1). Leaders there also worry about loss: a Worthington leader said elders had moved away, and an Albert Lea youth leader said many young people are starting to forget their heritage ([The Globe, Jan. 2, 2024](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/karen-people-of-southwest-minnesota-celebrate-the-new-year); [Albert Lea Tribune, March 2, 2026](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/i-want-to-be-someone-who-people-can-count-on-woman-aims-to-serve-the-karen-community-in-any-way-she-can-823002de)). Firsts keep coming: the first Karen officer in St. Paul in 2014, the first sergeant in 2024, the first licensed Karen language teachers in 2025. KOM now serves people from many countries and updated its mission in 2023 to refugee and immigrant communities broadly ([KOM press kit](https://mnkaren.org/about/press-kit/)).

**Disagreements in print.** The clearest are over how to count people, what to call them, what share are Christian, and whether wrist-tying is religious. No published account of political divisions inside the Minnesota community was found.

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **Institution building in one generation.** A volunteer group formed in 2003 became an agency of over 30 staff, serving refugees of many backgrounds ([KOM history](https://mnkaren.org/about/history-of-kom/)).
- **Reviving small towns and congregations.** In Worthington, a tiny congregation once prayed for five new families, and about 60 now fill the Karen service ([AP via KSAT, 2024](https://www.ksat.com/news/national/2024/10/31/takeaways-from-aps-report-on-how-immigration-transformed-a-minnesota-farm-town/)). In Albert Lea and Rushmore, Karen congregations bought church buildings that their earlier congregations no longer used ([Albert Lea Tribune, 2019](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/karen-church-gives-building-same-purpose-but-different-congregation-713625/); [The Globe, 2023](https://www.dglobe.com/news/local/new-life-breathed-into-rushmore-church-by-karen-community)). Albert Lea's mayor publicly thanked Karen residents for their family traditions and work ethic in 2025 ([Albert Lea Tribune, Dec. 22, 2025](https://www.albertleatribune.com/news/karen-new-year-celebration-brings-music-culture-fun-639a96a9)).
- **Bridges between families and institutions.** Karen success coaches, interpreters, a county community health worker and a police reserve officer connect Karen families with schools, clinics and police in Albert Lea (sections 3, 9 and 10).
- **Work in the state's food plants and factories.** Karen workers have joined major Minnesota employers named in section 3.
- **Keeping language alive.** Language passed down through churches, temples and camps has now reached licensed public school classrooms ([Sahan Journal, 2023](https://sahanjournal.com/education/karen-language-classes-st-paul-public-schools-refugees-minnesota/)).
- **Craft and story.** Weavers brought a backstrap tradition to a national stage, and Karen artists brought an old legend to Minnesota theaters ([Smithsonian, 2025](https://festival.si.edu/blog/karen-weaving-circle); [MPR News, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/05/19/karen-story-muyehpen-finds-new-life)).
- **New models of care.** Culture brokers, the postpartum home visit program and Karen-focused addiction work grew out of partnership with community leaders ([KOM, 2023](https://mnkaren.org/news/a-partnership-from-the-beginning-kom-and-m-health-fairview/)).
- **Showing up for one another.** Funeral giving, shared meals and the practical help of people such as Saw Poe Aye, who helps new arrivals settle in, are reported as everyday life ([MDH, 2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/docs/people/wic/localagency/culture/karen.pdf); [Sahan Journal, Aug. 18, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/tps-cut-myanmar-minnesota-karen-community-trump-immigration/)).

## What could not be established

- **An actual Karen count, and Karen-only income, poverty, schooling or home ownership.** Every number in sections 1 and 2 is either an estimate or a broader Burmese figure. State refugee arrival data count people from Burma, of every ethnicity, and only those whose first U.S. home was Minnesota.
- **Current counts for Roseville, Worthington, Austin and Albert Lea.** The latest figures found are a 2014 Austin Welcome Center count, a 2014 Worthington family estimate, a 2019 regional estimate for Albert Lea and Austin, and 2024 and 2026 church figures. Census place-level data for people of Burmese origin could not be retrieved, and no Roseville count of any kind was found.
- **A Karen New Year in Austin.** Albert Lea (2018 and 2025) and Worthington (2024) gatherings are documented; no Austin gathering was found. The 2027 St. Paul venue had not been posted when checked.
- **The Gregorian date of the lunar first day of the Karen New Year for 2027 and 2028, and the lunar date of wrist-tying.** These could not be confirmed; the organizers' weekends are what can be stated.
- **"Karen National Wrist-tying Day"** as a formal name. It did not appear on any page opened.
- **A 2027 or 2028 date for Martyrs' Day or wrist-tying gatherings.** None was posted.
- **Wealthy families, named law, accounting or insurance firms, or a chamber of commerce.** None found.
- **Whether listed shops are open today.** Review and map sites such as Yelp and Toast could not be opened, and most listings carry no dated sign of operation.
