# Corrections to the existing Cambodian Minnesota page

Page: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/khmer

## Errors or claims that should change

1. **Claim:** Minnesota Compass (ACS 2019–2023) puts the Cambodian community near 11,984, with 73.8 percent in the Twin Cities and 26.2 percent in Greater Minnesota; 27.7 percent 17 or younger; 11.0 percent 65 or older; 50.5 percent foreign-born; 61.2 percent speak a language other than English; 29.4 percent speak English less than "very well"; 72.3 percent high school or higher; 15.5 percent bachelor's or higher; median household income about $78,100; poverty 4.3 percent; 68.7 percent owner-occupied; household size 3.28; 7.3 percent uninsured; 12.1 percent with a disability.
   **Source says:** Compass now shows the 2020–2024 ACS: 11,521 (±2,142); 72.0 / 28.0 percent; 30.0 percent 17 or younger; 10.0 percent 65+; 47.6 percent foreign-born; 57.3 percent other language; 26.6 percent English less than "very well"; 73.1 percent high school or higher; 15.8 percent bachelor's or higher; median household income $80,388 (2024 dollars, ±$10,104); 5.3 percent in poverty; 56.5 percent owner-occupied (±10.8); household size 3.08; 6.2 percent uninsured; 15.8 percent with a disability (±3.4). The page's figures are one release out of date, and the homeownership and disability figures moved the most. The page also gives no margins of error, which for this population are large.
   **Link:** https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cambodian .
2. **Claim:** "MNopedia describes it [Watt Munisotaram] as the only Cambodian Buddhist temple in Minnesota."
   **Source says:** MNopedia does say this, but it is not accurate. Rochester has a Cambodian Buddhist temple: the Post Bulletin (May 24, 2004) described the community's monastery in Marion Township, and KTTC (April 24, 2026) reports the Buddhist Support Society's Cambodian New Year at Watt Dhamma Visudhikaram, 4462 29th St. SE, Rochester; KIMT (2025) says the temple has served a Rochester Cambodian community of more than 800 for over 20 years and was building a new worship center. The page should name the Rochester temple and not repeat "only."
   **Links:** https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/watt-munisotaram ; https://www.postbulletin.com/news/history-150th-anniversary-tab-cambodian-buddhists-mark-25-years-of-growth ; https://kttc.com/2026/04/24/cambodian-new-year-celebrations-take-place-rochester-this-weekend/ ; https://www.kimt.com/news/local/rochester-welcomes-cambodian-sister-city-during-new-year-celebration/article_35512cb7-d4e7-4e87-8100-79262b34315f.html .
3. **Claim (Disability section):** "We found no study specific to Minnesota," and the CURA/MN8/Wilder needs assessment "page reports no findings."
   **Source says:** Wilder Research published the findings in June 2022 (18 interviews: language and literacy, food, health coverage, voting, immigration, with recommendations for culturally specific mental health services and stigma reduction). A 2010 University of Minnesota study (Lee, Lytle, Yang and Lum, International Journal of Aging and Human Development 71(4)) examined depression literacy among Hmong and Cambodian elders in the Twin Cities through focus groups with nine professionals. Neither is a disability study as such, but both are Minnesota-specific and relevant.
   **Links:** https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/pdf-file/MN8_CommunityNeedsAssessmentReport_6-22.pdf ; https://research-portal.najah.edu/migrant/2859/ .
4. **Claim:** Partners include "the United Cambodian Association of Minnesota historically" and "a historical partner."
   **Source says:** UCAM is current, not only historical. Its site lists current leadership (Yorn Yan, president and CEO) and an adult day center in Mendota Heights that accepts EW, CADI, DD and BI waivers, Alternative Care and ECS across seven metro counties. The page itself elsewhere lists UCAM as active; "historically" should be dropped.
   **Link:** https://www.ucam.info/ .
5. **Claim:** Cambodian Church of the Nazarene, Crystal: "a Sunday English service at 10:00 and a Cambodian service at 11:00."
   **Source says:** The church's own site (cambnaz.org) did not respond when checked. The City of Crystal lists "Mpls Cambodian Church of the Nazarene," 6421 45th Ave. N., 763-537-7878, and a second congregation (Refuge International Ministries) at the same address. Service times could not be confirmed.
   **Link:** https://www.crystalmn.gov/our_city/religious_and_charity_organizations .
6. **Claim:** Minnesota Eight: "Some were released .. In late February 2017 .. Nin was released."
   **Source says:** Supported. Note that accounts differ on the detention date (Sahan Journal says Aug. 26, 2016; MN8's director told New Breath Foundation Aug. 29, 2016). CEHD and New Breath agree three were released and five deported.
   **Links:** https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/deportations-minnesota-mn8-hmong-laos-cambodia-vietnam/ ; https://new-breath.org/?p=6959 ; https://connect.cehd.umn.edu/cehds-cambodian-connections .
## Claims checked and supported

- Watt Munisotaram: 1982 founding; first abbot sent from Washington, D.C.; moves from Minneapolis to St. Paul (1984) and Eagan (1988); land bought 1988; construction from 2002 to a design by Yav Socchea; $1.5 million and thousands at the July 2007 consecration; 40 acres; ten resident monks (AP, 2023); public hours Thursday–Sunday (9 a.m.–5 p.m.); Great Gate of more than $1 million after six years of fundraising, unveiled August 2025; Iddhimuni Moeng Sang as abbot (Sahan Journal, Nov. 2025). Sources: MNopedia; MPR News 2007; AP via CBS Minnesota 2023; Sahan Journal July 30 and Nov. 17, 2025; wattmunisotaram.org.
- Wattanak Dance Troupe: founding dates differ (2000 per Theater Mu; 2008 per the troupe); began rehearsing at the temple; now based in Minneapolis; "over 40 students" on its home page.
- Darina Siv: executive director of UCAM at her death in March 2002; came to St. Paul in 1982 (MPR News archive).
- UCAM formed January 1993; Adult Day Services opened Oct. 16, 2014.
- Minnesota8 at 550 Rice St., St. Paul; Montha Chum executive director (MPR News, Aug. 28, 2026).
- CAPMN at 1068 Payne Ave., St. Paul; has run Cambodian Heritage Day (2023, 2025).
- Vichet Chhuon is an associate professor in Curriculum and Instruction at the University of Minnesota.
- Sahan Journal (Nov. 2025) reported about 12,000 Cambodians in Minnesota, citing Compass.
## Things that could not be supported from an opened source

- "Some 1.5-generation gang history in U.S. cities" — no Minnesota source was found. If kept, it needs a source and a date; as written it attaches a stereotype to a group.
- "Women's labor — factory, business, and the professional second generation — sits next to elder authority" and "Households are often larger than the nuclear English form expects" — no Minnesota source compares household size or gender roles to other groups. Compass's average household size is 3.08 (2020–2024).
- The sampeah greeting, name-order practices, LGBTQ experience, and the claim that gender match "may be requested in maternal and intimate care" — no Minnesota source was found; they may be reasonable but are unsourced.
- Pchum Ben and April 17 as observed in Minnesota — no Minnesota account was found of either being publicly observed here; Khmer New Year at Watt Munisotaram is documented (MNopedia, 2016 photographs), but no 2026 date was published by the temple.
- The Lowell, Massachusetts figures (Sokhary Chau, Rady Mom) and the national figures (Dith Pran, Haing S. Ngor, Sichan Siv, Loung Ung and others) were not rechecked; they are outside Minnesota.
