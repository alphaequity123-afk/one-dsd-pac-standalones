# Cambodian (Khmer) Minnesotans

A note on names: most sources here say "Cambodian" for the national and refugee community and "Khmer" for the language, the majority ethnic group and the culture. Organizations in Minnesota use both, as in the United Cambodian Association of Minnesota and the Khmer language classes it and others offer. This page uses both words the way the sources do.

## 1. People and place

**How many.** Minnesota Compass, using the American Community Survey (ACS) for 2020 to 2024, counts 11,521 Cambodian Minnesotans, give or take 2,142. It places 72.0 percent in the Twin Cities and 28.0 percent in Greater Minnesota, each give or take 6.9 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cambodian)). A narrower ACS count, of people who report Cambodian as their only Asian group and no other race, finds 8,291, give or take 1,165, for the same years ([ACS 2020-2024 table B02015 via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B02015&geo_ids=050|04000US27,04000US27)). The one-year 2024 ACS, counting Cambodian alone or in combination with other groups, gives 10,447, give or take 2,956 ([ACS 2024 table B02018 via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B02018&geo_ids=04000US27)). These numbers differ because they count different things, and all are survey estimates for a small population with wide margins. Reporters and officials round to about 12,000 ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 17, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cambodian-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/); [Pioneer Press via Yahoo, March 26, 2025](https://www.yahoo.com/news/families-cambodians-lao-vietnamese-affected-230000000.html)). The state Council on Asian Pacific Minnesotans used 10,101 in a 2025 legislative briefing ([Council on Asian Pacific Minnesotans, Jan. 28, 2025](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2025/finstlocalgov/20250128/finstlocalgov_20250128_CAPM-Overview.pdf)). Older estimates were lower: MPR News cited 6,000 to 9,000 in 2007 ([MPR News, July 9, 2007](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/2007/07/09/new-cambodian-buddhist-temple-opens-hampton)), and the U.S. representative for Minnesota's Fourth District spoke of "over four thousand" in 2011 ([Congressional press release, April 19, 2011](https://mccollum.house.gov/press-release/mccollum-honors-cambodian-genocide-victims)).

**Age and birthplace.** In the Compass profile, 30.0 percent are 17 or younger, 60.0 percent are 18 to 64, and 10.0 percent are 65 or older. Just under half, 47.6 percent (give or take 5.6), were born outside the United States ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cambodian)).

**Where.** The ACS single-race count puts the largest county groups in Dakota (about 1,772), Olmsted (about 1,480), Ramsey (about 1,375), Scott (about 1,366) and Hennepin (about 1,084) counties, each with margins of roughly 350 to 525. By city, Rochester (about 1,328), St. Paul (about 984), Shakopee (about 911), Eagan (about 684), Lakeville, Burnsville, Minneapolis, Savage, Bloomington and Prior Lake lead, again with wide margins ([ACS 2020-2024 B02015, counties](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B02015&geo_ids=050|04000US27,04000US27); [ACS 2020-2024 B02015, places](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B02015&geo_ids=160|04000US27)). This is a suburban and southern-Minnesota map more than an inner-city one. A 2017 state Senate resolution said Cambodians "resettled primarily in the Twin Cities and Rochester" ([Minnesota Senate S.R. 72, 2017](https://www.senate.mn/resolutions/display_resolution.php?ls=90&bill_type=SR&bill_number=0072&ss_number=0&ss_year=2017)). Faribault was an early landing place: one family arrived on a Faribault farm in the winter of 1981 ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 17, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cambodian-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)), and the Associated Press reported in 2018 that a small wave of Cambodian refugees came to Faribault in the 1980s to work at the Jennie-O plant ([AP via Fox News, Aug. 25, 2018](https://www.foxnews.com/us/somali-immigrants-find-growing-acceptance-in-minnesota)). Rochester's community traces itself to a family that moved there from Faribault in 1979 ([Post Bulletin, May 24, 2004](https://www.postbulletin.com/news/history-150th-anniversary-tab-cambodian-buddhists-mark-25-years-of-growth)).

**How people came.** The Khmer Rouge took Phnom Penh on April 17, 1975 ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cambodian-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)). MNopedia notes that significant Cambodian immigration to the U.S. began only in 1979, that 86 percent of foreign-born Cambodians in the U.S. in 1990 had arrived after 1980, and that most came as refugees ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/watt-munisotaram)). The 2017 Senate resolution says most Cambodian refugees reached Minnesota between the mid-1980s and early 1990s ([S.R. 72](https://www.senate.mn/resolutions/display_resolution.php?ls=90&bill_type=SR&bill_number=0072&ss_number=0&ss_year=2017)). A few came earlier. Sahan Journal profiled a former Cambodian navy supply officer who fled in 1975 and was sponsored by Grace Lutheran Church in Deephaven, settling first in Minnetonka; he said he chose Minnesota because other refugees spoke well of it ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cambodian-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)). Churches and Catholic Social Services (now Catholic Charities) helped resettle families; in Rochester, the first Cambodian Buddhist family's father worked as a resettlement counselor there ([Post Bulletin, 2004](https://www.postbulletin.com/news/history-150th-anniversary-tab-cambodian-buddhists-mark-25-years-of-growth)). Others came later on visas tied to employment and family ([Asia Matters for America, 2023](https://asiamattersforamerica.org/articles/first-annual-cambodian-heritage-day-showcases-minnesotas-cambodian-community)).

**Generations and languages.** Elders who survived the Khmer Rouge years now live alongside a "1.5 generation" who came as children and U.S.-born children and grandchildren. The abbot of Watt Munisotaram said in 2025 that U.S.-born Cambodians may know less of the culture and Buddhism, but parents work hard to bring them to the temple ([Sahan Journal, Nov. 17, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cambodian-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)). Among Cambodian Minnesotans 5 and older, 57.3 percent speak a language other than English at home and 26.6 percent speak English less than "very well," each with a margin near 7 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cambodian)). In 2022 interviews with 18 Cambodian Minnesotans, mostly women 55 and older, 12 said they speak Khmer fluently but read and write it only a little, because poverty and family duties pulled them out of school; many said their U.S.-born grandchildren understand little Khmer ([Wilder Research for MN8, June 2022](https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/pdf-file/MN8_CommunityNeedsAssessmentReport_6-22.pdf)). One grandparent in that study said the language gap is part of the reason for choosing to live alone.

## 2. All walks of life

No single number describes this community. Figures below are from the Compass 2020 to 2024 profile, in 2024 dollars, unless noted; margins are wide because the population is small ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cambodian)).

- **Comfortable and well-off households.** Median household income is $80,388, give or take $10,104. More than a third of households, 36.5 percent, have incomes of $100,000 or more, and 84.6 percent of people live at twice the poverty line or higher.
- **Homeowners and renters.** 56.5 percent of households own their homes and 43.5 percent rent (each give or take 10.8 points). Among renters, 31.1 percent pay more than they can comfortably afford (give or take 14.4). Median gross rent is $1,361.
- **Working families.** 86.8 percent of adults 18 to 64 are employed, men and women at similar rates. Average household size is 3.08.
- **People with less.** 5.3 percent live below the poverty line (give or take 2.8); many age groups are too small for Compass to publish. 26.9 percent of adults 25 and older did not finish high school, while 15.8 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more.

Behind the averages are very different lives. In the 2022 Wilder interviews, 12 of 18 participants had no high school diploma, 9 of the 14 who answered had household incomes of $20,000 or less, 13 lived on fixed retirement or Social Security income, and 11 had used a food shelf ([Wilder Research, June 2022](https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/pdf-file/MN8_CommunityNeedsAssessmentReport_6-22.pdf)). That was a small, older group recruited by an advocacy organization, not a random sample.

Public examples of success span many fields. A 2017 Senate resolution noted Cambodian Minnesotans who own businesses, lead nonprofits, practice as doctors, pharmacists and nurses at Mayo Clinic and elsewhere, and work as administrators, teachers and counselors in Rochester, Faribault and St. Paul schools ([S.R. 72](https://www.senate.mn/resolutions/display_resolution.php?ls=90&bill_type=SR&bill_number=0072&ss_number=0&ss_year=2017)). The former navy officer profiled by Sahan Journal worked in Minnesota as an accountant, stockbroker, insurance agent and Subway franchise owner before retiring to Waconia ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cambodian-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)). Kolap Restaurant's founders ran Sarun's Auto Repair for years before opening the restaurant in 2003 ([Frogtown Walking Tour, WFNU](https://wfnu.org/kolap/)). Vichet Chhuon is an associate professor at the University of Minnesota ([University of Minnesota](https://ci.umn.edu/vichet-chhuon)). Journalist Dymanh Chhoun, who arrived from a Thai refugee camp in 1993 knowing no English and was the first in his family to graduate from college, became a TV photojournalist and later a Sahan Journal multimedia journalist ([Hubbard School, University of Minnesota, March 9, 2021](https://hubbardschool.umn.edu/news/2021-03-09-alumni-spotlight-dymanh-chhoun); [Sahan Journal, July 30, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minnesota-cambodian-buddhist-watt-munisotaram-great-gate/)). Hardship sits in the same families: Sahan Journal's 2025 interviews describe children bullied with racist names in Faribault, parents and siblings lost to the Khmer Rouge, and the cost of community events rising for working families ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cambodian-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)).

## 3. Work and the economy

**What the numbers show.** Employment among working-age adults is high, 86.8 percent. Most workers (78.5 percent) drive or ride in a car to work, and 28.1 percent commute 30 minutes or more ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cambodian)). Compass does not publish an occupation or industry breakdown for this group, and no Minnesota source was found that does.

**Early work.** The first refugee generation took the jobs open to them. A Cambodian teacher who arrived in 1981 taught bilingual civics at Edison High School in Minneapolis while working 32 hours a week on the night assembly line at Control Data, according to a 2022 Senate resolution honoring him ([Minnesota Senate S.R. 125, 2022](https://www.senate.mn/resolutions/pdf/ls92/2021_2022/SR0125_O0.pdf)). In Faribault, meat processing at Jennie-O drew Cambodian families in the 1980s ([AP via Fox News, 2018](https://www.foxnews.com/us/somali-immigrants-find-growing-acceptance-in-minnesota)). In 1984 MPR News described about 25 new Southeast Asian restaurants in the Twin Cities and refugee-owned groceries, import shops, beauty parlors and car repair shops; the state refugee coordinator then estimated about 70 refugee-owned businesses statewide, across Vietnamese, Cambodian, Lao and Hmong owners ([MPR News, Nov. 16, 1984](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1984/11/16/southeast-asian-refugee-businesses-growing-in-the-twin-cities)).

**Today.** The jobs named in public sources include health care (the 2017 Senate resolution names Mayo Clinic), schools, social services, law and advocacy, tech support, journalism, food service and small business. Examples: a CAPMN founder works in IT at the University of Minnesota Rochester; another is a legal advocate manager at Tubman ([CAPMN founders page](https://www.capmn.org/founders)). Sokunthea Bentley was described in a 2005 governor's release as president and CEO of the International Translation Bureau ([Governor's office, 2005, via Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2010/other/101582/www.governor.state.mn.us/mediacenter/pressreleases/printerfriendly/PROD006665.html)); no current source was found for that firm. Food trucks and home cooks are a newer path (section 4). No Minnesota source was found on Cambodian membership in unions or on labor organizing specific to this community.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

No Cambodian commercial district in Minnesota was found, and named Cambodian businesses in public sources are few. This list is short because the evidence is thin, not because businesses are absent. See businesses.json for the status of each.

- **Kolap Restaurant**, 601 N. Dale St., St. Paul (Frogtown/Summit-University). Opened in 2003 by a couple who fled the Khmer Rouge; their eldest son, who enlisted in the Marines, now runs it. Its own site lists hours (closed Monday) and online ordering ([Kolap Restaurant](https://www.kolaprestaurant.com/)). A 2024 review praised its prohok ktees, samchok (rice soup), lot chha and sour soups ([My Annoying Opinions, Nov. 12, 2024](https://myannoyingopinions.com/2024/11/12/kolap-2-st-paul-mn/)).
- **Cheng Heng**, 448 University Ave. W., St. Paul. Long described by food writers as the Twin Cities' leading Cambodian restaurant, with a photo menu of loth chha, banh xiung and nhum salad ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine](https://mspmag.com/locations/Cambodia-Cheng-Heng/); [My Annoying Opinions, May 9, 2023](https://myannoyingopinions.com/2023/05/09/cheng-heng-the-return-st-paul-mn/)). No 2025 or 2026 source confirming it is open was found.
- **Home cooks and food brands.** In April 2025, Cambodian cooks Phon Sann and Sina War hosted the first "Immigrant Kitchen" dinner of the BIPOC Foodways Alliance ([MPR News, April 9, 2025](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2025/04/09/appetites-new-dinner-series-highlights-immigrant-home-chefs)). Sina War, whose food brand is Twin Tiger, described a multigenerational kitchen where she cooked two pots of rice, a softer one for elders ([BIPOC Foodways, Mecca Bos](https://meccabos.substack.com/p/reintroducing-sina-war)).
- **Food trucks.** No opened source named a currently operating Cambodian food truck (see "What could not be established").
- **Vendors at community events.** Cambodian Heritage Day 2025 drew 41 vendors ([City of Bloomington, Feb. 4, 2026](https://www.bloomingtonmn.gov/node/18736)), and Rochester's 2026 New Year listed more than 20 food and merchandise vendors ([KTTC, April 24, 2026](https://kttc.com/2026/04/24/cambodian-new-year-celebrations-take-place-rochester-this-weekend/)). The vendors are not named.
- **Groceries.** In the 2022 Wilder interviews, elders said they shop at Asian groceries on University Avenue in St. Paul and could find Khmer foods, though some herbs run out and prices push changes in the week's meals ([Wilder Research, 2022](https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/pdf-file/MN8_CommunityNeedsAssessmentReport_6-22.pdf)). No currently confirmed Cambodian-owned grocery was found.
- **No Cambodian chamber of commerce** in Minnesota was found.

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

**Theravada Buddhism and the watt.** **Watt Munisotaram**, 2925 220th Street East, in Empire Township near Hampton and Farmington in Dakota County, is run by the Minnesota Cambodian Buddhist Society, founded in 1982. Its own site calls it the largest Cambodian Buddhist temple in the country and lists public hours Thursday to Sunday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. ([Watt Munisotaram](https://www.wattmunisotaram.org/)). MNopedia traces the congregation from a rented Minneapolis house to St. Paul in 1984 (chosen for bus access) and Eagan in 1988, before neighbor complaints about parking pushed it to 40 acres of farmland bought that year; the main temple, designed by Cambodian architect Yav Socchea, was built from 2002 and consecrated in 2007 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/watt-munisotaram)). MPR News reported thousands at the 2007 consecration and a $1.5 million cost ([MPR News, July 9, 2007](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/2007/07/09/new-cambodian-buddhist-temple-opens-hampton)). In 2023 the AP reported ten monks in residence ([AP via CBS Minnesota, Feb. 2023](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-buddhist-temple-opens-up-sacred-dance-troupe/)). In August 2025 the temple unveiled a Great Gate that took six years of fundraising and more than $1 million, at a 37th-anniversary ceremony with more than 100 monks; two temple artists came from Cambodia to build it ([Sahan Journal, July 30, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minnesota-cambodian-buddhist-watt-munisotaram-great-gate/)). The 2017 Senate resolution says the name means "Temple Minnesota" ([S.R. 72](https://www.senate.mn/resolutions/display_resolution.php?ls=90&bill_type=SR&bill_number=0072&ss_number=0&ss_year=2017)); the AP gave it as roughly "the place to enjoy learning from wise men."

**A second temple in Rochester.** MNopedia calls Watt Munisotaram "the only Cambodian Buddhist temple in Minnesota" ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/watt-munisotaram)), but Rochester has its own. The Post Bulletin reported in 2004 that Rochester's Cambodian Buddhists worshipped in homes in the early 1980s, bought a house for a temple about 1986 and built a monastery in Marion Township in 2003, facing some local opposition and harassment of monks ([Post Bulletin, May 24, 2004](https://www.postbulletin.com/news/history-150th-anniversary-tab-cambodian-buddhists-mark-25-years-of-growth)). Today the Buddhist Support Society holds Khmer New Year at **Watt Dhamma Visudhikaram**, 4462 29th St. SE ([KTTC, April 24, 2026](https://kttc.com/2026/04/24/cambodian-new-year-celebrations-take-place-rochester-this-weekend/); [KROC](https://krocnews.com/rochester-is-entering-into-new-sister-city-relationship/)). In 2025, KIMT reported a new worship center half built and said the temple has served a Rochester Cambodian community of more than 800 people for over 20 years ([KIMT, 2025](https://www.kimt.com/news/local/rochester-welcomes-cambodian-sister-city-during-new-year-celebration/article_35512cb7-d4e7-4e87-8100-79262b34315f.html)).

**Christians.** The Post Bulletin's 2004 story quoted a Rochester elder estimating that of about 2,000 Cambodians in that area, half were Buddhist, 35 percent Christian, and the rest both, neither or other; he said more young people were becoming Christian through friends, marriage or difficulty understanding Khmer at the temple ([Post Bulletin, 2004](https://www.postbulletin.com/news/history-150th-anniversary-tab-cambodian-buddhists-mark-25-years-of-growth)). That is one person's estimate from 22 years ago. The **Minneapolis Cambodian Church of the Nazarene** meets at 6421 45th Ave. N. in Crystal ([City of Crystal](https://www.crystalmn.gov/our_city/religious_and_charity_organizations)); Sahan Journal photographed an elder there in 2025 ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cambodian-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)). Cambodian Heritage Day 2025 opened with blessings from Watt Munisotaram monks and from a pastor, Sokurt Sengly Suos ([CAPMN, CHD 2025](https://www.capmn.org/chd-2025)), a sign that both faiths are part of public community life.

**Practice and change.** Dance at the temple ties Buddhist, Hindu and animist stories together; troupe leader Garrett Sour said in 2023 he was still working out how Buddhism fits his own life, and the monks worried about young people's growing distance from religion ([AP via CBS Minnesota, 2023](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-buddhist-temple-opens-up-sacred-dance-troupe/)). The Khmer Rouge targeted religion; Sahan Journal cites the Documentation Center of Cambodia that Christians and ethnic minorities were among those targeted ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cambodian-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)), and the Post Bulletin names the Cham ethnic group among those targeted ([Post Bulletin, Jan. 2026](https://www.postbulletin.com/news/local/for-cambodians-living-in-the-u-s-ice-scenes-are-too-familiar)). No Minnesota source was found on Cham Cambodians in the state or on people of no faith.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

- **Khmer New Year (Choul Chnam Thmey), mid-April.** It marks the end of the harvest; Cambodia's dates are usually April 13 or 14 to 16. MNopedia records it at Watt Munisotaram, with a procession photographed on April 16, 2016 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/watt-munisotaram)), and the Buddhist society bought 40 acres so there would be room for big celebrations ([MPR News, 2007](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/2007/07/09/new-cambodian-buddhist-temple-opens-hampton)). Rochester's Buddhist Support Society holds its festival on a later weekend, explaining that farmers finish harvesting first: in 2026 it ran Friday, April 24, with a monks' blessing, and public days April 25 and 26 from 9 a.m. to 10 p.m., with a parade, live bands, more than 20 vendors and a visit from the Cambodian ambassador ([KTTC, April 24, 2026](https://kttc.com/2026/04/24/cambodian-new-year-celebrations-take-place-rochester-this-weekend/)). In 2025 it ran April 25 to 27, with food offerings to ancestors, sand hills, chanting, games, the passing of the New Year's angel, and the signing of a sister-city pact between Rochester and Tboung Khmum Province ([KIMT, 2025](https://www.kimt.com/news/local/rochester-welcomes-cambodian-sister-city-during-new-year-celebration/article_35512cb7-d4e7-4e87-8100-79262b34315f.html); [KROC](https://krocnews.com/rochester-is-entering-into-new-sister-city-relationship/)). Students also hold their own: the Cambodian Student Association of Minnesota called its April 2018 Khmer New Year evening at the University of Minnesota's St. Paul campus its biggest event of the year ([SHE-CAN, 2018](https://shecan.global/scholar-blogs/celebrating-cambodian-culture-with-my-minnesota-community-a-qa-with-she-can-scholar-sreytom-tim/)). No 2026 date for Watt Munisotaram's New Year was found.
- **Visakha Bochea (Vesak).** The Buddha's birth, enlightenment and death. MNopedia shows a procession at Watt Munisotaram on May 20, 2016 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/watt-munisotaram)).
- **Meak Bochea (Magha Puja).** In early February 2023, dozens in white joined a candlelit procession and two hours of chanting in Khmer at Watt Munisotaram ([AP via CBS Minnesota, 2023](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-buddhist-temple-opens-up-sacred-dance-troupe/)).
- **Pchum Ben**, the two-week autumn period of offerings for the dead. In Cambodia it ends in a major ceremony each September or October ([Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum](https://tuolsleng.gov.kh/en/?p=602)). No Minnesota source describing how it is kept here was found.
- **Cambodian Genocide Remembrance, April 17**, the date the Khmer Rouge took Phnom Penh in 1975. A 2025 U.S. House resolution supported naming April 17 Cambodian Genocide Remembrance Day ([H.Res. 338, 119th Congress](https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hres338/BILLS-119hres338ih.htm)). No Minnesota public April 17 event was found.
- **Cambodian Heritage Day.** Organized by the Cambodian American Partnership of Minnesota: first held Sept. 23, 2023, at the Minnesota History Center in St. Paul, with dance, a fashion show, food and artifacts for "hundreds" of visitors ([Asia Matters for America](https://asiamattersforamerica.org/articles/first-annual-cambodian-heritage-day-showcases-minnesotas-cambodian-community); [KAAL](https://www.kaaltv.com/uncategorized/minnesota-history-center-host-cambodian-heritage-day/)); and a second time on Sept. 27, 2025, at Oak Grove Middle School in Bloomington, with more than 900 guests and a proclamation from the governor ([CAPMN](https://www.capmn.org/chd-2025); [City of Bloomington](https://www.bloomingtonmn.gov/node/18736)). No 2026 date was found.
- **Temple anniversaries and blessings.** Watt Munisotaram marked its 37th year with the Great Gate unveiling, Aug. 8 to 10, 2025 ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minnesota-cambodian-buddhist-watt-munisotaram-great-gate/)). Wattanak dancers hold a summer ceremony each year honoring the spirits of past dancers ([AP via CBS Minnesota](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-buddhist-temple-opens-up-sacred-dance-troupe/)).
- **The 50-year commemoration.** In fall 2025 the Minnesota Humanities Center held a Joint Commemoration for Cambodian, Hmong, Lao and Vietnamese communities and Vietnam veterans: a digital exhibit from Oct. 25 and a festival at St. Paul's Union Depot on Nov. 1 and 2 ([Minnesota Humanities Center](https://www.mnhum.org/program/50th-anniversary-joint-commemoration/); [Union Depot](https://www.uniondepot.org/event/50th-anniversary-joint-commemoration/2025-11-01/)).

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Households.** Compass counts about 3,047 Cambodian households with an average size of 3.08. About one in five people (20.8 percent) live alone; married couples with children are 14.8 percent of households and single mothers with children 12.7 percent ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cambodian)). These are small-sample estimates.

**Many generations.** Families described in public sources are large and close: one elder told Sahan Journal his Minnesota family has grown to 300 or 400 people ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cambodian-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)). Sina War grew up in a multigenerational household where every adult brought a dish and children prepared herbs and rice ([BIPOC Foodways](https://meccabos.substack.com/p/reintroducing-sina-war)). The Wilder interviews show the strain too: elders who depend on children to drive them, interpret or explain elections, and grandparents who cannot talk with grandchildren in either language ([Wilder Research, 2022](https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/pdf-file/MN8_CommunityNeedsAssessmentReport_6-22.pdf)). A younger participant said it is easier to talk about feelings in English, and that talking with parents who lived through the Khmer Rouge means choosing words carefully.

**Food.** Rice anchors the table; the 2022 interviews mention rice prices and herbs as everyday concerns ([Wilder Research](https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/pdf-file/MN8_CommunityNeedsAssessmentReport_6-22.pdf)). Restaurant menus in St. Paul feature sour soups, prohok ktees, lot chha noodles, loc lac beef and congee ([My Annoying Opinions, 2024](https://myannoyingopinions.com/2024/11/12/kolap-2-st-paul-mn/); [Mpls.St.Paul Magazine](https://mspmag.com/locations/Cambodia-Cheng-Heng/)). Food shelves rarely carry white rice, fish sauce or Asian herbs, interviewees said.

**Dress and ceremony.** Traditional clothing appears at New Year, heritage days and dance; elders told Sahan Journal they hope clothing, food, ceremonies and language pass to the next generation ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cambodian-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)). No Minnesota source was found on weddings, naming practices, or end-of-life customs as practiced here.

**Aging.** Ten percent are 65 or older ([Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cambodian)). UCAM runs an adult day center in Mendota Heights for elders and people with disabilities and an elder independent-living program with citizenship classes, group shopping, rides to medical appointments and meals ([UCAM](https://www.ucam.info/); [211 United Way](https://search.211unitedway.org/search/3619846e-ef7e-5354-b7ed-94a93191dd19)).

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Dance.** **Wattanak Dance Troupe**, now based in Minneapolis, began as a small group rehearsing at Watt Munisotaram. Its own site dates its formal founding to 2008 by Master Yousedy Peov, a former member of Cambodia's Royal Dance Troupe; Theater Mu dates the troupe to 2000 with Sodanny Eir and Peov as founders ([Wattanak Dance Troupe](https://www.wattanakdancetroupe.org/about); [Theater Mu](https://theatermu.org/selapak)). Several of its leaders began training with Peov as children or teenagers. The troupe performed at the Walker Art Center's 2021 Choreographers' Evening, whose curator noted that nearly nine in ten Cambodian classical dancers did not survive the Khmer Rouge ([Walker Art Center](https://www.walkerart.org/reader/choreographers-evening-2021-program-notes/)); opened the Jungle Theater and Theater Mu's *Cambodian Rock Band* in 2022 ([Theater Mu](https://www.theatermu.org/news/2022/6/10/wattanak-dance-troupe-at-opening-night-of-cambodian-rock-band)); and staged *Following The Serpent's Veil: Preah Thong Neang Neak*, a classical dance on Cambodia's founding legend, at the Southern Theater on July 10 and 11, 2026 ([Southern Theater](https://southerntheater.org/shows/following-the-serpents-veil-preah-thong-neang-neak)). In 2023 an open call nearly doubled the troupe, mostly with children ([AP via CBS Minnesota](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/minnesota-buddhist-temple-opens-up-sacred-dance-troupe/)). A second group, Romdoul Dance Troupe, performed at Heritage Day 2025 ([CAPMN](https://www.capmn.org/chd-2025)).

**Youth arts and music.** CAPMN's "Art of Identity" program offers classical and folk dance, Khmer language, classical Cambodian instruments, and Koun, a youth collective producing a Khmer youth magazine, in the Twin Cities and Rochester ([CAPMN programs](https://www.capmn.org/programs)).

**Theater and writing.** Theater Mu's 2022 "Selapak" evening featured spoken-word poet Peuo Tuy, actor Deryck Hak and writer Cody Kour ([Theater Mu](https://theatermu.org/selapak)). Peuo Tuy now teaches in St. Paul Public Schools ([University of Minnesota, Oct. 2025](https://ci.umn.edu/events/2025-10-ridgs-book-event-conversation-vichet-chhuon-and-peuo-tuy)).

**Food as culture.** See section 4 for the Immigrant Kitchen series.

**Media.** No Minnesota Khmer-language newspaper, radio or TV program was found. Journalists with Cambodian roots work in mainstream Minnesota media, including Dymanh Chhoun ([Hubbard School](https://hubbardschool.umn.edu/news/2021-03-09-alumni-spotlight-dymanh-chhoun)). In January 2026 Rochester advocate Kim Sin posted Khmer-language videos on immigration rights that drew thousands of views ([Post Bulletin, Jan. 2026](https://www.postbulletin.com/news/local/for-cambodians-living-in-the-u-s-ice-scenes-are-too-familiar)), and Watt Munisotaram posts ceremonies to its own video channel ([Watt Munisotaram](https://www.wattmunisotaram.org/)).

**Sports.** No Minnesota source was found on Cambodian sports leagues, tournaments or athletes.

**Museums and centers.** No Cambodian museum or cultural center exists in Minnesota by any source found; the watt grounds and the Red Building community center there, reopened in 2017, serve that role ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/watt-munisotaram)).

## 9. Civic and political life

**Organizations.**
- **United Cambodian Association of Minnesota (UCAM)**, Mendota Heights, formed in January 1993 from the Khmer Association of Minnesota and the Refugee and Immigrant Resource Center. It calls itself the only human service organization in the state dedicated to Cambodian immigrants and refugees, and lists Yorn Yan as president and CEO ([UCAM](https://www.ucam.info/)).
- **Cambodian American Partnership of Minnesota (CAPMN)**, 1068 Payne Ave., St. Paul, founded by Chinda Kim Gregor, Lyda Morgan, Sophia Neou, Rothana Seng and Kim Sin; it gained nonprofit status in June 2024 ([CAPMN founders](https://www.capmn.org/founders); [Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cambodian-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)).
- **MN8 (Minnesota8)**, 550 Rice St., St. Paul, which works to end detention and deportation in Southeast Asian communities ([MN8](https://minnesota8.org/)). It grew from the 2016 campaign for eight detained Cambodian Minnesotans and marked ten years with a gala on Sept. 24, 2026 ([MPR News, Aug. 28, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/08/28/ten-years-on-minnesota8s-fight-is-far-from-over)).
- **Minnesota Cambodian Buddhist Society**, which runs Watt Munisotaram ([Watt Munisotaram](https://www.wattmunisotaram.org/)), and the **Buddhist Support Society** in Rochester ([KTTC](https://kttc.com/2026/04/24/cambodian-new-year-celebrations-take-place-rochester-this-weekend/)). Kim Sin founded the Cambodian Association of Rochester, Minnesota, according to a Rochester Community and Technical College text ([RCTC via LibreTexts](https://socialsci.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Communication/Intercultural_Communication/Developing_Intercultural_Communication_Competence_%28Halverson-Wente_and_Halverson-Wente%29/03%3A_Case_Studies%3A_Communicating_with_Co-Cultures/03.2%3A_Guest_2__Meet_Meet_Kim_Sin_and_learn_about_the_Cambodian-American_Community)).

**Elected office.** No Cambodian American elected official in Minnesota was found. The state's Council on Asian Pacific Minnesotans has carried a Cambodian representative in the past; a 2005 release named Sokunthea Bentley to that seat ([Governor's release](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2010/other/101582/www.governor.state.mn.us/mediacenter/pressreleases/printerfriendly/PROD006665.html)). In 2025 the Council reported convening more than 20 planning meetings with Cambodian, Lao and Vietnamese communities about the 50-year commemoration ([Council on Asian Pacific Minnesotans, 2025](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2025/finstlocalgov/20250128/finstlocalgov_20250128_CAPM-Overview.pdf)). The current Cambodian member, if any, could not be confirmed.

**Recognition at the Capitol.** A 2017 Senate resolution, as introduced, honored "the Cambodian Minnesotan story" ([S.R. 72](https://www.senate.mn/resolutions/display_resolution.php?ls=90&bill_type=SR&bill_number=0072&ss_number=0&ss_year=2017)), and in 2022 two state senators, one Republican and one DFL, introduced a resolution honoring Pengsan Ou for more than 30 years of weekend service at Watt Munisotaram ([S.R. 125](https://www.senate.mn/resolutions/pdf/ls92/2021_2022/SR0125_O0.pdf)). In March 2026, SF 4005, carried by three state senators, proposed an Arts and Cultural Heritage Fund grant to CAPMN; it was referred to committee ([Minnesota Legislature](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/bills/94/2026/0/SF/4005)).

**Debates.** In March 2025, descendants of Cambodian, Lao and Vietnamese veterans and refugees asked lawmakers to name their communities in 50th-anniversary funding. A Republican state representative said Cambodian and Vietnamese groups had received none of an earlier appropriation; two Hmong American state representatives questioned why backers had not reached out sooner; one of them called the idea of "us versus them" a false narrative and suggested listing all four groups, possibly in separate bills. The Pioneer Press noted past disagreements with the Hmong community over recognition in state grants ([Pioneer Press via Yahoo, March 26, 2025](https://www.yahoo.com/news/families-cambodians-lao-vietnamese-affected-230000000.html)). Interest in politics in Cambodia also continues: Pengsan Ou attended a weekly community meeting on the Paris Peace Agreements and human rights in Cambodia ([S.R. 125](https://www.senate.mn/resolutions/pdf/ls92/2021_2022/SR0125_O0.pdf)).

**Voting and engagement.** In the 2022 Wilder interviews, 11 of 18 had taken part in politics, mostly by voting; those who did not vote were mainly noncitizens. Most valued voting but said they relied on children or popular opinion to choose candidates ([Wilder Research](https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/pdf-file/MN8_CommunityNeedsAssessmentReport_6-22.pdf)). MN8 trains community members in their rights and as observers of immigration enforcement ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 2, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/deportations-minnesota-mn8-hmong-laos-cambodia-vietnam/)).

## 10. Education

**Attainment.** Among adults 25 and older, 73.1 percent finished high school and 15.8 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cambodian)). The gap between elders who lost school to war and children who reach college is wide. Dymanh Chhoun described arriving at 7 with no English, falling behind, being bullied and expelled for fighting back, then finding mentors, finishing at Normandale and graduating from the University of Minnesota in 2011 ([Hubbard School](https://hubbardschool.umn.edu/news/2021-03-09-alumni-spotlight-dymanh-chhoun)).

**Scholars and teachers.** Vichet Chhuon, associate professor of curriculum and instruction at the University of Minnesota, studies Cambodian American students' academic and ethnic identities ([University of Minnesota](https://ci.umn.edu/vichet-chhuon)); his book *Cambodian American Youth, Identity, and Schooling* (based on California research) was discussed at the university in October 2025 ([University of Minnesota event](https://ci.umn.edu/events/2025-10-ridgs-book-event-conversation-vichet-chhuon-and-peuo-tuy)). University faculty and students have worked with deported Cambodian Americans in Cambodia since 2018 ([CEHD Connect, 2022](https://connect.cehd.umn.edu/cehds-cambodian-connections)). A 2024 McNair scholar, a first-generation Cambodian American, studied how deportation affects Cambodian American families ([University of Minnesota CEHD](https://www.cehd.umn.edu/mcnair-scholar-2024-sophea-om)). The 2017 Senate resolution notes Cambodian teachers, counselors and administrators in Rochester, Faribault and St. Paul schools ([S.R. 72](https://www.senate.mn/resolutions/display_resolution.php?ls=90&bill_type=SR&bill_number=0072&ss_number=0&ss_year=2017)).

**Students.** The Cambodian Student Association of Minnesota is listed among the University of Minnesota's Asian American student groups ([University of Minnesota](https://cla.umn.edu/asianamerican/undergraduate/organizations)).

**Khmer language.** CAPMN runs a Khmer language class for youth ([CAPMN programs](https://www.capmn.org/programs)), and UCAM lists language classes among its cultural programs ([UCAM](https://www.ucam.info/)). Wilder's interviewers recommended events where elders teach younger people to read and write Khmer ([Wilder Research](https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/pdf-file/MN8_CommunityNeedsAssessmentReport_6-22.pdf)). No Khmer program in a Minnesota public school was found, and no school-district enrollment count by Cambodian or Khmer language was found.

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Coverage.** 6.2 percent have no health insurance, give or take 3.2 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cambodian)). In the 2022 interviews, 14 of 16 who answered had coverage, most through the state; barriers named were language, cost, transportation and a lack of culturally specific physical and mental health care ([Wilder Research](https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/pdf-file/MN8_CommunityNeedsAssessmentReport_6-22.pdf)). Wilder's recommendations for MN8 included culturally specific mental health services and education to reduce stigma.

**Trauma and memory, carefully.** The 2017 Senate resolution says survivors arrived with "a high rate of post-traumatic stress disorder" ([S.R. 72](https://www.senate.mn/resolutions/display_resolution.php?ls=90&bill_type=SR&bill_number=0072&ss_number=0&ss_year=2017)); that is a legislative statement, not a Minnesota study. A 2010 University of Minnesota study of nine mental health professionals serving Hmong and Cambodian elders in the Twin Cities found limited knowledge of specific disorders, culture-specific beliefs about causes of depression and little awareness of professional help ([Lee, Lytle, Yang and Lum, 2010](https://research-portal.najah.edu/migrant/2859/)). In January 2026, a Rochester advocate said scenes of immigration enforcement were stirring elders' memories of people "getting shot, people getting taken away" ([Post Bulletin, Jan. 2026](https://www.postbulletin.com/news/local/for-cambodians-living-in-the-u-s-ice-scenes-are-too-familiar)). Many people also describe healing: Sahan Journal reported Watt Munisotaram draws hundreds for ceremonies several times a year, and at its 2007 consecration a Cambodian Ramsey County deputy said he hoped it would help preserve the culture ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cambodian-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/); [MPR News, 2007](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/2007/07/09/new-cambodian-buddhist-temple-opens-hampton)).

**Providers and practices.** No Minnesota clinic or provider group specific to Cambodian patients was found. UCAM's adult day center provides health, social and nutrition services and culturally suited meals ([UCAM](https://www.ucam.info/)). The Minnesota Department of Health posts materials in Khmer and offers an interpreter on its general line ([MDH](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/translation/khmer.html)). No Minnesota source was found on use of traditional healers (kru khmer) today.

## 12. Disability

**Minnesota numbers.** Compass reports that 15.8 percent of Cambodian Minnesotans have a disability (give or take 3.4 points) and 11.9 percent of those under 65 (give or take 3.7), from 2020 to 2024 ACS data ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cambodian)). With 10 percent of the community 65 or older, and with elders telling Sahan Journal of starvation and forced labor under the Khmer Rouge ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cambodian-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)), questions worth asking are what produced the difficulties people report, how much reflects age, and how much reflects war, work injuries or unmet care. No Minnesota source answers those questions.

**Who names disability.** In the only study found on Cambodian American parents of children with disabilities (11 Khmer-speaking parents; the abstract does not say where they live), parents held differing views on what disability means and what causes it, and named language and cultural differences as challenges ([Werunga, Kim and Rosales, 2024](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/jsaaea/vol19/iss1/12)). Whether those views hold in Minnesota is unknown. The 2010 Twin Cities study found culture-specific beliefs about the causes of depression among elders ([Lee et al., 2010](https://research-portal.najah.edu/migrant/2859/)). Wilder heard of stigma around mental health ([Wilder Research](https://www.wilder.org/wp-content/pdf-file/MN8_CommunityNeedsAssessmentReport_6-22.pdf)).

**What a label opens.** UCAM's adult day center accepts the Elderly, CADI, Developmental Disabilities and Brain Injury waivers, Alternative Care, Essential Community Supports and private pay, and serves seven metro counties ([UCAM](https://www.ucam.info/)). It is the one Cambodian-led service found that is built to take disability funding. Under the state's settings rules UCAM describes, people new to adult day services must be 55 or older, so younger disabled adults are pointed toward other day services.

**Gaps and questions.** No Minnesota study was found on Cambodian children in special education, autism identification, interpreter use in IEP meetings, or disability among deportees and their families. No Khmer-language disability organization in Minnesota was found. Questions for anyone planning services: Who in the family decides? Is Khmer reading assumed when many elders read little? Does an English-only notice reach a household whose children translate? Does fear of immigration enforcement keep families from applying ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/deportations-minnesota-mn8-hmong-laos-cambodia-vietnam/))?

## 13. What is changing now

- **Deportation and enforcement, dated.** In August 2016 ICE detained eight Cambodian Minnesotans with old convictions; accounts agree three were released and five deported ([CEHD Connect, 2022](https://connect.cehd.umn.edu/cehds-cambodian-connections); [New Breath Foundation, Sept. 22, 2025](https://new-breath.org/?p=6959)). MN8's director said in November 2025 that people are now often deported within six to eight weeks of detention, much faster than in 2016, and described a brother facing deportation over a broken window that counted as an "aggravated felony" because of a one-year sentence ([Sahan Journal, Dec. 2, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/deportations-minnesota-mn8-hmong-laos-cambodia-vietnam/)). MN8 held an emergency rally against Southeast Asian deportations in August 2025 ([New Breath Foundation](https://new-breath.org/?p=6959)). In January 2026, the Post Bulletin reported at least three people "of East Asian descent," in its words, taken into custody in Rochester by federal immigration officials, one for a conviction more than 25 years old, according to a family member; community members said fear was causing elders to "lay low" ([Post Bulletin, Jan. 2026](https://www.postbulletin.com/news/local/for-cambodians-living-in-the-u-s-ice-scenes-are-too-familiar)). These are group-level reports; they say nothing about any individual's status.
- **Fifty years and recognition.** 2025 brought the 50-year commemoration, a gubernatorial proclamation at Heritage Day, and debate at the Capitol over who is named in commemoration funding (sections 6 and 9). In 2026, a bill to fund CAPMN was introduced ([SF 4005](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/bills/94/2026/0/SF/4005)).
- **Generations.** Elders and leaders worry that U.S.-born youth know less Khmer and culture, while young dancers, students and writers are building new forms (sections 1, 8 and 10). A Rochester leader said in 2025 that rising costs make it harder to hold community events ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/immigration/cambodian-americans-fifty-year-anniversary/)).
- **Building.** Watt Munisotaram's Great Gate opened in 2025; Rochester's temple was building a new worship center ([Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/minnesota-cambodian-buddhist-watt-munisotaram-great-gate/); [KIMT, 2025](https://www.kimt.com/news/local/rochester-welcomes-cambodian-sister-city-during-new-year-celebration/article_35512cb7-d4e7-4e87-8100-79262b34315f.html)). Rochester signed a sister-city pact with Tboung Khmum Province in April 2025 ([KROC](https://krocnews.com/rochester-is-entering-into-new-sister-city-relationship/)).
- **Numbers shifting.** Estimates have risen from "over four thousand" cited in 2011 and 6,000 to 9,000 in 2007 to about 11,500 in the 2020 to 2024 survey (section 1). Part of that reflects counting methods, not only growth.

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- Refugees founded the Minnesota Cambodian Buddhist Society in 1982 and, with its funds and gifts from Buddhist communities in Minnesota and beyond, built what the temple and MNopedia call the largest Cambodian Buddhist temple in the country, on Dakota County farmland ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/watt-munisotaram)).
- Classical dance, nearly lost under the Khmer Rouge, is taught in Minnesota by a master trained after the genocide to students who now teach others ([Wattanak Dance Troupe](https://www.wattanakdancetroupe.org/about)).
- Community members built lasting institutions: MN8, CAPMN, UCAM and Rochester's Buddhist Support Society ([MN8](https://minnesota8.org/); [CAPMN](https://www.capmn.org/founders); [UCAM](https://www.ucam.info/)).
- Cambodian Minnesotans work as health workers, teachers, journalists, scholars, legal advocates, business owners and public employees, and volunteers give UCAM more than 10,000 hours a year by its own count ([UCAM](https://www.ucam.info/); [S.R. 72](https://www.senate.mn/resolutions/display_resolution.php?ls=90&bill_type=SR&bill_number=0072&ss_number=0&ss_year=2017)).
- Community voices have pushed state and local leaders to see Cambodians as distinct from a single "Southeast Asian" or "Asian" category, from sister-city ties in Rochester to the 2025 commemoration, where Cambodian members sat on the advisory council ([Minnesota Humanities Center](https://www.mnhum.org/program/50th-anniversary-joint-commemoration/); [Pioneer Press via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/families-cambodians-lao-vietnamese-affected-230000000.html)).

## What could not be established

- **A firm population count.** Estimates run from about 8,300 (single race) to about 11,500 (Compass), all with wide margins; no source reconciles them. No breakdown of ethnic minorities within the community (for example Cham or Chinese-Cambodian) was found.
- **Occupations and industries** for Cambodian Minnesotans; union history specific to the community.
- **Current status of most businesses.** Only Kolap's own site was confirmed; Cheng Heng could not be confirmed open, and no food truck could be named from an opened source in 2025 or 2026. No Cambodian-owned grocery, professional firm, salon or chamber was found.
- **2026 dates at Watt Munisotaram** for New Year, Visakha Bochea, Pchum Ben or Kathen; the temple's events page was empty when checked. No Minnesota account of Pchum Ben or April 17 observance was found.
- **Christian congregations' size and history**, beyond the Crystal Nazarene church's address; the Post Bulletin's 2004 faith estimate is the only figure. Nothing on Cham Cambodians in Minnesota.
- **Disability-specific Minnesota evidence**: no study, school data or organization specific to Cambodian disability in Minnesota.
- **Elected officials** of Cambodian descent in Minnesota, and the current Cambodian seat on the Council on Asian Pacific Minnesotans.
- **Sports and Khmer-language media** based in Minnesota.
- **Blocked or unavailable when checked:** Carleton College's Religions in Minnesota exhibit on Watt Munisotaram (no response), KARE 11 (refused), PubMed Central and MDPI copies of a 2024 study of Cambodian refugee mental health (refused), the state's aging and disability resource directory (refused), Explore Minnesota (refused). Sahan Journal limited repeated requests, so its story on Cambodian food trucks could not be read. Facts that depended on these are left out.
