# Filipino in Minnesota

## 1. People and place

**How many.** Counts of Filipino Minnesotans differ a great deal, depending on who is counted. Minnesota Compass, using the American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024, puts the population at 26,559, give or take 2,422 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/filipino)). A June 2026 presentation by the State Demographic Center lists 28,388 Filipino Minnesotans in the 2024 one-year survey, sixth among Asian groups in the state after Hmong, Asian Indian, Chinese, Korean and Vietnamese Minnesotans ([State of Asian Pacific Minnesotans, June 2026](https://mn.gov/capm/assets/SusanBrower_StateOfAsianPacificMinnesotans_June2026_tcm1051-759113.pdf)). The State Demographic Center's own 2026 chartbook uses a much narrower rule: only people coded as Filipino by race, and not also part of another of its cultural groups. By that rule it counts 11,800, give or take 2,400, for 2019 to 2023 ([Economic Status of Minnesotans 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). Much of the gap comes from definitions: the chartbook places people who belong to more than one of its groups in a separate "multiple cultural groups" category. Any one number leaves some people out. Community groups use their own round figures. The Philippine Consulate General in Chicago spoke of "more than 24,000 Filipinos" in the state in November 2025 ([Philippine Consulate General in Chicago, Nov. 17, 2025](https://chicagopcg.com/pressrelease-details-7125.html)), the Philippine Center of Minnesota uses the same figure ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Pcm)), and a 2022 radio report gave 20,000 to 25,000 ([Power 104.7 FM, Oct. 27, 2022](https://www.power1047.fm/post/filipino-minnesotans-celebrate-rich-and-vibrant-culture)).

**Growth over time.** Older state proclamations show how small the community once was. A proclamation signed by Gov. Rudy Perpich counted "over 2,500" Philippine Minnesotans ([Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/sos/film/pdf/36719.pdf)), and Gov. Jesse Ventura's 2002 Philippine Day proclamation counted "more than 6,000" ([Minnesota Secretary of State, 2002](https://officialdocuments.sos.mn.gov/Files/GetDocument/38735)). These were proclamations, not surveys, so they show direction rather than exact size.

**Where people live.** Compass places 71.0 percent in the Twin Cities region and 29.0 percent in Greater Minnesota, each give or take 3.8 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/filipino)). Unlike some immigrant groups, Filipino Catholics in the Twin Cities "are not concentrated in one part" of the metro, according to Dodge Flores of the Filipino Catholic Community of Minnesota in 2019 ([The Catholic Spirit, Dec. 4, 2019](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/filipino-catholics-prepare-for-christmas-with-nine-day-mass-devotion/)). Community anchors are spread out to match: the Philippine Center of Minnesota is at 1380 Frost Avenue in Maplewood ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/fil-minnesotan-association)); the Cultural Society of Filipino Americans has been tied to Burnsville ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa)); a Filipino grocery and a Filipino restaurant share a corner in Mounds View ([Kusina](https://kusinaflavors.com/)); and Filipino food businesses have opened in Champlin and in downtown Rochester (section 4). The owner of the Mounds View grocery said in 2022 that customers drove in from North Dakota, Iowa, Duluth, Hibbing and Wisconsin ([Sahan Journal, Jan. 28, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/filipino-village-grocery-store-making-it-in-minnesota-small-business-entrepreneurship/)). In the Fargo-Moorhead area, a group called FilAmMinDak has drawn Filipino families from Minnesota and the two Dakotas to a summer Pista sa Nayon ([InForum](https://www.inforum.com/newsmd/pista-sa-nayon-celebration-in-full-swing-in-fargo)).

**Age, gender and birthplace.** In the Compass profile, 28.4 percent are 17 or younger and 6.5 percent are 65 or older. The largest single age band is 25 to 34, at 18.8 percent. Women and girls are 59.3 percent of the population, give or take 3.2 points. Foreign-born residents are 41.6 percent, give or take 3.9 points, and 3.0 percent lived outside the United States a year earlier ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/filipino)). In the narrower State Demographic Center group, the median age is 40, against 38 for all Minnesotans, and about three in four were born abroad (8,800 of 11,800). Of the foreign-born adults 16 to 64 in that group, about 2,700 of 7,400 had been in the U.S. 10 years or less ([Economic Status of Minnesotans 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). Taken together, the two sources describe a community with many immigrant adults, a U.S.-born generation, and ongoing new arrivals.

**How people came.** Minnesota's tie to the Philippines began with war, not migration. The 13th Minnesota Volunteer Infantry went to the Philippines in 1898 and fought in the Philippine-American War ([Philippine Study Group of Minnesota](https://www.crcworks.org/plaquefacts.pdf)). Early Filipino Minnesotans were few. A Filipino Minnesotan teacher said at a 2002 Capitol ceremony that Filipinos had lived in Minnesota "since 1907" ([Lita Malicsi remarks, Feb. 4, 2002](https://crcworks.org/malicsi.pdf)); that date comes from a speech, and no other source was found for it. In 1953, twelve Filipino students at the University of Minnesota formed the group that became the Fil-Minnesotan Association ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/fil-minnesotan-association)). MNopedia's history of the Cultural Society of Filipino Americans says most Filipinos who came to the U.S. in the 1960s and 1970s arrived on work visas, many as doctors, engineers and nurses, and that "very few came to Minnesota." Those who did faced the cold and a new culture; later arrivals were "more diverse in their career paths, economic status, and education" ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa)). Newer routes are visible too: in 2022, Willmar Public Schools hired teachers from the Philippines on J-1 exchange visas, which allow up to five years of teaching ([Sahan Journal, Sept. 12, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/news-partners/willmar-public-school-hire-teachers-philippines-nicaragua-teacher-shortage-minnesota/)). Other paths show up in individual stories: a Mounds View grocer came at 16 as an adoptee in 1997, and his wife, from the same Philippine community, moved to Minnesota in 2012 ([Sahan Journal, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/filipino-village-grocery-store-making-it-in-minnesota-small-business-entrepreneurship/)).

**Languages.** Among Filipino Minnesotans 5 and older, 58.3 percent speak only English at home and 41.7 percent speak another language. Some 10.8 percent, give or take 2.6 points, speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/filipino)). The State Demographic Center, cutting the same survey question differently, found only about 300 of 11,400 in its narrower group who did not speak English well or at all ([Economic Status of Minnesotans 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). The languages themselves are many. The founder of a St. Paul arts center described the Philippines as having "over 100 languages" ([Power 104.7 FM, 2022](https://www.power1047.fm/post/filipino-minnesotans-celebrate-rich-and-vibrant-culture)). MNopedia calls Tagalog the national language; the Cultural Society of Filipino Americans began offering free Tagalog classes in 2018, in part for U.S.-born children and spouses ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa)). Cebuano also appears in Minnesota business life: the Mounds View restaurant Kusina explains that its name means "Kitchen" in Cebuano ([Kusina](https://kusinaflavors.com/)). A nurse who belongs to a Ham Lake parish told The Catholic Spirit in 2026 that "a lot of families" from the island of Negros live in Minnesota ([The Catholic Spirit, April 16, 2026](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/father-valencia-leaves-legacy-in-medical-mission-trip-to-philippines/)). No Minnesota count by Philippine language was found.

## 2. All walks of life

No single picture fits. Figures below are from the Compass 2020 to 2024 profile, in 2024 dollars, unless noted ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/filipino)). Margins of error are wide because the group is small.

- **Comfortable and well-off households.** Median household income is $99,593, give or take $14,657. Nearly half of the 7,663 households, 48.9 percent, take in $100,000 or more.
- **Professionals and business owners.** Among adults 25 and older, 52.2 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more, including 12.2 percent with a graduate or professional degree. Visible careers include the chair of the Hennepin County Board ([Hennepin County](https://www.hennepincounty.gov/government/leadership/board-of-commissioners/district-2)); nurse leader Patrick Simon Soria, described as "the first Filipino American registered board member" of the Minnesota Board of Nursing ([Twin Cities Business](https://tcbmag.com/notable/dr-patrick-simon-s-soria/)); and owners of restaurants, groceries and an arts center (sections 4 and 8). Ramon Tan, who came from the Philippines, opened the United Noodles market in Minneapolis in 1972 ([Minnesota Daily, Feb. 6, 2020](https://mndaily.com/arts-entertainment/aegrocery/02/06/2020/snoadmin/)).
- **Middle-income and working families.** Owner-occupied homes are 57.6 percent of households, give or take 6.3 points. Among adults 18 to 64, 78.0 percent are employed, 81.0 percent of men and 76.1 percent of women. Another 12.9 percent of people live between the poverty line and twice that line, above poverty but not by much.
- **People in poverty and under strain.** Poverty is 8.2 percent, give or take 3.1 points. Among young adults 18 to 24, it is 19.0 percent, though that estimate carries a margin of 8.2 points. Some 8.0 percent have no health insurance, 10.5 percent of households have no vehicle, and 23.0 percent of households spend too much of their income on housing. Among adults 25 and older, 6.8 percent did not finish high school.

**Earnings tell a more modest story than household income.** The State Demographic Center's narrower Filipino group shows a median of $50,000 a year for full-time, year-round workers 16 to 64, in 2023 dollars and give or take $9,800. That is below the statewide median of $61,900 and far below the $106,200 for Asian Indian Minnesotans in the same table ([Economic Status of Minnesotans 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf); [State of Asian Pacific Minnesotans, June 2026](https://mn.gov/capm/assets/SusanBrower_StateOfAsianPacificMinnesotans_June2026_tcm1051-759113.pdf)). The same chartbook found 83 percent of that group's adults 16 to 64 in the labor force, about the same as the statewide 82 percent, and about 6 percent in poverty. Higher household income alongside lower individual earnings suggests households with more than one worker, but no Minnesota source was found that says so directly.

**Nationally, as background.** Pew Research Center put the median Filipino household income in the U.S. at $106,400 in 2023, while median personal earnings of Filipinos 16 and older were $48,000 ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-filipinos-in-the-u-s/)). The same split between strong household income and modest individual pay shows up in Minnesota.

**Over time.** Compass's earlier 2019 to 2023 profile, quoted on the existing page, reported a median household income of $95,275 in 2022 dollars and poverty of 7.3 percent. Because the survey years overlap and the margins are wide, the change to the 2020 to 2024 figures does not show a real trend.

## 3. Work and the economy

**Health care.** Nursing and medicine are the best-documented thread in Filipino Minnesotan working life, though not the whole story. MNopedia names doctors, engineers and nurses among the professionals who came in the 1960s and 1970s ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa)). A Philippine Nurses Association of Central Minnesota exists, and its president-elect, Patrick Simon Soria, sits on the Minnesota Board of Nursing ([Twin Cities Business](https://tcbmag.com/notable/dr-patrick-simon-s-soria/)). The Minnesota Nurses Association spotlighted Soria and Nina Angelie Lio-Soria in May 2025. Both described nursing as a family tradition, and both said they had been "underestimated" early in their careers by a stereotype that Asian American professionals are passive ([Minnesota Nurses Association, May 27, 2025](https://mnnurses.org/aanhpi-heritage-month-spotlight-patrick-ninas-nursing-journey/)). A nurse who worked 20 years in a Philippine hospital before coming to Minnesota in 2016 helped organize a 2025 medical mission from an Annandale parish to her home region ([The Catholic Spirit, April 16, 2026](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/father-valencia-leaves-legacy-in-medical-mission-trip-to-philippines/)). Among physicians, Dr. Tara Gustilo, who is Filipina, led the obstetrics and gynecology department at Hennepin Healthcare until she was removed as chair (section 9) ([Sahan Journal, March 21, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/health/tara-gustilo-ob-gyn-hennepin-healthcare-discrimination-lawsuit/)). No Minnesota count of Filipino nurses or doctors was found.

**Teaching and public life.** Sarah Lancaster, daughter of a Filipino immigrant mother, was the 2022 Minnesota Teacher of the Year and the first educator of Asian or Pacific Islander heritage to win it; she teaches first grade in Onamia ([Sahan Journal, May 2, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/education/sarah-lancaster-minnesota-teacher-of-year-onamia/)). The Willmar district's hiring of teachers from the Philippines shows a newer, shorter-term labor stream tied to shortages ([Sahan Journal, Sept. 12, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/news-partners/willmar-public-school-hire-teachers-philippines-nicaragua-teacher-shortage-minnesota/)).

**Work patterns.** The State Demographic Center found that of about 7,800 workers 16 to 64 in its narrower group, about 6,600 usually work 35 or more hours a week ([Economic Status of Minnesotans 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). Compass counts 13,992 Filipino workers 16 and older, 73.8 percent of whom drive to work ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/filipino)). No Minnesota breakdown of Filipino workers by industry or occupation was found, and no source documented a Filipino role in a Minnesota union beyond individual nurses' membership.

**Entrepreneurship.** Owners describe building businesses with their own savings and family labor. The Rott family renovated a vacant Mounds View building themselves and opened the Filipino Village Grocery Store in November 2020 "from our savings," with no bank loans; Herman Rott kept running his pool and recreation business on the side ([Sahan Journal, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/filipino-village-grocery-store-making-it-in-minnesota-small-business-entrepreneurship/)). Josie Schuette ran a weekly pop-up outside a Brooklyn Park church before opening Ku•ma•in in Champlin ([CCX Media](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-woman-opens-filipino-restaurant-kumain-in-champlin/)). Rochester's first Filipino restaurant opened in a skyway food court in November 2023 ([KTTC, Dec. 6, 2023](https://www.kttc.com/2023/12/06/new-southeast-asian-business-is-first-filipino-restaurant-rochester/)).

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

Filipino commercial life in Minnesota is small and scattered, with no district of its own. A Mpls.St.Paul Magazine critic wrote that the city's first Filipino-focused restaurant, Subo, opened downtown in 2009 and did not last more than a year, and welcomed Apoy in Kingfield, nine years later, as the next try ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/apoy-review/)). At a 2023 Macalester College panel, a panelist listed Kusina, Pinoy Fusion, Apoy, Ku•Ma•In and Phil Oriental Imports as newer places, and said Filipino restaurants had been a rarity before 2019 ([The Mac Weekly, Nov. 2023](https://themacweekly.com/2023/11/flavors-of-home-a-celebration-of-filipino-food-in-the-twin-cities/)).

Places confirmed operating in 2025 or 2026:
- **United Noodles**, 2015 E. 24th St., Minneapolis, and a Woodbury store. A pan-Asian market founded in 1972 by Ramon Tan, who came from the Philippines; a 2020 article named Annie Dingle and Eric Fung as co-owners ([Minnesota Daily, 2020](https://mndaily.com/arts-entertainment/aegrocery/02/06/2020/snoadmin/); [United Noodles](https://www.unitednoodles.com/)).
- **Lumpia City**, Minnesota State Fair. New in 2025 with an ube butter banana French toast lumpia ([Sahan Journal, 2025](https://sahanjournal.com/news/minnesota-state-fair-new-foods-reviews-2025/)), back in 2026 with a longanisa and cheese curd lumpia on Liggett Street ([FOX 9](https://www.fox9.com/news/minnesota-state-fairs-new-foods-2026)).
- **Philippine Center of Minnesota**, 1380 Frost Ave., Maplewood. A community center rather than a business, opened in 2009; it hosted a Philippine consulate outreach in November 2025 that delivered 217 passport, dual-citizenship and other services ([Philippine Consulate General in Chicago, Nov. 17, 2025](https://chicagopcg.com/pressrelease-details-7025.html)).

Places whose current operation could not be confirmed: Filipino Village Grocery Store and Kusina, a turo-turo (point-and-choose) restaurant, side by side at 2408 County Road I in Mounds View ([Sahan Journal, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/filipino-village-grocery-store-making-it-in-minnesota-small-business-entrepreneurship/); [Kusina](https://kusinaflavors.com/)); Ku•ma•in in Champlin ([CCX Media](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-woman-opens-filipino-restaurant-kumain-in-champlin/)); Taste of the Philippines in Rochester's First Avenue food court ([Downtown Rochester](https://www.downtownrochestermn.com/go/taste-of-the-philippines)); Apoy at 4301 Nicollet Ave. in Minneapolis ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/apoy-review/)); the Pinoy Fusion food truck ([Roaming Hunger](https://roaminghunger.com/pinoy-fusion/)); and Phil-Oriental at 789 University Ave. W. in St. Paul, which a food host in 2013 called the only source she knew for Filipino ingredients ([MPR News, Sept. 25, 2013](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2013/09/25/appetites-asian-markets)). The Macalester panel also named Inday's Kitchen food truck and Manila Sizzling Wok and Grill ([The Mac Weekly](https://themacweekly.com/2023/11/flavors-of-home-a-celebration-of-filipino-food-in-the-twin-cities/)).

Food also travels through events. Josie Schuette and realtor Vera Jones organized an inaugural Minnesota Lumpia Festival at Champlin's Mississippi Crossings, with a lumpia-eating contest ([CCX Media](https://ccxmedia.org/news/minnesota-lumpia-festival-happening-this-weekend-in-northwest-metro/)). CSFA's Philippine Day brings vendors of Filipino food, international shipping services and clothing ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa)). The Mounds View grocer said most stock comes from the Philippines through Chicago, and that about 90 percent of customers were Filipino ([Sahan Journal, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/filipino-village-grocery-store-making-it-in-minnesota-small-business-entrepreneurship/)).

No Filipino chamber of commerce, Filipino-run law or accounting firm, Filipino-language newspaper or radio program in Minnesota was found. The short business list reflects how few Filipino-run businesses are documented, not a narrow look.

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

**Catholic life is the most visible thread.** Pew Research Center's national survey found that three-quarters of Filipino Americans are Christian, "mostly Catholic," that regular attendance is more common among Filipino and Korean Americans than other large Asian American groups, and that 66 percent of Filipino Catholics keep an altar, shrine or religious symbol at home ([Pew Research Center, Oct. 11, 2023](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2023/10/11/religion-among-asian-americans/)). Those are national figures. No Minnesota survey of Filipino religious life was found.

In Minnesota, the Filipino Catholic Community of Minnesota, formed in 1995, has organized Simbang Gabi, the nine evening Masses before Christmas, at a different metro parish each night. In 2019 it also held a monthly Tagalog Mass at 3 p.m. on the last Sunday. Its 2019 schedule ran through Guardian Angels in Oakdale, St. Odilia in Shoreview, St. Jerome in Maplewood, St. Bonaventure in Bloomington, Pax Christi in Eden Prairie, St. Joseph in New Hope, St. Louis King of France in St. Paul, Epiphany in Coon Rapids and St. John Neumann in Eagan, ending with a 6 a.m. "Misa de Gallo." Its president then, Dodge Flores, said nightly attendance ran from 50 to 150, and that the Masses were mostly in English and open to all ([The Catholic Spirit, Dec. 4, 2019](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/filipino-catholics-prepare-for-christmas-with-nine-day-mass-devotion/)). In Rochester, the Co-Cathedral of St. John the Evangelist held a Simbang Gabi Mass with Bishop Robert Barron and a children's program on Dec. 16, 2022 ([Co-Cathedral of St. John the Evangelist](https://www.sj.org/updates-list/simbang-gabi-mass)). Devotion to the Santo Niño, the child Jesus, came to Guardian Angels in Oakdale in January 2011 with Sinulog dancers and about 400 people at what organizers called the 10th annual Sinulog Festival ([The Catholic Spirit, Jan. 19, 2011](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/filipinos-gather-to-celebrate-feast-of-santo-nino/)). No current schedule for either the Tagalog Mass or the Sinulog was found.

**A loss in 2025.** Father Victor Valencia, from the island of Negros, came to Minnesota in 2007, served as chaplain to the Filipino Catholic Community, and was pastor of St. Jerome in Maplewood and, for 11 years, of St. Ignatius in Annandale. He built a sister-parish tie between Annandale and Pacuan, Philippines, and died during a medical mission there in August 2025 ([The Catholic Spirit, April 16, 2026](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/father-valencia-leaves-legacy-in-medical-mission-trip-to-philippines/); [The Catholic Spirit, 2019](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/filipino-catholics-prepare-for-christmas-with-nine-day-mass-devotion/)). The 2026 article describes the Annandale parish carrying the mission on without him.

**Other faiths and none.** The Cultural Society's Pamana dance program includes a suite on the rituals of the Philippines' "Muslim minority" ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa)), and the co-founder of Indigenous Roots in St. Paul stresses the Philippines' many Indigenous nations ([Power 104.7 FM, 2022](https://www.power1047.fm/post/filipino-minnesotans-celebrate-rich-and-vibrant-culture)), but no Minnesota congregation of Filipino Muslims, Iglesia ni Cristo members or Filipino Protestants was found. No source described Filipino Minnesotans who practice no religion, though the national Pew figures imply a quarter are not Christian. Readers should not assume a Filipino family is Catholic, or that a Catholic family's faith is the center of its community life.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

- **The Christmas season.** In the sources found, this is the fullest part of the Minnesota calendar. Simbang Gabi runs in the days before Christmas (section 5). The Fil-Minnesotan Association started Paskong Pilipino, a Christmas program for children, in 1976 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/fil-minnesotan-association)). The Cultural Society began Pasko sa Nayon in 1978, with performances, a skit, games, a bazaar, lunch and Santa Claus, and its carolers sing at Masses and parties to raise money for causes ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa); [GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Csfamn)). In 2019 the society's carolers sang at 5:30 a.m. before the closing Misa de Gallo in Oakdale ([The Catholic Spirit, 2019](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/filipino-catholics-prepare-for-christmas-with-nine-day-mass-devotion/)). Christmas Day itself, Dec. 25, is a regular holiday in the Philippines ([Proclamation No. 1006](https://lawphil.net/executive/proc/proc2025/proc_1006_2025.html)). No 2025 or 2026 dates for Paskong Pilipino or Pasko sa Nayon were found.
- **Feast of the Santo Niño and Sinulog.** Celebrated in the Visayas on the third Sunday of January; Minnesota's 2011 event was the 10th held here ([The Catholic Spirit, 2011](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/filipinos-gather-to-celebrate-feast-of-santo-nino/)).
- **Lent, Holy Week and Easter.** Ash Wednesday fell on Feb. 18 and Easter on April 5 in 2026 ([USCCB 2026 calendar](https://www.usccb.org/resources/2026cal.pdf)); Good Friday, April 3, and Maundy Thursday were regular holidays in the Philippines that year ([Proclamation No. 1006](https://lawphil.net/executive/proc/proc2025/proc_1006_2025.html)). No Minnesota Filipino Holy Week event was found.
- **Philippine Day.** The Cultural Society's largest public event, with dance, song, fashion, martial arts and vendors, at Aldrich Arena in Maplewood. MNopedia dates the first Philippine Day to 1988 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa)); Gov. Ventura's proclamation says the society had sponsored it "since 1987" and set the 2002 date as March 24 ([Minnesota Secretary of State](https://officialdocuments.sos.mn.gov/Files/GetDocument/38735)). No announcement for 2026 or 2027 was found.
- **Philippine Independence Day, June 12.** The Philippines' national day ([Proclamation No. 1006](https://lawphil.net/executive/proc/proc2025/proc_1006_2025.html)). No 2026 Minnesota observance was found. In 2026 the Philippine consulate invited Minnesota to join events for the 80th anniversary of Philippines–U.S. diplomatic relations ([Philippine Consulate General in Chicago, Nov. 17, 2025](https://chicagopcg.com/pressrelease-details-7125.html)).
- **Filipino American History Month, October.** A St. Paul arts center marked it in 2022 with "Basa, Kanta at Sayaw" (read, sing and dance), with readings in Tagalog and English and singing by lolas, or grandmothers ([Power 104.7 FM, Oct. 27, 2022](https://www.power1047.fm/post/filipino-minnesotans-celebrate-rich-and-vibrant-culture)). The Filipino Association at Macalester held a panel of Filipino chefs on Oct. 19, 2023 ([The Mac Weekly](https://themacweekly.com/2023/11/flavors-of-home-a-celebration-of-filipino-food-in-the-twin-cities/)).
- **EDSA People Power anniversary, Feb. 25.** Marks the 1986 uprising; the 40th anniversary in 2026 was a working day in the Philippines ([Proclamation No. 1006](https://lawphil.net/executive/proc/proc2025/proc_1006_2025.html)). The Philippine Study Group of Minnesota describes itself as the last remaining Minnesota group formed in response to the Marcos dictatorship ([Walker Art Center](https://www.walkerart.org/whats-on/buhay-na-buhay-an-evening-curated-by-the-mn-filipinx-community/)), but no Minnesota EDSA event was found.
- **Rizal Day, Dec. 30, and All Saints' and All Souls' Days, Nov. 1 and 2.** Both are holidays in the Philippines ([Proclamation No. 1006](https://lawphil.net/executive/proc/proc2025/proc_1006_2025.html)). No Minnesota observance was found.
- **Pamana.** The Cultural Society's premier dance showcase, held once every four years, moved to the Ames Center in Burnsville in 2009 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa)). The next date was not found.
- **Multicultural festivals.** The Cultural Society first took part in the Festival of Nations in 1972 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa)). The festival returned in 2026 as the International Festival of Minnesota, April 10 and 11 at Saint Paul RiverCentre ([IFest-MN](https://www.ifestmn.org/); [Hoodline, April 6, 2026](https://hoodline.com/2026/04/st-paul-s-global-party-roars-back-after-seven-year-timeout/)); whether a Philippine program took part was not confirmed. Other one-time events include the Minnesota Lumpia Festival in Champlin ([CCX Media](https://ccxmedia.org/news/minnesota-lumpia-festival-happening-this-weekend-in-northwest-metro/)) and "Buhay Na Buhay," a free evening at the Walker Art Center on July 27, 2023, curated by Minnesota Filipino groups ([Walker Art Center](https://www.walkerart.org/whats-on/buhay-na-buhay-an-evening-curated-by-the-mn-filipinx-community/)).

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Households.** Compass counts 7,663 Filipino households averaging 2.72 people. Married couples with children under 18 are 20.9 percent of households and married couples without children at home 21.6 percent; 23.9 percent are people living alone, 11.9 percent unmarried couples and 10.9 percent other family households ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/filipino)). Living alone is about as common as any family arrangement, which runs against a picture of only large, multigenerational homes.

**Mixed families.** Many public stories involve families that join Filipino and other heritages. A Cultural Society dancer who is from the Philippines and married to an American taught her two children Filipino folk dance from age 6 ([Power 104.7 FM, 2022](https://www.power1047.fm/post/filipino-minnesotans-celebrate-rich-and-vibrant-culture)); Indigenous Roots was co-founded by Mary Anne Ligeralde Quiroz, who is Filipino, and her partner Sergio Cenoch; the two also lead a Mexican/Nahua/Aztec dance group ([Propel Nonprofits](https://propelnonprofits.org/case-studies/indigenous-roots/); [Power 104.7 FM](https://www.power1047.fm/post/filipino-minnesotans-celebrate-rich-and-vibrant-culture)). The large gap between Compass's broad count and the State Demographic Center's Filipino-only count (section 1) suggests that mixed heritage is common, though no source measured it directly.

**Raising children between cultures.** MNopedia says many second-generation Filipino Minnesotans did not learn their parents' languages, leaving a culture "lost in translation," and that parents sought to recreate a "village" to raise their children ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa)). Responses have included the Fil-Minnesotan Association's 1990s seminars on "Relationships in a Bi-cultural Family," run with the University of Minnesota, and a youth group that grew out of them ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/fil-minnesotan-association)); and the Cultural Society's free children's camp (since 2005), teen camp, family campout (since 1980), Children's Winterfest (since 2000) and Tagalog classes ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa); [GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Csfamn)).

**Aging.** Compass puts 6.5 percent of Filipino Minnesotans at 65 or older ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/filipino)). The Fil-Minnesotan Seniors affiliated with the Fil-Minnesotan Association in the 1990s ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/fil-minnesotan-association)), and the Philippine Center's founders described services for seniors as one of its purposes ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Pcm)). The 2022 Indigenous Roots event featured singing by lolas ([Power 104.7 FM](https://www.power1047.fm/post/filipino-minnesotans-celebrate-rich-and-vibrant-culture)). No study of Filipino elders in Minnesota was found.

**Food.** Minnesota sources describe adobo, a pork or chicken dish in vinegar, garlic and soy sauce; dinuguan, a pork stew in a dark gravy made with pig's blood, which a Fargo-area organizer joked is called "chocolate meat"; lumpia; pancit noodles; lechon kawali; sisig; halo-halo; cassava cake; and leche flan ([InForum](https://www.inforum.com/newsmd/pista-sa-nayon-celebration-in-full-swing-in-fargo); [KTTC, 2023](https://www.kttc.com/2023/12/06/new-southeast-asian-business-is-first-filipino-restaurant-rochester/); [Mpls.St.Paul Magazine](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/apoy-review/); [Downtown Rochester](https://www.downtownrochestermn.com/go/taste-of-the-philippines)). The Mounds View grocers stock malunggay (moringa), puto cheese, pan de sal, pichi-pichi and chorizo de Cebu, and said Filipinos will "drive and get groceries for a month" ([Sahan Journal, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/business-work/filipino-village-grocery-store-making-it-in-minnesota-small-business-entrepreneurship/)). The chef at Kusina said she tells diners, "you're coming to a home" ([The Mac Weekly, 2023](https://themacweekly.com/2023/11/flavors-of-home-a-celebration-of-filipino-food-in-the-twin-cities/)).

**Dress.** Formal dress appears mainly on stage and at celebrations. The Pamana show includes Maria Clara dresses with flared sleeves from the Spanish colonial era, and woven clothing of the northern mountain peoples ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa)). No source described everyday dress among Filipino Minnesotans.

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Stage and dance.** Filipino Minnesotans have built theater for decades. In 1966 the Fil-Minnesotan Association staged "Portrait of the Artist as Filipino" to raise money for a Filipiniana collection at the University of Minnesota library; it later produced the Filipiniana series of original musicals and, in 1995, a large production at the World Theater in St. Paul ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/fil-minnesotan-association)). The Cultural Society's Pamana Dance Troupe, first performed in 1981, presents four suites: the Cordillera of the northern mountains, the Sarimanok drawn from Muslim royal and war dances, the Spanish-era Abanico, and the rural Sa Nayon, including the Maglalatik coconut-shell dance ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa)). Theater Mu, which American Theatre calls the Midwest's largest Asian American theater company, was co-founded in 1992 by a group that included Diane Espaldon, a Filipina professional ([MNopedia, Theater Mu](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/theater-mu); [American Theatre](https://www.americantheatre.org/2025/06/02/theater-mu-names-fran-de-leon-new-artistic-director/)). Fran de Leon became its artistic director in 2025; announcing the choice, managing director Anh Thu T. Pham said, "Our own experiences are not only in the Filipino or Vietnamese communities" ([American Theatre, June 2, 2025](https://www.americantheatre.org/2025/06/02/theater-mu-names-fran-de-leon-new-artistic-director/)).

**Music.** Isabella Dawis, a Filipina American writer and performer from Minneapolis, has won the Kleban Prize and the Fred Ebb Award and held a 2023 to 2025 Jerome Hill Artist Fellowship. With Paolo Debuque, a Filipino and Chinese American conductor, and pianist Walter Tambo, she gave a free "Music from the Philippines" concert at St. Paul's Landmark Center on Feb. 13, 2025 ([Landmark Center](https://www.landmarkcenter.org/event/courtroom-concert-thousand-tongues-music-from-the-philippines-isabella-dawis-soprano-paolo-debuque-tenor-walter-tambo-piano/); [Minnesota Opera](https://mnopera.org/biography/isabella-dawis/)).

**Visual art and cultural centers.** The Walker Art Center mounted the first retrospective of Pacita Abad, a painter who left the Philippines for the U.S. in the 1970s to escape political persecution, in 2023 ([MPR News, April 14, 2023](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/04/14/arts-briefs-walker-art-center-unveils-pacita-abad-exhibit)) and invited Minnesota Filipino groups to curate a free evening around it ([Walker Art Center](https://www.walkerart.org/whats-on/buhay-na-buhay-an-evening-curated-by-the-mn-filipinx-community/)). Indigenous Roots on St. Paul's East Side, co-led by Mary Anne Ligeralde Quiroz, has shown illustrations of the Philippines' regional dances ([Propel Nonprofits](https://propelnonprofits.org/case-studies/indigenous-roots/); [Power 104.7 FM](https://www.power1047.fm/post/filipino-minnesotans-celebrate-rich-and-vibrant-culture)). The Minnesota Historical Society holds a 2010 to 2011 Filipino Community Oral History Project and Fil-Minnesotan Association papers ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/fil-minnesotan-association)).

**Sports.** The Fil-Minnesotan Association launched an annual basketball tournament in 2007, and photographs show it organizing tennis tournaments by 1993 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/fil-minnesotan-association)). Matt Dumba, a Filipino Canadian defenseman, played for the Minnesota Wild and in 2020 helped found the Hockey Diversity Alliance; he told NBC he was often the only player of color on his youth teams in Calgary ([NBC News, 2020](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/asian-america/filipino-canadian-nhl-player-speaks-out-against-racism-hockey-n1232361)).

**Media.** No Filipino newspaper, radio show or online outlet based in Minnesota was found. Community news reaches Minnesotans through organizations, parishes and the Philippine consulate's online channels ([Philippine Consulate General in Chicago](https://chicagopcg.com/pressrelease-details-7025.html)).

## 9. Civic and political life

**Elected office.** A Hennepin County commissioner elected in 2018, described by the county as "the first Filipino-American elected in Minnesota," and a colleague were the first people of color elected to the county board since 1852; her colleagues have chosen her as board chair each year since 2023 ([Hennepin County](https://www.hennepincounty.gov/government/leadership/board-of-commissioners/district-2)). She was the DFL-endorsed incumbent in the 2026 District 2 race, with the general election on Nov. 3, 2026 ([Naomi Kritzer's election guide, July 26, 2026](https://naomikritzer.com/tag/makeda-zulu/)).

**Organizations.** The Walker Art Center's 2023 partner list shows the range: the Cultural Society of Filipino Americans; the Fil-Minnesotan Association, which said it served "over 14,000" Filipino Minnesotans; the Philippine Study Group of Minnesota; Filipinx for Immigrant Rights and Racial Justice Minnesota, founded in 2016 in response to "the rise in anti-immigrant rhetoric"; the Philippine Student Association of Minnesota; University of the Philippines Alumni in Minnesota; Indigenous Roots; and a San Francisco-based Filipina women's network ([Walker Art Center](https://www.walkerart.org/whats-on/buhay-na-buhay-an-evening-curated-by-the-mn-filipinx-community/)). The Fil-Minnesotan Association, led in 2025 by Dodge Flores, still works with the Philippine consulate on outreach ([Philippine Consulate General in Chicago](https://chicagopcg.com/pressrelease-details-7025.html)). The Walker's list dates the Cultural Society's founding to 1971, while MNopedia gives Sept. 17, 1972. The Cultural Society has given to hometown and student groups, including Negrenses del Oriental, and earmarked about $100,000 for disaster recovery in 2019 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa)). A Philippine Nurses Association of Central Minnesota is active (section 3).

**Correcting the Capitol plaque.** A plaque in the Capitol rotunda honored the 13th Minnesota for battling "to free the oppressed peoples" of the Philippines. The Philippine Study Group of Minnesota argued that the regiment's battles were against Filipinos fighting for their own republic ([Philippine Study Group of Minnesota](https://www.crcworks.org/plaquefacts.pdf)). A 1999 bill directed the Capitol Area Architectural and Planning Board and the Minnesota Historical Society to work with the group on a corrective plaque and proposed $10,000 for it ([S.F. 603, 1999](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/bills/81/1999/0/SF/603/versions/latest)). The new plaque was unveiled on Feb. 4, 2002, with the Philippine ambassador present ([Lita Malicsi remarks](https://crcworks.org/malicsi.pdf)).

**Ties to the Philippines.** The Philippine consulate in Chicago serves Minnesota. In November 2025 the consul general met Minnesota's secretary of state to discuss overseas voter registration for Philippine elections, which began that December ([Philippine Consulate General in Chicago, Nov. 17, 2025](https://chicagopcg.com/pressrelease-details-7125.html)).

**Disagreement inside the community.** Filipino Minnesotans do not share one politics. FIRM was founded in response to anti-immigrant rhetoric (above). In a separate debate, Dr. Tara Gustilo sued Hennepin Healthcare in 2022, saying she was demoted for "colorblind" views and opposition to what she called "racially segregated" care; Hennepin Healthcare's lawyer rejected that description of its practices ([Sahan Journal, March 21, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/health/tara-gustilo-ob-gyn-hennepin-healthcare-discrimination-lawsuit/)). The final outcome of that case could not be confirmed.

## 10. Education

**Attainment.** Among Filipino Minnesotans 25 and older, 93.2 percent finished high school and 52.2 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/filipino)). In the State Demographic Center's narrower group, about 4,900 of 8,600 adults 25 to 64, or 57 percent, hold a bachelor's degree or more ([Economic Status of Minnesotans 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). Still, 6.8 percent of adults 25 and older did not finish high school ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/filipino)).

**Students and campuses.** The oldest Filipino organization found in Minnesota sources began with University of Minnesota students in 1953 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/fil-minnesotan-association)). Today the university's Asian American Studies program lists a Philippine Student Association among related groups ([University of Minnesota](https://cla.umn.edu/asianamerican/undergraduate/organizations)), and Macalester College has a Filipino Association that hosts October programs ([The Mac Weekly](https://themacweekly.com/2023/11/flavors-of-home-a-celebration-of-filipino-food-in-the-twin-cities/)). The Cultural Society has given to the Philippine Students Association at the University of Minnesota and to a group called Philippine Scholars ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa)).

**Language and heritage learning.** No Minnesota public school Tagalog or Filipino language program was found. Community classes fill the gap: the Cultural Society's free Tagalog classes since 2018 and its children's and teen camps ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa)), and the Fil-Minnesotan Association's "Akademy," one of its four pillars since 2008 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/fil-minnesotan-association)).

**Educators.** Sarah Lancaster, 2022 Minnesota Teacher of the Year, used grants to stock her school's library with books featuring Asian American and Pacific Islander characters, saying children need "to see themselves in literature" ([Sahan Journal, May 2, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/education/sarah-lancaster-minnesota-teacher-of-year-onamia/)). Teachers from the Philippines filled vacancies in Willmar in 2022; a district official said without them there would have been five more openings ([Sahan Journal, Sept. 12, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/news-partners/willmar-public-school-hire-teachers-philippines-nicaragua-teacher-shortage-minnesota/)).

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Coverage.** Some 8.0 percent of Filipino Minnesotans have no health insurance, give or take 3.0 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/filipino)).

**Caregivers as community.** Filipino health workers are prominent in community life (section 3), and community groups have long organized care. The Fil-Minnesotan Association held free health screening clinics for "the underserved" in the late 1990s and supported a children's cardiac center in the Philippines in 1992 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/fil-minnesotan-association)). The Philippine Center's founders listed preventive services for new immigrants, seniors and youth among its aims ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Pcm)). Minnesota parishes and nurses have run medical missions to the Philippines, most recently from Annandale to Pacuan in 2025 and to Bayawan City in March 2026 ([The Catholic Spirit, April 16, 2026](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/father-valencia-leaves-legacy-in-medical-mission-trip-to-philippines/)).

**Culture in care.** The two Filipino American nurses spotlighted by the Minnesota Nurses Association named malasakit, deep empathy and concern, as a value they bring to patients, and said misunderstandings, language gaps and "lack of representation" create real barriers for patients ([Minnesota Nurses Association, 2025](https://mnnurses.org/aanhpi-heritage-month-spotlight-patrick-ninas-nursing-journey/)). Soria has served on the Minnesota Equitable Health Care Task Force ([Twin Cities Business](https://tcbmag.com/notable/dr-patrick-simon-s-soria/)).

**What is missing.** Minnesota Compass argues that grouping all Asian Minnesotans together hides differences between communities ([Minnesota Compass, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-asian-communities)). No Minnesota Department of Health report with Filipino-specific health outcomes was found, and no Filipino-focused clinic was found. No Minnesota source documented traditional healing practices among Filipino Minnesotans.

## 12. Disability

**What the numbers show.** Compass reports that 7.6 percent of Filipino Minnesotans have a disability, give or take 2.1 points, and 6.3 percent of those under 65, give or take 2.3 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/filipino)). The State Demographic Center's 2026 chartbook includes a disability table for adults 18 to 64, but the Filipino figure is not reported there (marked "NA") ([Economic Status of Minnesotans 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)).

**Questions the evidence raises.**
- Is the Compass rate a true picture, or do some families answer the survey's disability questions differently than others? With margins this wide, and with the state's own table suppressed, no Minnesota source answered this.
- About one in ten speaks English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/filipino)). How often do Filipino families meet disability services in English, and do they ask for interpreters in Tagalog, Cebuano or other languages? No Minnesota data was found.
- Health care is a prominent field in community stories (section 3). Does professional familiarity with disability systems help families reach services, or does it raise expectations that relatives will provide care themselves? This is an open question; no source was found either way.
- Who in the family names a disability, and who decides whether to seek a label for a child or elder? No Minnesota study of Filipino families was found.

**Practical signals.** The Philippine consulate's Minnesota outreach allows one companion for "persons with disability," seniors and minors ([Philippine Consulate General in Chicago](https://chicagopcg.com/outreachmn.html)). The 2023 Walker evening curated by Filipino groups included a quiet "sensory calming haven" and accessible seating ([Walker Art Center](https://www.walkerart.org/whats-on/buhay-na-buhay-an-evening-curated-by-the-mn-filipinx-community/)).

**Organizations.** No Minnesota disability organization led by or focused on Filipino Minnesotans was found, and no University of Minnesota or Institute on Community Integration study of Filipino families was located. Minnesota-specific Filipino disability evidence is thin, and this section reports that plainly rather than filling it with national material.

## 13. What is changing now

- **Growth and mixing.** The broad count has risen from "over 2,500" in a Perpich-era proclamation and "more than 6,000" in 2002 to 26,559 in the 2020 to 2024 survey and 28,388 in the 2024 one-year estimate ([Legislative Reference Library](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/sos/film/pdf/36719.pdf); [Minnesota Secretary of State](https://officialdocuments.sos.mn.gov/Files/GetDocument/38735); [Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/filipino); [State of Asian Pacific Minnesotans](https://mn.gov/capm/assets/SusanBrower_StateOfAsianPacificMinnesotans_June2026_tcm1051-759113.pdf)). Because these figures use different definitions, the exact rate of growth is uncertain.
- **Newer arrivals on temporary visas.** Teachers on J-1 visas in Willmar are expected to go home after up to five years ([Sahan Journal, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/news-partners/willmar-public-school-hire-teachers-philippines-nicaragua-teacher-shortage-minnesota/)). Most foreign-born Filipino adults in the state demographer's group have been in the U.S. more than 10 years ([Economic Status of Minnesotans 2026](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)).
- **Food going public.** From a single St. Paul grocery a food host knew of in 2013 to Lumpia City at the State Fair in 2025 and 2026, Filipino food has become more visible ([MPR News, 2013](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2013/09/25/appetites-asian-markets); [FOX 9](https://www.fox9.com/news/minnesota-state-fairs-new-foods-2026)). Several restaurants opened in the early 2020s, but their current status could not all be confirmed (section 4).
- **Leadership turnover.** The death of Father Valencia in August 2025 took away the Filipino Catholic Community's longtime chaplain ([The Catholic Spirit, April 16, 2026](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/father-valencia-leaves-legacy-in-medical-mission-trip-to-philippines/)). The Fil-Minnesotan Association remained active with the consulate in late 2025 ([Philippine Consulate General in Chicago](https://chicagopcg.com/pressrelease-details-7025.html)), but its own website could not be reached in October 2026.
- **Immigration enforcement.** The Department of Homeland Security launched "Operation Metro Surge" in the Twin Cities on Dec. 1, 2025; DHS said in its first announcement that those arrested came from Somalia, Mexico and El Salvador ([FOX 9, Dec. 4, 2025](https://www.fox9.com/news/ice-arrests-twin-cities-dec-4-2025)), and by mid-January it said more than 3,000 people had been detained ([KSTP](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/what-does-a-scaled-back-ice-operation-look-like/)). No source found reported on how the operation affected Filipino Minnesotans in particular, and this profile does not guess.
- **Election year.** The first Filipino American elected in Minnesota, a Hennepin County commissioner, was on the Nov. 3, 2026 ballot for another term ([Naomi Kritzer's election guide](https://naomikritzer.com/tag/makeda-zulu/)).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **A deep bench of caregivers and professionals.** Nurses, doctors and teachers of Filipino heritage serve hospitals, clinics and classrooms from the Twin Cities to Onamia and Willmar, and one chairs the Hennepin County Board ([Minnesota Nurses Association](https://mnnurses.org/aanhpi-heritage-month-spotlight-patrick-ninas-nursing-journey/); [Sahan Journal](https://sahanjournal.com/education/sarah-lancaster-minnesota-teacher-of-year-onamia/); [Hennepin County](https://www.hennepincounty.gov/government/leadership/board-of-commissioners/district-2)).
- **Organizations that last.** The Fil-Minnesotan Association (1953) and the Cultural Society of Filipino Americans (1972) have run for more than half a century, building a community center and decades of dance, theater, Christmas and youth programs ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/fil-minnesotan-association); [MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa)).
- **Correcting the record.** The Philippine Study Group of Minnesota persuaded the state to add a corrective plaque to its own Capitol ([S.F. 603](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/bills/81/1999/0/SF/603/versions/latest); [Lita Malicsi remarks](https://crcworks.org/malicsi.pdf)).
- **Generosity across borders.** Disaster funds, medical missions and sister parishes link Minnesota towns to the Philippines ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa); [The Catholic Spirit, 2026](https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/father-valencia-leaves-legacy-in-medical-mission-trip-to-philippines/)).
- **Shared arts.** Theater Mu's founding, the Pamana dancers, a Minneapolis writer and singer of national note, and community-curated nights at the Walker have enriched Minnesota's cultural life well beyond the community ([MNopedia, Theater Mu](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/theater-mu); [Walker Art Center](https://www.walkerart.org/whats-on/buhay-na-buhay-an-evening-curated-by-the-mn-filipinx-community/)).

Reports that Kalsada, a St. Paul Filipino restaurant, had closed appeared only on pages that blocked access. No Filipino law, accounting, insurance, tax, travel or salon business, chamber of commerce or media outlet was found.

- **Work by industry, and nurses by number.** No Minnesota breakdown of Filipino workers by occupation was found.
- **Health and disability.** No Filipino-specific Minnesota health or disability report, clinic or advocacy organization was found. The state's disability table for this group is suppressed.
