# Corrections to the existing Filipino Minnesota page
Page: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/filipino

## Outdated figures

The page quotes Minnesota Compass for 2019 to 2023 throughout. Compass now shows 2020 to 2024 figures (https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/filipino). The older vintage is no longer shown, so the page's numbers could not be rechecked against it; the current figures are:

| About 25,203 people | 26,559 (give or take 2,422) |
| 73.5% Twin Cities, 26.5% Greater Minnesota | 71.0% and 29.0% (each give or take 3.8) |
| 29.9% age 17 or younger; 5.1% 65+ | 28.4%; 6.5% |
| Foreign-born 38.4% | 41.6% |
| English only 59.6%; other language 40.4%; LEP 8.4% | 58.3%; 41.7%; 10.8% speak English less than "very well" |

The page also presents Compass as the only count. Other official counts differ widely: 28,388 in the 2024 one-year survey (State Demographic Center presentation, June 2026, https://mn.gov/capm/assets/SusanBrower_StateOfAsianPacificMinnesotans_June2026_tcm1051-759113.pdf) and 11,800 for people coded only as Filipino in 2019 to 2023 (Economic Status of Minnesotans 2026, https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf). Both. The page should note that the count depends on whether people of mixed heritage are included.

## Errors

- **"California, Hawaii, New York, and Illinois hold large U.S. communities."** Pew Research Center's fact sheet names California (1.6 million), Hawaii (310,000), Texas (220,000), Florida (180,000) and Nevada (180,000) as the states with the largest Filipino populations; New York and Illinois are not in that list. https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-filipinos-in-the-u-s/.
- **Repeated sections.** Several passages (for example "Origins and the Founding of a People," "People, place, and perspective" and "Family") appear more than once on the page, word for word, perhaps because the same text is reused in the learning sections. This is an editing matter, not a factual error.

## Claims that could not be supported from an opened source

- **"Health systems placed workers in towns that still hold families"** and **"58.6 percent female, which is a labor fact in a nursing and care stream."** No Minnesota source was found that explains the Greater Minnesota share or the gender balance by health-care recruitment. Both are stated as causes without evidence.
- **"Iglesia ni Cristo .. also live here."** No Minnesota Iglesia ni Cristo congregation, or Filipino Protestant or Muslim congregation, was found. The claim may be true but is unsupported.
- **"Cebuano, Ilocano, and others live in this state."** Cebuano is supported indirectly (a Mounds View restaurant names itself in Cebuano, https://kusinaflavors.com/, and a 2026 article mentions many families from Negros, https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/news/local-news/father-valencia-leaves-legacy-in-medical-mission-trip-to-philippines/). No Minnesota source mentioning Ilocano speakers was found.
- **"CECLC is a door, not the whole community."** The page never explains what CECLC is. No Filipino organization by that name was found.
- **"4 July 1946 is the U.S. recognition date"** and **"Hart-Celler (1965)."** Not checked against an opened source in this review.
- **"Parish fiestas and hometown associations will not be on your state calendar."** Minnesota does have recurring, public Filipino events (Philippine Day since 1987 or 1988, Simbang Gabi, Pasko sa Nayon, Paskong Pilipino), and hometown groups such as Negrenses del Oriental have received support from the Cultural Society of Filipino Americans (https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/cultural-society-filipino-americans-minnesota-csfa). These can be put on a calendar when dates are announced.

## Missing from the page

The page names no Minnesota Filipino organization, person or place. Opened sources document the Fil-Minnesotan Association (1953) and its Philippine Center in Maplewood, the Cultural Society of Filipino Americans (1972), the Filipino Catholic Community of Minnesota (1995), the Philippine Study Group of Minnesota and its 2002 corrective Capitol plaque, Hennepin County Board Chair Irene Fernando, and the 2022 Minnesota Teacher of the Year, Sarah Lancaster. See profile.md.

## Claims checked and supported

- Martial law declared in 1972: MNopedia's CSFA entry cites Proclamation No. 1081 of September 1972.
- People Power / EDSA in February 1986: the Philippine 2026 holiday proclamation marks Feb. 25, 2026 as the 40th anniversary (https://lawphil.net/executive/proc/proc2025/proc_1006_2025.html).
- June 12 as Philippine Independence Day: same proclamation.
- Spanish surnames and a Spanish colonial era: MNopedia's CSFA entry describes the Spanish colonial era (1521 to 1898) in the Pamana dance program.
- Catholic Christianity as the largest faith: supported nationally by Pew (three-quarters Christian, mostly Catholic, https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2023/10/11/religion-among-asian-americans/); no Minnesota survey exists.
