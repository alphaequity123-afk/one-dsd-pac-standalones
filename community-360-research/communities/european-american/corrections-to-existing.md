# Corrections to the existing European American Minnesota page

Page: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/european-american

## Errors, outdated claims and claims that should change

1. **Claim:** The Minnesota State Demographic Center, using 2018 ACS data, counts German at about 1,801,700; Norwegian 810,300; Irish 516,500; Swedish 429,800; English 290,200; Polish 248,000.
   **Source says:** The State Demographic Center's mn.gov pages blocked access, so the 2018 figures could not be checked. The newer 2024 ACS one-year estimates differ a good deal: German 1,608,999 (give or take 22,427), Norwegian 662,072 (12,215), Irish 569,161 (14,439), English 360,916 (11,988), Swedish 354,188 (10,217), Polish 214,308 (8,126). English and Swedish are now about tied, within each other's margins of error, and Irish has grown while German and Norwegian have fallen. The page should use dated 2024 figures and note that 1.29 million answers were unclassified or not reported and about 206,000 people wrote "American."
   **Link:** https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B04006&geo_ids=04000US27 .
2. **Claim:** "The Star Tribune reported in 2020 that about 55 percent reported German ancestors and about 45 percent reported ancestors from Norway/Denmark/Sweden."
   **Source says:** The Star Tribune chart (by MaryJo Webster, 2018 ACS) says "About 45% of Minnesotans who reported multiple ancestries identified as Scandinavians.. but the majority listed German ancestry." The 45 percent applies only to people who reported more than one ancestry, not to all Minnesotans, and the chart text opened does not give 55 percent.
   **Link:** https://datawrapper.dwcdn.net/KT1Mq/3 .
3. **Claim:** "In May 2026 Sons of Norway announced that it was joining BetterLife.. after a unanimous vote of its international board, and that it would continue as Sons of Norway within BetterLife."
   **Source says:** BetterLife's May 2026 press release describes a "definitive agreement" that is "subject to regulatory approval and customary closing conditions," expected to close "later this summer," with full integration by year-end. It does not mention a board vote. No source opened confirmed that the deal closed. The page should say "announced an agreement" and not state that the merger is complete.
   **Link:** https://digital-release.wowktv.com/business/press-releases/cision/20260506CG52468/betterlife-announces-definitive-agreement-to-partner-with-sons-of-norway-extending-its-presence-to-more-than-400-communities .
4. **Claim:** The page profiles Fiorello La Guardia, Barbara Mikulski, Greta Garbo, Knute Rockne and Maria von Trapp as figures for this Minnesota page.
   **Source says:** The page's own text places these five in New York, Baltimore, Stockholm and Hollywood, Chicago and Notre Dame, and Austria. It gives no Minnesota connection for any of them. Minnesota figures with opened, Minnesota-based sources include Gov. Knute Nelson (born in Voss, Norway), Gov. Rudy Perpich (son of a Croatian immigrant), Archbishop John Ireland, conductor Stanisław Skrowaczewski and the founders of The Arc.
   **Links:** https://www.lrl.mn.gov/mngov/govbio?govid=27 ; https://www.duluthnewstribune.com/news/local/remembering-rudy-perpich-the-governor-who-turned-outstate-into-greater-minnesota ; https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/polish-immigration-minnesota .
5. **Claim:** In the Rudy Perpich profile, Perpich "earned a dental degree from Marquette University in 1954 and returned to Hibbing to practice."
   **Source says:** The Legislative Reference Library confirms a Hibbing dental office from 1954 to 1974, born 1928 at Carson Lake. Marquette was not mentioned in the sources opened. The Duluth News Tribune says his father Anton was a first-generation Croatian immigrant and his mother second-generation, which the page could add.
   **Links:** https://www.lrl.mn.gov/mngov/govbio?govid=41 ; https://www.duluthnewstribune.com/news/local/remembering-rudy-perpich-the-governor-who-turned-outstate-into-greater-minnesota .
6. **Claim:** St. Mary's Greek Orthodox Church hosts Taste of Greece, "held September 11-13 in 2026."
   **Source says:** Could not be confirmed. The parish and festival websites did not respond when checked. The date should be marked unconfirmed or removed.
   **Link:** none could be opened.
7. **Claim (by omission):** Festivals named on the page do not include several large current Minnesota heritage events.
   **Source says:** Events with opened 2026 sources include New Ulm Oktoberfest (Oct. 2-3 and 9-10, 2026), FinnFest USA in Minneapolis (Sept. 17-20, 2026, with a larger Minneapolis festival planned for September 2027), Mindekirken's Syttende Mai (May 17, 2026), St. Urho's Day in Finland, Minnesota (March 13-15, 2026), and St. Paul's 60th St. Patrick's Day parade. The page also omits Spring Grove's Syttende Mai and the fact that it will be replaced by a Homecoming in June 2027.
   **Links:** https://www.newulm.com/2026/08/12/new-ulm-oktoberfest-usa-today-10best-2026/ ; https://finnfest.us/program-overview/ ; https://alleynews.org/2026/05/join-mindekirken-for-syttende-mai/ ; https://northshorejournal.co/community/finland/purple-and-green-to-paint-finland-as-annual-st-urhos-celebration-returns/ ; https://www.visitsaintpaul.com/events-calendar/festivals/st-patricks-day/ ; https://www.springgrovemn.com/heritage .
## Claims stated as fact without a source (should be sourced, softened or removed)

8. **Claim:** "What people call Minnesota Nice often draws on unmarked Scandinavian-German Protestant civic culture: conflict-avoidance, sameness-as-fairness..." Also: "The year turns on Christmas, Lent, hunting open, and school sports," and "Nuclear family forms can feel like nature in these cultures."
   **Source says:** No opened Minnesota source supports these group generalizations. They describe a whole population without a study, sample or date. They should be removed or attributed to a named source.
  .
9. **Claim:** "Prince is Minnesota whether the room knows it or not" and "Do not correct AAVE as broken English," on this page.
   **Source says:** These lines are about other communities and do not describe European American Minnesotans. They may belong on other pages.
  .
## Claims checked and supported

- Sons of Norway founded Jan. 16, 1895, by 18 Norwegian immigrant men in Minneapolis, conceived in 1893 during a depression: supported by https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/sons-norway-cottonwood-county .
- Germanic-American Institute: Volksfest Association formed 1957; bought 301 Summit Ave. in 1965 for $57,500 from the Sisters of St. Benedict, who had owned it since 1948; became a nonprofit in 1994; founded the Twin Cities German Immersion School in 2005: supported by https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/place/germanic-american-institute . St. Paul Oktoberfest Sept. 11-12, 2026: supported by https://www.gaimn.org/st-paul-oktoberfest .
- The Arc: parents first gathered in Minneapolis in 1946; national convention Sept. 28, 1950, in Minneapolis: supported by https://arcminnesota.org/about/why-the-arc/history-of-the-arc-in-minnesota/ .
- John Ireland: arrived in St. Paul in 1852 from County Kilkenny; first archbishop of St. Paul in 1888; died Sept. 25, 1918: supported by https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/ireland-john-1838-1918 .
- Twin Cities Irish Fest on Harriet Island, Aug. 7-9, 2026: supported by https://www.visitsaintpaul.com/blog/guide-to-the-irish-fest/ .
- Lutheran Social Service traces its roots to Pastor Eric Norelius and the Vasa Children's Home, 1865: supported by https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/swedish-immigration-minnesota .
- St. Paul Sokol's local roots date to 1882, in the C.S.P.S. Hall on Michigan Street: supported by https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/st-paul-sokol .
- Polish American Cultural Institute at 75 Fifth St. W., Suite 431, St. Paul: supported by https://pacim.org/ .

The page's claims about Minneapolis Tidende circulation, the Danish American Center's membership, a U.S. senator's family history, the Celtic Junction and Center for Irish Music founding years, and the Fujii and Shiraishi stigma study were not rechecked.
