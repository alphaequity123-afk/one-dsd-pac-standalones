# Corrections to the existing African American Minnesota page

Page: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/african-american
Checked: October 30, 2026. Each item gives the claim, what the opened source says, the link and the date checked.

## Errors or claims that should change

1. **Claim:** "Melvin Carter III is the first Black mayor of St. Paul (2018-present)."
   **Source says:** He lost to Kaohly Her in November 2025, and she was sworn in Jan. 2, 2026. He was the first Black mayor, but he is no longer in office.
   **Links:** https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/01/02/first-woman-hmong-american-kaohly-her-to-lead-st-paul-as-new-mayor; https://www.governing.com/politics/how-st-pauls-mayor-lost-a-safe-re-election-bid. Checked Oct. 30, 2026.

2. **Claim:** "About 350,000 Black Minnesotans as of 2024, about 6 percent of the state."
   **Source says:** Minnesota Compass reported more than 450,000 Black residents, nearly 8 percent, in March 2024. Its 2020 census figure for non-Hispanic Black Minnesotans is 7 percent. The page's number is well below both.
   **Links:** https://mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-black-communities; https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/quizzes/compass-quiz-minnesotas-black-population. Checked Oct. 30, 2026.

3. **Claim:** "African Americans (descendants of U.S. slavery): about 200,000. Black immigrants: about 150,000-plus."
   **Source says:** Compass counts 272,870 (give or take 9,028) African American Minnesotans for 2020 to 2024. That group is defined by U.S. birth, not by descent from slavery, and it includes people of Liberian ancestry. Compass also lists African-origin groups separately. I found no source that counts descendants of U.S. slavery in Minnesota. The label on the page's number is not supported.
   **Links:** https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/african-american; https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/methodology. Checked Oct. 30, 2026.

4. **Claim:** "African American children in Minnesota were about twice as likely as white peers to be placed in out-of-home care," with the Act described in general terms.
   **Source says:** I could not open a current figure for that exact ratio. The state's own 2023 figure is 17 percent of the child population and 27 percent of child protection cases. Statewide implementation begins Jan. 1, 2027, and lawmakers passed $15 million for it in May 2026. The page should use dated state figures and add this status.
   **Links:** https://dcyf.mn.gov/sites/default/files/2025-12/MAAFPCWDA%20one-pager_FINAL.pdf; https://sahanjournal.com/?p=83857. Checked Oct. 30, 2026.

5. **Claim:** Juneteenth sites are "Theodore Wirth Park, North Commons, and Lake Bde Maka Ska."
   **Source says:** The 2026 statewide guide lists about 40 events. Theodore Wirth appears for a fun run. North Commons and Bde Maka Ska do not appear. Major 2026 sites include Webber Natural Pool, West Broadway, Lake and Minnehaha, 315 Fisk Street, and the State Capitol. The events also extend well beyond the Twin Cities.
   **Link:** https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/06/13/juneteenth-2026-events-minnesota/. Checked Oct. 30, 2026.

6. **Claim:** Rondo "displaced about 650 families."
   **Source says:** Sources differ, and none I opened gives 650: MNopedia's overview says more than 600 residents, AARP says more than 1,000 families and more than 100 Black-owned businesses, and the Spokesman-Recorder and MPR News say thousands of residents. Present a range and name the sources.
   **Links:** https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/african-americans-minnesota; https://www.aarp.org/livable-communities/getting-around/info-2023/before-the-highway-rondo-saint-paul-minnesota-marvin-anderson.html; https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/02/22/pilgrim-baptist-church-minnesota-history/; https://www.mprnews.org/story/2023/06/23/160-years-of-sanctuary-at-pilgrim-baptist-church. Checked Oct. 30, 2026.

7. **Claim:** Rondo Days "occurs on the third Saturday of July," with no mention of a gap.
   **Source says:** The festival did not occur from 2020 through 2023, and later returned after five years. 2026 fell on the third Saturday (July 18), at the MLK Recreation Center.
   **Links:** https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/st-pauls-rondo-days-canceled-for-fourth-straight-year; https://kstp.com/?p=3282241; https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/07/15/rondo-days-returns-to-celebrate-the-history-of-st-pauls-iconic-neighborhood. Checked Oct. 30, 2026.

8. **Claim:** "Prince Rogers Nelson was born in North Minneapolis in 1958."
   **Source says:** MNopedia says he was born in Minneapolis and grew up on the North Side. I could not support "born in North Minneapolis" as worded.
   **Links:** https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/african-americans-minnesota; https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/place/near-north-african-american-community-minneapolis. Checked Oct. 30, 2026.

9. **Claim:** Keith Ellison "first Muslim elected to Congress (2007)."
   **Source says:** MNopedia dates his chronology entry to 2007, while the existing page's own later section says he was elected in 2006. The page should use one consistent wording, such as elected 2006, seated 2007. I did not open a source on the "first Muslim" wording.
   **Link:** https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/african-americans-minnesota. Checked Oct. 30, 2026.

10. **Claim:** The name "Minneapolis Spokesman-Recorder" in the media list.
    **Source says:** The paper is the Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder.
    **Link:** https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/08/04/msr-92nd-anniversary-cecil-newman-legacy-2/. Checked Oct. 30, 2026.

11. **Claim:** Spelling "Dred and Harriett Scott."
    **Source says:** MNopedia spells it Harriet.
    **Link:** https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/african-americans-minnesota. Checked Oct. 30, 2026.

12. **Claim:** George Floyd Square described as "an informal gathering site."
    **Source says:** Street reconstruction began in 2026, and the city is seeking a developer for People's Way, with a dispute over who should lead it. The description should be updated.
    **Links:** https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/10/08/peoples-way-george-floyd-square-redevelopment/; https://www.fox9.com/news/george-floyd-day-remembrance-events-may-2026. Checked Oct. 30, 2026.

13. **Omission:** The Minnesota people I read on the page do not include Natalie Hudson.
    **Source says:** She became the first Black person to lead the Minnesota Supreme Court in October 2023 and retired Sept. 30, 2026. Add her, with that date.
    **Links:** https://www.fox9.com/news/minnesota-supreme-court-chief-justice-natalie-hudson-retire-september; https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/minnesota/news/natalie-hudson-is-named-the-first-black-chief-justice-of-minnesota-supreme-court. Checked Oct. 30, 2026.

## Claims where sources disagree

14. **Spokesman-Recorder merger.** The page says the papers merged in 2000. The paper itself agrees (2000), while WCCO says the merger came under Newman's widow and the City of Minneapolis says Newman merged them. They differ on who merged the papers and when, so the page should attribute it.
    **Links:** https://spokesman-recorder.com/2026/08/04/msr-92nd-anniversary-cecil-newman-legacy-2/; https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/minnesota/news/minnesota-spokesman-recorder-90th-anniversary; https://www.minneapolismn.gov/resident-services/property-housing/preservation/landmarks-districts/landmarks/minnesota-spokesman-recorder. Checked Oct. 30, 2026.

15. **First Rondo Days.** The page says 1983, which matches MNopedia and organizer Gayle Smaller. AARP gives both 1982 and 1983.
    **Links:** https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/place/rondo-neighborhood-st-paul; https://www.aarp.org/livable-communities/getting-around/info-2023/before-the-highway-rondo-saint-paul-minnesota-marvin-anderson.html. Checked Oct. 30, 2026.

16. **Sumner Field address.** MNopedia itself gives 1101 Olson Memorial Highway in its text and North Dupont Avenue in its chronology.
    **Link:** https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/place/near-north-african-american-community-minneapolis. Checked Oct. 30, 2026.

## Claims I could not support

17. **Hennepin County about 190,000, Ramsey County about 80,000.** These are roughly right only as counts of all Black residents, including African-born immigrants, not of African Americans.
    **Source says:** The Census Bureau's 2020 to 2024 survey counts 206,831 Hennepin residents (give or take 1,137) and 85,205 Ramsey residents (give or take 1,010) who list Black alone or with another race, and 170,160 and 66,371 who list Black alone. Of the Black-alone residents, about 111,600 in Hennepin and 45,200 in Ramsey were born in the United States. The page should name the year, say which measure it uses, and make clear that these totals include immigrants.
    **Links:** https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B02009&geo_ids=05000US27053,05000US27123,04000US27; https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B06004B&geo_ids=04000US27,05000US27053,05000US27123,05000US27003,05000US27037,05000US27163,05000US27109,05000US27145,05000US27137,05000US27139,05000US27013,05000US27027,16000US2743000,16000US2758000,16000US2707966,16000US2707948,16000US2754880,16000US2717000,16000US2706616,16000US2754214,16000US2708794,16000US2756896,16000US2713114,16000US2743252. Checked Oct. 31, 2026.
18. **Neighborhood lists for North, South, and suburban Minneapolis.** Named neighborhoods broadly fit MNopedia's descriptions, but I could not confirm each as having "significant Black populations" today.
19. **CROWN Act coverage in Minnesota.** I did not open the statute or a source.
20. **Andrea Jenkins's current office.** I could not open a source on her current status, so the page should not present her as a sitting council member without checking.
21. **Keith Ellison's third-term ballot on Nov. 3, 2026.** Not checked against a results or filing page. Coverage confirms he is Attorney General as of Oct. 21, 2026.
22. **National statistics (Pew, Census, religion and denominational membership, sports shares, and similar).** These fall outside Minnesota and were not checked.
23. **Page's statement that it found no Minnesota-based study on how African American Minnesotans understand disability, and no Black disabled-led organization.** No Black disabled-led organization was found on a further check either. The page could add that the St. Paul/Minneapolis Black Deaf Advocates is named in the governor's April 2024 Deaf History Month proclamation (https://mn.gov/governor/assets/04.01.24%20Deaf%20History%20Month_SIgned_tcm1055-620392.pdf, checked Oct. 31, 2026).

## Checked and found consistent

- Half of St. Paul's Black residents lived in Rondo by the 1930s; I-94 construction began in 1956 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/place/rondo-neighborhood-st-paul)).
- Duluth lynchings on June 15, 1920, the three names, and the Max Mason pardon ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/duluth-lynchings)).
- Nellie Stone Johnson as first Black person elected to a Minneapolis city office, 1945, and Local 665; the Lee family in 1931; NAACP St. Paul 1913 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/african-americans-minnesota)).
- Spokesman-Recorder founded 1934 by Cecil Newman, at 3744 Fourth Avenue South from 1958, local landmark 2015 ([MNopedia, Southside](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/place/southside-african-american-community-minneapolis)).
- Hallie Q. Brown founded 1929, Sabathani Community Center 1966, Penumbra 1976, KMOJ 1976, Legacy Stabilization Collaborative in 2026 ([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/07/06/twin-cities-black-nonprofits-come-together-to-form-new-collaborative)).
