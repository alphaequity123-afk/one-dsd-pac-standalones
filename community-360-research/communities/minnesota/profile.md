# Minnesotan culture in Minnesota

This page is about the shared, everyday culture of the state itself: the customs, institutions, habits and arguments that most Minnesotans meet whatever their background. It is written for Minnesotans looking at their own culture. Rural Minnesota as a place and its systems, American culture as a whole, and the state's many cultural communities each have their own profiles, so this page points to them rather than repeating them.

## 1. People and place

**How many, and how fast it is growing.** Minnesota Compass, using the American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024, counts 5,739,445 Minnesotans. It places 55.2 percent in the Twin Cities metro and 44.8 percent in Greater Minnesota ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)). The newer Census Bureau estimate summarized by USAFacts puts the 2025 population at about 5.83 million, the 22nd largest state, after growth of about 33,000 people between 2024 and 2025 ([USAFacts](https://usafacts.org/answers/how-many-people-live-in-the-us/state/minnesota/)). The two numbers differ because one is a five-year survey average and the other a single-year estimate.

**Where people live.** In 2024 five of the state's 87 counties held more than half the population: Hennepin (1.27 million), Ramsey (542,000), Dakota (453,200), Anoka (376,800) and Washington (284,000) ([USAFacts](https://usafacts.org/answers/how-many-people-live-in-the-us/state/minnesota/)). The rest of the state is far from uniform. Northeastern Minnesota's Mesabi Iron Range has mined ore since the 1890s and built what MNopedia calls "a multiethnic regional culture," drawing Scandinavian and then Eastern and Southern European workers ([MNopedia, Mesabi Iron Range](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/place/mesabi-iron-range)). The Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness in the far northeast holds more than 1,000 lakes and the state's highest point ([MNopedia, BWCAW](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/place/boundary-waters-canoe-area-wilderness-bwcaw)). Rochester's Mayo Clinic, Austin's Hormel and other regional centers appear later in this profile. For farm country, small towns and rural services, see the separate rural profile.

**Who.** Compass reports that about 1.5 million Minnesotans, 26 percent, are people of color, and 4.3 million, 74 percent, identify as non-Hispanic White, down from 83 percent in 2010. The share of people of color ranges from 7 percent of Minnesotans 80 and older to 42 percent of children under 5 ([Minnesota Compass, race and ethnicity](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/race-ethnicity)). Using single-race Census categories for 2024, USAFacts reports 75.9 percent non-Hispanic White, 7.9 percent non-Hispanic Black, 6.7 percent Hispanic and 1 percent non-Hispanic American Indian and Alaska Native ([USAFacts](https://usafacts.org/answers/how-many-people-live-in-the-us/state/minnesota/)). The two sets of figures use different definitions, which is why they do not match exactly. Each of the state's larger cultural communities has its own profile with its own numbers.

**Born here, and newcomers.** Foreign-born residents are 8.6 percent of the population, give or take 0.2 points. Among people 5 and older, 12.3 percent speak a language other than English at home, and 4.6 percent speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)). In 2012 MPR News reported that about seven of every ten Minnesotans were born in the state, against roughly six in ten nationally, and quoted retired state demographer Tom Gillaspy saying the homegrown share was shrinking ([MPR News, March 12, 2012](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2012/03/12/outsiders1-is-minnesota-nice-to-newcomers)). No newer Minnesota figure on in-state birth was found.

**Age.** Children 17 and younger are 22.9 percent of Minnesotans and people 65 and older are 17.3 percent ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)). USAFacts, using a different year, puts the older share at 18.3 percent, slightly above the national 18 percent ([USAFacts](https://usafacts.org/answers/how-many-people-live-in-the-us/state/minnesota/)).

> **Content note:** This passage describes the execution of 38 Dakota men and the holding of Dakota non-combatants in a concentration camp after the U.S.-Dakota War of 1862. Readers may pause or skip to the next heading, section 2, All walks of life.

**How the state was made, briefly.** Minnesota became the 32nd state on May 11, 1858 ([KSTP](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/minnesota-celebrates-163rd-statehood-day/)). MNopedia notes that the Dakota name for their homeland here is Mni Sota Makoce. Its account of the U.S.-Dakota War of 1862 records that afterward the federal government nullified its treaties with the Dakota, executed 38 Dakota men at Mankato, and held about 1,600 Dakota non-combatants in a concentration camp before their exile ([MNopedia, U.S.-Dakota War of 1862](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/us-dakota-war-1862)). Native nations and Native Minnesotans are not covered further on this page.

## 2. All walks of life

All figures in this section are from the Compass 2020 to 2024 profile, in 2024 dollars, unless noted ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)).

- **The well-off and the very wealthy.** The median household income is $86,893, give or take $560, and 43.6 percent of households take in $100,000 or more. At the top, a Forbes-based list published by Stacker on October 20, 2026, names Glen Taylor of Mankato, the printing-company founder, as the richest Minnesotan at $2.9 billion, followed by Stanley Hubbard ($1.8 billion), Jeffrey Michael and family ($1.7 billion) and four members of the MacMillan family tied to Cargill ([Stacker, Oct. 20, 2026](https://stacker.com/stories/minnesota/richest-billionaires-minnesota)). Taylor began with a $2 million wedding-service business in 1975 and lost majority control of the Timberwolves in 2025 after an arbitration ruling, Patch reported ([Patch, May 2, 2025](https://patch.com/minnesota/minneapolis/meet-richest-person-mn-worth-nearly-3-billion)).
- **Professionals, managers and owners.** Minnesota hosts the headquarters of 18 Fortune 500 companies on the 2026 list, by one Minnesota bank's count, from UnitedHealth Group (No. 3) to Fastenal of Winona (No. 492) ([Security Bank & Trust, June 12, 2026](https://www.security-banks.com/blog/minnesotas-fortune-500-companies)). Statewide figures on college degrees were not available on the Compass page, so they are not reported here.
- **Middle-income and working families.** Owner-occupied homes are 72.4 percent of households. Minnesota Housing says the state has the ninth-highest homeownership rate in the country ([Minnesota Housing](https://mnhousing.gov/home/policy-and-research/agency-plans/strategic-plan/address-homeownership-barriers-and-reduce-disparities/homeownership-rates-dashboard)). Another 12.9 percent of people live above the poverty line but below twice that line (6.2 percent at 100 to 149 percent, 6.7 percent at 150 to 199 percent). Average private-sector pay was $39.78 an hour in September 2026, flat over the year, according to the Department of Employment and Economic Development ([DEED, September 2026 employment analysis](https://mn.gov/deed/assets/Sep%202026%20Employment%20Analysis_final_tcm1045-283140.pdf)).
- **People in poverty and hardship.** Of all Minnesotans, 8.1 percent live below the poverty line, including 8.9 percent of children and 16.1 percent of people 18 to 24. Of households, 17.8 percent take in under $35,000, and 4.6 percent of people have no health insurance. Wilder Research's most recent statewide homeless study took place on October 26, 2023 ([Wilder Research](https://www.wilder.org/wilder_studies/homelessness-in-minnesota-2023-study)); MPR News described roughly 10,000 people in the state with unstable or no shelter ([MPR News, Dec. 3, 2024](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2024/12/03/new-data-shows-minnesotas-homeless-population-is-more-native-american-rural-than-ever)).

**The gap inside the averages.** The comfortable averages hide wide differences by race. Minnesota Housing reports that in 2024, 77 percent of White households owned their homes, compared with 62 percent of Asian and Pacific Islander, 54 percent of Latino, 47 percent of Native American and 28 percent of Black households ([Minnesota Housing](https://mnhousing.gov/home/policy-and-research/agency-plans/strategic-plan/address-homeownership-barriers-and-reduce-disparities/homeownership-rates-dashboard)). University of Minnesota professor Samuel L. Myers Jr. calls this "the Minnesota Paradox": by his account the state is one of the best places to live in America and, measured by racial gaps in unemployment, income, homeownership, arrests, test scores and more, among the worst for Black residents ([Humphrey School, Roy Wilkins Center](https://www.hhh.umn.edu/research-centers/roy-wilkins-center-human-relations-and-social-justice/minnesota-paradox)). He traces the gaps to policies that became "baked in," including racial covenants and the destruction of Black neighborhoods for Interstate 94. MNopedia dates Minneapolis racial covenants to 1910 and their legal ban to 1953 ([MNopedia, racial housing covenants](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/racial-housing-covenants-twin-cities)). The Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder launched a 2024 essay series by residents working on the paradox ([Minnesota Spokesman-Recorder, Nov. 14, 2024](https://spokesman-recorder.com/2024/11/14/minnesota-paradox-black-disparities/)).

No statewide Minnesota figure for household wealth (as opposed to income) was found.

## 3. Work and the economy

**The size and shape of work now.** In September 2026 the state's unemployment rate was 4.5 percent, up a tenth of a point. Minnesota added 35,167 payroll jobs over the year, a 1.2 percent gain against 0.3 percent nationally, with construction up 5.2 percent and the large health care and social assistance subsector up 1.6 percent ([DEED, September 2026](https://mn.gov/deed/assets/Sep%202026%20Employment%20Analysis_final_tcm1045-283140.pdf)).

**Big companies, and a few that are not on the lists.** The 2026 Fortune 500 Minnesota headquarters, as listed by Security Bank & Trust, are UnitedHealth Group, Target, U.S. Bancorp, Best Buy, CHS, 3M, General Mills, Ameriprise, C.H. Robinson, Land O'Lakes, Ecolab, Xcel Energy, Hormel Foods, Thrivent, Solventum, Securian, APi Group and Fastenal. Cargill, described there as the state's largest company by revenue, is privately held and does not appear, and Mayo Clinic is a nonprofit ([Security Bank & Trust](https://www.security-banks.com/blog/minnesotas-fortune-500-companies)). Twin Cities Business counted 17 Minnesota companies on the 2025 list ([TCB, June 3, 2025](https://tcbmag.com/fortune-500-list-contains-some-warnings-for-minnesota/)). Two of the 18, CHS and Land O'Lakes, are agricultural cooperatives. Some Minnesota products became household names worldwide. MNopedia dates SPAM to 1937, when Hormel of Austin introduced it to use surplus pork shoulder ([MNopedia, SPAM](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/spam)).

**Labor history.** Minnesota's labor story includes the 1934 Minneapolis Teamsters strike, in which Local 574 won wages and recognition despite what MNopedia calls the "violent reaction of the authorities" ([MNopedia, Teamsters strike](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/minneapolis-teamsters-strike-1934)), and the 1985 to 1986 Hormel strike in Austin, when about 1,500 workers of Local P-9 struck for 13 months over a 23 percent wage cut and the National Guard was called in ([MNopedia, Hormel strike](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/hormel-strike-1985-1986)). MNopedia notes that all but one operating mine on the Mesabi Range is unionized ([MNopedia, Mesabi](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/place/mesabi-iron-range)). A current state union membership rate could not be confirmed; the federal labor statistics pages and the labor federations' own reports were not available.

**Farm and food.** The farm crisis of the 1980s cut Minnesota's net farm income 58 percent between 1981 and 1983 and dropped average land values nearly 40 percent between 1982 and 1987, MNopedia records ([MNopedia, farm crisis](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/farm-crisis-1979-1987)). Farming today is covered in the rural profile.

**Care work.** Disability and elder services are a large employer. Citing state employment data, the Post Bulletin reported just under 20,000 jobs in residential facilities for people with intellectual and developmental disabilities, down 24.7 percent from 2014 to 2024, and another 64,000 jobs in services for older adults and people with disabilities ([Post Bulletin, Nov. 2025](https://www.postbulletin.com/health/more-workers-better-reimbursements-needed-to-preserve-services-for-developmentally-disabled-adults-report-says)).

**Nonprofits.** The Minnesota Council of Nonprofits counts more than 2,200 nonprofit and 240 business members and published a 2026 report on how nonprofits are handling federal funding cuts and "increased scrutiny of state dollars going to nonprofits" ([Minnesota Council of Nonprofits](https://minnesotanonprofits.org/)).

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

**What the survey shows.** Pew Research Center's 2023 to 2024 Religious Landscape Study, with a margin of error of plus or minus 5.7 points for Minnesota, finds 22 percent of adults in mainline Protestant churches, 20 percent evangelical Protestant, 18 percent Catholic and 1 percent in historically Black Protestant churches. Lutherans appear on both sides: 16 percent in the mainline Lutheran family, including 12 percent in the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America, and 7 percent in the evangelical Lutheran family, including 5 percent in the Lutheran Church-Missouri Synod. Muslims are 3 percent; Jewish, Buddhist and Hindu Minnesotans each register under 1 percent in this sample. The religiously unaffiliated are a large group: 5 percent atheist, 9 percent agnostic and 15 percent "nothing in particular" ([Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/religious-landscape-study/state/minnesota/)). In other words, Lutherans are the largest single family of churches, but less than a quarter of adults, and people with no religion now outnumber Catholics in this survey.

**Decline and closing.** A 2018 Star Tribune report, summarized by Patch, found the ELCA had nearly 200,000 fewer members in Minnesota than in 2000, had lost about 150 churches, and had more than 1,000 congregations with under 50 members. The same summary said Catholic numbers held steady but about 80 Catholic churches had closed since 2000 ([Patch, July 9, 2018](https://patch.com/minnesota/southwestminneapolis/minnesota-churches-are-steep-decline-report)). MPR News, interviewing Star Tribune reporter Jean Hopfensperger, summarized her reporting as showing that such churches were "the backbone of their communities" ([MPR News, Aug. 14, 2018](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2018/08/14/churches-are-closing-attendance-is-falling-why)).

**Catholic life.** The Archdiocese of Saint Paul and Minneapolis covered about 825,000 Catholics in 12 counties and 187 parishes, by a 2021 count; Archbishop Bernard Hebda has led it since 2016, and its auxiliary bishop named that year had served as vicar for Latino ministry ([EWTN News, Dec. 2021](https://www.ewtnnews.com/vatican/pope-francis-names-new-auxiliary-bishop-for-saint-paul-minneapolis-archdiocese)). On August 27, 2025, a mass shooting at Annunciation Catholic Church and School in Minneapolis killed two children and injured 29 others. A year later, local elected officials hosted a day of service and remembrance on August 27, at the Annunciation community's request ([MPR News, Aug. 27, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/08/27/annunciation-shooting-victims-honored-one-year-later-acts-of-service)).

**The church basement as civic space.** MNopedia's history of hotdish traces the earliest known recipe to the 1930 cookbook of the Ladies' Aid Society of Grace Lutheran Church in Mankato, and describes the dish as standard fare at "church luncheons," funerals and rallies ([MNopedia, hotdish](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/hotdish-mnopedia)). That is one documented example of how congregational life and everyday food culture overlap.

**Other faiths.** Muslim, Jewish, Hindu, Buddhist and other faith communities are covered in the community profiles where they are concentrated. No statewide count of mosques, synagogues and temples was found.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

Minnesota's shared calendar mixes the national holidays, the state's own legal holidays, the seasons of fishing and hunting, and large public festivals. Dates below are as announced by organizers or set by law.

**State legal holidays.** Minnesota law lists New Year's Day, Martin Luther King's Birthday (third Monday in January), Washington's and Lincoln's Birthday, Memorial Day, Juneteenth (June 19), Independence Day, Labor Day, Indigenous Peoples Day (second Monday in October), Veterans Day, Thanksgiving and Christmas. The executive branch also closes the Friday after Thanksgiving but does not treat Indigenous Peoples Day as a holiday; other branches and local governments choose for themselves ([Minn. Stat. 645.44](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/645.44)).

**Statehood Day, May 11.** Minnesota entered the Union on May 11, 1858. KSTP, citing the Minnesota Historical Society, reports that word did not reach Minnesota until May 13 ([KSTP](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/minnesota-celebrates-163rd-statehood-day/)). In 2026 the Historical Society displayed the state's two original constitutions, one signed by Republicans and one by Democrats because neither party would sign the other's, from May 6 to July 5 at the Minnesota History Center ([Minnesota Historical Society, May 4, 2026](https://www.mnhs.org/media/news/handwritten-history-spotlights-founding-of-minnesota)). It is not a day off.

**The Minnesota State Fair, "the Great Minnesota Get-Together."** Twelve days ending on Labor Day, on 322 acres between Minneapolis and St. Paul. The fair grew out of an 1855 territorial fair, became the state fair in 1859, and has taken no state money since 1949 ([Minnesota State Fair, About](https://www.mnstatefair.org/about)). The 2026 fair ran August 27 to September 7 and counted 1,923,325 visits, the seventh-highest total in its history; that count is of visits, not different people ([FOX 9, Sept. 2026](https://www.fox9.com/news/minnesota-state-fair-attendance-how-many-people-went-2026)). The 2027 fair is set for August 26 to Labor Day, September 6 ([Minnesota State Fair](https://www.mnstatefair.org/)). The fair publishes pages in Spanish, Hmong and Somali.

**Saint Paul Winter Carnival.** The 2027 carnival runs January 28 to February 7, with the King Boreas Grande Day Parade on Saturday, January 30, at 2 p.m., according to the organizer ([Saint Paul Winter Carnival](https://www.wintercarnival.com/p/rentals/faqs)).

**Fishing opener.** The Governor's Fishing Opener has been held since 1948. The 2026 event, the 78th, was May 8 and 9 in Stillwater on the St. Croix River ([KTTC, Aug. 27, 2025](https://www.kttc.com/2025/08/27/gov-walz-announces-2026-minnesota-fishing-opener-location/)); the 2027 event is set for Cold Spring on May 14 and 15 ([KTTC, June 25, 2026](https://www.kttc.com/2026/06/25/cold-springs-host-2027-governors-fishing-opener/)). MNopedia notes the Legislature named the walleye the state fish on opening day in 1965 ([MNopedia, walleye](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/walleye)).

**Hunting openers.** The Governor's Pheasant Hunting Opener, begun in 2011, was held in Northfield October 9 and 10, 2026 ([KTTC, Oct. 14, 2025](https://www.kttc.com/2025/10/14/2026-governers-pheasant-hunting-opener-be-held-northfield/)). The statewide firearms deer season opens Saturday, November 7, 2026, per the Department of Natural Resources ([Minnesota DNR](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/hunting/deer/index.html)).

**Halloween in Anoka.** MNopedia records that Anoka took the title "Halloween Capital of the World" in 1937, having thrown a citywide party to stop pranks ([MNopedia, Anoka Halloween](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/anoka-halloween-celebration)). The organizer calls 2026 its 106th festival season ([Anoka Halloween](https://anokahalloween.com/halloween/)).

**Summer and city festivals.** Grand Old Day on St. Paul's Grand Avenue is held the first Sunday in June; in 2026 it was June 7 ([Grand Avenue Business Association](https://www.grandave.com/grand-old-day/)). Twin Cities Pride, which calls itself Minnesota's second-largest festival, ran June 26 to 28, 2026, in Loring Park ([Minnesota Public Radio](https://www.mpr.org/pride); [Citizens for a Loring Park Community](https://loringpark.org/events/twin-cities-pride-festival-2026/)). The Minneapolis Aquatennial dates to 1940, when business leaders wanted a festival to rival St. Paul's Winter Carnival ([MNopedia, Aquatennial](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/minneapolis-aquatennial)); the downtown council says it runs four days in July, but 2026 and 2027 dates were not found. The Twin Cities Marathon ran October 4, 2026 ([Twin Cities in Motion](https://www.tcmevents.org/)).

**Remembrance days.** Rondo Days in St. Paul began in the 1980s to honor the Black neighborhood split by Interstate 94; it returned Saturday, July 18, 2026, at the Martin Luther King Jr. Recreation Center ([MPR News, July 15, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/episode/2026/07/15/rondo-days-returns-to-celebrate-the-history-of-st-pauls-iconic-neighborhood)). The annual Rise and Remember festival at George Floyd Square was held May 25, 2026, six years after Floyd's murder, ending with a candlelight vigil ([KAAL, May 2026](https://www.kaaltv.com/news/george-floyd-square-hosts-annual-festival-six-years-after-his-death/)). The Annunciation shooting was remembered on August 27, 2026, with a day of service hosted by local elected officials ([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/08/27/annunciation-shooting-victims-honored-one-year-later-acts-of-service)). These days belong to particular communities first; the African American profile covers Rondo Days and the George Floyd remembrance in depth.

**Heritage festivals that became statewide habits.** The Festival of Nations, begun in 1936 by the International Institute in St. Paul, grew "to reflect the increasing diversity of the state" ([MNopedia, Festival of Nations](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/festival-nations)); its 2026 and 2027 dates could not be confirmed. Spring Grove's Syttende Mai, a Norwegian-American celebration, is held the weekend nearest May 17; its organizer lists the next one as May 17 to 21, 2028, and the town holds a homecoming in its place every ten years ([Spring Grove Syttende Mai](https://www.sgsyttendemai.com/); [City of Spring Grove](https://www.springgrovemn.com/heritage)). The International Eelpout Festival in Walker began in 1980 as a winter tourism draw built around what MNopedia calls the state's ugliest fish ([MNopedia, Eelpout Festival](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/international-eelpout-festival-walker)).

## 7. Family, home and daily life

**Households.** Minnesota has 2,307,855 households averaging 2.41 people. Married couples without children under 18 are 30.0 percent of households, married couples with children 19.1 percent, single mothers with children 3.7 percent, single fathers 1.2 percent, and people living alone 29.9 percent ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)). Most people stay put: 87.8 percent lived in the same home a year earlier.

**Hotdish and the shared table.** MNopedia calls hotdish "a cultural touchstone in the Upper Midwest": typically ground meat, a creamed soup and a vegetable, topped with tater tots. It traces the earliest known recipe to a 1930 Lutheran church cookbook in Mankato, says the dish became essential during the Depression because it fed many cheaply, and notes that tater tots became the standard topping by the 1960s. It also records how the dish keeps changing: Minnesota's congressional delegation has held a hotdish contest since 2011, Rep. Ilhan Omar took second place in 2019 with a cumin-and-turmeric "Little Moga-Hot-dishu," and chef Yia Vang published a Hmong hotdish in 2021 ([MNopedia, hotdish](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/hotdish-mnopedia)). The Bundt pan has a similar story: MNopedia credits Nordic Ware founder H. David Dalquist, but says the request came from women of the Hadassah Society of Minnesota in the late 1940s who missed their old-country cake pans ([MNopedia, Bundt pan](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/bundt-pan)).

**Lakes, loons and the outdoors.** The Department of Natural Resources counts 11,842 lakes of 10 acres or more and 69,200 miles of rivers and streams. The most common lake name is Mud, and four counties (Mower, Olmsted, Pipestone and Rock) have no natural lakes at all ([Minnesota DNR](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/faq/mnfacts/water.html)). The common loon became the state bird in 1961; MNopedia says more loons live in Minnesota than in any state but Alaska ([MNopedia, common loon](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/common-loon)). The Honeycrisp apple, bred at the University of Minnesota and released in 1991, is the state fruit ([MNopedia, Honeycrisp](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/honeycrisp-apple)). No source was found measuring how many Minnesotans own or use lake cabins, so "going up north" is left here as a phrase, not a statistic.

**Weather as shared experience.** MNopedia's account of the 1991 Halloween Blizzard records more than 30 inches of snow from Duluth to Dodge Center, two days after hundreds of thousands of people attended the Twins' World Series parades ([MNopedia, Halloween Blizzard](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/halloween-blizzard-1991)). The climate behind that culture is changing. The DNR reports average temperatures nearly 3 degrees Fahrenheit warmer since 1895, nights warming 55 percent faster than days since 1970, winter warming much faster than summer, and the 10 warmest and wettest years on record all within the past 20 years ([Minnesota DNR climate fact sheet](https://files.dnr.state.mn.us/natural_resources/climate/change/climatechange-factsheet.pdf)).

**Manners and talk: the "Minnesota nice" claim.** "Minnesota nice" is a popular description, not a measured trait, and the sources disagree about what it means and where it came from.

- *The friendly version.* Star Tribune culture reporter Rachel Hutton told NPR in 2024 that Minnesotans are "literally nice," polite and willing to push a stranger's car out of a snowbank, but also given to understatement and indirectness. The authors of a transplant's guide, Corey Bonnema and Jerilyn Veldof, gave NPR the example of a relative asking "is it hot in here" instead of asking for the air conditioning ([NPR, Aug. 9, 2024](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/nx-s1-5065632)).
- *Where it might come from.* Playwright Syl Jones, in a 2009 MPR essay, tied it to the "Jante law" that Danish-Norwegian novelist Aksel Sandemose described in the 1930s, a set of unwritten rules against thinking oneself special; Jones argued it is "more about keeping up appearances" than about being nice ([MPR News, Dec. 14, 2009](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2009/12/14/syljones)). University of St. Thomas sociologist Lisa Waldner told MPR in 2019 that there is no definitive documentation, but pointed to Northern European settlers who clustered by group and "co-existed, but.. didn't intermingle" ([MPR News, Nov. 21, 2019](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2019/11/21/ask-a-sotan-revisiting-minnesota-nice)).
- *The newcomer's complaint.* MPR's 2012 "Outsiders" series heard from dozens of transplants who found Minnesotans friendly but hard to befriend. It quoted a newcomer's line, "Minnesotans are friendly. They just don't want any more friends," passed on by the Minneapolis Regional Chamber of Commerce president, and reported that professionals of color felt isolation more often ([MPR News, March 12, 2012](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2012/03/12/outsiders1-is-minnesota-nice-to-newcomers)). The same story noted that Seattle has a similar reputation, which suggests the pattern is not unique to Minnesota. University of Minnesota psychology researcher Grace Vieth, in 2023, linked it to long-standing friendships and long winters that keep people within existing circles ([University of Minnesota, Sept. 25, 2023](https://cla.umn.edu/psychology/news-events/story/vieth-navigating-social-isolation-newcomer-minnesota)).
- *The sharper critique.* Waldner argued that lifelong residents can use the reputation to sidestep hard conversations about race and gender, a denial that she said can sustain gaps in housing and education ([MPR News, 2019](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2019/11/21/ask-a-sotan-revisiting-minnesota-nice)). Samuel Myers Jr. opened his account of the Minnesota Paradox by asking how George Floyd's killing and the unrest that followed could happen in a state "known for being 'nice'" ([Humphrey School](https://www.hhh.umn.edu/research-centers/roy-wilkins-center-human-relations-and-social-justice/minnesota-paradox)).

None of these sources measured niceness, indirectness or friendship across the state. They are interviews, essays and expert opinion, and they describe a reputation, not any particular person.

**Aging.** Minnesotans 65 and older number about one in six ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)), and 7.9 percent of them live in poverty. Care for older adults is a major line of work, discussed in section 3.

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Theater and music institutions.** The Guthrie Theater, founded by Tyrone Guthrie, Oliver Rea and Peter Zeisler, was one of the first major resident theaters in the country, and MNopedia credits it with a large role in the local theater scene ([MNopedia, Guthrie Theater](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/guthrie-theater)). The Minnesota Orchestra began as the Minneapolis Symphony Orchestra in 1903 ([MNopedia, Minnesota Orchestra](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/minnesota-orchestra)). Penumbra Theatre, founded in 1976 out of a cultural arts program that Lou Bellamy was hired to run at a Summit-University community center in St. Paul, is described by MNopedia as one of the premier legacy Black theaters in the United States ([MNopedia, Penumbra](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/penumbra-theatre-company)).

**The Minneapolis Sound.** Prince was born in Minneapolis on June 7, 1958, grew up on the Northside, and sold more than 100 million albums ([MNopedia, Prince](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/prince-1958-2016)). MNopedia credits the "Minneapolis Sound" to Prince, his side bands, and producers Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis, and names the community center The Way and the city's school music programs as crucial ingredients ([MNopedia, Minneapolis Sound](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/minneapolis-sound-music-genre)). First Avenue, which opened in the old Greyhound depot in 1970, is still booking shows, including its annual Halloween party ([MNopedia, First Avenue](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/place/first-avenue-7th-street-entry); [First Avenue](https://first-avenue.com/)). Paisley Park in Chanhassen, Prince's home and studio, is open for tours five days a week ([Paisley Park](https://www.paisleypark.com/)).

**Radio and the storytelling of Minnesota.** *A Prairie Home Companion* made the World Theater in St. Paul its home on March 4, 1978; Minnesota Public Radio bought the theater in 1980 and renamed it for F. Scott Fitzgerald in 1994 ([MNopedia, A Prairie Home Companion](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/prairie-home-companion)). MNopedia describes the show as NPR's Saturday-night staple, built around Garrison Keillor's Lake Wobegon monologue, fictional sponsors and music.

**Sports.** The Twins arrived in 1961 and won World Series titles in 1987 and 1991 ([MNopedia, Twins](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/minnesota-twins)). The Vikings joined the NFL as its 14th franchise in 1960, named for the region's Nordic traditions ([MNopedia, Vikings](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/minnesota-vikings)). The Lynx won WNBA titles in 2011, 2013, 2015 and 2017 ([MNopedia, Lynx](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/minnesota-lynx)), and the Minnesota Frost won the first two Walter Cups of the Professional Women's Hockey League, in 2024 and 2025, both times from the fourth playoff seed ([PWHL, Nov. 13, 2025](https://www.thepwhl.com/en/teams/minnesota-frost/news/2025/november/13/minnesota-frost-2025-26-season-preview)). The Post Bulletin calls the boys' high school hockey tournament one of the best high school sports tournaments in the country; in March 2026 it brought 16 teams from places such as Dodge County and Hibbing/Chisholm to St. Paul's Grand Casino Arena, the renamed Xcel Energy Center ([Post Bulletin, March 4, 2026](https://www.postbulletin.com/sports/prep/all-you-need-to-know-about-the-tourney-the-2026-minnesota-boys-hockey-state-tournament)).

**State symbols.** The 1893 state flag was redesigned in 2023 after public input to a redesign commission ([MNopedia, state flag](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/minnesota-state-flag)).

## 9. Civic and political life

**Voting.** The Secretary of State's office reported 76.35 percent turnout of eligible voters in the 2024 presidential election, second to Wisconsin's 76.64 percent; Minnesota led the nation among voters 18 to 29, at 62 percent against 47 percent nationally ([KSTP, 2025](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/local-news/minnesotans-ranked-2nd-in-voter-turnout-1st-among-young-voters/)). MPR News reported on October 30, 2026, that early votes could top 1 million before the November 3 election ([MPR News Election 2026 page](https://www.mprnews.org/politics/election-2026)).

**Volunteering.** In 2023, 40.3 percent of Minnesotans 16 and older said they had volunteered in the past year, third among the states ([America's Health Rankings](https://www.americashealthrankings.org/explore/measures/volunteerism_d/MN)). The League of Women Voters of Minnesota organizes statewide and local voter education ([League of Women Voters of Minnesota](https://www.lwvmn.org/)).

**Party traditions.** The Democratic-Farmer-Labor Party took shape in 1944 from the merger of the Democrats and Farmer-Laborites, which Hubert Humphrey actively supported ([MNopedia, Humphrey](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/person/humphrey-hubert-h-1911-1978)). In 1998 Jesse Ventura won the governorship on the Reform Party ticket with 37 percent of the vote, defeating better-known and better-financed major-party candidates ([MNopedia, Ventura](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/governorship-jesse-ventura)). The 1971 "Minnesota Miracle," passed after the longest special session in state history, shifted school costs from local property taxes to the state; MNopedia notes that before it, rich districts such as Edina had both the lowest tax rates and the highest spending per pupil ([MNopedia, Minnesota Miracle](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/minnesota-miracle-legislation)).

**Rights, and arguments about them.** The 1967 State Act Against Discrimination, renamed the Minnesota Human Rights Act in 1973, passed with support from both parties; sexual orientation was added in 1993 with Republican Gov. Arne Carlson's backing ([MNopedia, Minnesota Human Rights Act](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/minnesota-human-rights-act)). Minnesota is also where Jack Baker and Michael McConnell became, in 1970, the first same-sex couple in the country to apply for a marriage license ([MNopedia, Baker v. Nelson](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/baker-v-nelson)), and in 2012 its voters became the first to reject a constitutional ban on same-sex marriage at the ballot ([MNopedia, Amendment 1](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/minnesota-amendment-1)).

> **Content note:** This passage describes the 2025 shootings of Minnesota legislators, in which Melissa Hortman and her husband were killed. Readers may pause or skip to the next paragraph.

**A hard two years.** On June 14, 2025, former House Speaker Melissa Hortman and her husband, Mark, were shot and killed, and Sen. John Hoffman, his wife and their daughter were attacked. Vance Boelter pleaded guilty in federal court and was sentenced in July 2026 to two life terms plus 40 years; his state murder trial has been delayed to next year ([MPR News, lawmaker shootings coverage](https://www.mprnews.org/crime-law-and-justice/minnesota-lawmaker-shootings-hortman-hoffman)). MPR reported that candidates in 2026 are limiting solo campaigning and hiring security ([MPR News Election 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/politics/election-2026)). On January 5, 2026, Gov. Tim Walz announced he would not seek a third term amid fraud allegations in state-funded programs, criticizing "Republican opportunists" and President Trump for "demonizing our Somali neighbors." Northern News Now traced the dispute to the 2022 Feeding Our Future indictments over an alleged $250 million scheme and to a House fraud committee created in 2025 ([Northern News Now, Jan. 5, 2026](https://www.northernnewsnow.com/2026/01/05/walz-ends-re-election-bid-amid-fraud-allegations-timeline/)). MPR's 2026 coverage profiles the two candidates for governor, U.S. Sen. Amy Klobuchar and Minnesota House Speaker Lisa Demuth ([MPR News Election 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/politics/election-2026)).

**Debate inside the state.** Whether the state is as fair as its reputation is itself a public argument. The same record holds the gaps Myers calls the "Minnesota Paradox" (section 2) and the bipartisan laws described above.

## 10. Education

**High school.** Eighty-five percent of the class of 2025 graduated in four years, a record, after a dip to 83 percent in 2021; MPR reported gains among Asian, Black and Native American students, students in special education and students from low-income families, while the state watched results for students who do not speak English at home ([MPR News, April 3, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/04/03/minnesota-graduation-rates-hit-record-high-in-2025)).

**Tests.** In 2026, 48.6 percent of students met state reading standards on an updated test that cannot be compared with earlier versions, and 44.9 percent met math standards. MPR noted that many families opt out, especially in high school ([MPR News, Sept. 30, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/09/30/student-math-reading-standards-minnesota-weighs-testing-criteria)). The Department of Education reported that about 35,000 educators, 78 percent of those required, had completed "science of reading" training, and that at least 76.5 percent of students attended more than 90 percent of the time ([Minnesota Department of Education bulletin, Sept. 2026](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/MNMDE/bulletins/42d0cae)).

**The enforcement surge and schools.** The department reported that three of four schools saw higher absences during the winter 2025 to 2026 federal immigration operation, that about 440,000 fewer school meals were served, and that 80 percent of public-school leaders said it hurt students' ability to learn ([MDE bulletin](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/MNMDE/bulletins/42d0cae)).

**School funding history.** The 1971 Minnesota Miracle (section 9) reshaped how the state pays for schools, starting from 434 independent districts in 1970 ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/minnesota-miracle-legislation)).

**Higher education.** The University of Minnesota Twin Cities lists about 25,000 faculty and staff and more than 600,000 living alumni ([University of Minnesota](https://twin-cities.umn.edu/about-us)). Its research reaches daily life: the Honeycrisp apple came from its horticultural research center ([MNopedia, Honeycrisp](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/honeycrisp-apple)). The Minnesota State colleges and universities system's site was not available.

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

**Overall.** America's Health Rankings placed Minnesota sixth among the states in its 2025 annual report ([America's Health Rankings](https://www.americashealthrankings.org/explore/measures/Overall/MN)). Uninsured Minnesotans were 4.6 percent in the 2020 to 2024 survey ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)).

**Drinking.** The same rankings put Minnesota 40th for excessive drinking: 19.4 percent of adults reported binge or heavy drinking in 2024 ([America's Health Rankings, excessive drinking](https://www.americashealthrankings.org/explore/measures/ExcessDrink/MN)).

**Suicide.** The state's 2023 suicide death rate was 14.2 per 100,000, ranking 10th ([America's Health Rankings, suicide](https://www.americashealthrankings.org/explore/measures/Suicide/MN)).

**Medical institutions.** Mayo Clinic in Rochester is, in Myers's words, "an international leader in medical research" ([Humphrey School](https://www.hhh.umn.edu/research-centers/roy-wilkins-center-human-relations-and-social-justice/minnesota-paradox)); Mayo's own facts page was not available. 

**Gaps.** Statewide health disparities by race are documented by the Minnesota Department of Health, but its pages were not available. Community profiles report the figures for each group.

## 12. Disability

**How many.** Compass counts 11.7 percent of Minnesotans with a disability, give or take 0.2 points, and 8.3 percent of those under 65 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/minnesota)). The Arc Minnesota estimates about 90,000 Minnesotans with intellectual and developmental disabilities ([The Arc Minnesota](https://arcminnesota.org/)). The Minnesota Council on Disability notes more than 500,000 valid disability parking certificates and plates ([Minnesota Council on Disability](https://www.disability.state.mn.us/)). These counts measure different things and should not be added together.

> **Content note:** This passage describes forced sterilization and deaths in Minnesota state institutions. Readers may pause or skip to the next paragraph.

**A history that still shapes trust.** MNopedia records that Minnesota passed a sterilization law in 1925, that at least 2,204 people were sterilized, and that the true number is uncertain because no central board kept records ([MNopedia, eugenics in Minnesota](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/eugenics-minnesota)). In *Welsch v. Likins*, Judge Earl Larson ruled on February 15, 1974, that people committed to state institutions had a constitutional right to minimally adequate care; a 1987 settlement aimed to cut restraints and add direct care staff, and the case was dismissed in 1989 ([MNopedia, Welsch v. Likins](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/welsch-v-likins)). Self-advocates formed Remembering With Dignity in 1994 to replace numbered graves of about 13,000 people who died in state hospitals, and helped win a state apology signed by Gov. Tim Pawlenty in 2010 ([MNopedia, Remembering With Dignity](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/remembering-dignity)).

**Who defines disability here, and what a label opens.** The questions this raises are practical ones. A disability certification, a diagnosis of intellectual disability or a needs assessment each follows its own definition. Which one does a person meet, and who decided? What would the label open (a waiver, school services, workplace accommodations, a parking certificate), and what might it close or cost? The Human Rights Act, as revised in 1993, prohibits discrimination because of disability ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/minnesota-human-rights-act)), and the Council on Disability's materials explain "Employment First," informed choice and reasonable accommodations ([Minnesota Council on Disability](https://www.disability.state.mn.us/)). Disability Hub MN offers free help by phone, chat or email in all languages with benefits, housing and transition planning ([Disability Hub MN](https://disabilityhubmn.org/)). The Metropolitan Center for Independent Living offers independent-living and employment services ([MCIL](https://mcil-mn.org/)).

**Services in flux.** The Department of Human Services announced in March 2026 that "Waiver Reimagine," its plan to fold four disability waivers into two and set individual budgets, would not launch on its legislated date of January 1, 2027, citing among other things a data breach at a vendor that supports the MnCHOICES assessment system ([KIMT, March 24, 2026](https://www.kimt.com/news/minnesota-delays-new-disability-waiver-program-amid-data-breach/article_087b6a4b-5e4e-4cfe-a19c-65410b8fe2a4.html)). Staffing is the other pressure: in a national survey of about 500 providers cited by the Post Bulletin, 88 percent reported moderate or severe staffing problems and 62 percent had turned away new referrals; Minnesota's residential disability facility jobs fell 24.7 percent from 2014 to 2024 ([Post Bulletin](https://www.postbulletin.com/health/more-workers-better-reimbursements-needed-to-preserve-services-for-developmentally-disabled-adults-report-says)).

**What produced an impairment.** No Minnesota source was found that broke down disability statewide by cause (work injury, aging, illness, violence, birth). That question is better asked of the person than assumed. MDH and DHS statistical reports were not available.

**Open questions.** Of the roughly 90,000 Minnesotans the Arc estimates have intellectual and developmental disabilities, how many receive waiver services, and how many wait or go without? How many people who qualify for help never apply because of cost, distrust rooted in the institution era, language, or distance? The sources found do not answer these, and they are listed here as questions, not findings.

## 13. What is changing now

> **Content note:** This passage describes the fatal shootings of two people by federal officers in Minneapolis in January 2026. Readers may pause or skip to the next paragraph.

**A federal enforcement surge and its aftermath.** "Operation Metro Surge," a federal immigration enforcement operation, began December 1, 2025, and grew, by federal officials' own court filing, to roughly 3,000 officers. Federal officers fatally shot two people in Minneapolis, Renee Good on January 7 and Alex Pretti on January 24, 2026, prompting protests. Border official Tom Homan announced the operation's end in February 2026 ([FOX 9, Feb. 15, 2026](https://www.fox9.com/news/operation-metro-surge-how-many-federal-agents-have-left-minnesota)). The effects on schools are described in section 10. Group-level effects on immigrant communities are covered in their profiles.

**Politics after violence and scandal.** The assassination of Melissa Hortman, Walz's decision not to run, and the fraud allegations form the background of the 2026 election (section 9). The Minnesota Council of Nonprofits reports nonprofits facing federal cuts and closer scrutiny of state grants ([Minnesota Council of Nonprofits](https://minnesotanonprofits.org/)).

**Grief and rebuilding in Minneapolis.** Reconstruction of the George Floyd Square intersection was set to start June 8, 2026, and take two years, after years of debate over whether to rebuild at all ([MPR News, May 21, 2026](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/05/21/city-minneapolis-george-floyd-square-agape-peoples-way)). The Annunciation shooting anniversary was marked with service projects ([MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/08/27/annunciation-shooting-victims-honored-one-year-later-acts-of-service)).

**Growth and a changing age profile.** The state added about 33,000 people from 2024 to 2025 ([USAFacts](https://usafacts.org/answers/how-many-people-live-in-the-us/state/minnesota/)), and the share of people of color is far higher among young children than among older adults ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/race-ethnicity)). The state's youngest residents are far more diverse than its oldest.

**Faith and community life.** Congregations, long a center of town life, keep shrinking and closing (section 5), while the religiously unaffiliated are now nearly three in ten adults ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/religious-landscape-study/state/minnesota/)).

**Winters.** Winter is warming much faster than summer, and the DNR's fact sheet describes delayed freeze-up limiting access to wildlife areas, changed sap-running seasons, and projections that walleye will fade from smaller, warmer lakes while largemouth bass thrive ([Minnesota DNR](https://files.dnr.state.mn.us/natural_resources/climate/change/climatechange-factsheet.pdf)).

**Business leadership.** Best Buy's chief executive Corie Barry is stepping down October 31, 2026, and Target has a new chief executive, Michael Fiddelke, by one bank's summary ([Security Bank & Trust](https://www.security-banks.com/blog/minnesotas-fortune-500-companies)).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **Showing up.** High turnout, especially among young voters, and one of the highest volunteering rates in the country ([KSTP](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/local-news/minnesotans-ranked-2nd-in-voter-turnout-1st-among-young-voters/); [America's Health Rankings](https://www.americashealthrankings.org/explore/measures/volunteerism_d/MN)).
- **Institutions built to last.** A self-supporting State Fair, a century-old orchestra, the Guthrie, Penumbra, and a dense nonprofit sector ([Minnesota State Fair](https://www.mnstatefair.org/about); [MNopedia, Minnesota Orchestra](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/minnesota-orchestra); [Minnesota Council of Nonprofits](https://minnesotanonprofits.org/)).
- **Ideas that traveled.** The Honeycrisp, the Bundt pan, SPAM, the Minneapolis Sound and the hotdish, each with roots in Minnesota institutions or neighborhoods ([MNopedia, Honeycrisp](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/honeycrisp-apple); [MNopedia, Bundt pan](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/bundt-pan); [MNopedia, SPAM](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/spam); [MNopedia, Minneapolis Sound](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/minneapolis-sound-music-genre)).
- **Early moves on rights.** The bipartisan 1967 human rights law and the 1993 expansion; the self-advocates who won an apology for the state hospital era ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/minnesota-human-rights-act); [MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/remembering-dignity)).
- **Health and schools.** A top-ten health ranking and a record graduation rate, alongside the gaps reported above ([America's Health Rankings](https://www.americashealthrankings.org/explore/measures/Overall/MN); [MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/04/03/minnesota-graduation-rates-hit-record-high-in-2025)).
- **A culture that keeps absorbing new people.** Hotdish recipes that now include Rep. Ilhan Omar's spiced contest entry and chef Yia Vang's Hmong version, a State Fair that publishes in Spanish, Hmong and Somali, and a Festival of Nations that has grown to reflect the state's increasing diversity ([MNopedia, hotdish](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/thing/hotdish-mnopedia); [Minnesota State Fair](https://www.mnstatefair.org/about); [MNopedia, Festival of Nations](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/event/festival-nations)).
