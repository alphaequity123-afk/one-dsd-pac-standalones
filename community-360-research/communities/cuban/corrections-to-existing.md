# Corrections to the current Cuban Minnesota page

Page checked: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/cuban
Date checked: October 31, 2026

## Errors and outdated figures

1. **Population figure is outdated, and the page contradicts itself.**
   - As stated: "about 9,406 (ACS 2019–2023)" with 67.3 percent in the Twin Cities and 32.7 percent in Greater Minnesota. Later, in the "Organizations and media" section, the same page says "Minnesota Compass lists 8,775 Cuban Minnesotans in American Community Survey data for 2019–2023."
   - What the opened source says: Minnesota Compass now shows 8,775 (give or take 1,811) for 2020 to 2024, with 69.4 percent in the Twin Cities and 30.6 percent in Greater Minnesota (each give or take 8.7 points). The 8,775 figure belongs to 2020 to 2024, not 2019 to 2023.
   - Link: https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cuban
   - Note: the Census Bureau's Hispanic-origin count for 2020 to 2024 is lower, 6,536 (give or take 994): https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=04000US27,16000US2743000,16000US2758000,05000US27053,05000US27123,05000US27003,05000US27037,05000US27109,05000US27163. The page should say which definition it uses and give margins.

2. **Other Compass figures on the page are from the earlier release.** Current Compass (2020 to 2024) values, all from https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cuban:
   - Age 17 or younger: page 34.1 percent; now 31.6 percent (±7.3).
   - Age 65 and older: page 8.9 percent; now 9.9 percent (±4.9).
   - Foreign-born: page 21.9 percent; now 22.8 percent (±7.9).
   - Language other than English (age 5+): page 39.3 percent; now 36.8 percent (±9.8). English only: page 60.7; now 63.2. Speaks English less than "very well": 11.4 percent (unchanged, ±5.6).
   - Poverty: page 10.5 percent; now 9.4 percent (±4.7).
   - Owner-occupied: page 45.0 percent (renters 55.0); now 49.7 percent (renters 50.3), ±12.1.
   - Disability: page 21.5 percent (20.7 under 65); now 22.7 percent (±9.8), 22.6 under 65 (±10.9).
   - Uninsured: page 4.3 percent; now 4.5 percent (±2.1).
   - Average household size: page 2.84; now 2.80.
   - High school or higher (25+): page 82.2 percent; now 88.1 percent. Bachelor's or higher: page 33.3; now 35.7 (±9.5).
   - Median household income: page "about $90,000 (2022 dollars, averaged)"; now $87,497 in 2024 dollars, give or take $26,805. The wide margin should be stated.

3. **Victor's 1959 Cafe ownership.**
   - As stated: started by Niki Stavrou and her then-husband, Victor, who was Cuban; Heavy Table named Stavrou as owner in 2010.
   - What the opened source says: Minnesota Monthly (May 8, 2015) says the restaurant "was opened by Victor Valens, a native of Cuba, after he found no other Cuban restaurants in the Twin Cities." No opened page named Niki Stavrou; the Heavy Table and Family Destinations Guide pages cited on the current page were not confirmed. The two accounts may both be partly right, but the page should not state ownership without a current source.
   - Link: https://www.minnesotamonthly.com/food-drink/eating-cuban-in-the-twin-cities/

4. **El Cubano address and location.**
   - As stated: "St. Paul area, 870 Dodd Road."
   - Could not support: no opened page gave the address; the only opened page (a travel guide) places it in St. Paul and describes a "Cuban and Dominican" sign. A web listing about a Cuban-born owner named Geordanys Rodriguez Turro refers to a different El Cubano in Shoreline, Washington (https://intentionalist.com/b/el-cubano/), so that ownership should not be applied to the Minnesota restaurant.

5. **Cuban Adjustment Act wording.**
   - As stated: "a path some people can use after a year of presence."
   - What the opened sources say: this is correct for current law; the National Immigrant Justice Center (2022) describes a one-year physical presence requirement for Cubans inspected and admitted or paroled. The original 1966 text required two years (Immigration History). Correct as stated; the page may want to note that the requirement changed.
   - Links: https://immigrationjustice.us/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Cuban-Adjustment-Practice-Advisory-FINAL.pdf ; https://immigrationhistory.org/item/cuban-adjustment-act-of-1966/

6. **Zoilo Versalles death details.**
   - As stated: "He died at his home in Bloomington on June 9, 1995."
   - What the opened source says: SABR says he was found dead in his rented home on June 11, 1995, and that coroner's tests showed he had apparently died two days earlier; SABR's data lists June 9, 1995, Bloomington. Close to the page, but "rented home" and the discovery date add accuracy.
   - Link: https://sabr.org/bioproj/person/zoilo-versalles/

## Claims checked and supported

- MPR News 2014: about 3,600 Minnesotans identifying as Cuban, about 1,000 Cuban-born, and Tony Oliva's quote "I've been waiting for that for 50 years." Supported: https://www.mprnews.org/story/2014/12/18/cuba-minnesota
- Wet foot/dry foot ended in January 2017; Mariel was about 125,000 people in 1980. Supported by Pew (May 7, 2026): https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/05/07/10-facts-about-cubans-in-the-u-s/
- Marisella Veiga: left Cuba in the early 1960s, father arrived with 14 cents and worked as an assistant comptroller at a St. Paul meat distributing company, host family. Supported by NBC News (May 31, 2016): https://www.nbcnews.com/news/latino/cuban-minnesota-engaging-memoir-tells-marisella-veiga-s-family-journey-n583291 . The exact departure date of Dec. 30, 1960 was not checked.
- Minnesota Cuban Film Festival: running since 2010, 17th edition in spring 2026, coordinated with MSP Film and ICAIC. Supported: https://www.prensa-latina.cu/2026/04/05/festival-de-cine-cubano-de-minnesota-tiende-puentes/ and https://mspfilm.org/series/minnesota-cuban-film-festival/
- Cuban American Youth Orchestra grew out of the Minnesota Orchestra's 2015 Cuba tour. Supported: https://www.mprnews.org/story/2019/05/18/minnesota-cuban-american-youth-orchestra-first-tour
- Tony Oliva born in Pinar del Río, signed 1961, 1,917 hits, 220 home runs, .304 average, Hall of Fame 2022. Supported: https://ordway.org/news/cuban-star-power-to-electrify-the-ordway-on-sept-24/ and https://unanimodeportes.com/black-history-month-herencia/black-history-month-tony-oliva-una-leyenda-cubana-de-los-twins/
- Versalles as the first Latin American-born MVP, foreclosure and sale of his MVP trophy. Supported by SABR (which notes the "first" claim has been debated).
- Camilo Pascual named in 2014 among the first inductees of the re-established Cuban Baseball Hall of Fame. Supported by SABR: https://sabr.org/bioproj/person/camilo-pascual/

## Could not support or not checked

- "Compass puts the average household size at about 2.84" and other 2019 to 2023 figures: Compass no longer displays that release, so the older values could not be checked; they are superseded either way.
- The claim that Pascual "led the American League in strikeouts in each of his first three seasons in Minnesota" and was inducted into the Twins Hall of Fame in 2012 was not confirmed from an opened page.
- The claim that Oliva is "the only person to have been part of all three Twins pennant winners" was not checked.
- The page's statements about Our Lady of Guadalupe being "the first Spanish-speaking Catholic church in Minnesota" were not checked; its website could not be reached.
- The page's general guidance sections (for example, statements about what "the room" or "the desk" will meet) are not sourced claims and were not checked.
- The page says "we found no Cuban American association, club, church ministry or business group here." This research also found none.
