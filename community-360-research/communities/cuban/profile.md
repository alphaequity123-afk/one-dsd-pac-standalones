# Cubans in Minnesota

## 1. People and place

**A small community, counted in different ways.** Minnesota Compass, which builds its cultural community profiles from self-reported origin, ancestry, birthplace and parents' characteristics, counts 8,775 Cuban Minnesotans in American Community Survey data for 2020 to 2024, give or take 1,811 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cuban); [Minnesota Compass, Sept. 18, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-hispanic-and-latino-communities)). The Census Bureau's narrower count, people who answer the Hispanic-origin question as Cuban, is 6,536, give or take 994, in the same 2020 to 2024 survey ([Census Reporter, ACS 2024 five-year table B03001](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=04000US27,16000US2743000,16000US2758000,05000US27053,05000US27123,05000US27003,05000US27037,05000US27109,05000US27163)). The single-year 2024 estimate is 5,714, give or take 1,530 ([Census Reporter, ACS 2024 one-year](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_1yr?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=04000US27)), and the Minnesota Council on Latino Affairs reported about 7,100 from the 2021 one-year survey ([MCLA, March 2023](https://mn.gov/mcla/assets/03_31_23%20-%20Demographic%20Report%20-%20MCLA_tcm1099-602196.pdf)). These are sample estimates for a small group; the margins are wide, and the differences between years mostly reflect sampling, not real growth or loss. In 2014, MPR News cited about 3,600 Minnesotans identifying as Cuban, about 1,000 of them born in Cuba ([MPR News, Dec. 18, 2014](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2014/12/18/cuba-minnesota)). Because the methods differ, the 2014 and 2024 figures should not be read as a growth rate. Within the Latino population, Compass lists Cuban Minnesotans after Puerto Rican, Salvadoran, Guatemalan and Ecuadorian Minnesotans in size ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/updated-minnesotas-latinos-numbers-overview-minnesota-compass-data)).

**Mostly born in the United States.** Compass puts the foreign-born share at 22.8 percent, give or take 7.9 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cuban)). The Census table on place of birth counts about 1,535 Minnesotans born in Cuba, with a margin of 899 ([Census Reporter, table B05006](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US27)). That makes Minnesota's community very different from the national picture. Pew Research Center counts 2.9 million people of Cuban origin in the U.S. in 2024, and 57 percent of them are immigrants ([Pew, May 7, 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/05/07/10-facts-about-cubans-in-the-u-s/)). In Minnesota, by contrast, roughly three in four Cuban residents were born in the U.S. The Compass profile is young: 31.6 percent are 17 or younger and 9.9 percent are 65 or older ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cuban)). Nationally, Pew reports a median age of 38.2, the oldest of the Hispanic origin groups ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/05/07/10-facts-about-cubans-in-the-u-s/)).

**Where.** Compass places 69.4 percent in the Twin Cities and 30.6 percent in Greater Minnesota, each give or take 8.7 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cuban)). By the Census origin count for 2020 to 2024, Hennepin County has about 2,188 (give or take 465) and Ramsey County about 1,035 (give or take 705). Minneapolis has about 966 and St. Paul about 784, with very large margins. Smaller estimates appear for Stearns County (403), Washington (264), Anoka (261), Wright (212), Scott (197), Dakota (177) and St. Louis (151), most with margins nearly as large as the estimates ([Census Reporter, Minnesota and selected counties](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=04000US27,16000US2743000,16000US2758000,05000US27053,05000US27123,05000US27003,05000US27037,05000US27109,05000US27163); [Census Reporter, all Minnesota counties](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B03001&geo_ids=050%7C04000US27)). No Minnesota neighborhood has been described as a Cuban district in any source found. In September 2026, a Cuban content creator described on social media how a small Minnesota town gave her room "to work, study, be there for my family," and other Cubans living in Minnesota towns replied; the outlet that reported it did not name the town ([CubaHeadlines, Sept. 9, 2026](https://www.cubaheadlines.com/articles/339930)).

**How people came.** Cuban arrival in Minnesota came in several distinct streams, and families often know which one is theirs.

- *The 1960s.* After the 1959 revolution, a quarter million Cubans came to the U.S. in the 1960s and another quarter million in the 1970s, by Pew's count ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/05/07/10-facts-about-cubans-in-the-u-s/)). NBC News, reporting on writer Marisella Veiga's memoir, said close to 300 Cuban families had settled in Minnesota in the 1960s, most in the Twin Cities. Veiga's family came by way of Miami because her father was looking for work; with help from a host family, he became an assistant comptroller at a St. Paul meat distributing company ([NBC News, 2016](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/latino/cuban-minnesota-engaging-memoir-tells-marisella-veiga-s-family-journey-n583291)). One 1960s arrival, Gustavo Gomez, came to the U.S. in 1961 and moved to Minneapolis in the 1970s to work for 3M, according to MNopedia's description of his oral history ([MNopedia, last modified Jan. 14, 2026](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/positively-gay-cuban-refugee-task-force)). Jose Luciano, who ran for the Minnesota House in 1996, is described by the Minnesota Historical Election Archive as a Cuban refugee who came to the U.S. in 1962 ([Minnesota Historical Election Archive](https://mn.electionarchives.lib.umn.edu/candidate/jose-luciano)).
- *Baseball.* A separate path ran through the Washington Senators, who signed many Cuban players through scout Joe Cambria and moved to Minnesota as the Twins in 1961. Tony Oliva recalled that when he arrived, Jim Kaat told him a third of the team was Cuban, and that several of those players stayed in Minnesota ([MLB.com en Español, 2022](https://www.mlb.com/es/news/tony-oliva-de-camada-cubana-en-mellizos-al-salon)). See section 8.
- *Mariel, 1980.* About 125,000 Cubans crossed from Mariel Harbor to Florida between April and October 1980. Thousands were held at Fort McCoy in western Wisconsin, and none could leave without an American sponsor. MNopedia reports that religious groups in the Twin Cities recruited sponsors but gave priority to families, and that a Minneapolis group, the Positively Gay Cuban Refugee Task Force, helped about 90 gay Cuban men resettle in Minnesota that summer. Some, such as Rene Valdes, built lives here; others left for Miami and elsewhere ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/positively-gay-cuban-refugee-task-force)). Historian René Esparza writes that single men, queer people and darker-skinned Cubans were the ones judged "less attractive" for sponsorship and sent to camps ([American Historical Association, 2023](https://studio.m-anage.com/aha/2023/webprogram/Paper34195.html)). MPR's archive holds a May 23, 1980, report titled "Cuban refugees in Minnesota" and a June 10, 1980, item titled "Quie on Cubans" ([MPR Archive](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1980/05/23/cuban-refugees-in-minnesota); [MPR Archive](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1980/06/10/cathy-kennedy-quie-on-cubans)). Roberto Fonts, who came in the boatlift and moved to Minnesota soon after, ran a communications business in St. Paul when MPR interviewed him in 2014 ([MPR News, 2014](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2014/12/18/cuba-minnesota)).
- *The 1990s.* Felix Versaguis left Cuba in 1990 by paddling an inner tube toward Florida with friends; by 2014 he taught Spanish at North Hennepin Community College ([MPR News, 2014](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2014/12/18/cuba-minnesota)). Pianist Nachito Herrera, a White Bear Lake resident, moved to Minnesota from Cuba more than 20 years before a January 2022 FOX 9 report ([FOX 9, Jan. 13, 2022](https://www.fox9.com/news/world-renowned-minnesota-jazz-musician-to-headline-music-festival-in-cuba)); dancer Yeniel "Chini" Perez Domenech moved to Minnesota in 2011 ([McKnight Fellowships](https://www.mcknightdancechoreo.org/fellows//chini-perez)).
- *Since 2017.* The "wet foot, dry foot" policy, which let Cubans who reached U.S. soil stay and seek legal status, ended in January 2017. Pew estimates 475,000 Cuban immigrants were in the U.S. without legal status by July 2023, and at least 250,000 more entered by the end of 2024 ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/05/07/10-facts-about-cubans-in-the-u-s/)). Minnesota's share of these recent arrivals is not documented. The Minnesota Department of Health was notified of 23 "Cuban/Haitian entrants" arriving in 2024, a federal category it does not split by country, and notes that it is not told of every arrival ([MDH Refugee Health Report 2024](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/24rhrpt.pdf)).

**Languages.** Among Cuban Minnesotans 5 and older, 63.2 percent speak only English at home, 36.8 percent speak another language, and 11.4 percent (give or take 5.6 points) speak English less than "very well" ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cuban)). The other language is usually Spanish. Compass does not break out Spanish dialects, and no Minnesota source was found on how Cuban Spanish speakers experience Spanish-language services here.

## 2. All walks of life

Every figure below comes from the Compass 2020 to 2024 profile, in 2024 dollars, unless noted ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cuban)). The community is small, so the margins of error are wide; read these as rough shapes.

- **Comfortable and well-off households.** Median household income is $87,497, give or take $26,805, and 41.2 percent of households take in $100,000 or more. Average household size is 2.80.
- **Professionals and owners.** Among adults 25 and older, 35.7 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more, including 10.5 percent with a graduate or professional degree. In 2024 Compass noted that Cuban, Colombian and Puerto Rican adults finish high school at rates 12 to 20 points above Hispanic Minnesotans overall ([Minnesota Compass, Sept. 18, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-hispanic-and-latino-communities)). Public examples of professional and business life include a Mariel arrival who ran a communications firm in St. Paul and a college Spanish teacher ([MPR News, 2014](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2014/12/18/cuba-minnesota)), a Cuban-born IBM product introduction specialist who ran for the state Senate from Olmsted County in 1992 ([Minnesota Historical Election Archive](https://mn.electionarchives.lib.umn.edu/candidate/consuelo-p-stacy)), and internationally touring musicians (section 8). Tony Oliva has spent more than six decades with the Twins as player, coach, broadcaster and ambassador ([Unanimo Deportes, 2022](https://unanimodeportes.com/black-history-month-herencia/black-history-month-tony-oliva-una-leyenda-cubana-de-los-twins/)).
- **Middle-income and working families.** Owner-occupied homes are 49.7 percent of households, give or take 12.1 points. Among adults 18 to 64, 70.1 percent are employed, give or take 10.4 points. Another 22.3 percent of people live between the poverty line and one and a half times that line, above poverty but with little room.
- **People in poverty and under strain.** Of people for whom poverty is measured, 9.4 percent live below the poverty line, give or take 4.7 points. About half of renter households (49.5 percent, give or take 21.0) pay too much of their income for housing. And 11.9 percent of adults 25 and older did not finish high school. Hardship is part of this community's public history too: Zoilo Versalles, the Twins shortstop who won the 1965 American League MVP award, spent his later years in the Minneapolis area out of work for long stretches, lost his house to foreclosure, sold his MVP trophy and lived on disability and Social Security payments and a small pension before his death in Bloomington in 1995 ([SABR](https://sabr.org/bioproj/person/zoilo-versalles/)).

**How households live.** Compass counts 1,970 Cuban-headed households. Married couples without children under 18 are 26.5 percent, married couples with children 16.8 percent, people living alone 27.0 percent and unmarried couples 17.4 percent. Other categories are too small to report. Just 4.5 percent lack health insurance ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cuban)).

**Wealth.** No source was found on Cuban Minnesotans' wealth, savings or business ownership beyond these household figures.

## 3. Work and the economy

Compass reports that 70.1 percent of Cuban Minnesotans aged 18 to 64 are employed, nearly the same for men (69.4) and women (70.8), with margins of about 13 points. Most workers drive to work (80.9 percent), and about 60 percent commute less than 20 minutes ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cuban)). Compass's 2024 review found employment across Minnesota's Latino groups ranging from 72 to 80 percent ([Minnesota Compass, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-hispanic-and-latino-communities)). No published breakdown of Cuban Minnesotans' industries or occupations was found; the group is too small for standard Census tables on that subject.

What the record shows instead is a range of individual work lives over 60 years: a meat distributor's office in 1960s St. Paul ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/latino/cuban-minnesota-engaging-memoir-tells-marisella-veiga-s-family-journey-n583291)); 3M and Control Data, where a 1961 arrival and a 1980 Mariel arrival worked ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/positively-gay-cuban-refugee-task-force)); a downtown St. Paul shoe repair shop whose Cuban owner, Mario Diaz, spoke with MPR in 1993 about Minnesota business and politics ([MPR Archive, Sept. 25, 1993](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1993/09/25/a-profile-on-a-cuban-cobbler-in-downtown-st-paul)); IBM in Rochester ([Minnesota Historical Election Archive](https://mn.electionarchives.lib.umn.edu/candidate/consuelo-p-stacy)); sales ([Minnesota Historical Election Archive](https://mn.electionarchives.lib.umn.edu/candidate/jose-luciano)); teaching, communications and professional baseball. These are individual stories, not a profile of the community's work.

**Minnesota's economic ties to Cuba.** Minnesota's members of Congress were unusually active on Cuba policy after the 2014 thaw. A 2015 Twin Cities Business article tied that to export interests in agriculture and medical devices, including Cargill's lobbying, and quoted the Humphrey School's dean as saying Minnesota's lack of a large Cuban American community left its lawmakers freer than those from Florida or New Jersey ([Twin Cities Business, July 3, 2015](https://tcbmag.com/why-is-minnesotas-congressional-delegation-so-focused-on-cuba/)). In 2003 MPR reported a shipment of cattle and bison from Minnesota and other Midwestern states reaching Cuba under the farm-sales exception to the embargo ([MPR Archive search](https://archive.mpr.org/search?q=cuban)). These are Minnesota's trade interests, not the Cuban community's own views.

**Unions and labor history.** No Minnesota source was found on Cuban Minnesotans in unions or labor organizing.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

Minnesota has no Cuban business district, chamber or association that any source found. The visible Cuban commercial life is a handful of restaurants and cafés, plus musicians and dance teachers. Several restaurants could not be confirmed as open in 2025 or 2026; businesses.json records each with its status.

- **Victor's 1959 Cafe**, 3756 Grand Ave. S., in the Kingfield area of south Minneapolis, is the most widely documented Cuban restaurant in Minnesota sources. Minnesota Monthly wrote in 2015 that it was opened by Victor Valens, a native of Cuba, after he found no Cuban restaurants in the Twin Cities ([Minnesota Monthly, May 8, 2015](https://www.minnesotamonthly.com/food-drink/eating-cuban-in-the-twin-cities/)). Reviewers describe walls covered in diners' signatures and a menu of ropa vieja, Cuban sandwiches and café con leche ([The Infatuation](https://www.theinfatuation.com/minneapolis/reviews/victors-1959-cafe)), and it appeared on Diners, Drive-Ins and Dives ([Flavortown USA](https://www.flavortownusa.com/posts/4/our-first-diners-drive-ins-and-dives-in-minneapolis-was-victors-1959-cafe-in-2008)). Its own website could not be opened, so its current hours were not confirmed.
- **Guava's Cuban Cafe**, on Chicago Avenue in south Minneapolis, describes itself on its own site as serving "authentic Cuban food," with online ordering, catering and live-music nights ([Guava's Cuban Cafe](https://www.guavascubancafe.com/)). A 2018 report placed it at 5607 Chicago Ave. ([Hoodline, 2018](https://hoodline.com/2018/12/check-out-the-3-newest-businesses-to-open-in-minneapolis/)). Who owns it was not established.
- **El Cubano**, in the St. Paul area, displays a sign advertising Cuban and Dominican food, according to a travel guide ([Family Destinations Guide](https://familydestinationsguide.com/minnesota-delicious-cuban-restaurants/)). Its address, owners and current status were not confirmed from an opened page. (A Seattle restaurant of the same name has a different, Cuban-born owner; the two should not be confused, per [Intentionalist](https://intentionalist.com/b/el-cubano/).)
- **Francisco's Restaurant**, Rochester. Francisco Corripio, who left Cuba with his family at age 3 and spent decades in Miami, opened a Cuban café in a downtown Rochester skyway food court in 2015 after coming north for care at Mayo Clinic ([Post Bulletin, Nov. 11, 2015](https://www.postbulletin.com/lifestyle/big-authentic-tastes-from-little-havana)). His daughter Lisette Nunez-James and her husband later moved it to a sit-down space near Saint Marys, serving Cuban and Jamaican dishes ([Post Bulletin, Feb. 2, 2023](https://www.postbulletin.com/business/franciscos-shares-authentic-caribbean-dishes-with-rochester)). No 2025 or 2026 confirmation was found.
- **Yuma Fritas**, 808 Broadway Ave. S., Rochester, opened in December 2025 with a "Cubanish" menu built around the frita, a Cuban street burger. Its owners say they were inspired by a trip to Miami; the article does not describe them as Cuban ([Post Bulletin, Dec. 22, 2025](https://www.postbulletin.com/business/yuma-fritas-is-open-and-serving-up-cubanish-fare)).

**Music and dance as small businesses.** Pianist Nachito Herrera listed Minnesota concerts in White Bear Lake and Stillwater for fall 2025 ([Nachito Herrera](https://www.nachitoherrera.com/)). Charanga Tropical, a Twin Cities band that describes itself as bringing together Cuban and American musicians, was booked at Crooners in Minneapolis on Sept. 5, 2026 ([Happening Next](https://happeningnext.com/event/caribbean-nights-with-charanga-tropical-eid1ef0l1ta4uta)). Dancer Chini Perez Domenech teaches Cuban and other Latin dances from school workshops to nightclubs ([McKnight Fellowships](https://www.mcknightdancechoreo.org/fellows//chini-perez)).

**Media.** No Cuban-run news outlet in Minnesota was found. Diaspora outlets such as CubaHeadlines cover Cubans in Minnesota occasionally, especially immigration enforcement ([CubaHeadlines, Minnesota tag](https://www.cubaheadlines.com/tags/minnesota)).

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

No Minnesota source was found that measures the religious life of Cuban Minnesotans, and no Cuban congregation, Cuban ministry or Cuban religious feast was found at any Minnesota parish or church. Families who practice may simply attend Spanish-language or English-language congregations; the Church of the Incarnation in south Minneapolis, for example, runs a bilingual English and Spanish parish website ([Church of the Incarnation](https://www.incarnationmpls.org/)), but no source ties it to Cuban families.

**Our Lady of Charity.** La Virgen de la Caridad del Cobre is the patroness of Cuba. Tradition places her discovery in 1612 near the Bay of Nipe, found by three workers from the copper mine at El Cobre, one of them an enslaved African boy ([EWTN](https://www.ewtn.com/catholicism/library/virgen-of-charity-of-cobre--la-virgen-de-la-caridad-del-cobre-8931)). Her feast falls on September 8 ([New York Latin Culture](https://www.newyorklatinculture.com/our-lady-of-charity-caridad-del-cobre/)). In Chicago, for example, a parish that is home to the city's Cuban community held a bilingual Mass for her on Sept. 6, 2025 ([Mary, Mother of God Parish, Chicago](https://www.motherofgodchicago.org/parish-news/caridad-de-cobre-2025)). No Minnesota celebration was found.

**Afro-Cuban religion.** Santería, also called Lucumí or Regla de Ocha, came to the Americas with enslaved Yoruba people, and its orishas include Oshun, Yemaya, Shango and Ogun ([Walker Art Center](https://www.walkerart.org/reader/dayme-arocena-the-cedar-terrell-lamarr-review/)). In the Minnesota sources found, it appears mainly through the arts. When Cuban singer Daymé Arocena played The Cedar in Minneapolis, she opened with a chant for Eleggua and dedicated a song to her own orishas, a Walker reviewer wrote ([Walker Art Center](https://www.walkerart.org/reader/dayme-arocena-the-cedar-terrell-lamarr-review/)). Choreographer Osnel Delgado's classes at Northrop included Cuban dance styles tied to Santería ([Northrop](https://www.northrop.umn.edu/osnel-delgado)). No Minnesota house of worship, botánica or practitioner community was found. Whether Cuban Minnesotans practice Santería, and how many, is unknown.

**People of other faiths or none.** No Minnesota source was found on Cuban Minnesotans who are Protestant, Jewish, of another faith or of no faith.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

- **January 1** is listed in Cuba as the anniversary of the triumph of the revolution ([Cuba Travel](https://www.cuba.travel/en/useful-information/hours-and-holidays)). For some families it is a day of loss and exile and for others a day of history; Joe Nunez described arguing with his mother in their south Minneapolis kitchen in the 1970s about how the U.S. should treat Castro's government, and said younger Cuban Americans did not share their elders' "visceral hatred" of it ([MPR News, 2014](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2014/12/18/cuba-minnesota)). No Minnesota event on this date was found.
- **October 10** is the anniversary of the start of Cuba's wars of independence and a holiday in Cuba ([Cuba Travel](https://www.cuba.travel/en/useful-information/hours-and-holidays)). **May 20** is listed as Cuban Independence Day by calendar publishers ([Calendarific](https://calendarific.com/holidays/2026/CU)) but is not on the holiday list of Cuba's tourism ministry site. No Minnesota observance was found for either date.
- **September 8**, the feast of Our Lady of Charity, is described in section 5. No Minnesota celebration was found.
- **Christmas, Christmas Eve (Nochebuena), Easter and Three Kings Day.** No Minnesota source was found describing how Cuban Minnesotans keep these days.
- **Hispanic Heritage Month**, Sept. 15 to Oct. 15, is a U.S. observance ([U.S. Census Bureau, 2026](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/facts-for-features/2026/hispanic-heritage-month.html)). Minnesota's governor proclaimed Latino Heritage Month in 2022 ([Office of the Governor, Sept. 15, 2022](https://mn.gov/governor/assets/09.15.22%20Latino%20Heritage%20Month%20%28002%29%20Signed_tcm1055-540650.pdf)). Its most visible Cuban moment in recent years was a Sept. 24, 2022, Ordway concert by Nachito Herrera and the Havana Jazz All Stars, introduced by Tony Oliva ([Ordway, Sept. 7, 2022](https://ordway.org/news/cuban-star-power-to-electrify-the-ordway-on-sept-24/)).
- **Minnesota Cuban Film Festival.** Running since 2010, the festival held its 17th edition on Wednesdays from March 4 to April 1, 2026, at The Main in Minneapolis, with a discussion after each film ([MSP Film](https://mspfilm.org/series/minnesota-cuban-film-festival/)). It is coordinated by the Minnesota Cuba Committee with MSP Film and Cuba's national film institute, ICAIC. The 2026 closing night drew the head of Cuba's diplomatic mission in Washington and featured singer Gloria "La Niña" Rivera ([Prensa Latina, April 5, 2026](https://www.prensa-latina.cu/2026/04/05/festival-de-cine-cubano-de-minnesota-tiende-puentes/)). The 2025 festival ran Wednesdays from April 30 to June 4 ([MyNortheaster, 2025](https://www.mynortheaster.com/event/16th-minnesota-cuban-film-festival/2025-04-30/)) and the 2024 festival from Feb. 28 to April 4 ([MyNortheaster, 2024](https://www.mynortheaster.com/event/15th-minnesota-cuban-film-festival/)). Its organizers describe it as building bridges between the U.S. and Cuban peoples and work with Cuba's state film institute (section 9), so it reflects one view of Cuba among many.
- **Twins events.** Tony Oliva appeared at the Twins Experience at the 2026 Minnesota State Fair on Aug. 31 ([Minnesota Twins, 2026](https://www.mlb.com/press-release/twins-announce-plans-for-2026-minnesota-state-fair)).

## 7. Family, home and daily life

Compass household figures appear in section 2. The memoir record gives the fullest Minnesota picture. Marisella Veiga told NBC News that she learned to ice skate before she spoke English, that her parents raised the children "to adjust and not look back," and that a circle of Cuban friends in Minnesota made the adjustment possible. Her family later returned to Florida ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/latino/cuban-minnesota-engaging-memoir-tells-marisella-veiga-s-family-journey-n583291)). NBC News reported on her book, *We Carry Our Homes With Us*, from the Minnesota Historical Society Press, on May 31, 2016.

Generational difference runs through Minnesota accounts. Joe Nunez's 2014 recollection of kitchen-table arguments over Cuba policy is one example ([MPR News, 2014](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2014/12/18/cuba-minnesota)). Julio Bécquer, a 1961 Twin, described leaving his new wife behind in Cuba when he decided to keep playing in the U.S., saying it "was not a political decision" ([The View Level](https://theviewlevel.substack.com/p/the-cuban-senators-and-the-twinkie)). Separation from relatives on the island is a recurring theme; Oliva told MPR in 2014 that he had been waiting "50 years" for the two countries to restore relations ([MPR News, 2014](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2014/12/18/cuba-minnesota)).

**Food.** Cuban food in Minnesota shows up in restaurants (section 4): Cuban sandwiches, ropa vieja, plantains, yuca, guava pastries and café con leche ([Minnesota Monthly, 2015](https://www.minnesotamonthly.com/food-drink/eating-cuban-in-the-twin-cities/); [Post Bulletin, 2015](https://www.postbulletin.com/lifestyle/big-authentic-tastes-from-little-havana)). One Rochester restaurateur said he opened his café because he missed the food he grew up with in Miami ([Post Bulletin, 2015](https://www.postbulletin.com/lifestyle/big-authentic-tastes-from-little-havana)). No Minnesota source on Cuban home cooking, dress, childhood, aging or marriage customs was found.

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Baseball.** When the Senators became the Twins in 1961, they brought a group of Cuban players signed by scout Joe Cambria, including Camilo Pascual, Zoilo Versalles, Pedro Ramos, Julio Bécquer and later Tony Oliva ([MLB.com en Español](https://www.mlb.com/es/news/tony-oliva-de-camada-cubana-en-mellizos-al-salon)). Pascual flourished in Minnesota as a star pitcher, SABR writes, and in 2014 he was among the first 10 people named to the re-established Cuban Baseball Hall of Fame in Havana ([SABR](https://sabr.org/bioproj/person/camilo-pascual/)). Versalles, born in Havana's Marianao district, won the 1965 MVP award, which SABR calls the first by a Latin American player ([SABR](https://sabr.org/bioproj/person/zoilo-versalles/)). Bécquer, born in Havana in 1931, settled in Minnesota and died in Hopkins in 2020 at 88 ([Twins Trivia, Nov. 6, 2020](https://twinstrivia.com/2020/11/06/julio-becquer-a-member-of-1961-minnesota-twins-team-passes-away/)).

Tony Oliva, born in Pinar del Río in 1938, signed with the Twins in 1961, won the 1964 Rookie of the Year award and three batting titles, and was chosen for the National Baseball Hall of Fame in December 2021 and inducted in 2022 ([Unanimo Deportes](https://unanimodeportes.com/black-history-month-herencia/black-history-month-tony-oliva-una-leyenda-cubana-de-los-twins/); [Ordway, 2022](https://ordway.org/news/cuban-star-power-to-electrify-the-ordway-on-sept-24/)). Unanimo Deportes profiled him as an Afro-Latino legend for Black History Month. Oliva has said he spoke no English when he arrived and that the organization, his teammates and Minnesota fans made him feel at home from the start ([MLB.com en Español](https://www.mlb.com/es/news/tony-oliva-de-camada-cubana-en-mellizos-al-salon)). He has also been a commentator on the Twins' Spanish-language radio broadcasts ([MLB.com en Español](https://www.mlb.com/es/news/tony-oliva-de-camada-cubana-en-mellizos-al-salon)).

**Music.** Ignacio "Nachito" Herrera, a classically trained pianist who performed Rachmaninoff with the Havana Symphony at 12 and later became pianist, arranger and musical director of the band Cubanismo, has made Minnesota his base for more than two decades ([Nachito Herrera](https://www.nachitoherrera.com/); [Ordway, 2022](https://ordway.org/news/cuban-star-power-to-electrify-the-ordway-on-sept-24/)). He has received Minnesota's Immigrants of Distinction award ([Ordway](https://ordway.org/news/cuban-star-power-to-electrify-the-ordway-on-sept-24/)), headlined the Havana International Jazz Festival in 2022 ([FOX 9, 2022](https://www.fox9.com/news/world-renowned-minnesota-jazz-musician-to-headline-music-festival-in-cuba)), and raises money for children's health care in Cuba ([Nachito Herrera](https://www.nachitoherrera.com/)). In 2007 he joined the Minnesota Youth Symphonies for a Latin program ([MPR Archive](https://archive.mpr.org/search?q=cuban)). Charanga Tropical plays Cuban danzón, boleros and salsa with violins and flute, and says it is the only American group to have performed at Havana's International Danzón Festival, in 2015 ([Happening Next](https://happeningnext.com/event/caribbean-nights-with-charanga-tropical-eid1ef0l1ta4uta)). Some Twin Cities bands play Cuban son without Cuban members; MPR noted in 2011 that none of Malamanya's musicians were Cuban ([MPR News, Sept. 16, 2011](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2011/09/16/malamanya-moves-to-cuban-son)).

**Dance.** Chini Perez Domenech, from Matanzas, danced with the Afro-Cuban folkloric company Afrocuba de Matanzas before moving to Minnesota in 2011. He has worked with Twin Cities dance artists, performs with Rueda de la Calle and his own Chini Company, and received a McKnight dancer fellowship ([McKnight Fellowships](https://www.mcknightdancechoreo.org/fellows//chini-perez)). Northrop's residency for Cuban choreographer Osnel Delgado included community classes by two Minneapolis-based, Cuban-born dancers, Rene Thompson and Perez ([Northrop](https://www.northrop.umn.edu/osnel-delgado)).

**Literature and film.** Veiga's memoir is the main published Minnesota Cuban life story ([NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/latino/cuban-minnesota-engaging-memoir-tells-marisella-veiga-s-family-journey-n583291)). The Minnesota Cuban Film Festival (section 6) shows films made in Cuba; its 2025 lineup included *Fresa y chocolate*, about a gay artist in 1979 Havana ([MSP Film](https://mspfilm.org/series/minnesota-cuban-film-festival/)).

**Cultural exchange.** The Cuban American Youth Orchestra grew out of the Minnesota Orchestra's 2015 tour to Havana. Founded by Minnesota Orchestra clarinetist Rena Kraut, it took about 25 U.S. and 25 Cuban young musicians on its first tour in Cuba in 2019 ([MPR News, May 18, 2019](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2019/05/18/minnesota-cuban-american-youth-orchestra-first-tour)). It is an exchange program, not an organization of Cuban Minnesotans.

## 9. Civic and political life

**Elected office.** No Cuban-born or Cuban American person was found to have held state or federal elected office in Minnesota. The Minnesota Historical Election Archive lists two Cuban-born candidates for the Legislature: Consuelo P. Stacy of Olmsted County, a DFL candidate for state Senate in 1992 who won 31.6 percent, and Jose Luciano of Minneapolis, a Republican House candidate in 1996 who won 19.4 percent; Luciano later ran for McLeod County commissioner in 2020 ([Minnesota Historical Election Archive](https://mn.electionarchives.lib.umn.edu/candidate/consuelo-p-stacy); [Minnesota Historical Election Archive](https://mn.electionarchives.lib.umn.edu/candidate/jose-luciano)). The two ran for different parties, a small sign of the range of politics in the community.

**Debates over Cuba.** Cuban Minnesotans disagree about the island's government and U.S. policy, as Cuban Americans elsewhere do. In 2014, Oliva, Fonts and Versaguis welcomed the restoration of diplomatic ties, and Nunez said younger Cuban Americans were more open to it than their parents ([MPR News, 2014](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2014/12/18/cuba-minnesota)). After protests broke out in Havana in July 2021, supporters of the Cuban people gathered in Minneapolis to speak out ([FOX 9, July 15, 2021](https://www.fox9.com/video/955881)). The Minnesota Cuba Committee, which helps run the film festival, partners with Cuba's state film institute, and the head of Cuba's mission in Washington praised "the solidarity with Cuba that exists here" at its 2026 closing night ([Prensa Latina, 2026](https://www.prensa-latina.cu/2026/04/05/festival-de-cine-cubano-de-minnesota-tiende-puentes/)). A committee spokesman, Greg Klave, told KSTP that Minnesota "has had an integral relationship with Cuba for over 100 years" ([KSTP](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/local-news/interview-minnesota-cuba-film-festival/)). No source says how many Cuban Minnesotans take part in the committee. Nationally, Pew reports that Cuban Americans have historically leaned Republican, though that support has softened at times; in an October 2025 survey, 40 percent approved of the Trump administration's immigration approach, compared with 21 percent of Hispanics overall ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/05/07/10-facts-about-cubans-in-the-u-s/)). No Minnesota poll of Cuban residents was found.

**LGBTQ history.** The 1980 Positively Gay task force is one of the earliest documented links between Minnesota's gay community and refugee resettlement. MNopedia describes both its help and its strains: culture shock, sponsors who resented houseguests, and refugees who disliked their sponsors ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/positively-gay-cuban-refugee-task-force)). Esparza's research describes how some refugees "pushed back" against being cast as victims ([AHA, 2023](https://studio.m-anage.com/aha/2023/webprogram/Paper34195.html)).

**Organizations.** No Cuban American association, club or mutual-aid group in Minnesota was found. Latino-serving agencies serve Cuban Minnesotans along with others, but no source was found on which ones Cuban families use.

## 10. Education

Among Cuban Minnesotans 25 and older, 88.1 percent finished high school and 35.7 percent hold a bachelor's degree or more, compared with 73 percent high school completion among all Hispanic Minnesotans in Compass's 2024 analysis ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cuban); [Minnesota Compass, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-hispanic-and-latino-communities)). Nationally, Pew finds that foreign-born Cubans are less likely than Americans overall to hold a bachelor's degree, while U.S.-born Cubans are more likely ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/05/07/10-facts-about-cubans-in-the-u-s/)). Because most Cuban Minnesotans were born in the U.S., the national pattern would lead one to expect higher attainment here, and the Compass figures are consistent with that, though no Minnesota study has examined it.

Individual educators appear in the record: a Spanish teacher at North Hennepin Community College ([MPR News, 2014](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2014/12/18/cuba-minnesota)) and dance teachers who have taught Cuban styles in schools and studios ([McKnight Fellowships](https://www.mcknightdancechoreo.org/fellows//chini-perez); [Northrop](https://www.northrop.umn.edu/osnel-delgado)). No school, language program, student association or scholar devoted to Cuban Minnesotans was found.

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

Only 4.5 percent of Cuban Minnesotans lack health insurance, give or take 2.1 points ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cuban)). No clinic or health provider tied to the Cuban community was found. New arrivals who come as Cuban or Haitian "entrants" are offered the state's refugee health screening, but MDH reports them together with Ukrainian parolees and does not publish Cuban results separately ([MDH, 2024](https://www.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stats/24rhrpt.pdf)).

Rochester appears in Cuban Minnesota partly because of Mayo Clinic. The founder of one Rochester Cuban café first came for treatment there and stayed ([Post Bulletin, 2015](https://www.postbulletin.com/lifestyle/big-authentic-tastes-from-little-havana); [Post Bulletin, 2023](https://www.postbulletin.com/business/franciscos-shares-authentic-caribbean-dishes-with-rochester)). Nachito Herrera has spoken publicly about a severe case of COVID-19 in March 2020 that left him in a coma, and he wrote a song, "Esperanza," for the doctors and nurses who treated him ([FOX 9, 2022](https://www.fox9.com/news/world-renowned-minnesota-jazz-musician-to-headline-music-festival-in-cuba)). No Minnesota source on traditional healing among Cuban Minnesotans was found.

## 12. Disability

**The Minnesota number, and its limits.** Compass reports that 22.7 percent of Cuban Minnesotans have a disability, give or take 9.8 points, and 22.6 percent of those under 65, give or take 10.9 ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cuban)). For comparison, Compass estimates that at least 12 percent of all Minnesotans have a disability ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/disability)), and the Mexican Minnesotan rate is 8.1 percent ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/mexican)). The Cuban estimate rests on a small sample: its true value could plausibly be anywhere from about 13 to 32 percent. The community is also young, and Compass shows disability rising steeply with age statewide, so a young group would usually be expected to have a lower rate, not a higher one ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/disability)).

**Questions the evidence raises but does not answer.**
- Is the high estimate real, or a product of a small sample? A larger survey or several more years of data would be needed to know.
- If it is real, what lies behind it? The survey identifies disability through four basic areas of functioning and two areas that affect independent living, and Compass notes that its estimates likely undercount ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/disability)). No Minnesota source breaks the Cuban figure down by type or age, so there is no evidence on whether it reflects children's learning or developmental disabilities, adults' physical conditions, mental health, or something else.
- Who answers the question? Most Cuban Minnesotans were born here, and Compass builds its count from several questions about origin, ancestry and birthplace. Whether people who identify as Cuban on one survey question differ in age or health from those who do not has not been studied.

**Who defines disability here.** No Minnesota source was found on how Cuban Minnesotan families talk about disability, which words they use, or how a diagnosis opens or closes doors for them. Recent arrivals may bring expectations formed by Cuba's own school and health systems, but no study of how those expectations meet Minnesota's services was found.

**People and organizations.** No Minnesota organization serving Cuban people with disabilities was found, and no Cuban Minnesotan disability advocate who has spoken publicly on the topic was found. Families reach the same statewide doors as other Minnesotans, such as county assessments and school special education services.

## 13. What is changing now

**Federal policy has shifted twice in a decade.** The end of "wet foot, dry foot" in January 2017 meant Cubans arriving without visas were treated like other immigrants. The Biden administration's parole program for Cubans, Haitians, Nicaraguans and Venezuelans let many arrivals live and work legally; the Trump administration canceled it in 2025 ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/05/07/10-facts-about-cubans-in-the-u-s/)). The Cuban Adjustment Act of 1966 still lets Cubans who were inspected and admitted or paroled and have been in the U.S. for at least a year apply for permanent residence ([National Immigrant Justice Center, 2022](https://immigrationjustice.us/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Cuban-Adjustment-Practice-Advisory-FINAL.pdf)); the original 1966 law required two years ([Immigration History](https://immigrationhistory.org/item/cuban-adjustment-act-of-1966/)). How many Cuban Minnesotans these changes affect is not known.

**Enforcement in Minnesota.** In February 2026, border czar Tom Homan said the months-long federal immigration enforcement surge in Minnesota known as Operation Metro Surge would end ([NPR, Feb. 12, 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/02/12/nx-s1-5712552/operation-metro-surge-to-end-in-minnesota)). CubaHeadlines, a Cuban diaspora outlet, reported on ICE arrests of Cuban nationals in Minnesota in December 2025 and January 2026 ([CubaHeadlines, Minnesota tag](https://www.cubaheadlines.com/tags/minnesota)). No Minnesota outlet was found that reported on the effect on Cuban families as a group. Nationally, Pew found in October 2025 that 47 percent of Cuban Americans worried that they or someone close to them could be deported, about the same as Hispanics overall ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/05/07/10-facts-about-cubans-in-the-u-s/)).

**Newer arrivals outside the metro.** The September 2026 social media exchange about life in a small Minnesota town, and the Compass finding that about 3 in 10 Cuban Minnesotans live in Greater Minnesota, point to a community not limited to the Twin Cities ([CubaHeadlines, 2026](https://www.cubaheadlines.com/articles/339930); [Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cuban)).

**An aging baseball generation.** Of the 1960s Twins Cubans, Versalles died in 1995 and Bécquer in 2020 ([SABR](https://sabr.org/bioproj/person/zoilo-versalles/); [Twins Trivia](https://twinstrivia.com/2020/11/06/julio-becquer-a-member-of-1961-minnesota-twins-team-passes-away/)). Oliva, now in his late 80s, still appeared in public at the State Fair in August 2026 ([Minnesota Twins, 2026](https://www.mlb.com/press-release/twins-announce-plans-for-2026-minnesota-state-fair)).

**Ties to the island.** Diaspora media report long blackouts and food shortages on the island; one young Cuban creator described blackouts lasting up to four days before he left ([CubaHeadlines](https://www.cubaheadlines.com/articles/303415)). Minnesota residents raise money for health care in Cuba ([Nachito Herrera](https://www.nachitoherrera.com/)) and travel to the island through cultural exchange ([Prensa Latina, 2026](https://www.prensa-latina.cu/2026/04/05/festival-de-cine-cubano-de-minnesota-tiende-puentes/)). No Minnesota source measured remittances or family travel.

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **Education and stability.** Compared with Minnesota's Latino population overall, Cuban Minnesotans have high rates of high school and college completion, a median household income above that of Mexican Minnesotans ($87,497 against $71,697, though the Cuban figure carries a wide margin), and very low uninsurance ([Minnesota Compass, Mexican](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/mexican); [Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/cuban); [Minnesota Compass, 2024](https://www.mncompass.org/data-insights/articles/importance-data-disaggregation-minnesotas-hispanic-and-latino-communities)).
- **The Twins.** Cuban stars such as Pascual, Versalles and Oliva helped carry the early Twins to winning seasons and the 1965 World Series, Versalles won the 1965 MVP award, and Oliva has stayed with the organization for more than 60 years ([The View Level](https://theviewlevel.substack.com/p/the-cuban-senators-and-the-twinkie); [SABR](https://sabr.org/bioproj/person/zoilo-versalles/); [Unanimo Deportes](https://unanimodeportes.com/black-history-month-herencia/black-history-month-tony-oliva-una-leyenda-cubana-de-los-twins/)).
- **Music and dance.** Cuban-born musicians and dancers have brought Cuban piano, Cuban-style salsa, son, chachachá and rumba to Minnesota stages, schools and clubs ([Northrop](https://www.northrop.umn.edu/osnel-delgado); [Ordway](https://ordway.org/news/cuban-star-power-to-electrify-the-ordway-on-sept-24/); [McKnight Fellowships](https://www.mcknightdancechoreo.org/fellows//chini-perez)).
- **Bridges.** Cuban Minnesotans and their neighbors have built musical and cultural ties between Minnesota and Cuba for decades, including the film festival and the youth orchestra ([MSP Film](https://mspfilm.org/series/minnesota-cuban-film-festival/); [MPR News, 2019](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2019/05/18/minnesota-cuban-american-youth-orchestra-first-tour)).
- **Remembered history.** The 1980 Mariel resettlement left a record, now in MNopedia and Minnesota Historical Society collections, of Minnesotans who sponsored refugees and of refugees who made lives here ([MNopedia](https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/group/positively-gay-cuban-refugee-task-force)).

## What could not be established

- **Exact size.** Counts range from about 5,700 to 8,800 depending on survey year and definition. All carry wide margins.
- **Where people live in detail.** County and city counts have margins nearly as large as the estimates; no neighborhood or town has been documented as a Cuban center.
- **Occupations and industries.** No breakdown for Cuban Minnesotans was found.
- **Recent arrivals.** How many Cubans have come to Minnesota since 2017, or under the 2023 to 2025 parole program, is not published. MDH reports Cuban and Haitian entrants together.
- **Religious life.** No Cuban congregation, ministry, feast-day Mass, Santería house or botánica in Minnesota was found.
- **Organizations.** No Cuban American association, club, chamber or media outlet in Minnesota was found.
- **Disability.** No Minnesota study, organization or community voice on disability among Cuban Minnesotans was found, and the reason for the high Compass estimate is unknown.
- **Celebrations.** No Minnesota observance of Jan. 1, May 20, Oct. 10, Sept. 8 or Nochebuena by Cuban Minnesotans was found.
- **Restaurants.** Victor's 1959 Cafe, Guava's Cuban Cafe, El Cubano and Francisco's could not be confirmed as open in 2025 or 2026 from pages that could be opened. Victor's own website and several gift-card and review sites blocked access.
- **Sahan Journal and Star Tribune.** Neither site could be reached, so any coverage of Cuban Minnesotans in those outlets is not reflected here.
- **History of the 1960s arrival.** No Minnesota agency record of how Cuban families were resettled here in the 1960s was found. A 1986 report, *The Freedom Flotilla Six Years Later: From Mariel to Minnesota*, by the group now called The Advocates for Human Rights, is cited by MNopedia but could not be found online.
