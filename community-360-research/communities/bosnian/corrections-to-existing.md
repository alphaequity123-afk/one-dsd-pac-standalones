# Corrections to the existing Bosnian Minnesota page

Page: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/bosnian

## Errors or claims that should change

1. **Claim:** "The CLAS ten language block says Serbo-Croatian (Bosnian)" (repeated throughout, including "The CLAS ten includes Serbo-Croatian (Bosnian) on the standing language block" and "Language: the block says Serbo-Croatian (Bosnian), and that is notice").
   **Source says:** The DHS Language Block policy published 09/01/17 says the block gives notice "in 15 languages: Amharic, Arabic, Burmese, Cambodian (Khmer), Cantonese (Chinese), French, Hmong, Karen, Korean, Lao, Oromo, Russian, Somali, Spanish, and Vietnamese." Serbo-Croatian/Bosnian is not on it. The older 10-language list that did include "Serbo-Croatian (Bosnian)" appears in the DHS Limited English Proficiency Plan, DHS-4210 (4-07), from 2007. The page presents an outdated list as current.
   **Links:** https://mn.gov/dhs/assets/language-block-required-documents-available-general-public_tcm1053-646949.pdf ; https://www.migrationpolicy.org/sites/default/files/language_portal/Limited%20English%20Proficiency%20Plan.pdf . (The current DHS LEP web pages could not be opened; if a newer block exists, it should be checked.)

2. **Claim:** "Between 1993 and 2003, more than 2,300 primary Bosnian refugees resettled in Minnesota .. (MSP Magazine, looking back at Minnesota's refugee history)."
   **Source says:** The number is consistent with the state's own record, which is the better source to cite. MDH's Primary Refugee Arrivals to Minnesota, 1979-2025, counts 2,379 Bosnian arrivals from 1993 through 2003 (2,380 total, with one in 1986), peaking at 471 in 1998. The MSP Magazine article could not be found, so the citation itself could not be checked. The "about 140,000" U.S. figure was not checked against an opened source.
   **Link:** https://www.health.mn.gov/communities/rih/stats/reftotals.pdf .
3. **Claim:** "In Minnesota the pattern is Twin Cities .. Greater Minnesota households exist because work and kin exist."
   **Source says:** This understates Greater Minnesota. Rochester has a well-documented community dating to June 1993, with local estimates of 600 to 2,000 people at different times (Post Bulletin 2011, 2013, 2024). Pelican Rapids received Bosnian families from 1995 and a large group of Bosnian Roma in August 1996. Moorhead schools counted 25 Bosnian-speaking students in 2017-18. The Census count of Bosnia-born residents in the Twin Cities metro (1,368, give or take 844, in 2024) is only about two-thirds of the statewide figure (2,114, give or take 964).
   **Links:** https://www.postbulletin.com/community/how-a-magazine-lying-on-a-desk-in-croatia-in-1993-led-100s-of-bosnians-to-rochester ; https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/Ellison.pdf ; https://www.moorheadschools.org/documents/download/?id=387183 ; https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B05006,B16001&geo_ids=31000US33460 .
4. **Claim:** "ACS ancestry cells exist in some vintages" (implying a Bosnian ancestry figure can be cited).
   **Source says:** The 2024 ACS ancestry table for Minnesota (B04006) has no Bosnian line; it lists Croatian, Serbian, Slovene and "Yugoslavian." Usable Census figures are place of birth (Bosnia and Herzegovina: 2,114, give or take 964) and language spoken at home ("Serbo-Croatian": 3,503, give or take 1,642). The page could give these dated numbers instead of saying none exist.
   **Links:** https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B04006&geo_ids=04000US27 ; https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US27 ; https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B16001&geo_ids=04000US27 .
5. **Claim:** "Bosnians in the Rochester area gather to mark the 30th anniversary of the Srebrenica genocide, July 2025" (photo caption) and the dates list, which treats Srebrenica Day only as "literacy for the desk."
   **Source says:** Correct as to 2025. KAAL reports the first peace march around Cascade Lake, held on the Friday of the anniversary (July 11, 2025), and an all-day soccer tournament the next day. The page could add that the U.N. General Assembly designated July 11 as an international day on May 23, 2024.
   **Links:** https://www.kaaltv.com/news/rochester-remembers-30th-anniversary-of-srebrenica-genocide/ ; https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/world/2024/05/23/un-will-vote-on-commemorating-the-1995-srebrenica-genocide-annually-which-serbs-vehemently-oppose/ .
6. **Claim:** "1 March is Independence Day for some, while Dayton's anniversary is not a greeting."
   **Source says:** Accurate but incomplete. March 1 and November 25 (Statehood Day) are both holidays in the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina and are not observed in Republika Srpska, which marks November 21 (Dayton) instead. Statehood Day is missing from the page.
   **Links:** https://n1info.ba/english/news/bosnia-and-herzegovina-marks-independence-day/ ; https://n1info.ba/english/news/bosnia-and-herzegovina-celebrates-statehood-day/ ; https://www.paragraf.ba/neradni-dani-republike-srpske.html .
## Claims that could not be supported

7. **Claim:** "The Islamic Community of Bosniaks in Minnesota has been a named Minneapolis door."
   **Source says:** The organization exists: a registered Minneapolis religious nonprofit with a mosque at 668 Broadway St. NE, about 1,200 congregants in 2017, and a 2016-2017 cemetery case in Chisago County. Its current activity, leaders and contact details could not be confirmed from any page dated 2025 or 2026.
   **Links:** https://www.minneapolis.org/calendar/holidays/ramadan/ ; https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/search?q=Bosniaks+Minnesota ; https://vnews.com/2017/01/26/turned-away-in-chisago-county-bosnian-muslims-search-anew-for-cemetery-land-7727144/ .
8. **Claims about clinical patterns** ("PTSD, unexplained pain, sleep, and alcohol use sit in this diaspora's clinical literature"; alcohol use "part of the unvarnished picture for some households").
   **Source says:** No Minnesota source was found for these statements, and the page cites none. They should either be sourced or removed, since they describe a community's health without evidence.
  .
9. **Claim:** Kumovi (godparent ties) "can be as binding as blood"; handshake customs; "Sevdah / sevdalinka"; soccer "club names from home can be identity."
   **Source says:** No Minnesota source was found for these cultural statements. Rochester sources do confirm soccer as a visible community activity (a Bosnian team, United BiH, and an annual July tournament since about 2011).
   **Link:** https://www.postbulletin.com/sports/bosnian-led-soccer-tournament-promotes-peace .
## Claims checked and supported

- Elvis Budimlic: Omarska and about 200 days in camps; Money magazine story; arrival June 1993 sponsored by Catholic Charities; 67 Bosnian refugees to Rochester June 1993 through 1996; 600 to 1,000 today (Post Bulletin, 2024). Supported. The Rochester Magazine article gives June 15, 1993, and says he was held 68 days at Omarska and 132 at Manjača.
- Armin Budimlic: arrived 1994; joined IMAA 1998; executive director (IMAA staff page lists him, Oct. 31, 2026; Rochester Magazine says since 2018); 2021 DHS Civic Engagement Award. Supported.
- Luka Garza: Timberwolves 2022-2024; Bosnia and Herzegovina national team 2023 (12.4 points, 7.0 rebounds); NBA.com lists him with the Boston Celtics as of Oct. 31, 2026. Supported.
- Srebrenica: more than 8,000 Bosniak men and boys killed in July 1995. Supported (AP, 2024).
- Intercultural Mutual Assistance Association: founded 1984; programs as listed. Supported (IMAA site).
