# Bosnian in Minnesota

This page is about people in Minnesota whose families come from Bosnia and Herzegovina. Most who came as refugees in the 1990s were Bosniaks (Bosnian Muslims), but the word "Bosnian" also covers Bosnian Croats, Bosnian Serbs, Bosnian Roma and people from mixed marriages. Minnesota sources on this community are thin compared with larger groups, so this page says plainly where the record stops.

## 1. People and place

**How many: no single count.** Minnesota Compass publishes profiles for 26 of the state's largest cultural communities, including Lebanese (7,232) and Ukrainian (22,254), but there is no Bosnian profile ([Minnesota Compass](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities)). The pieces that do exist measure different things:

- **Born in Bosnia and Herzegovina.** The 2024 American Community Survey one-year estimate counts 2,114 Minnesota residents born in Bosnia and Herzegovina, give or take 964 ([U.S. Census Bureau, Table B05006, 2024 ACS 1-year, via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US27)). The margin of error is nearly half the estimate, so the true number could be anywhere from about 1,150 to about 3,080. This count leaves out U.S.-born children and grandchildren.
- **Speaking the language at home.** The same survey counts 3,503 Minnesotans age 5 and older who speak "Serbo-Croatian" at home (the Census Bureau's label, which takes in Bosnian, Croatian, Serbian and Montenegrin), give or take 1,642. Of those, 2,479 speak English "very well" and 1,024 (give or take 779) speak it less than very well ([Census Bureau, Table B16001, 2024 ACS 1-year, via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B16001&geo_ids=04000US27)).
- **Ancestry.** The ancestry table has no "Bosnian" line at all. It lists 9,736 Minnesotans of Croatian ancestry, 2,889 Serbian, 8,703 Slovene and 5,125 "Yugoslavian" ([Census Bureau, Table B04006, 2024 ACS 1-year, via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B04006&geo_ids=04000US27)). Many of these people descend from much older South Slav immigration (see below), so these numbers cannot be read as a Bosnian count.
- **Twin Cities share.** In the Minneapolis-St. Paul metro area the 2024 survey counts 1,368 people born in Bosnia and Herzegovina (give or take 844) and 2,646 Serbo-Croatian speakers (give or take 1,553) ([Census Bureau, metro tables, via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B05006,B16001&geo_ids=31000US33460)). The margins are too wide to say exactly how many live outside the metro. Two other counts point the same way. Stacker, using Census Bureau five-year estimates in a piece published Aug. 27, 2025, put the metro's Bosnia-born population at 1,297, ranked 48th among countries of birth ([Stacker](https://stacker.com/minnesota/minneapolis/biggest-sources-immigrants-minneapolis)). The 2000 Census counted 1,401 metro residents born in Bosnia and Herzegovina ([George Washington University GUM project, 2000 Census table](https://gum.columbian.gwu.edu/node/333)). Taken together, the Bosnia-born population of the Twin Cities appears to be about the same size it was in 2000, though a census count and survey estimates are not strictly comparable.

Community estimates are a different kind of number, and they disagree with one another. In 2017 the Star Tribune described the Islamic Community of Bosniaks in Minneapolis as a congregation of about 1,200 ([Star Tribune via Valley News, Jan. 26, 2017](https://vnews.com/2017/01/26/turned-away-in-chisago-county-bosnian-muslims-search-anew-for-cemetery-land-7727144/)). For Rochester, a local businessman told the Post Bulletin in 2011 there were 1,500 to 2,000 Bosnians ([Post Bulletin, Oct. 23, 2011](https://www.postbulletin.com/business/bosnian-immigrants-plan-to-open-european-grocery-in-nw-rochester)); a soccer organizer guessed about 1,000 in 2013 ([Post Bulletin, July 10, 2013](https://www.postbulletin.com/sports/bosnian-led-soccer-tournament-promotes-peace)); and the Post Bulletin's 2024 estimate was 600 to 1,000 ([Post Bulletin, March 26, 2024](https://www.postbulletin.com/community/how-a-magazine-lying-on-a-desk-in-croatia-in-1993-led-100s-of-bosnians-to-rochester)). None of these is a survey.

**How people came.** The Minnesota Department of Health's record of primary refugee arrivals counts 2,380 refugees from Bosnia between 1979 and 2025. All but one came between 1993 and 2003: 59 in 1993, 102 in 1994, 205 in 1995, 203 in 1996, 374 in 1997, 471 in 1998, 394 in 1999, 290 in 2000, 207 in 2001, 59 in 2002 and 15 in 2003 ([MDH, Primary Refugee Arrivals to Minnesota, 1979-2025](https://www.health.mn.gov/communities/rih/stats/reftotals.pdf)). These are people first resettled in Minnesota. Families who moved here later from Iowa, Missouri, other states or Europe are not in the table. The same table counts 72 refugees from Croatia (1996 to 2001), 32 from Serbia (1998 to 2000) and 74 from Kosovo (1999) ([MDH](https://www.health.mn.gov/communities/rih/stats/reftotals.pdf)).

> **Content note:** This passage describes the 1995 Srebrenica genocide. Readers may skip to "Where in Minnesota."

Behind the arrivals is the 1992 to 1995 war. Bosnia and Herzegovina voted for independence in a referendum on March 1, 1992, with 63.4 percent turnout and 99.7 percent in favor, and the United States recognized it on April 7, 1992 ([N1 Sarajevo, 2021, homeland background](https://n1info.ba/english/news/bosnia-and-herzegovina-marks-independence-day/)). In July 1995, Bosnian Serb forces overran the U.N. "safe area" of Srebrenica and killed at least 8,000 Bosniak men and boys, the Associated Press reports ([AP via WDIV, May 23, 2024](https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/world/2024/05/23/un-will-vote-on-commemorating-the-1995-srebrenica-genocide-annually-which-serbs-vehemently-oppose/)).

**Where in Minnesota.**
- *Rochester and Olmsted County.* Rochester's Bosnian community traces its start to one man's choice. In February 1993, Elvis Budimlic, released from the Omarska and Manjača camps, told a U.N. interviewer in Zagreb he wanted a small, safe city; a copy of Money magazine naming Rochester a "Best Place to Live" settled it. He and his wife arrived June 15, 1993, sponsored by Catholic Charities and the sisters of Assisi Heights. Relatives and friends followed, and Catholic Charities counted 67 Bosnian refugees relocated to Rochester from June 1993 through 1996 ([Rochester Magazine, March 7, 2024](https://www.postbulletin.com/rochester-magazine/elvis-budimlic-spent-200-days-in-concentration-camps-before-resettling-in-rochester); [Post Bulletin, March 26, 2024](https://www.postbulletin.com/community/how-a-magazine-lying-on-a-desk-in-croatia-in-1993-led-100s-of-bosnians-to-rochester)). By 2002, MPR described Bosnians as Rochester's newest wave of immigration, struggling to find housing and mortgages ([MPR News archive listing, March 26, 2002](https://archive.mpr.org/search?q=bosnian)).
- *The Twin Cities.* The Islamic Community of Bosniaks has a mosque on Broadway Street NE in Northeast Minneapolis ([Meet Minneapolis](https://www.minneapolis.org/calendar/holidays/ramadan/)). WCCO in 2012 interviewed refugees from Mostar and Sarajevo who were living and working in Minneapolis ([WCCO, April 6, 2012](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/bosnian-refugees-mark-20th-anniversary-of-war/)).
- *Pelican Rapids and west central Minnesota.* Lutheran Social Services placed the first two Bosnian families in Pelican Rapids in 1995, where the West Central Turkeys plant offered work ([MPR, May 5, 1999](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1999/05/05/mainstreet-radio-hidden-rainbow-the-changing-face-of-minnesota-rural-refugees)). An employee there persuaded the company to sponsor two families. Then, over two weeks in August 1996, a large group of Bosnian Roma from Sanski Most, first resettled elsewhere in the United States, followed a respected clan member to the town of about 1,800 ([Joan Ellison, Rural Minnesota Journal, 2012](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/Ellison.pdf)). MPR noted in 1999 that many Bosnian refugees settled in rural Minnesota and the Dakotas ([MPR, April 19, 1999](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1999/04/19/bosnian-refugees-recall-horror-of-war)).
- *Moorhead and the Fargo area.* Moorhead schools counted 25 students with Bosnian as their home language in 2017-18, 15 of them receiving English learner services ([Moorhead Area Public Schools, EL Plan 2018-19](https://www.moorheadschools.org/documents/download/?id=387183)). Fargo, across the river, received Bosnian families through Lutheran Social Services in the late 1990s ([Minnesota State University Moorhead, May 2014](https://news.mnstate.edu/2014/05/msum-student-reflects-on-journey-from-refugee-to-college-graduate/)).

**An older layer.** Bosnia's link to Minnesota did not start in 1993. A Serbian Orthodox parish history says the church in Chisholm on the Mesabi Iron Range was built by miners who came from Lika, Bosnia and Montenegro in the early 1900s ([Orthodox Times, March 18, 2021](https://orthodoxtimes.com/bishop-longin-of-new-gracanica-midwestern-america-made-canonical-visit-to-parishes-in-minnesota/)). Those families are now several generations American and are a different story from the war refugees.

**Generations and languages.** Many who arrived as children are now adults raising U.S.-born children. Two Rochester cousins who arrived at age 4 had tested out of English classes by third grade and still spoke Bosnian fluently at high school graduation in 2015 ([Post Bulletin, June 5, 2015](https://www.postbulletin.com/newsmd/best-bosnian-friends-move-on-from-century-together)). Bosnian, Croatian and Serbian are close enough to be understood across groups, but the name of the language matters to many speakers. The Minnesota Department of Health publishes materials under a combined "Bosnian/Croatian/Serbian" label ([MDH Translated Materials](https://www.health.mn.gov/communities/translation/index.html)).

## 2. All walks of life

No Minnesota source gives income, poverty, home ownership or education rates for Bosnian Minnesotans. The Census numbers above are too small to break down, and Minnesota Compass has no profile. What exists are individual stories, which show a wide range.

**Professionals and the comfortable middle class.** Elvis Budimlic, who arrived in 1993 with no car and worked three part-time jobs, earned a computer science degree from Winona State in 2000, worked at IBM and by 2024 was a senior cloud application developer at Boston Scientific in Rochester. His wife worked as an IV technician at Mayo Clinic before retiring ([Rochester Magazine, March 7, 2024](https://www.postbulletin.com/rochester-magazine/elvis-budimlic-spent-200-days-in-concentration-camps-before-resettling-in-rochester)). Dr. Ognjen Gajić, a Sarajevo-trained physician and former member of the Sarajevo band Zabranjeno pušenje, is an intensive care specialist in pulmonary medicine at Mayo Clinic in Rochester and a professor at the Mayo Clinic School of Medicine ([BHAAAS, July 16, 2019](https://www.bhaaas.org/en/ognjen-gajic-nekad-svirao-u-pusenju-a-danas-jedan-od-vodecih-ljekara-u-klinici-mayo-wp)). Sehida Cerimovic, who came as a refugee in 1997, is a patient care staff manager at Community University Health Care Center in Minneapolis ([MDH Refugee Health stories](https://www.web.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stories.html)). Among the Twin Cities refugees WCCO interviewed in 2012 were a former Sarajevo TV anchor and a man working for Lutheran Social Services ([WCCO, 2012](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/bosnian-refugees-mark-20th-anniversary-of-war/)).

**Business owners.** Nedzad Nukic opened Northgate Imports, a used-car and repair business in northwest Rochester, in 2010 and planned a European grocery in 2011 ([Post Bulletin, Oct. 23, 2011](https://www.postbulletin.com/business/bosnian-immigrants-plan-to-open-european-grocery-in-nw-rochester)). A Bosnian father and son ran Stir Fry Express restaurants in Sioux Falls, Duluth and, until October 2026, Rochester ([KTTC, Oct. 9, 2026](https://www.kttc.com/2026/10/09/stir-fry-express-closes-nw-rochester/)). See section 4.

**Working families.** Early arrivals took the jobs they could get. Budimlic washed pots at a monastery and assembled trophies before finding office work ([Rochester Magazine, 2024](https://www.postbulletin.com/rochester-magazine/elvis-budimlic-spent-200-days-in-concentration-camps-before-resettling-in-rochester)). In Pelican Rapids, adults worked at the turkey plant, and by 2012 Bosnian young people worked at the local McDonald's and Bosnian residents worked at the grocery checkout ([Ellison, 2012](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/Ellison.pdf)). A Rochester soccer organizer interviewed in 2013 worked as a chef at Charter House ([Post Bulletin, 2013](https://www.postbulletin.com/sports/bosnian-led-soccer-tournament-promotes-peace)).

**Hardship.** The hardest documented arrival was in Pelican Rapids in 1996. Newcomers lived six to twelve in two-bedroom apartments, had no food, money or English, and few drove, while the county's social services were a half-hour away. Volunteers found one woman near death after giving birth at home, and in 1997 Otter Tail County put $25,000 toward a local response ([Ellison, 2012](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/Ellison.pdf)). The same account says many of the Bosnian Roma adults had little schooling, that their children had never been to school because of the war, and that the families became, in the author's words, the town's "underclass" for a time. That is one local writer's description of the late 1990s, not a measure of the community today. Elsewhere, a 2002 MPR report described Bosnian families in Rochester struggling with a tight housing market and with getting mortgages ([MPR archive listing](https://archive.mpr.org/search?q=bosnian)).

**Lost class status.** Jennifer Erickson, an anthropologist from Luverne who worked as a Lutheran Social Services case manager and studied refugees in Fargo, across the river from Moorhead, told MPR in 2010 that some of the Bosnians who struggled most had been middle or upper class at home, a status that U.S. resettlement did not recognize ([MPR News, July 8, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/07/08/studying-fargo)). She also said more than half of Fargo's Bosnian refugees were Roma. Her observations are about Fargo, North Dakota, in 2006-2007, not Minnesota statewide.

No wealthy Bosnian Minnesotan families were found in public sources. One notable Bosnian American philanthropist, Sanela Diana Jenkins, lives in California and is not a Minnesota figure.

## 3. Work and the economy

There is no Minnesota data on the jobs Bosnian Minnesotans hold. What sources show:

- **Health care.** Rochester's Bosnian community grew up around Mayo Clinic, and Mayo appears often in work stories: a Mayo intensive care physician from Sarajevo ([BHAAAS, 2019](https://www.bhaaas.org/en/ognjen-gajic-nekad-svirao-u-pusenju-a-danas-jedan-od-vodecih-ljekara-u-klinici-mayo-wp)), an IV technician ([Rochester Magazine, 2024](https://www.postbulletin.com/rochester-magazine/elvis-budimlic-spent-200-days-in-concentration-camps-before-resettling-in-rochester)), and two Century High graduates who planned in 2015 to study nursing at Rochester Community and Technical College ([Post Bulletin, 2015](https://www.postbulletin.com/newsmd/best-bosnian-friends-move-on-from-century-together)). In Minneapolis, a Bosnian manager leads patient care staff at Community University Health Care Center ([MDH](https://www.web.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stories.html)).
- **Technology and manufacturing.** IBM and Boston Scientific in Rochester ([Rochester Magazine, 2024](https://www.postbulletin.com/rochester-magazine/elvis-budimlic-spent-200-days-in-concentration-camps-before-resettling-in-rochester)).
- **Food processing.** West Central Turkeys in Pelican Rapids, a farmer-owned plant built in 1956, employed early Bosnian arrivals ([Ellison, 2012](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/Ellison.pdf); [MPR, 1999](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1999/05/05/mainstreet-radio-hidden-rainbow-the-changing-face-of-minnesota-rural-refugees)).
- **Refugee services and interpreting.** Bosnian refugees became helpers. In Pelican Rapids, early arrivals volunteered as drivers and translators and were later paid through a grant, and Lutheran Social Services hired a part-time Bosnian translator in 1998 ([Ellison, 2012](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/Ellison.pdf)). In Rochester, Armin Budimlic joined the Intercultural Mutual Assistance Association in 1998 and has led it since 2018 ([Rochester Magazine, 2024](https://www.postbulletin.com/rochester-magazine/elvis-budimlic-spent-200-days-in-concentration-camps-before-resettling-in-rochester); [IMAA staff page](https://imaa.net/7166-2/)).
- **Construction, cars, food service.** A Rochester car dealer, a restaurant family, a chef ([Post Bulletin, 2011](https://www.postbulletin.com/business/bosnian-immigrants-plan-to-open-european-grocery-in-nw-rochester); [KTTC, 2026](https://www.kttc.com/2026/10/09/stir-fry-express-closes-nw-rochester/); [Post Bulletin, 2013](https://www.postbulletin.com/sports/bosnian-led-soccer-tournament-promotes-peace)). A 1998 MPR feature told of a well-known musician from the former Yugoslavia who ended up working at a Minnesota bakery ([MPR archive listing, Dec. 24, 1998](https://archive.mpr.org/search?q=bosnian)).

No Minnesota source was found on union membership, wages or self-employment rates for this community.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

Minnesota has no Bosnian business district, and the survey of Bosnian-owned businesses here is short. That itself is a finding: the Post Bulletin noted in 2011 that other immigrant groups in Rochester had stores importing food from home, "something Bosnians have not been able to do," as a Bosnian resident planning a grocery put it ([Post Bulletin, Oct. 23, 2011](https://www.postbulletin.com/business/bosnian-immigrants-plan-to-open-european-grocery-in-nw-rochester)). In October 2026 a Lake City man of Bosnian background said the Rochester Stir Fry Express had been the one place nearby to get Bosnian kebab and baklava, and mourned its closing ([KTTC, Oct. 9, 2026](https://www.kttc.com/2026/10/09/stir-fry-express-closes-nw-rochester/)).

Named businesses (details in businesses.json):
- **Stir Fry Express, Rochester.** Opened February 2026 in the former Pancheros building in northwest Rochester; owned by a father and son who came from Bosnia; served Bosnian kebab, bulaklak and baklava alongside its main menu. Closed permanently by Oct. 9, 2026, per a sign on the door ([KTTC](https://www.kttc.com/2026/10/09/stir-fry-express-closes-nw-rochester/)). The same owners' Duluth and Sioux Falls locations could not be confirmed open.
- **Northgate Imports, 632 11th Ave. NW, Rochester.** Used foreign vehicles, repair and service, opened in 2010 by a Bosnian immigrant ([Post Bulletin, 2011](https://www.postbulletin.com/business/bosnian-immigrants-plan-to-open-european-grocery-in-nw-rochester)). A current dealer listing shows vehicles in stock ([CarEdge](https://caredge.com/dealer/northgate-imports-inc--mn-rochester)); current ownership was not confirmed.
- **Europe Grocery Store, 1109 Seventh St. NW, Rochester.** Planned in 2011 to sell ćevapi, somun bread and Bosnian sweets and juices ([Post Bulletin, 2011](https://www.postbulletin.com/business/bosnian-immigrants-plan-to-open-european-grocery-in-nw-rochester)). No source was found showing it opened or operates today.

No Bosnian restaurant, bakery or market in the Twin Cities could be confirmed open. No Bosnian chamber of commerce, law firm, accounting firm or media outlet in Minnesota was found.

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

**Islam.** Most Bosnian refugees to Minnesota were Bosniaks, whose Islam goes back to the Ottoman centuries. The main Bosniak institution in the state is the **Islamic Community of Bosniaks in Minnesota** (often shortened ICBMN), a registered Minneapolis religious nonprofit ([ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer](https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/search?q=Bosniaks+Minnesota)). Its mosque is at 668 Broadway St. NE, listed by Meet Minneapolis among Minneapolis mosques for Ramadan prayer ([Meet Minneapolis](https://www.minneapolis.org/calendar/holidays/ramadan/)). It is listed as Minnesota's member congregation of the Islamic Community of Bosniaks in North America ([Islamic Community of Bosniaks in North America directory](https://izbsa.com/pronadite-medzlis-kojem-pripadate/)). In January 2017 its imam was Jasmin Suljkanovic and the congregation numbered about 1,200 ([Star Tribune via Valley News](https://vnews.com/2017/01/26/turned-away-in-chisago-county-bosnian-muslims-search-anew-for-cemetery-land-7727144/)). No current list of its leaders, service times or programs was found.

**A fight over a cemetery.** Bosniak families had been burying their dead in Muslim sections of cemeteries in Roseville and Burnsville, where plots were running out. In 2016 the congregation agreed to buy a 16-acre parcel in Chisago Lake Township, with 2 acres for a "green" burial ground and the rest left in farming. They expected about four burials a year; the land cost $140,000, which a leader said was hard for a small community to raise ([Star Tribune via Valley News, 2017](https://vnews.com/2017/01/26/turned-away-in-chisago-county-bosnian-muslims-search-anew-for-cemetery-land-7727144/)). The planning commission approved, but on Dec. 21, 2016, the County Board voted 3-2 to deny the permit amid neighbors' objections about groundwater and traffic and anti-Muslim comments on social media. After the U.S. Justice Department opened an inquiry under the federal religious land use law, and after the group appealed in the Minnesota Court of Appeals, the board reversed itself 4-0 on March 15, 2017 ([MPR News, March 16, 2017](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2017/03/16/chisago-county-board-reconsiders-now-supports-muslim-cemetery); [Chisago County Press, March 23, 2017](https://chisagocountypress.com/news/2017/mar/23/islamic-cemetery-denial-is-reversed-4-0/)). Whether the cemetery has since been developed was not found.

**Rochester.** A 1998 MPR report described Rochester Muslims opening a new mosque in a three-story building on a main street ([MPR archive, Jan. 26, 1998](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1998/01/26/roch-ramadannew-mosque-heal-rift)); it does not name Bosnian members. No Bosnian-specific congregation in Rochester was found. In Fargo-Moorhead, Bosnians were among the members of the area mosque in 2003 ([The Forum, April 19, 2003](https://www.inforum.com/newsmd/special-report-f-m-muslim-community-enjoying-steady-growth)).

**Christianity.** Bosnian Croats are mostly Catholic and Bosnian Serbs mostly Orthodox. Serbian Orthodox parishes stand in Duluth (St. George, built 1924), Hibbing (Holy Archangel Michael) and Chisholm (St. Basil of Ostrog, the state's oldest), and their founders included miners from Bosnia ([Orthodox Times, 2021](https://orthodoxtimes.com/bishop-longin-of-new-gracanica-midwestern-america-made-canonical-visit-to-parishes-in-minnesota/)). No source was found on Bosnian Croat or Bosnian Serb refugees of the 1990s in Minnesota parishes.

**Differences within.** Practice varies. A mixed Serb-Bosniak couple interviewed by WCCO described the war as something no one in their circles believed could happen ([WCCO, 2012](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/bosnian-refugees-mark-20th-anniversary-of-war/)). A Rochester refugee from Prijedor recalled that most families there had someone married across religious or ethnic lines ([Rochester Magazine, 2024](https://www.postbulletin.com/rochester-magazine/elvis-budimlic-spent-200-days-in-concentration-camps-before-resettling-in-rochester)). No Minnesota source was found on how many Bosnian Minnesotans practice a faith, or none.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

- **Ramazanski bajram (Eid al-Fitr) and Kurban bajram (Eid al-Adha).** The two Bajrams follow the lunar calendar. In Bosnia, the Islamic Community set Eid al-Fitr 2026 on Friday, March 20, and calls the second day of the holiday the Day of the Martyrs ([N1 Sarajevo, 2026, homeland background](https://n1info.ba/english/news/bosnias-islamic-community-announces-official-schedule-for-eid-al-fitr-2026)). A Bosnian public holiday list gives March 20-21 and May 27-28, 2026, for the two Bajrams ([Paragraf.ba, 2026 list, homeland background](https://www.paragraf.ba/neradni-dani-republike-srpske.html)). No Minnesota announcement of the Minneapolis mosque's Bajram prayers was found.
- **Ramadan.** The Bosniak mosque is listed among Minneapolis mosques holding Ramadan prayer ([Meet Minneapolis](https://www.minneapolis.org/calendar/holidays/ramadan/)). No dated Minnesota iftar announcement was found.
- **Srebrenica remembrance, July 11.** In July 2025, Bosnians in Rochester marked the 30th anniversary with the city's first peace march around Cascade Lake on Friday, July 11, and an all-day soccer tournament the next day at the Watson Sports Complex. "Many of the Bosnians in the Rochester community are actual survivors," one organizer told ABC 6 ([KAAL-TV, July 2025](https://www.kaaltv.com/news/rochester-remembers-30th-anniversary-of-srebrenica-genocide/)). The United Nations General Assembly named July 11 the International Day of Reflection and Commemoration of the 1995 Genocide in Srebrenica on May 23, 2024, by a vote of 84 to 19 with 68 abstentions ([AP via WDIV](https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/world/2024/05/23/un-will-vote-on-commemorating-the-1995-srebrenica-genocide-annually-which-serbs-vehemently-oppose/); [Global Centre for R2P](https://www.globalr2p.org/resources/resolution-78-282/)). No Minnesota governor's or city proclamation for the day was found.
- **Rochester's early-July soccer tournament.** Starting about 2011, three Bosnian-born Rochester men organized a tournament each early July, timed, an organizer said, to thank the United States around Independence Day and to remember Srebrenica. In 2013 six teams played at the Watson complex, including the Rochester team United BiH and a Bosnian team from Des Moines, and a Somali team won ([Post Bulletin, July 10, 2013](https://www.postbulletin.com/sports/bosnian-led-soccer-tournament-promotes-peace)).
- **Independence Day, March 1, and Statehood Day, Nov. 25.** In Bosnia both are holidays in the Federation but not in Republika Srpska ([N1, 2021](https://n1info.ba/english/news/bosnia-and-herzegovina-marks-independence-day/); [N1, Statehood Day](https://n1info.ba/english/news/bosnia-and-herzegovina-celebrates-statehood-day/)). No Minnesota celebration of either was found.
- **Orthodox Christmas (January 7) and Easter; Catholic Christmas and Easter.** In 2026 Orthodox Christmas fell on Jan. 7 and Orthodox Easter on April 12 ([Paragraf.ba](https://www.paragraf.ba/neradni-dani-republike-srpske.html)). Serbian Orthodox parishes in Duluth, Hibbing and Chisholm keep the Orthodox calendar ([Orthodox Times](https://orthodoxtimes.com/bishop-longin-of-new-gracanica-midwestern-america-made-canonical-visit-to-parishes-in-minnesota/)), but no source ties their celebrations to Bosnian-born families.
- **A national gathering.** Sehida Cerimovic of Minneapolis says Bosnians from across America gather every year, and families hold reunions to keep traditions alive ([MDH](https://www.web.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stories.html)). The source does not name the gathering.

## 7. Family, home and daily life

Sources describe close extended families that moved in chains. In Rochester, one man's arrival in 1993 was followed by his sister, his brother's family, his mother and oldest brother, then friends and friends of friends; by 2024 about 30 relatives of one brother lived in the Rochester area ([Rochester Magazine, 2024](https://www.postbulletin.com/rochester-magazine/elvis-budimlic-spent-200-days-in-concentration-camps-before-resettling-in-rochester); [Post Bulletin, 2024](https://www.postbulletin.com/community/how-a-magazine-lying-on-a-desk-in-croatia-in-1993-led-100s-of-bosnians-to-rochester)). The brothers' mother, who died in 2019, was remembered as "like a mother to many" Bosnian newcomers in her apartment building ([Rochester Magazine, 2024](https://www.postbulletin.com/rochester-magazine/elvis-budimlic-spent-200-days-in-concentration-camps-before-resettling-in-rochester)).

Parents' hopes for children show up in school stories. Two Rochester cousins said their parents told them school "is going to be beneficial to you guys, not to us" ([Post Bulletin, 2015](https://www.postbulletin.com/newsmd/best-bosnian-friends-move-on-from-century-together)).

**Food.** Sources name ćevapi (grilled minced-meat sausages, served with onion and sour cream in somun flatbread), Bosnian kebab, bulaklak and baklava as foods people miss ([Post Bulletin, 2011](https://www.postbulletin.com/business/bosnian-immigrants-plan-to-open-european-grocery-in-nw-rochester); [KTTC, 2026](https://www.kttc.com/2026/10/09/stir-fry-express-closes-nw-rochester/)).

**Return trips and memory.** Some families go back to visit. The Rochester cousins planned a graduation trip to Bosnia, Croatia and Montenegro ([Post Bulletin, 2015](https://www.postbulletin.com/newsmd/best-bosnian-friends-move-on-from-century-together)). Others find return hard: Budimlic went back to Prijedor in 2016 for the first time since 1992 and said, "I can't relax" ([Rochester Magazine, 2024](https://www.postbulletin.com/rochester-magazine/elvis-budimlic-spent-200-days-in-concentration-camps-before-resettling-in-rochester)).

**Death and burial.** The Bosniak congregation's planned cemetery followed the Muslim custom of "green burial," in a shroud and without a casket or embalming chemicals ([Valley News, 2017](https://vnews.com/2017/01/26/turned-away-in-chisago-county-bosnian-muslims-search-anew-for-cemetery-land-7727144/); [Chisago County Press, 2017](https://chisagocountypress.com/news/2017/mar/23/islamic-cemetery-denial-is-reversed-4-0/)). That is part of why a dedicated burial ground mattered (section 5).

No Minnesota source was found on marriage patterns, aging, elder care, dress or household size in this community.

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Sports.** Soccer is the community's most visible public activity in Minnesota. United BiH, a Rochester-based Bosnian team, played in the early-July tournament its members organized, and players framed the event as being about bringing people together across religions ([Post Bulletin, 2013](https://www.postbulletin.com/sports/bosnian-led-soccer-tournament-promotes-peace)). The 2025 Srebrenica anniversary also included an all-day tournament ([KAAL-TV, 2025](https://www.kaaltv.com/news/rochester-remembers-30th-anniversary-of-srebrenica-genocide/)). Luka Garza, whose mother is from Bosnia and Herzegovina, played for the Minnesota Timberwolves from 2022 to 2024 and joined the Bosnia and Herzegovina national team in 2023; NBA.com lists him with the Boston Celtics as of October 2026 ([NBA.com bio](https://www.nba.com/player/1630568/luka-garza/bio); [NBA.com](https://www.nba.com/player/1630568/luka-garza)).

**Music.** Dr. Ognjen Gajić of Mayo Clinic was a member of the original line-up of Zabranjeno pušenje, one of Sarajevo's best-known rock bands ([BHAAAS, 2019](https://www.bhaaas.org/en/ognjen-gajic-nekad-svirao-u-pusenju-a-danas-jedan-od-vodecih-ljekara-u-klinici-mayo-wp)).

**Books and talks.** In 2012 Rochester Community and Technical College and IMAA brought Chicago author Sanela Ramic Jurich to Rochester to talk about her war novel "Remember Me" ([Post Bulletin, March 12, 2012](https://www.postbulletin.com/lifestyle/bosnian-author-to-visit)).

No Minnesota Bosnian newspaper, radio show, folk dance group, festival booth or cultural center was found.

## 9. Civic and political life

**Organizations.** The Islamic Community of Bosniaks in Minnesota is the main community body found (section 5). The Intercultural Mutual Assistance Association in Rochester, founded in 1984, helped resettle Bosnians in the 1990s and is now led by a Bosnian refugee; it offers family services, health access, language services, a volunteer match program and victim services across southeast Minnesota, with general operating funding from Mayo Clinic ([IMAA](https://imaa.net/); [IMAA staff](https://imaa.net/7166-2/)). In 2021 the Minnesota Department of Human Services gave Armin Budimlic a Civic Engagement Award among its Outstanding Refugee Awards ([DHS, July 19, 2021](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/MNDHS/bulletins/2e86827)).

**Standing up for rights.** The cemetery dispute was the community's most public civic fight. The applicant, a war refugee who said he had been here 23 years, said in 2017 he had "never felt the hatred and animosity that I do now"; after the reversal he thanked the commissioners and said the group would work to get to know its new neighbors ([Valley News, 2017](https://vnews.com/2017/01/26/turned-away-in-chisago-county-bosnian-muslims-search-anew-for-cemetery-land-7727144/); [Chisago County Press, 2017](https://chisagocountypress.com/news/2017/mar/23/islamic-cemetery-denial-is-reversed-4-0/)). CAIR-Minnesota's Jaylani Hussein welcomed the outcome ([MPR, 2017](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2017/03/16/chisago-county-board-reconsiders-now-supports-muslim-cemetery)).

**Minnesota and Bosnia.** About 1,100 Minnesota National Guard members deployed to Bosnia in September 2003 for peacekeeping, the state's largest overseas deployment since World War II, and Gov. Tim Pawlenty visited the Srebrenica memorial that December ([AP via Post Bulletin, Dec. 16, 2003](https://www.postbulletin.com/news/governor-honors-massacre-victims)). During the war, the Minneapolis-based Center for Victims of Torture sent a team to Bosnia and Croatia to plan trauma counseling in Sarajevo ([MPR, May 19, 1994](https://archive.mpr.org/stories/1994/05/19/surviving-the-siege)).

**Elected officials.** No Bosnian-born or Bosnian American elected official in Minnesota was found.

**Current debate.** In October 2026 Sahan Journal reported that federal prosecutors had charged 10 Minnesotans with voting as non-citizens, including immigrants from Bosnia, as well as Lithuania, Germany, Liberia, Canada and Ghana. Secretary of State Steve Simon said his office helped with the cases and put the number in perspective as 10 out of 8.5 million voters ([Sahan Journal, Oct. 30, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/democracy-politics/vote-without-fear-minnesota-community-conversation)). The report names no one, and this says nothing about any individual.

**Inside the community.** Genocide denial and the war's divisions are live issues. A Rochester tournament director who lost 18 relatives at Srebrenica, including his father, said in 2013 that he held no ill will toward Serbian people ([Post Bulletin, 2013](https://www.postbulletin.com/sports/bosnian-led-soccer-tournament-promotes-peace)). In Bosnia itself, March 1 and Nov. 25 are still not observed in Republika Srpska, and the U.N. Srebrenica day was opposed by Serbia and Bosnian Serb leaders ([N1](https://n1info.ba/english/news/bih-officials-and-citizens-mark-independence-and-pay-tribute-to-war-victims); [AP via WDIV](https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/world/2024/05/23/un-will-vote-on-commemorating-the-1995-srebrenica-genocide-annually-which-serbs-vehemently-oppose/)). Across the river in Fargo, the anthropologist Jennifer Erickson told MPR in 2010 that attempts to start Bosnian community organizations there often failed, in part because of tensions between Bosniaks and Bosnian Roma ([MPR News, 2010](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2010/07/08/studying-fargo)). That is one scholar's view of one city. No Minnesota source was found on how these debates play out among Bosnian Minnesotans of different backgrounds.

## 10. Education

**Children.** Bosnian children who arrived in the 1990s went through English learner programs; the Rochester cousins mentioned above tested out by third grade and earned presidential scholarships at Rochester Community and Technical College ([Post Bulletin, 2015](https://www.postbulletin.com/newsmd/best-bosnian-friends-move-on-from-century-together)). A Rochester classroom in 2022 still had greetings posted in Bosnian among 13 languages ([Sahan Journal, June 6, 2022](https://sahanjournal.com/education/minnesota-schools-ethnic-studies-natalia-benjamin-teacher-of-the-year/)). In Moorhead, 25 students spoke Bosnian at home in 2017-18 ([Moorhead schools](https://www.moorheadschools.org/documents/download/?id=387183)). In Pelican Rapids in the 1990s, some Bosnian Roma children had never been to school because of the war, and classmates taught them school routines without a shared language ([Ellison, 2012](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/Ellison.pdf)).

Statewide, the Minnesota Department of Education reported 348 home languages in 2024-25 but publishes rankings only for the top 12, and Bosnian is not among them ([MDE, English Learners in Minnesota 2024-25](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2026/other/261381.pdf)).

**Adults and college.** Adult refugees went to Rochester Community and Technical College and Winona State University ([Rochester Magazine, 2024](https://www.postbulletin.com/rochester-magazine/elvis-budimlic-spent-200-days-in-concentration-camps-before-resettling-in-rochester)). A Bosnian refugee who grew up in Fargo earned a business degree at Minnesota State University Moorhead in 2014 ([MSUM, 2014](https://news.mnstate.edu/2014/05/msum-student-reflects-on-journey-from-refugee-to-college-graduate/)). Arrivals also leaned on community agencies while studying and working: Armin Budimlic's family relied on IMAA's services when they arrived in 1994, and he joined its staff in 1998 ([Med City Beat, 2018](https://www.medcitybeat.com/news-blog/2018/intercultural-mutual-assistance-association-feature)).

No Bosnian-language Saturday school, college student association or Minnesota scholar of Bosnian studies was found.

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

> **Content note:** This passage describes wartime detention camps and survivors of rape and torture. Readers may skip to the next paragraph, "Community workers."

**War trauma and care.** Some adults came straight from camps: the U.N. first resettled released prisoners who had suffered the harshest treatment, according to a Rochester survivor's account ([Post Bulletin, March 26, 2024](https://www.postbulletin.com/community/how-a-magazine-lying-on-a-desk-in-croatia-in-1993-led-100s-of-bosnians-to-rochester)). In January 1993 MPR reported that Bosnian women survivors of rape would be brought to Minnesota for treatment ([MPR archive listing, Jan. 7, 1993](https://archive.mpr.org/search?q=bosnian)). In 1998 NPR described the Minneapolis Center for Victims of Torture treating refugees "from places like Cambodia, Somalia and Bosnia" ([NPR, Nov. 3, 1998](https://www.npr.org/1998/11/03/1024030/treating-torture-victims-part-11)). In Pelican Rapids, the University of Minnesota brought emergency medical and dental clinics to the town after the 1996 arrivals ([Ellison, 2012](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/Ellison.pdf)).

**Community workers.** Bosnian Minnesotans now work inside the system: at Mayo Clinic, at Community University Health Care Center, and at IMAA, whose health access program helps refugees and immigrants navigate health care and social services ([IMAA](https://imaa.net/); [MDH](https://www.web.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stories.html)).

**What wellbeing means to people.** Asked what makes Minnesota a healthy home, Sehida Cerimovic named education, quality health care, parks and trails, and a community that "stays very connected" ([MDH](https://www.web.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stories.html)).

**Language access.** MDH translates materials into Bosnian/Croatian/Serbian ([MDH](https://www.health.mn.gov/communities/translation/index.html)). The Minnesota Department of Human Services' 2007 plan listed "Serbo-Croatian (Bosnian)" among its 10 primary languages ([DHS LEP Plan, 2007](https://www.migrationpolicy.org/sites/default/files/language_portal/Limited%20English%20Proficiency%20Plan.pdf)). The language block DHS adopted in September 2017 lists 15 languages and does not include it ([DHS Language Block policy, 2017](https://mn.gov/dhs/assets/language-block-required-documents-available-general-public_tcm1053-646949.pdf)). The Census Bureau counts about 1,000 Minnesotans (give or take 779) who speak Serbo-Croatian and speak English less than very well ([Census Bureau, B16001](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B16001&geo_ids=04000US27)). Their ages are not published.

No Minnesota study of Bosnian Minnesotans' physical or mental health was found.

## 12. Disability

**Minnesota evidence.** No Minnesota report, from MDH, DHS, the University of Minnesota's Institute on Community Integration or a community group, was found that counts or describes disability among Bosnian Minnesotans. No published Census disability figure for Bosnian Minnesotans was found.

**What the record shows instead.**
- Torture and camp survival. Refugees released from camps such as Omarska were among the first resettled, and one Rochester survivor lost 65 pounds there ([Rochester Magazine, 2024](https://www.postbulletin.com/rochester-magazine/elvis-budimlic-spent-200-days-in-concentration-camps-before-resettling-in-rochester)). Minnesota's torture treatment center served Bosnian survivors ([NPR, 1998](https://www.npr.org/1998/11/03/1024030/treating-torture-victims-part-11)).
- Children whose schooling the war cut short arrived in Pelican Rapids in 1996 ([Ellison, 2012](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/Ellison.pdf)). Missed schooling is not a disability, but it can be mistaken for one, or hide one.

**Questions worth asking, not answers.**
- When war injuries, landmine injuries or long-term effects of torture are present, who in the family names them as a disability, and who calls them something else?
- Does a disability label open services, such as Social Security or waiver programs, or does it feel like one more mark of the war? No Minnesota source addresses this.
- For older adults who speak little English, are disability and aging services reaching them in Bosnian, given that the current DHS language block does not include it?
- How do the Bosniak congregation and IMAA connect families to disability services, if at all? No source was found.

## 13. What is changing now

- **A business closes (October 2026).** A Bosnian-owned Rochester restaurant that a customer called the only place nearby for Bosnian kebab closed after eight months ([KTTC, Oct. 9, 2026](https://www.kttc.com/2026/10/09/stir-fry-express-closes-nw-rochester/)).
- **Voting prosecutions (October 2026).** Federal charges against 10 Minnesotans for voting as non-citizens included immigrants from Bosnia, per Sahan Journal; the Secretary of State said such cases are "very occasional" ([Sahan Journal, Oct. 30, 2026](https://sahanjournal.com/democracy-politics/vote-without-fear-minnesota-community-conversation)).
- **New public remembrance (2024-2025).** The U.N. designation of July 11 in 2024 and Rochester's first Srebrenica peace march in 2025 show remembrance moving into public spaces ([AP via WDIV, 2024](https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/world/2024/05/23/un-will-vote-on-commemorating-the-1995-srebrenica-genocide-annually-which-serbs-vehemently-oppose/); [KAAL-TV, 2025](https://www.kaaltv.com/news/rochester-remembers-30th-anniversary-of-srebrenica-genocide/)).
- **No new arrivals.** No refugees from Bosnia have been recorded in Minnesota since 2003 ([MDH](https://www.health.mn.gov/communities/rih/stats/reftotals.pdf)). Those who came as refugees have now lived here more than two decades, and their children are adults.
- **Generations.** Leadership has passed to former refugees: the Rochester agency that helped resettle Bosnians in the 1990s is led by one of them ([IMAA staff](https://imaa.net/7166-2/)). The children's generation is in the workforce; in 2015, two Rochester graduates who had arrived as preschoolers were headed into nursing ([Post Bulletin, 2015](https://www.postbulletin.com/newsmd/best-bosnian-friends-move-on-from-century-together)).
- **Language access.** Serbo-Croatian (Bosnian) was on DHS's 2007 list of 10 primary languages but is not on the 15-language block in the 2017 policy ([DHS, 2007](https://www.migrationpolicy.org/sites/default/files/language_portal/Limited%20English%20Proficiency%20Plan.pdf); [DHS, 2017](https://mn.gov/dhs/assets/language-block-required-documents-available-general-public_tcm1053-646949.pdf)).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **Helpers who became leaders.** People resettled in the 1990s turned around and resettled others: as drivers and translators in Pelican Rapids, as case managers in Rochester, and now as the executive director of IMAA ([Ellison, 2012](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/Ellison.pdf); [Rochester Magazine, 2024](https://www.postbulletin.com/rochester-magazine/elvis-budimlic-spent-200-days-in-concentration-camps-before-resettling-in-rochester); [DHS, 2021](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/MNDHS/bulletins/2e86827)).
- **Health care.** From Mayo Clinic's intensive care units to a Minneapolis community clinic, Bosnian Minnesotans care for patients ([BHAAAS, 2019](https://www.bhaaas.org/en/ognjen-gajic-nekad-svirao-u-pusenju-a-danas-jedan-od-vodecih-ljekara-u-klinici-mayo-wp); [MDH](https://www.web.health.state.mn.us/communities/rih/stories.html)).
- **Holding memory with grace.** The Rochester soccer tournament combines thanks to the United States with remembrance of Srebrenica and an open invitation to other communities ([Post Bulletin, 2013](https://www.postbulletin.com/sports/bosnian-led-soccer-tournament-promotes-peace)).
- **Defending religious freedom.** The Bosniak congregation's cemetery case ended with Chisago County reversing itself, a result that matters for any small faith community seeking land ([MPR, 2017](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2017/03/16/chisago-county-board-reconsiders-now-supports-muslim-cemetery)).
- **Changing small towns.** In Pelican Rapids, the Bosnian arrivals pushed residents to expand the Multicultural Committee, English classes and a refugee services office; the author credits that work for the town no longer being "a community in crisis" ([Ellison, 2012](https://www.ruralmn.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/Ellison.pdf)).

- **Income, poverty, home ownership, education and job data** for Bosnian Minnesotans. None exists in public sources found.
- **Current life of the Minneapolis Bosniak congregation**: leaders, services, Bajram times, school, size today. Its own website could not be found. Whether the Chisago cemetery was built is unknown.

- **The Bosnian Roma families in Pelican Rapids** today: no source after 2012.
- **Twin Cities businesses.** No Bosnian restaurant, bakery or market in the Twin Cities could be confirmed. The Duluth and Sioux Falls locations of Stir Fry Express and Rochester's Europe Grocery Store could not be confirmed open.
- **Disability, aging and mental health data** specific to Bosnian Minnesotans.
- **Minnesota celebrations** of Bosnian Independence Day, Statehood Day, or a Twin Cities Srebrenica remembrance; and any Minnesota proclamation for July 11.

- **Blocked pages.** Mayo Clinic's own staff and news pages, the Minnesota DHS website (other than one PDF), the University of Minnesota Digital Conservancy, the Blinken OSA Archivum catalog of the American Refugee Committee's Balkan program records (so that agency's Bosnia work could not be described), and the U.N. press release could not be opened.
