# Corrections to the existing Korean Minnesota page

Page: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/korean

## Outdated

1. **Claim:** All Compass figures, labeled 2019 to 2023: about 27,204 people; 74.6 percent Twin Cities and 25.4 percent Greater Minnesota; 37.1 percent age 17 or younger; 2.7 percent 65 and older; 43.7 percent foreign-born; 82.4 percent English only; 17.6 percent other language; 7.9 percent English less than "very well"; 95.7 percent high school; 59.2 percent bachelor's; median household income about $79,524 (2022 dollars); poverty 4.4 percent; 64.2 percent owner-occupied.
   **Source says:** Compass now reports 2020 to 2024: 28,180 (plus or minus 2,630); 76.2 percent Twin Cities; 23.8 percent Greater Minnesota; 36.4 percent 17 or younger; 2.6 percent 65 and older; 44.7 percent foreign-born; 81.5 percent English only; 18.5 percent other language; 8.7 percent English less than "very well"; 95.9 percent high school; 63.6 percent bachelor's; median household income $102,938 (2024 dollars, plus or minus $20,667); poverty 3.7 percent; 64.1 percent owner-occupied. Household size 2.29 and 37.4 percent living alone are unchanged. Compass also has a disability row (5.5 percent; 5.0 percent under 65) and an uninsured row (4.0 percent) the page does not use. Every repeated "7.9 percent LEP" and "4.4 percent poverty" line on the page should be updated.
   **Link:** https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/korean .
2. **Claim:** The page's discussion of adoptee citizenship and "named harms" stops at the Child Citizenship Act and 2025 bills.
   **Source says:** Korean Quarterly reported in January 2026 that three Korean adoptees who are U.S. citizens were detained and later released during the federal immigration operation in the Twin Cities, and that Adoptee Hub held rights and safety sessions. South Korea's Truth and Reconciliation Commission opened a third round of adoption fraud investigations on Feb. 26, 2026. These are dated, attributed developments the page should add.
   **Links:** https://koreanquarterly.org/front_page_below_fold/on-ice-action-and-walking-around-asian/ ; https://koreanquarterly.org/mn-news/small-organization-big-job/ .
3. **Claim:** Organizations list does not include Adoptee Hub, the Minnesota Association for Korean Americans, the Korean Cultural Association, Sejong Academy or the Korean Institute's current schedule.
   **Source says:** Adoptee Hub (birth search, reunification, mental health) and MAKA (about $385,000 in scholarships since 1995) are active; Sejong Academy is a Korean immersion charter school in St. Paul, now about 90 percent Karen students per Sahan Journal (2025).
   **Links:** https://www.adopteehub.org/aboutus ; https://koreanquarterly.org/mn-news/scholarship-support-for-three-decades/ ; https://sahanjournal.com/education/sejong-academy-maplewood-korean-immersion-karen-students/ .
## Claims that could not be supported, or that need a different source

4. **Claim:** "CAAL and community sources have estimated .. often cited near 15,000 historically and sometimes 15,000–20,000 in later community reporting."
   **Source says:** No opened source gave 15,000 to 20,000. Sahan Journal (July 29, 2024) used "about 10,000-15,000" Korean adoptees in Minnesota. The CAAL source was not located. Recommend replacing with the Sahan figure and attributing it.
   **Link:** https://sahanjournal.com/health/minnesota-adoption-genetics-family-health-history/ .
5. **Claim:** Korean Culture Camp entry: "By 1980 about 4,000 adopted Koreans lived in Minnesota, by the Minnesota Historical Society's count roughly 10 percent of all Koreans in the United States at the time," attributed to MNopedia.
   **Source says:** The MNopedia entry on Korean transracial adoption, read in full, does not contain the 4,000 figure or the 10 percent comparison. It does say that adoption began in 1955 through Children's Home Society, with formal programs in 1967 (CHS) and 1969 (LSS). The 1980 figure may come from another MNHS publication, but it is not in the cited page. The "Baby From Abroad" program name was also not in the opened page.
   **Link:** https://www.mnhs.org/mnopedia/search/index/korean-transracial-adoption-minnesota .
6. **Claim:** "Seollal and Chuseok are public in some congregations."
   **Source says:** Not supported by any opened source. The public Seollal and Chuseok events documented are run by community organizations: the Korean Cultural Association and JangmiArts (Seollal, Feb. 21, 2026; earlier with the Korean American Association of Minnesota) and AK Connection (Chuseok Festival, Sept. 26, 2026).
   **Links:** https://sahanjournal.com/arts-culture/lunar-new-year-horse-celebrate-minnesota/ ; https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/lunar-new-year-minnesota-celebrations/ ; https://akconnection.org/event/ak-connections-chuseok-festival/ .
7. **Claim:** Disability section: "We found no Minnesota study of Korean families and disability, and nothing on adoptees with disabilities."
   **Source says:** A University of Minnesota study (Keyes et al., 2008) assessed 514 international adoptees, most born in South Korea per NPR's report, alongside domestic adoptees and nonadopted teens in Minnesota. It found most adopted teens psychologically healthy, with roughly double the odds of contact with a mental health professional and of a disruptive behavior disorder. Compass also reports a Korean disability rate (5.5 percent). The page should add these, with care.
   **Links:** https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/the-mental-health-of-us-adolescents-adopted-in-infancy/ ; https://www.npr.org/transcripts/90184184 ; https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/korean .
8. **Claim:** "Korean Institute of Minnesota .. GuideStar profile says it completed its 50th year in 2025."
   **Source says:** The GuideStar profile was not reopened. The fall 2026 semester dates (Sept. 19 to Dec. 5) were confirmed through AK Connection's listing.
   **Link:** https://akconnection.org/event/korean-institute-of-minnesota-fall-semester/ .
9. **Claim:** Korean Culture Camp: "VOA reported that enrollment peaked at about 700 in the 1990s."
   **Source says:** Not rechecked; the VOA page was not opened.
