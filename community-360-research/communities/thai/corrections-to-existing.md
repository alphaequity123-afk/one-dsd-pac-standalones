# Corrections to the existing Thai Minnesota page

Page: https://one-dsd-people-access-culture-production.up.railway.app/minnesota-communities/thai
Checked: October 31, 2026. Each item gives the claim as stated, what the opened source says, the link and the date checked.

## Errors, unsupported figures, or claims that should change

1. **Claim:** "Minnesota Compass (ACS 2019–2023) ... about 6,738 people," repeated throughout, with all the derived figures: 37.5 percent age 17 or younger, 4.9 percent 65 or older, 37.4 percent foreign-born, 33.3 percent English only, 66.7 percent another language, 30.6 percent English less than "very well," 86.4 percent high school, 29.5 percent bachelor's, median household income about $72,888, poverty 17.8 percent, 46.4 percent owner-occupied, household size 3.15, 18.6 percent living alone, 59.7 percent female.
   **Source says:** Minnesota Compass does not currently list a Thai profile; its cultural-community list of 29 groups has no Thai row, and the Thai profile address returns "Page not found." An Internet Archive record shows the page existed on March 13, 2026, but the archived copy could not be opened, so none of these figures could be confirmed. The Census Bureau's own counts are lower than 6,738: 4,342 Thai alone or in any combination in the 2020 Census (2,239 Thai alone), and 4,639 (plus or minus 904) in the ACS 2020-2024. The State Demographer used 4,518 (ACS 2024) in June 2026. The state's 2026 economic status report does not report Thai separately (it is inside "Other Asian"). Recommend replacing the Compass row with the Census counts and stating plainly that no current Minnesota income, poverty, education or housing figures for Thai Minnesotans are published.
   **Links:** https://www.mncompass.org/cultural-communities ; https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/thai ; https://archive.org/wayback/available?url=mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/thai ; https://data.census.gov/api/access/data/table?id=DECENNIALDDHCA2020.T01001&g=040XX00US27&t=3860 ; https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B02018&geo_ids=04000US27,01000US ; https://mn.gov/capm/assets/SusanBrower_StateOfAsianPacificMinnesotans_June2026_tcm1051-759113.pdf ; https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

2. **Claim:** "about 60.8 percent in the Twin Cities and 39.2 percent in Greater Minnesota," and the repeated framing that the Greater Minnesota share is large.
   **Source says:** Not supported by any opened source. The 2020 Census places about 3,121 of 4,342 people naming Thai (about 72 percent) in the seven metro counties (Hennepin, Ramsey, Dakota, Anoka, Scott, Washington, Carver). The ACS 2020-2024 puts 3,289 (plus or minus 803) of 4,639 in the Minneapolis-St. Paul metro area, about 71 percent. Greater Minnesota is closer to 28 to 30 percent. Olmsted County (Rochester) is the largest outside the metro.
   **Links:** https://data.census.gov/api/access/data/table?id=DECENNIALDDHCA2020.T01001&g=040XX00US27$0500000&t=3860 ; https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B02018,B02015&geo_ids=31000US33460 . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

3. **Claim:** "about 59.7 percent female."
   **Source says:** Could not be confirmed from Compass (see item 1). The 2020 Census gives 58.7 percent female among those reporting Thai alone or in combination (2,548 of 4,342) and 67 percent among those reporting Thai alone (1,503 of 2,239). If kept, cite the Census and give both.
   **Links:** https://data.census.gov/api/access/data/table?id=DECENNIALDDHCA2020.T02002&g=040XX00US27&t=3860 ; https://data.census.gov/api/access/data/table?id=DECENNIALDDHCA2020.T02002&g=040XX00US27&t=3803 . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

4. **Claim:** Thai Association of Minnesota "was established as a nonprofit in 1995 ... It predates the state's Thai temple."
   **Source says:** Asia Matters says 1995. But ThaiAM's own events page lists a "Celebration of ThaiAM's 25th year Anniversary" on Oct. 13, 2019 (pointing to 1994), and its history page posts a 2013 article titled in Thai "Looking back 41 years of the Thai Association" (pointing to about 1972 for an earlier association). Report the founding date as uncertain and give all three.
   **Links:** https://asiamattersforamerica.org/articles/thai-culture-in-minnesota ; https://www.thaiofmn.org/event/thaiam ; https://www.thaiofmn.org/About-Us/our-history . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

5. **Claim:** Wat Promwachirayan "has room for up to five resident monks."
   **Source says:** The GiveMN profile says "at most 5 monks," but the temple's own About Us page now says "The temple has 10 resident monks." Use the temple's current statement, or give both.
   **Links:** https://www.watthaimn.us/about-us ; https://www.givemn.org/organization/Watpromwachirayan . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

6. **Claim:** "Late 2003: Founded ... First located in Becker."
   **Source says:** Partly. The temple itself dates its anniversaries from 2003 (16th in 2019, 22nd in 2025) but says it was registered as a nonprofit, under the name Wat Pah Nanachat Minnesota, on April 22, 2004 at a Shorewood address, before buying 28 acres in Becker; it moved to Elk River in 2006 and decided to move to St. Louis Park on Jan. 9, 2017. The page could add these details and the former name.
   **Link:** https://www.watthaimn.us/about-us . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

7. **Claim:** "The state's Legacy program lists a $25,000 grant for its May 2024 festival."
   **Source says:** Could not be checked; the Legacy page blocked access. Not contradicted, but unconfirmed.
   **Link:** https://www.legacy.mn.gov/node/10032725 (blocked). Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

8. **Claim:** Minnesota Songkran Festival "listed by Explore Minnesota at Wat Promwachirayan in a later edition."
   **Source says:** Explore Minnesota was blocked. A third-party 2026 calendar lists the 2026 festival at Wat Promwachirayan on May 16 and 17, 2026. No organizer page or news report confirming it took place was found. Suggest stating the 2026 dates and venue with that limit.
   **Link:** https://www.northernstar.org/Portals/0/2026%20Outreach_Web_Cultural%20Events_260415a.pdf . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

9. **Claim:** Royal Thai Consulate-General, Chicago: "Its own website was down when we tried it."
   **Source says:** Outdated. The site was up on Oct. 31, 2026, with 2026 news items.
   **Link:** https://cgchicago.thaiembassy.org/en/ . Checked Oct. 31, 2026.

## Claims that could not be supported by any opened Minnesota source

10. Statements presented as Minnesota fact without a source: "Spirit practice exists in some rooms beside Theravada"; luk thung, mor lam, pop and chant "are all here"; "conservative family and wat climates can mean silence or exile" for queer and trans Thai Minnesotans; "Mental-health stigma is real in some families"; "Gender match may be requested in maternal and intimate care"; "Under-identification in schools is a pattern to watch"; and that Thai Minnesotans are met "on waivers, child welfare, health care, cash, food, aging, and employment." No Minnesota source was found for any of these. They should be removed, sourced, or rephrased as questions.

11. "Thailand (1939)," "1932," the 1913 Surname Act, and the description of a Malay-Muslim south are homeland history and were not checked for this review.

12. The people profiled in the stories section (Tammy Duckworth, Charles Djou, Tiger Woods, Pim Techamuanvivit, Thakoon Panichgul, Maurissa Tancharoen, Jet Tila, Cherry Chevapravatdumrong, Michelle Waterson-Gomez, Chanchanit Martorell, Johnny Damon, Rattawut Lapcharoensap, Pitchaya Sudbanthad, Pimone Triplett, Nalutporn Krairiksh) and Los Angeles Thai Town have no Minnesota connection shown on the page. These sources were not rechecked. Minnesota figures documented in opened sources include Supenn Harrison and her daughters Cyndy Harrison and Jenny Reilly (Sawatdee); the founders of Wat Promwachirayan and the Thai Cultural Council (Dee Noree, Korawan Muangmode); and restaurateurs such as On Khumchaya and Paul (Pornchai) Chaiyamart.
