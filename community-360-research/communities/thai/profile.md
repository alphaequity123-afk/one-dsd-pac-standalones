# Thai in Minnesota

## 1. People and place

**A small community, counted in different ways.** Thai Minnesotans number in the low thousands, and the figure depends on the question asked.

- In the 2020 Census, 4,342 Minnesotans reported Thai alone or in combination with another race or Asian group, and 2,239 reported Thai alone ([U.S. Census Bureau, 2020 Census Detailed DHC-A, table T01001, Thai alone or in any combination](https://data.census.gov/api/access/data/table?id=DECENNIALDDHCA2020.T01001&g=040XX00US27&t=3860); [same table, Thai alone](https://data.census.gov/api/access/data/table?id=DECENNIALDDHCA2020.T01001&g=040XX00US27&t=3803)).
- The American Community Survey for 2020 to 2024 estimates 4,639 Minnesotans of Thai alone or in any combination, give or take 904, and 2,177 Thai alone, give or take 622 ([ACS 2020-2024 tables B02018 and B02015 via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B02018&geo_ids=04000US27,01000US)).
- The single-year 2024 survey gives 4,518, give or take 1,536 ([ACS 2024 1-year, table B02018, via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/latest?table_ids=B02018&geo_ids=04000US27)). Minnesota State Demographer Susan Brower used that same 4,518 figure in a June 27, 2026 presentation, where Thai ranked eleventh among the state's Asian and Pacific groups, after Cambodian and before Pakistani ([State Demographic Center, "The State of Asian Pacific Minnesotans," June 27, 2026](https://mn.gov/capm/assets/SusanBrower_StateOfAsianPacificMinnesotans_June2026_tcm1051-759113.pdf)).

These margins of error are wide. A fair reading is roughly 4,000 to 5,500 people who name Thai at all, about half of whom name only Thai. The state's main economic report on cultural groups does not report Thai separately; it folds Thai into "Other Asian" ([State Demographic Center, The Economic Status of Minnesotans 2026, Table ii](https://mn.gov/admin/assets/The%20Economic%20Status%20of%20Minnesotans%202026_tcm36-717691.pdf)). Minnesota Compass does not list a Thai profile among its cultural community profiles as of this check, and its former Thai page address now returns "Page not found" ([Minnesota Compass, cultural communities list](https://www.mncompass.org/cultural-communities); [former Thai page](https://www.mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/thai)). An internet archive record shows that page existed as recently as March 13, 2026 ([Internet Archive availability record](https://archive.org/wayback/available?url=mncompass.org/topics/demographics/cultural-communities/thai)), but the archived copy could not be opened, so its figures are not used here.

**Growth over time.** The Council on Asian-Pacific Minnesotans reported 575 Thai Minnesotans from the 1990 Census ([CAPM Biennium Report, Nov. 1996, Chart 1.3](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/pre2003/mandated/970013.pdf)) and 1,498 from the 2000 Census ([CAPM report citing Census 2000 Summary File 1](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/2004/mandated/040237.pdf)). Counting rules changed between censuses, so the trend is a direction, not a precise rate.

**Born in Thailand is not the same as Thai.** About 18,819 Minnesotans were born in Thailand, give or take 1,296, by the 2020-2024 survey, roughly four times the number who identify as Thai ([ACS 2020-2024, table B05006, via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B05006&geo_ids=04000US27,01000US)). Many of them are Hmong, Lao, Karen or other Southeast Asian Minnesotans born in Thai refugee camps. Two examples from public records: State Rep. Ethan Cha was born in Thailand and is Hmong American; the Legislative Reference Library notes his campaign site said he was born in a Thai refugee camp ([Minnesota Legislators Past & Present](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/legdb/fulldetail.aspx?ID=15621); [Minnesota Historical Election Archive](https://mn.electionarchives.lib.umn.edu/candidate/ethan-cha)). Metropolitan Council member Chai Lee is also listed as born in Thailand and Hmong ([Minnesota Historical Election Archive](https://mn.electionarchives.lib.umn.edu/candidate/chai-lee)). The Thai Cultural Council of Minnesota made the same point in its 2024 festival booklet, noting that most Southeast Asian Minnesotans "found refuge in Thailand" before coming here ([Thai Cultural Council of Minnesota, Songkran 2024 booklet filed with the Minnesota Senate](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2024/comm/20240319/Comm_20240319_SF5050-Songkran-2024-Full-Version.pdf)). A record that shows "Thailand" as a birthplace says nothing, on its own, about a person's people, language or faith.

**Where.** By the 2020 Census, the largest counts of people naming Thai (alone or in combination) were in Hennepin County (1,334), Ramsey (630), Dakota (440), Anoka (267), Olmsted (188), Scott (187) and Washington (170). The seven-county metro held about 3,121 of 4,342, or about 72 percent. Outside the metro, Olmsted, Stearns (88), Wright (77), St. Louis (75), Blue Earth (71), Mower (69) and Rice (65) had the most ([2020 Census DHC-A, T01001, Minnesota counties](https://data.census.gov/api/access/data/table?id=DECENNIALDDHCA2020.T01001&g=040XX00US27$0500000&t=3860)). By city, Minneapolis (473), St. Paul (373) and Rochester (157) led, followed by Burnsville, Brooklyn Park, Shakopee, Eden Prairie, Apple Valley, Bloomington and Edina, each around 80 to 106 ([2020 Census DHC-A, T01001, Minnesota places](https://data.census.gov/api/access/data/table?id=DECENNIALDDHCA2020.T01001&g=040XX00US27$1600000&t=3860)). The newer survey estimates for counties and cities carry margins about as large as the estimates themselves, so they are not repeated here. The Census Bureau publishes these detailed counts only where enough people of the group live, so small towns with a few Thai families do not appear at all.

In daily life the community has no single neighborhood. Its most visible fixed point is Wat Promwachirayan (Wat Thai of Minnesota) at 2544 Highway 100 South in St. Louis Park, which moved there from Elk River in 2017 ([Wat Promwachirayan, About Us](https://www.watthaimn.us/about-us)). The Thai Association of Minnesota's business directory lists restaurants, food trucks and groceries across the metro and in Greater Minnesota towns such as Brainerd, Bemidji, Chisago City, Ely, Cloquet, Pine City and Worthington ([ThaiAM business listing](https://www.thaiofmn.org/resource/business-listing)) (section 4).

**Women outnumber men.** In the 2020 Census, women and girls were 1,503 of the 2,239 Minnesotans who reported Thai alone, about 67 percent. In the working-age bands from 25 to 64, Thai-alone women outnumbered men 1,020 to 379. Among those naming Thai alone or in combination, the split was closer, 2,548 female to 1,794 male ([2020 Census DHC-A, table T02002, Thai alone](https://data.census.gov/api/access/data/table?id=DECENNIALDDHCA2020.T02002&g=040XX00US27&t=3803); [Thai alone or in any combination](https://data.census.gov/api/access/data/table?id=DECENNIALDDHCA2020.T02002&g=040XX00US27&t=3860)). No Minnesota study explains this pattern. As national background only: a 2018 University of California, Riverside dissertation, using national census microdata for 2011 to 2015, found that Thai-born married women aged 20 to 55 had the highest share married to white U.S. men (55.83 percent) of the Asian-born groups it compared, and traced the pattern partly to the U.S. military presence in Thailand in the 1960s and 1970s, with private-sector husbands more common after 2010. Its 38 interviews included one woman in Minnesota ([Suppatkul, UC Riverside, 2018](https://escholarship.org/content/qt3rk9g3j2/qt3rk9g3j2.pdf)). That is a national picture, not a description of any Minnesota family.

**Young people of mixed heritage.** Children are a much larger share of the "Thai in combination" group than of the "Thai alone" group. In 2020, 1,456 of the 4,342 people naming Thai at all were under 18, about one in three, compared with 352 of the 2,239 naming Thai alone, about one in six ([2020 Census DHC-A, T02002](https://data.census.gov/api/access/data/table?id=DECENNIALDDHCA2020.T02002&g=040XX00US27&t=3860)). Many Thai Minnesotan children, in other words, are reported with more than one heritage.

**How people came.** The paths documented in Minnesota sources are study, marriage and family, and work.

- *Students.* Supenn Harrison came to the University of Minnesota for graduate school in 1972, by her company's account, and stayed after marrying ([Sawatdee, Our History](https://www.sawatdee.com/our-history); [Andrew Zimmern, "5 Questions: Supenn Harrison"](https://andrewzimmern.com/5-questions-supenn-harrison/)). Paul (Pornchai) Chaiyamart, who opened Thai Pepper in West St. Paul in 2019, was also a University of Minnesota graduate student ([West St. Paul Reader, May 1, 2020](https://weststpaulreader.com/2020/05/01/paul-chaiyamart-thai-pepper/)). In May 2025, Thailand's ambassador put current Thai enrollment at the university at "around 30," mainly in engineering and finance ([Royal Thai Embassy, May 2025](https://washingtondc.thaiembassy.org/en/content/university-of-msp)).
- *Family.* Vat Pinipat, a retired Bangkok attorney, moved to St. Paul in 2001 when his daughter, a new University of Minnesota graduate student, invited her parents, and the family opened Krua Thai on University Avenue ([Twin Cities Business, "Family Thai," undated](https://tcbmag.com/family-thai/)).
- *Work.* Restaurant jobs have been an entry point. A Lao-born cook who had lived in Thailand found work at Amazing Thailand in Uptown two days after arriving in 2007 and learned English in its kitchen ([CCX Media, May 27, 2022](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-resident-shares-thai-culture-at-minnesota-songkran-festival/)).

There is no Thai refugee history in Minnesota. Thailand was the first country of asylum for many Hmong, Lao and Karen families, but that is their story, told in their own profiles.

**Generations and languages.** The Census Bureau groups Thai with Lao and other Tai-Kadai languages, so there is no Minnesota count of Thai speakers alone. In 2020 to 2024, 7,808 Minnesotans aged 5 and older spoke "Thai, Lao, or other Tai-Kadai languages" at home, and 3,743 of them spoke English less than "very well" ([ACS 2020-2024, table B16001, via Census Reporter](https://api.censusreporter.org/1.0/data/show/acs2024_5yr?table_ids=B16001&geo_ids=04000US27)); most of that group is likely Lao, since Lao Minnesotans outnumber Thai more than two to one by the State Demographer's 2024 counts ([State Demographic Center, June 2026](https://mn.gov/capm/assets/SusanBrower_StateOfAsianPacificMinnesotans_June2026_tcm1051-759113.pdf)). The Council on Asian Pacific Minnesotans, using 2009 to 2011 survey data, reported that more than a quarter of Thai households in Minnesota were linguistically isolated, meaning no one 14 or older spoke English very well ([CAPM Annual Report 2012-2013](https://mn.gov/capm/assets/AnnualReport2012-2013_tcm1051-114384.pdf)). That figure is more than a decade old. Community bodies treat passing on Thai language to children as a goal: the Thai Cultural Council's stated mission includes teaching "language and cultural traditions to their children and subsequent generations" ([Thai Cultural Council of Minnesota, About](https://www.thaiculturemn.us/about)), and the Thai Association of Minnesota's board lists each member with a Thai nickname beside an English or legal name, such as "Ple," "Nan" and "Gift" ([ThaiAM, 2026 team](https://www.thaiofmn.org/About-Us/current-team)).

## 2. All walks of life

Minnesota-specific income, poverty, home ownership and education figures for Thai Minnesotans could not be found in any source that was open as of this check (see section 1 and "What could not be established"). What follows combines the national Thai picture, clearly labeled, with documented Minnesota lives across the range.

**National background (not Minnesota).** Pew Research Center, analyzing people who identify as Thai alone in 2021 to 2023, reports a median household income of $82,000 in 2023, compared with $105,600 for Asian American households overall; 12 percent living in poverty; 64 percent homeownership; and 49 percent of adults 25 and older with a bachelor's degree or more. Immigrants are 74 percent of this population, with a median age of 48.1, and 59 percent of Thai Americans 5 and older speak English proficiently, 49 percent among immigrants ([Pew Research Center, "Facts about Thai in the U.S.," May 1, 2025](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-thai-in-the-u-s/)). That profile, older and immigrant-heavy, differs from Minnesota's Thai group in at least one way the census shows: here, a large share of children are of mixed heritage.

**Well-off and business-owning.** Supenn Harrison built Minnesota's best-known Thai restaurant company from a State Fair egg-roll stand begun in 1976; Mpls.St.Paul Magazine credits her with "Minneapolis's first official Thai restaurant," the Siam Cafe, in 1979, and her first Sawatdee in Lowertown St. Paul in 1983. Her daughters, CEO Cyndy Harrison and VP Jenny Reilly, now run the company ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine, June 23, 2024](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/1984-flashback-global-becomes-local/)). Art Veerothai is listed by the Thai Association of Minnesota as the contact for Veero Auto Group, a Ford and Chrysler-Dodge-Jeep-Ram dealership in Morris ([ThaiAM business listing](https://www.thaiofmn.org/resource/business-listing); [Veero Auto Group](https://www.veeroautogroup.com/)).

**Professionals.** Dr. Vacharee Peterson, a dentist, is described in an undated church article as a member of the Thai Church of Minnesota ([Converge, undated](https://converge.org/article/god-provides-converge-global-worker-in-twin-cities-inroad-to-thai-community)), and ThaiAM lists her Maplewood practice ([ThaiAM business listing](https://www.thaiofmn.org/resource/business-listing)). Dr. Tatpicha Nunta was the main sponsor of the 2024 Royal Kathina ceremony at Wat Promwachirayan ([Thai Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Oct. 2024](https://www.mfa.go.th/en/content/pr-chicago-10302024-2)). ThaiAM's directory lists a realtor, a financial professional, an interpreting service, photographers and massage therapists ([ThaiAM business listing](https://www.thaiofmn.org/resource/business-listing)).

**Middle-income and working families.** Many Thai Minnesotans work in or own small restaurants. On Khumchaya, owner of On's Kitchen on University Avenue, began cooking at age eight in Thailand ([Minnesota Monthly directory](https://www.minnesotamonthly.com/directory/dining/ons-kitchen-thai-cuisine/)). Lanna Thai in St. Anthony Park describes itself as "a small, family run restaurant" whose owner is from Chiang Mai ([Lanna Thai](https://lannathaimn.com/)). Joy's Thai Cuisine in Lakeville is open seven days, 10:30 a.m. to 9 p.m., by its own site ([Joy's Thai Cuisine](https://www.joysthaicuisine.com/)), the kind of long-hours schedule common in family restaurants.

**Hardship.** No Minnesota poverty rate for Thai Minnesotans could be found. The best available state signal of hardship is language: more than a quarter of Thai households were linguistically isolated in 2009 to 2011 ([CAPM Annual Report 2012-2013](https://mn.gov/capm/assets/AnnualReport2012-2013_tcm1051-114384.pdf)). Restaurant work itself can be hard: the Lao-born cook at Amazing Thailand told CCX Media, "I didn't have time to go to school, only working" ([CCX Media, May 27, 2022](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-resident-shares-thai-culture-at-minnesota-songkran-festival/)). Students on temporary visas, older spouses far from Thai-speaking neighbors, and workers in small kitchens are all part of this community, and none of these situations shows up in a statewide average.

## 3. Work and the economy

**Food.** Food service is the most visible Thai work in Minnesota. Sawatdee's history traces a line from a janitorial job at Abbott Northwestern Hospital, taken when Supenn Harrison could not find work in her field after graduate school, to a restaurant group that at its peak included Minneapolis, St. Paul, Bloomington, St. Cloud, Maple Grove and Eden Prairie locations ([Sawatdee, Our History](https://www.sawatdee.com/our-history); [Andrew Zimmern](https://andrewzimmern.com/5-questions-supenn-harrison/)). Cyndy Harrison credits her mother with likely inventing the one-to-five chili scale that Twin Cities diners still use ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine, June 23, 2024](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/1984-flashback-global-becomes-local/)). Today the Thai Association of Minnesota's directory lists about 50 restaurants, food trucks, farmers market stalls and groceries, from St. Paul's University Avenue to Bemidji, Brainerd, Ely and Worthington ([ThaiAM business listing](https://www.thaiofmn.org/resource/business-listing)). Not every restaurant serving Thai food is Thai-owned; owners include Lao, Hmong and other Southeast Asian Minnesotans, and this profile names ownership only where a source says so.

**Massage and wellness.** ThaiAM lists Thai massage businesses in Savage, Mound and White Bear Lake ([ThaiAM business listing](https://www.thaiofmn.org/resource/business-listing)). In April 2026, the Thai consul-general in Chicago met the president of the Nuad Thai and Spa Association of America during a training program for Thai massage practitioners in the Midwest ([Royal Thai Consulate-General, Chicago, April 2026](https://cgchicago.thaiembassy.org/en/)). No Minnesota count of Thai massage workers was found.

**Other trades and professions.** The same directory lists an auto repair shop in Bloomington with Dee Noree, president of Wat Promwachirayan, as its contact ([ThaiAM business listing](https://www.thaiofmn.org/resource/business-listing); [Thai Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Jan. 2025](https://www.mfa.go.th/en/content/chicago-300425-4)), a car dealership, a dentist, a realtor, a food-carving instructor who sells instructional DVDs ([Creative Carving](https://www.creative-carving.com/)), a mushroom-farming chef behind Thai-American fusion restaurants in Bemidji and Brainerd ([The Blue Oyster](https://www.blueoysterrestaurant.com/)), a junk-removal service, a DJ, photographers and an interpreter.

**Students and skilled workers.** Thai students at the University of Minnesota are mainly in engineering and finance, the embassy reported in 2025 ([Royal Thai Embassy, May 2025](https://washingtondc.thaiembassy.org/en/content/university-of-msp)). ThaiAM has said it planned a "Minnesota Professional Connect" career initiative and seminars on health insurance, green cards and citizenship ([ThaiAM, What We Do](https://www.thaiofmn.org/About-Us/what-we-do)); whether these are running in 2026 could not be confirmed.

**Trade ties.** The Council on Asian-Pacific Minnesotans sponsored a trade mission to Singapore, Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand in August 1992 ([CAPM report, 1993](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/pre2003/mandated/930560.pdf)). On May 9, 2025, Thai embassy and consulate officials, with representatives of the Council on Asian Pacific Minnesotans and the Thai community, visited Mayo Clinic in Rochester and met Rochester Mayor Kim Norton and Mayo's medical director for international practice in Asia Pacific to explore medical, academic and economic cooperation ([Thai Ministry of Foreign Affairs, May 2025](https://www.mfa.go.th/en/content/chicago230525-1)). Thailand's Trade Center in Chicago and the Tourism Authority of Thailand co-organized the Minnesota Songkran Festival in 2024 ([Thai Ministry of Foreign Affairs, June 2024](https://www.mfa.go.th/en/content/pr-chicago-06052024-2)).

No union, labor organizing history or Thai chamber of commerce specific to Minnesota was found.

## 4. Businesses and commercial life

There is no Thai commercial district in Minnesota. The densest cluster of Thai and pan-Southeast Asian restaurants is on University Avenue West in St. Paul, where Bangkok Thai Deli (333 University), Basil Cafe (585 University), On's Kitchen (1613 University) and the grocery 88 Oriental Foods (291 University) sit within a few blocks ([ThaiAM business listing](https://www.thaiofmn.org/resource/business-listing); [Bangkok Thai Deli](https://bangkokthaideli.com/); [Basil Cafe](https://www.585basilcafe.com/)). The Thai Association of Minnesota keeps the community's own directory, which it says is "Update-In-Progress" and not an endorsement ([ThaiAM business listing](https://www.thaiofmn.org/resource/business-listing)).

Businesses confirmed open from their own current sites, or from a 2025 or 2026 report, as of this check include:

- **Sawatdee** (Minneapolis, St. Paul, Maple Grove, Bloomington, plus a food truck), the Harrison family's restaurants ([Sawatdee](https://www.sawatdee.com/)).
- **Bangkok Thai Deli**, St. Paul, with posted hours seven days and a note that it is "Now OPEN on TUESDAYS!" ([Bangkok Thai Deli](https://bangkokthaideli.com/)).
- **Lanna Thai**, St. Anthony Park, St. Paul, owner from Chiang Mai ([Lanna Thai](https://lannathaimn.com/)).
- **Krungthep Thai**, Rice Street, St. Paul, which says it stayed open through Rice Street reconstruction ([Krungthep Thai](https://www.krungthepthai.co/)).
- **Pad Thai Restaurant**, Grand Avenue, St. Paul, posting a Nov. 1, 2026 closure for a private event ([Pad Thai](https://padthaisp.com/)).
- **On's Thai Express**, Highland Park, St. Paul, posting a March 15, 2026 weather closure ([On's Thai Express](https://onsthaiexpress.com/)).
- **Taste of Thailand and Kanom Thai** (Selby Avenue, St. Paul), **Moon House Thai Cuisine** (Rice Street, St. Paul, at the address ThaiAM lists for Thai Street Market), **Amazing Thailand** (Uptown, where the Thai ambassador hosted a community dinner in May 2025), **Joy's Thai Cuisine** (Lakeville), **Sala Thai Kitchen** (Lakeville), **Thai Table** and **Plymouth Thai Kitchen** (Plymouth), **Karta Thai** (Minneapolis and Roseville), **Chaska Thai**, **Jay's Kitchen Thai Street Food** (Chisago City), **Ban Thai** (White Bear Lake), **Khun Nai Thai** (Minneapolis), **Basil Cafe** (St. Paul), **ThaiPop** (Rochester), **Thai-riffic** (Rochester, opened May 2025), **The Blue Oyster** (Brainerd and Bemidji), **Veero Auto Group** (Morris), **Creative Carving**, and **MinneBun**, a Saturday and Sunday bao stand at the St. Paul Farmers' Market whose fillings include Thai basil pork and green curry chicken (see businesses.json for links and evidence).

In all, 54 businesses are listed in this survey: 25 confirmed open and 29 listed by ThaiAM or older reporting but not confirmable from an opened page; they are recorded in businesses.json as "could not confirm open." The overall list is a survey, not a census: for example, it does not capture restaurants that are Thai-owned but not listed by ThaiAM, and it does not capture Thai cooks and managers working in restaurants owned by others.

**Turnover.** Restaurants come and go. Sen Yai Sen Lek in Northeast Minneapolis, which billed itself as the area's first Thai restaurant when it opened in 2008, closed after 14 years in 2022; its owners were Joe and Holly Hatch-Surisook ([Racket, Feb. 24, 2022](https://racketmn.com/sen-yai-sen-lek-and-dipped-debris-are-closing)). Krungthep Thai closed its Eat Street location in November 2016 after four years while its other location continued ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine, Nov. 14, 2016](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/foodie/closing-krungthep-thai-on-eat-street/)); a Krungthep Thai now operates on Rice Street ([Krungthep Thai](https://www.krungthepthai.co/)). Nong's Thai Cuisine in Golden Valley, open since 2010, reopened in May 2022 after a three-month closure caused by a sewer-line failure; owner Pallop Ratnasingsa called it "a family business" and said of his staff, "We need [the staff] more than they need us" ([CCX Media, May 24, 2022](https://ccxmedia.org/news/nongs-thai-restaurant-reopens-after-three-month-closure/)).

**Media.** No Minnesota-based Thai-language newspaper, radio program or website was found. ThaiAM communicates by email newsletter, Facebook and a texting list, having stopped paper newsletters ([ThaiAM home page](https://www.thaiofmn.org/)). The Thai Cultural Council posts festival news on its site ([Thai Cultural Council of Minnesota](https://www.thaiculturemn.us/)). Regional coverage comes through the Thai Ministry of Foreign Affairs' Chicago consulate news pages ([example, Oct. 2024](https://www.mfa.go.th/en/content/pr-chicago-10302024-2)).

## 5. Religion and spiritual life

**Theravada Buddhism and the wat.** Wat Promwachirayan, also called Wat Thai of Minnesota, practices Theravada Buddhism ([Wat Promwachirayan home page](https://www.watthaimn.us/)). By its own history, a Thai Dhammaduta (missionary) monk from California was invited to teach meditation in Minnesota in 2004; the temple, first named Wat Pah Nanachat Minnesota, was registered as a nonprofit on April 22, 2004, at a Shorewood address, then bought 28 acres in Becker, moved in 2006 to a house in Elk River, and on Jan. 9, 2017 decided to move closer to the community, buying a former church at 2544 Highway 100 South in St. Louis Park. That building was the Lutheran Church of the Reformation, whose congregation voted to dissolve in 2013 and held its final service on Jan. 12, 2014 ([St. Louis Park Historical Society](https://slphistory.org/reformation/)). A senior Bangkok monk, now abbot of Wat Pho (Wat Phra Chetuphon), gave the temple its present name in 2017. In June 2022 the Council of Thai Bhikkhus in the U.S. recognized it as its 71st affiliated temple. The temple says it is led by Phra Maha Chamnian Jirapanyo and has 10 resident monks ([Wat Promwachirayan, About Us](https://www.watthaimn.us/about-us)). Its GiveMN profile, older, says "at most 5 monks" and describes Wednesday public meditation, Sunday Thai dance for all ages, Thai cooking and language classes, and a library of English and Thai books ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Watpromwachirayan)). Both accounts are given because they differ. In 2018 the temple's president, Dee Noree, told the St. Louis Park High School paper that most events draw 200 to 300 people and some about a thousand, that the temple had 80 parking spaces and hoped for about 200, and that it runs on "a really limited budget," funded mostly by a warm-weather Sunday market; plans for a new service hall and meditation hall were to follow in later phases ([The Echo, Nov. 1, 2018](https://slpecho.com/news/2018/11/01/wat-promwachirayan-proposes-future-construction/)). The temple reports finishing a parking lot in August 2019 ([Wat Promwachirayan, About Us](https://www.watthaimn.us/about-us)).

The temple's anniversary is counted from 2003: it held its 16th anniversary in 2019 and its 22nd on June 8, 2025 ([Wat Promwachirayan, About Us](https://www.watthaimn.us/about-us); [events page](https://www.watthaimn.us/wat-thai-events)). Asia Matters for America credits a donation from Supenn Harrison in late 2003 with making the founding possible, and says temple president Dee Noree bought the St. Louis Park building in early 2017 ([Asia Matters for America, 2017](https://asiamattersforamerica.org/articles/thai-culture-in-minnesota)). The temple's treasurer told a St. Louis Park student newspaper in 2017 that the building had been empty for two years and that the open house was meant for the whole community, "not just Buddhists or those of Thai descent" in the paper's paraphrase ([The Echo, May 2, 2017](https://slpecho.com/news/2017/05/02/temple-strives-to-include-community/)).

The temple's 2025 calendar shows the shape of the Thai Buddhist year in Minnesota: a New Year's Eve chanting service; Magha Puja (Feb. 8); Songkran Day (April 13); Visakha Puja (May 11); Asalha Puja and the start of Buddhist Lent (July 12); Sart Thai and Salakkapat offerings (Sept. 21); the end of Buddhist Lent (Oct. 12); Thailand's National Day and Father's Day (Dec. 7); and twice-yearly "Pah Bahs" robe and offering events ([Wat Promwachirayan, events page](https://www.watthaimn.us/wat-thai-events)). Most observances fall on the Sunday or Saturday nearest the lunar date (section 6).

**Royal and consular ties.** The temple's patron, according to the Thai Cultural Council, is Somdet Phra Maha Thirachan of Wat Phra Chetuphon in Bangkok ([Songkran 2024 booklet](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2024/comm/20240319/Comm_20240319_SF5050-Songkran-2024-Full-Version.pdf)); a resolution introduced in the Minnesota Senate congratulated the festival's organizers and welcomed him to the 2024 festival ([Minnesota Senate Resolution 103, as introduced May 13, 2024](https://www.senate.mn/resolutions/pdf/ls93/2023_2024/SR0103_O0.pdf)). On Oct. 27, 2024, a Royal Kathina ceremony, an offering of robes to monks after the rains retreat, was held at the temple with Thailand's deputy consul-general ([Thai Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Oct. 2024](https://www.mfa.go.th/en/content/pr-chicago-10302024-2)).

**Other Buddhist places named by the community.** ThaiAM's nonprofit list names Wat Promwachirayan; the Meditation Center of Minnesota in Elk River (formerly Samnaksong Chaimongkol, listed with a Thai name); Minnesota Meditation Center USA in Osseo, listed with the name Watphradhamakaya; Wat Sirijundhawas in St. Paul; several Lao temples in Forest Lake, Farmington, Anoka and East Bethel; and Watt Munisotaram, the Cambodian temple in Hampton ([ThaiAM non-profit listing](https://www.thaiofmn.org/resource/non-profit-listing)). That Thai families are pointed to Lao and Cambodian temples reflects shared Theravada practice; it does not mean the communities are the same. Websites for the Elk River and Osseo centers could not be found or opened.

**Christians.** Two Thai Christian congregations are listed. Thai Fellowship Church (คริสตจักรสายสัมพันธ์ไทย) launched Sept. 8, 2024 inside Grace Fellowship in Brooklyn Park and meets Sundays at 10:30 a.m. in Thai with English translation ([Thai Fellowship Church](https://www.thaifellowship.com/)). The Thai Christian Fellowship Church, or Thai Church of MN, is listed at Community of Nations Church in St. Paul ([ThaiAM non-profit listing](https://www.thaiofmn.org/resource/non-profit-listing)); a Converge article describes its pastor as a Bethel Seminary student ([Converge](https://converge.org/article/god-provides-converge-global-worker-in-twin-cities-inroad-to-thai-community)). That same Converge article treats Thai people as an "unreached people group" for evangelism and estimates 9,750 Thai in the Twin Cities, a figure far above any census count and not sourced ([Converge](https://converge.org/article/god-provides-converge-global-worker-in-twin-cities-inroad-to-thai-community)).

**Muslims and others.** No Minnesota source was found on Thai Muslims, Thai Catholics or Thai Minnesotans who follow no religion. Their absence from the record is not evidence of absence.

## 6. Celebrations and observances

**Songkran (Thai New Year), April 13 to 15.** In Thailand it is a three-day public holiday, April 13 to 15 in 2026, marked, in the Tourism Authority of Thailand's words, by "merit-making, water-pouring rituals for elders, and temple visits," along with water festivities ([Singapore Embassy in Bangkok, Thai holiday list 2026](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/251/8e1ca282-3965-4e24-8b61-764cc74923bd/Thai%20Public%20Holidays%20Updated%20May%202026.pdf); [Tourism Authority of Thailand, March 26, 2026](https://www.tatnews.org/2026/03/songkran-festival-2026-to-proceed-nationwide-welcoming-global-visitors-to-thailands-unesco-recognised-new-year-celebration/)). In Minnesota it is kept twice: a temple Songkran Day on the actual date (Sunday, April 13, 2025 at the wat) ([Wat Promwachirayan events](https://www.watthaimn.us/wat-thai-events)), and a large public festival in May.

**Minnesota Songkran Festival.** Organized by the Thai Cultural Council of Minnesota with Wat Promwachirayan.

- 2022: first festival, May 28 and 29, in front of the State Capitol ([Wat Promwachirayan, About Us](https://www.watthaimn.us/about-us)), billed as "a healing festival" after the pandemic and amid rising anti-Asian hate, organizer Korawan Muangmode told CCX Media ([CCX Media, May 27, 2022](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-resident-shares-thai-culture-at-minnesota-songkran-festival/)).
- 2023: May 27 and 28 at the Capitol, free, with Muay Thai, Miss Universe Thailand, tuk-tuk rides and a papaya-salad contest ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine, May 25, 2023](https://mspmag.com/arts-and-culture/minnesota-songkran-festival/)).
- 2024: May 18 and 19 at the Capitol. The program ran from a 10:30 a.m. alms offering through a "Four Regions of Thailand" parade, fashion shows, Muay Thai, a 6 p.m. Loy Krathong lantern segment, a Baai Sri Su Kwan blessing parade, a papaya salad contest and a drag show ([festival booklet](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2024/comm/20240319/Comm_20240319_SF5050-Songkran-2024-Full-Version.pdf)). Thailand's consulate estimated about 40,000 attendees; U.S. Senators Amy Klobuchar and Tina Smith delivered congratulatory remarks at the opening ([Thai MFA, June 2024](https://www.mfa.go.th/en/content/pr-chicago-06052024-2); [Thai MFA, May 31, 2024](https://mfa.go.th/en/content/pr-chicago-05312024-2)).
- 2025: fourth festival, May 10 and 11 at the Capitol, opened by Thailand's ambassador; Public Safety Commissioner Bob Jacobson presented a governor's proclamation ([Thai MFA, May 2025](https://www.mfa.go.th/en/content/chicago-230525-3); [Royal Thai Embassy, May 21, 2025](https://washingtondc.thaiembassy.org/en/content/minnesota-songkran-festival-2025)).
- 2026: May 16 and 17 at Wat Promwachirayan in St. Louis Park, 11 a.m. to 8 p.m. Saturday and 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. Sunday, as listed in a third-party community calendar ([Northern Star Scouting 2026 outreach calendar](https://www.northernstar.org/Portals/0/2026%20Outreach_Web_Cultural%20Events_260415a.pdf)). No organizer page or news report confirming the 2026 festival was held was found, and no reason for the move from the Capitol was found.

The Thai Cultural Council has described it as "one of America's largest Thai festivals" ([festival booklet](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2024/comm/20240319/Comm_20240319_SF5050-Songkran-2024-Full-Version.pdf)); the Thai consulate called it "one of the largest Thai Festivals in the United States" ([Thai MFA, June 2024](https://www.mfa.go.th/en/content/pr-chicago-06052024-2)). Both describe it as open to all Southeast Asian and Asian American communities, not only Thai.

**MinnesoThai Thai Street Food Festival.** A free fall festival run by the Thai Cultural Council and the temple, with proceeds supporting the temple. The council announced Sept. 19 and 20, 2026 at the temple ([Thai Cultural Council of Minnesota](https://www.thaiculturemn.us/)). An earlier edition on the weekend of Sept. 13 to 15, 2024 included a beer and wine garden, a drag show and a papaya-eating contest ([FOX 9](https://www.fox9.com/news/what-do-minnesota-things-do-weekend-sept-13-15)).

**Buddhist holy days.** Makha Bucha (full moon of the third lunar month; March 3 in Thailand in 2026), Visakha Bucha (sixth month; May 31, 2026), Asanha Bucha (eighth month; July 29, 2026) and the start of the rains retreat, Khao Phansa (July 30, 2026) ([Singapore Embassy holiday list](https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/251/8e1ca282-3965-4e24-8b61-764cc74923bd/Thai%20Public%20Holidays%20Updated%20May%202026.pdf)). At the Minnesota wat these are held on the nearest weekend (section 5). The end of the rains retreat, Ok Phansa, is followed by Kathina robe offerings.

**Loy Krathong.** The full moon of the twelfth Thai lunar month, usually November, when people float small offerings on water. A travel guide gives Nov. 25, 2026; other calendars give Nov. 24; the lunar date decides it ([UME Travel guide](https://www.umetravel.com/loy-krathong-festival/)). In Minnesota it has appeared as a lantern segment at the 2024 Songkran festival ([festival booklet](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2024/comm/20240319/Comm_20240319_SF5050-Songkran-2024-Full-Version.pdf)) and as ThaiAM's award-winning Festival of Nations exhibit theme in 2012 ([ThaiAM, Festival of Nations](https://www.thaiofmn.org/gallery/festival-of-nations)). No stand-alone Minnesota Loy Krathong event in 2025 or 2026 was found.

**Royal and national days.** Thai Mother's Day, Aug. 12, the birthday of Queen Mother Sirikit, and Father's Day and National Day, Dec. 5, the birthday of the late King Bhumibol, are public holidays in Thailand ([Human Resources Online, 2026 list](https://www.humanresourcesonline.net/full-list-of-thailand-s-2026-public-holidays)). The Minnesota wat marked "Thailand's National Day and Father's Day" on Sunday, Dec. 7, 2025, and a "Her Majesty Queen" observance on June 8, 2025 ([events page](https://www.watthaimn.us/wat-thai-events)). At the 2024 Songkran festival the consul-general held a ceremony for King Vajiralongkorn's July 28 birthday ([Thai MFA, May 31, 2024](https://mfa.go.th/en/content/pr-chicago-05312024-2)). ThaiAM organized a eulogy for King Bhumibol after his death in October 2016 ([ThaiAM, What We Do](https://www.thaiofmn.org/About-Us/what-we-do)).

**Community gatherings.** ThaiAM's Thai Picnic, held for "decades" at Island Lake County Park, took place Aug. 27, 2023, Aug. 25, 2024 and Aug. 24, 2025, after a pandemic pause ([ThaiAM events](https://www.thaiofmn.org/event/thaiam); [What We Do](https://www.thaiofmn.org/About-Us/what-we-do)). Thai-Minnesota Children's Day was held in 2017 and 2018 ([ThaiAM gallery](https://www.thaiofmn.org/gallery/thai-mn-childrens-day)). ThaiAM ran a Thai booth at the Festival of Nations from the 1990s until it withdrew after 2019 ([ThaiAM, Festival of Nations](https://www.thaiofmn.org/gallery/festival-of-nations)).

## 7. Family, home and daily life

Minnesota sources on Thai family life are few. What they show:

- **Mixed households.** The census shows many children of mixed heritage and many more Thai-alone women than men (section 1). Several public lives fit that pattern: Supenn Harrison married Minnesotan Bruce Harrison ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/1984-flashback-global-becomes-local/)), and ThaiAM's 2026 board includes members with Thai given names and non-Thai family names ([ThaiAM team](https://www.thaiofmn.org/About-Us/current-team)). These are illustrations, not a rule.
- **Three generations in one business.** Sawatdee is run by Supenn Harrison's daughters, and her grandchildren staff its food truck ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/1984-flashback-global-becomes-local/)). Krua Thai was opened by a father and daughter ([Twin Cities Business](https://tcbmag.com/family-thai/)).
- **Names.** ThaiAM lists people with both a legal name and a Thai nickname, which is how many Thai adults are known day to day ([ThaiAM team](https://www.thaiofmn.org/About-Us/current-team)).
- **Children and culture.** The Thai Arts and Culture School of Minnesota has taught language, arts, music and dance at the temple since late 2022, through a Thai university's program for Thai and international youth abroad ([Wat Promwachirayan, About Us](https://www.watthaimn.us/about-us); [Thai Cultural Council](https://www.thaiculturemn.us/)). ThaiAM's 2024 and 2025 picnics included a Children's Market where children ran their own stalls ([ThaiAM events](https://www.thaiofmn.org/event/thaiam)).
- **Food.** Food carries memory: Supenn Harrison's first business sold her mother's egg rolls, made with ground beef instead of Thai ground pork for Minnesota tastes ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/1984-flashback-global-becomes-local/)); her University host family, TPT reports, "turned their noses up" at Thai cooking ([TPT Originals, 2018](https://www.tptoriginals.org/state-fair-start-for-thai-food-in-mn/)).
- **Aging.** No Minnesota source on Thai elders was found. As national background, Pew reports a median age of 48.1 among Thai immigrants, with 25 percent 65 or older ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/race-and-ethnicity/fact-sheet/asian-americans-thai-in-the-u-s/)). A small 1999 Bangkok study of 15 adult children caring for frail parents described katanyu katavedi, gratitude and the duty to repay parents, as sustaining family care ([Kespichayawattana, Chulalongkorn University, 1999](https://digital.car.chula.ac.th/cuns/vol11/iss3/6/)); it is from Thailand and is not evidence about any Minnesota family.

## 8. Arts, media, sports and culture

**Dance and performance.** The temple's Thai Cultural Group of MN is described as "professional dancers and performers" who use traditional Thai culture to engage Southeast Asian Minnesotans and youth ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Watpromwachirayan)). Songkran programs have included classical dance, Khon masked dance, Lanna dance, traditional instruments, Ram Wong circle dancing and fashion shows of regional dress ([Royal Thai Embassy, May 2025](https://washingtondc.thaiembassy.org/en/content/minnesota-songkran-festival-2025); [festival booklet](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2024/comm/20240319/Comm_20240319_SF5050-Songkran-2024-Full-Version.pdf)). In 2024 the festival brought Waewdao Sirisook, a Lanna dancer and umbrella painter from Chiang Mai ([Thai MFA, June 2024](https://www.mfa.go.th/en/content/pr-chicago-06052024-2)). ThaiAM's Festival of Nations exhibits, many of them award winners, presented themes such as the Damnoen Saduak floating market, Loy Krathong, Mae Nak Phra Khanong, the Royal Ploughing Ceremony and the Phi Ta Khon ghost festival ([ThaiAM, Festival of Nations](https://www.thaiofmn.org/gallery/festival-of-nations)).

**Food as culture.** Thai food is the community's best-known public art in Minnesota. The State Fair egg-roll stand and the Siam Cafe were the start ([TPT Originals](https://www.tptoriginals.org/state-fair-start-for-thai-food-in-mn/)); Supenn Harrison was the subject of a documentary on how Asian immigrants shaped Minnesota's food and economy ([Andrew Zimmern](https://andrewzimmern.com/5-questions-supenn-harrison/)). Nuntana Erickson teaches Thai fruit and vegetable carving through Creative Carving ([Creative Carving](https://www.creative-carving.com/)).

**Sports.** Muay Thai demonstrations are a standard part of Minnesota Songkran ([festival booklet](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2024/comm/20240319/Comm_20240319_SF5050-Songkran-2024-Full-Version.pdf)). No Thai-owned Muay Thai gym or Thai Minnesotan athlete of public note was confirmed from an opened source.

**Media.** See section 4. No Minnesota Thai outlet was found.

## 9. Civic and political life

**Organizations.**

- *Thai Association of Minnesota (ThaiAM).* A 501(c)(3) nonprofit whose mission is "to support and promote the well-being of the Thai community in MN" ([ThaiAM](https://www.thaiofmn.org/)). It names Sombat Supunniam as advisor and Kin Chan as 2026 president, with a 16-person team ([ThaiAM team](https://www.thaiofmn.org/About-Us/current-team)). Its founding date is uncertain: Asia Matters says it was established as a nonprofit in 1995 ([Asia Matters for America](https://asiamattersforamerica.org/articles/thai-culture-in-minnesota)); ThaiAM held a 25th-anniversary luncheon in October 2019 ([ThaiAM events](https://www.thaiofmn.org/event/thaiam)); and its history page posts a 2013 article titled, in Thai, "Looking back 41 years of the Thai Association," which would date an earlier association to about 1972 ([ThaiAM, Our History](https://www.thaiofmn.org/About-Us/our-history)). The likeliest reading is an older informal association later incorporated, but no source says so directly. ThaiAM helps the Thai consulate in Chicago run mobile consular services (passports and documents) in Minnesota ([What We Do](https://www.thaiofmn.org/About-Us/what-we-do)), and its volunteers packed meals for Feed My Starving Children in 2024 and 2025 ([ThaiAM events](https://www.thaiofmn.org/event/thaiam)).
- *Thai Cultural Council of Minnesota.* Founded in 2019 in St. Louis Park; it says it is officially recognized by Thailand's culture ministry and serves Minnesota and neighboring states ([Thai Cultural Council, About](https://www.thaiculturemn.us/about)). ThaiAM lists it as "a division of Wat Promwachirayan" ([ThaiAM non-profit listing](https://www.thaiofmn.org/resource/non-profit-listing)). Its president, Korawan (Yin) Muangmode, has led the Songkran festival ([festival booklet](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2024/comm/20240319/Comm_20240319_SF5050-Songkran-2024-Full-Version.pdf)).
- *Thai Student Association #317, University of Minnesota.* Its mission is to "uphold and advance Thai culture" on campus and across Minnesota; its page lists 18 members and nine officers ([Gopherlink](https://gopherlink.umn.edu/0317)). It co-hosted a 2011 Thai Night with ThaiAM to raise money for flood victims in Thailand ([ThaiAM, What We Do](https://www.thaiofmn.org/About-Us/what-we-do)).
- *"Friends of Thailand."* The embassy describes a network of "civic, business, cultural, and Asian-American leaders" who support Thai events in Minnesota ([Royal Thai Embassy, May 2025](https://washingtondc.thaiembassy.org/en/content/university-of-msp)).

**Government ties.** The Council on Asian-Pacific Minnesotans has had Thai community board members since at least the early 1990s, including Vallapa (Pat) Siverhus ([CAPM report, 1993](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/docs/pre2003/mandated/930560.pdf)) and Dr. Kalong Sujjapunroj, vice chair in 1996 ([CAPM Biennium Report, 1996](https://www.leg.mn.gov/docs/pre2003/mandated/970013.pdf)). State law has also made room for the festival: a 2022 session law let St. Paul issue the Thai Cultural Council a temporary malt liquor license for both days of Songkran on the Capitol grounds, and a 2024 Senate bill proposed extending it to the council's concessions contractors ([Minnesota Revisor, SF 5050, 2024, introduction](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/bills/93/2024/0/SF/5050/versions/0/)). CAPM co-organized the 2024 Songkran festival ([Thai MFA, June 2024](https://www.mfa.go.th/en/content/pr-chicago-06052024-2)). In 2024 a Minnesota Senate resolution was introduced welcoming a senior Thai monk to the festival, and both U.S. senators delivered congratulatory remarks at its opening (sections 5 and 6). State Sen. Eric Pratt attended in 2025 ([Royal Thai Embassy, May 21, 2025](https://washingtondc.thaiembassy.org/en/content/minnesota-songkran-festival-2025)).

**Elected officials.** No Thai American elected official in Minnesota was found. Officials who were born in Thailand and are sometimes assumed to be Thai, such as Rep. Ethan Cha and Met Council member Chai Lee, are Hmong (section 1).

**Voting in Thailand.** Thai citizens in Minnesota vote in Thai elections by mail through the consulate in Chicago. For the Feb. 8, 2026 general election and constitutional referendum, overseas registration ran Dec. 20, 2025 to Jan. 5, 2026 for the election and just Jan. 3 to 5 for the referendum, and U.S. voters returned ballots to consulates including Chicago ([Thailand Government Public Relations Department](https://thailand.prd.go.th/en/content/category/detail/id/48/iid/468842); [Prachatai English](https://prachataienglish.com/node/11778)). Prachatai reported that U.S. voters found the separate three-day referendum registration confusing ([Prachatai English](https://prachataienglish.com/node/11778)). No Minnesota-specific turnout figure was found.

**Debates and concerns.** Few are documented. Supporting Thailand during its 2025 border conflict with Cambodia surfaced at ThaiAM's 2025 picnic, where attendees made cards that ThaiAM says were delivered to Thai soldiers on the border ([ThaiAM events](https://www.thaiofmn.org/event/thaiam); [ThaiAM home](https://www.thaiofmn.org/)). Minnesota also has a large Cambodian community; no source describes how the conflict affected relations here. The 2024 Songkran program included a drag show, and the festival's 2022 organizer spoke against anti-Asian hate ([festival booklet](https://www.lrl.mn.gov/archive/minutes/senate/2024/comm/20240319/Comm_20240319_SF5050-Songkran-2024-Full-Version.pdf); [CCX Media](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-resident-shares-thai-culture-at-minnesota-songkran-festival/)). No source was found on debates within the community about either.

## 11. Health and wellbeing, broadly

No Minnesota health data for Thai Minnesotans were found. Thai is not broken out in the state's main reports, and the group is too small for most surveys.

What exists:

- **Language access.** More than a quarter of Thai households were linguistically isolated in 2009 to 2011 ([CAPM 2012-2013](https://mn.gov/capm/assets/AnnualReport2012-2013_tcm1051-114384.pdf)). ThaiAM lists a Thai interpreting service in Minneapolis ([ThaiAM business listing](https://www.thaiofmn.org/resource/business-listing)). The Minnesota Department of Health keeps a voluntary roster of spoken-language health care interpreters searchable by language ([MDH roster](https://www.health.state.mn.us/facilities/providers/interpreter/index.html))
- **Information on health insurance and immigration.** ThaiAM has offered or planned seminars on health insurance, green cards and citizenship ([ThaiAM, What We Do](https://www.thaiofmn.org/About-Us/what-we-do)) and posted a Thai-language COVID-19 guide ([ThaiAM home](https://www.thaiofmn.org/)).
- **Spiritual and community supports.** The temple offers weekly meditation to the public ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Watpromwachirayan)), and the 2022 festival was framed as a "healing festival" after the pandemic ([CCX Media](https://ccxmedia.org/news/brooklyn-park-resident-shares-thai-culture-at-minnesota-songkran-festival/)).
- **Traditional practice.** ThaiAM's directory lists Thai massage businesses in Savage, Mound and White Bear Lake (section 3); none could be confirmed open.

As national background only: a 2001 UC San Francisco nursing dissertation by Pratima Bhuttarowas studied how first-generation Thai immigrant women in midlife experience and respond to symptoms ([Bhuttarowas, UCSF, 2001](https://escholarship.org/content/qt9846j4v9/qt9846j4v9.pdf)); it is not about Minnesota.

## 12. Disability

**Minnesota evidence.** No Minnesota-specific data, study or organization on disability among Thai Minnesotans was found. No Minnesota autism, special education or disability-services report that shows Thai Minnesotans separately was found, and no published disability rate for Thai Minnesotans was found. With Compass's Thai profile gone (section 1), no single public table gives one. No Thai-specific disability organization in Minnesota could be found.

**Questions worth asking, given what is known.**

- *Who defines disability here?* In Minnesota, a person applying for Medical Assistance on the basis of disability is certified either by Social Security or by the State Medical Review Team at DHS, after a county or tribal worker submits the request; Disability Hub MN says the SMRT process can take from 6 weeks to a year ([Disability Hub MN, SMRT common questions](https://disabilityhubmn.org/top-topics/other/state-medical-review-team-smrt/common-questions/)). Families who came as adults may have known a different system: a 2024 review comparing Taiwan and Thailand counts 266 speech-language pathologists in Thailand, nearly all in hospitals and rare in schools ([Westby et al., Neuropsychiatric Disease and Treatment, 2024](https://www.dovepress.com/autism-in-taiwan-and-thailand-influences-of-culture-peer-reviewed-fulltext-article-NDT)). That is about Thailand, not about how any family here will respond.
- *What does a label open or close?* In a small 2024 Bangkok study (a focus group of 10 caregivers and interviews with 13 others, the interviews conducted in English), caregivers said people with disabilities often avoided the disability verification needed for a hiring program because of stigma, "one reason why so few caregivers pursued formal diagnoses"; one mother said a doctor warned that a disability card would make it harder for her son to get a job because it exempted him from military service ([Mallory, PLOS ONE, 2024](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0306141)). Karma came up in the focus group, but one interviewee put it as "Not so much the karma [...] but just the stigma." The study is about Thailand and is not evidence about Minnesota families.
- *Where do families find strength?* Twelve Bangkok mothers of adolescents with intellectual disabilities described prayer, merit-making and temple visits as sources of calm, used alongside formal services ([Tejakum, Truesdale and Cairns, Journal of Applied Research in Intellectual Disabilities, 2026](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/389208/1/389208.pdf)). In Minnesota, the wat is one place such practice could happen; whether families use it that way is not documented.
- *What produced an impairment?* No Minnesota source addresses occupational injury, aging or other causes among Thai Minnesotans. Restaurant and massage work are physically demanding, but no source links them to disability rates in this community, and none should be assumed.
- *Who speaks?* With many children of mixed heritage and many more Thai-alone women than men (section 1), the person who handles English paperwork may not be the person whose needs are being assessed. That is a planning question, not a finding.

**Access routes.** Disability Hub MN (1-866-333-2466) is the state's free navigation service ([Disability Hub MN](https://disabilityhubmn.org/)). PACER Center serves parents of children with disabilities and offers interpreters on request, though its website shows no Thai materials ([PACER, contact](https://www.pacer.org/contact-us/)). The Minnesota Disability Law Center offers free legal help ([Mid-Minnesota Legal Aid](https://mylegalaid.org/disability-law-center/)).

## 13. What is changing now

- **The counting problem (2026).** Minnesota Compass no longer lists a Thai profile, and the state's 2026 economic report folds Thai into "Other Asian" (section 1). Thai Minnesotans are counted only in the census's detailed tables, with wide margins.
- **Songkran moved (2026).** After four years on the Capitol grounds, the 2026 festival was listed for the temple grounds in St. Louis Park, May 16 and 17 ([Northern Star Scouting calendar](https://www.northernstar.org/Portals/0/2026%20Outreach_Web_Cultural%20Events_260415a.pdf)). The fall MinnesoThai festival was announced for the temple in September 2026 ([Thai Cultural Council](https://www.thaiculturemn.us/)).
- **Growing Christian presence (2024).** A new Thai-language congregation, Thai Fellowship Church, launched in Brooklyn Park in September 2024 ([Thai Fellowship Church](https://www.thaifellowship.com/)).
- **Second generation.** The temple's school (since 2022) and ThaiAM's children's projects aim at children who may not speak Thai at home (sections 7 and 10).
- **Thai politics from afar.** Thai Minnesotans voted by mail in Thailand's Feb. 8, 2026 election and referendum, and in 2025 some sent support to Thai soldiers during the border conflict with Cambodia (section 9).
- **Immigration climate.** In January 2026, Reuters reported some 3,000 federal immigration officers deployed in the Minneapolis area ([Reuters via Hawaii Tribune-Herald, Jan. 21, 2026](https://www.hawaiitribune-herald.com/2026/01/21/nation-world-news/ice-broke-into-minnesota-home-forced-barely-clothed-man-outside-into-snow/)). No report was found naming Thai Minnesotans among those affected. Thai nationals in Minnesota include students and others on temporary visas, but no source gives their numbers or status, and none is assumed here.
- **Restaurants.** Long-running places continue (Sawatdee since 1983; Amazing Thailand since 2007), and new ones open in Greater Minnesota, such as Thai-riffic, a quick-service Thai restaurant across from Mayo Clinic's Saint Marys campus in Rochester, which monks from the Rochester Cambodian temple blessed when it opened in May 2025 ([KTTC, May 8, 2025](https://www.kttc.com/2025/05/08/monks-bless-opening-new-thai-restaurant-downtown-rochester/)).

## 14. Strengths and contributions

- **Thai food in Minnesota begins here.** Supenn Harrison opened what Mpls.St.Paul Magazine calls Minneapolis's first official Thai restaurant in 1979 and Sawatdee in 1983; her family credits her with the chili-scale ordering that Twin Cities diners use ([Mpls.St.Paul Magazine](https://mspmag.com/eat-and-drink/1984-flashback-global-becomes-local/)). Restaurants and food trucks in ThaiAM's directory reach towns from Ely to Worthington ([ThaiAM business listing](https://www.thaiofmn.org/resource/business-listing)).
- **A large public festival from a small community.** A community of about 4,500 people organized a festival the Thai consulate estimated drew about 40,000 in 2024, free to all ([Thai MFA, June 2024](https://www.mfa.go.th/en/content/pr-chicago-06052024-2)).
- **A temple that opens its doors.** Wat Promwachirayan turned an empty church into a Buddhist temple and cultural center, offers free public meditation and dance, and says its mission includes being "a hub for Southeast Asian culture" ([Wat Promwachirayan](https://www.watthaimn.us/); [GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Watpromwachirayan)).
- **Volunteer service.** ThaiAM has run consular help days, school presentations, food-packing for Feed My Starving Children, and fundraising for flood victims in Thailand ([ThaiAM](https://www.thaiofmn.org/About-Us/what-we-do); [events](https://www.thaiofmn.org/event/thaiam)).
- **Bridges across Southeast Asian Minnesota.** Both the temple and the cultural council describe their work as serving Southeast Asian communities broadly, and the 2024 Songkran festival included performances from other Asian communities in Minnesota ([GiveMN](https://www.givemn.org/organization/Watpromwachirayan); [Thai MFA, June 2024](https://www.mfa.go.th/en/content/pr-chicago-06052024-2)).

## What could not be established

- **Minnesota socioeconomic figures.** No current, opened source gives income, poverty, home ownership, education, employment, health insurance or disability rates for Thai Minnesotans. Minnesota Compass no longer shows a Thai profile, its former page could not be opened in an archive, and the state's 2026 economic report folds Thai into "Other Asian." Figures circulating from that former profile (about 6,738 people, a $72,888 median household income, 17.8 percent poverty) could not be confirmed.
- **Why women far outnumber men among Thai-alone Minnesotans.** The census shows it; no Minnesota source explains it.
- **Thai speakers and K-12 students.** The census groups Thai with Lao; the state's English learner reports do not list Thai.
- **A count of Thai-owned businesses.** No chamber, directory or survey counts them. ThaiAM's list is self-reported and partly out of date.
- **2026 Songkran.** Only a third-party calendar lists the May 16 and 17, 2026 dates at the temple. Explore Minnesota's listing was blocked, and no organizer page or news report confirming the festival happened was found. The reason for leaving the Capitol was not found.
- **Other temples and churches.** Current websites for the Meditation Center of Minnesota (Elk River), Minnesota Meditation Center USA (Osseo) and the Thai Church of MN could not be found or opened.
- **Thai Muslims, Thai Catholics, and Thai Minnesotans of no religion.** No Minnesota source.
- **LGBTQ Thai Minnesotans.** Beyond drag performances on festival programs, no Minnesota source.
- **Elders, aging and caregiving** in Minnesota. No source.
- **Disability** among Thai Minnesotans. No Minnesota data, study or organization.
- **Sports and arts figures.** No Thai Minnesotan athlete, writer, visual artist or musician of public note was confirmed.
- **Blocked or unavailable pages:** Explore Minnesota (blocked), the Council on Asian Pacific Minnesotans board page for its Thai community member (blocked), the state Legacy grants page (blocked), Sahan Journal (rate-limited), Star Tribune (blocked), KARE 11 (blocked), and the Internet Archive copy of the Compass Thai page (unreachable).
