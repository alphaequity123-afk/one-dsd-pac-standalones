      Learning stages

      Explore foundations, intercultural practice, applied equity, and leadership at a pace that fits your work.

      1. Stage 1

        Welcome and orientation

        See what the program offers and find an easy place to begin, without needing to be an expert.

        • Choose a route that fits the work in front of you
        • Distinguish private learning from required processes
        • Know when a source or a person holds the needed authority

        Explore related resources →

      2. Stage 2

        Foundations

        Build shared language for equity, access, culture, bias, belonging, and the experiences people bring to the work.

        • Recognize barriers and unequal effects without reducing people to labels
        • Use cultural humility and plain language in everyday work
        • Connect individual experience with organizational conditions

        Explore related resources →

      3. Stage 3

        Intercultural practice

        Develop the ability to notice different perspectives, work across cultural difference, and adapt behavior with care.

        • Recognize when similarity is being emphasized over meaningful difference
        • Ask before assuming what another person needs or means
        • Use reflection, feedback, and coaching to practice a more adaptive response

        Explore related resources →

      4. Stage 4

        Applied equity

        Bring equity and access into workforce, policy, program, service, engagement, fiscal, and communication decisions early enough to shape them.

        • Use practical questions and tools on a current decision
        • Involve affected people and responsible partners before choices harden
        • Make something you can use, with owners and a review date

        Explore related resources →

      5. Stage 5

        Systems and structural practice

        Examine patterns, power, burden, disparities, and institutional conditions across more than one decision or team.

        • Move from isolated events to patterns supported by evidence
        • Identify structures that reproduce barriers or unequal results
        • Design accountability and learning without surveillance

        Explore related resources →

      6. Stage 6

        Leadership and continuity

        Sustain change through supervision, mentoring, sponsorship, shared governance, repair, resources, and learning over time.

        • Connect leadership behavior with structural responsibility
        • Build mentoring, feedback, repair, and development into the work
        • Create durable ownership, review cycles, and continuity through change

      Guided practice

      Consider equity and access in a program, policy, conversation, or session you are planning.

      • New program or service concept

        You have a program idea and want a clear way to consider equity and access before the design is settled.

      • Policy, form, or notice change

        You are preparing a policy, form, notice, or letter and want to make sure it is clear, accessible, and workable for the people who will use it.

      • Community engagement or co-design effort

        You are planning community engagement and want the work to be respectful, useful, and grounded in what people actually need.

      • Workplace culture and team climate

        You have noticed a concern with participation or team climate and want practical ways to improve everyday team practices.

      • Planning a learning or facilitation session

        You have a session to lead and want participants to leave with something they can use in their work.

      • Inclusive hiring and selection

        You are responsible for a hiring process and want it to find capable people the current process may be screening out.

      • Equity analysis for a decision

        You own or shape a decision and want to build equity into it while it can still change, and to know whether an equity scan or a full analysis is required.

      • Accessible and culturally responsive meeting

        You are running a meeting and want everyone to be able to take part, contribute, and know what was decided.

      • Reviewing a draft for equity, access, and plain language

        You have a draft and want a structured way to check it for equity, access, plain language, sources, and burden before release.

      • Procurement or contract equity review

        You are shaping a contract, grant, or procurement and want equity, accessibility, and language access built into the solicitation, evaluation, terms, and monitoring.

      • Leadership pathway or sponsorship plan for staff with disabilities

        You shape how people move into lead and leadership roles and want the pathway to work for staff with disabilities without asking anyone to disclose.

      • Equity Pause for a routine decision

        You are about to make a routine decision and want a short, honest check of who it affects and what you might change before it goes out.

      • Checking your own material for access, assumptions, and tone

        You wrote something and want to check it yourself in three passes before it goes out: whether people can read and reach it, whether it assumes knowledge or norms people may not share, and whether it invites rather than warns.
